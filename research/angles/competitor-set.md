# The competitor set and its monitoring (Keepa product data)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

One more tag is used here because this angle rests on a data supplier: [keepa-doc] is Keepa's own documentation of its own data (keepa.com/api-docs and Keepa's Java client on GitHub). It is authoritative for what a Keepa field means, never for what Amazon does.

## What this covers

Stage: all three, as market context. The competitor set is not a funnel stage of its own; it is the comparison group that every stage is read against. Exposure: who else wins the same search terms. Click-through: whose main image, title, price, badge and rating sit beside ours on the results page. Conversion: whose price, coupon, deal, stock and Buy Box state a shopper weighs on the detail page.

Skills fed:
- **listing-review**: the trigger family "competitor moves" (price cut, coupon or deal, stock-out, title or image change, review surge, variation split or merge) is read off the competitor set; which Keepa fields describe listing content and which only describe the market.
- **effect-review**: the competitor set's movement over the same window is the first thing subtracted before a change is credited. Both the set and that subtraction have limits, set out below. The gap fill adds the rule that the set is fixed from pre-period data, and that its behaviour after the change is read for signs of reaction before it is subtracted.
- **listing-change-log**: the change file records which competitor set the expectation was argued against, so the observation window compares like with like.
- **/listing-watch**: the standing-instruction file names the set, how often it is read, and what movement is worth telling the seller.

Why a senior operator cares: most "my listing got worse" moments are the market moving, not the listing. A competitor's coupon, a Lightning Deal, a rival stock-out or a category-wide seasonal swing can move our sessions and conversion more than any copy change. Without a fixed, recorded set and its own dated observations, cx cannot tell a listing problem from a market move, and cannot judge an effect. The two run days showed the cost. On day 1, cx picked three of five competitors before knowing what they were. On day 2, cx argued from figures it had no file for, and it lost "yesterday" because a fresh Keepa read overwrote yesterday's `product.json` (beta-browser run reports, 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14).

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### Rules: enforced by Amazon's systems or policies

**Brand Analytics is the one Amazon source that names other brands' winning ASINs per search term, and it is gated.**
- The Amazon Search Terms report (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_TERMS_REPORT`) "Contains data on the most-clicked ASINs by search keyword and department". Its schema says it covers "the top-3 clicked ASINs by search keyword and department". Its fields are `departmentName`, `searchTerm`, `searchFrequencyRank`, `clickedAsin`, `clickShareRank`, `clickShare` and `conversionShare`. [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics, read 2026-10-14; sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
- `clickShare` is "The fraction of clicks that the product received compared to the total clicks for the searchTerm within the departmentName". `conversionShare` is the same fraction for conversions. [official] (same schema)
- Availability: "Sellers and vendors who have the Brand Analytics Selling Partner API role, are registered in Amazon Brand Registry, and are a brand representative." It "can only be requested", not scheduled. Periods are `DAY`, `WEEK`, `MONTH` and `QUARTER`, and "Requests cannot span multiple reporting periods"; a week runs Sunday to Saturday. The report "is limited to approximately 12 million items", which "may cause this report to differ from other reporting". [official] (Analytics Reports)
- The Search Query Performance report gives, per ASIN and query, Amazon-wide totals beside the ASIN's own counts: `totalQueryImpressionCount` with `asinImpressionShare`, `totalClickCount` with `asinClickShare`, `totalCartAddCount` with `asinCartAddShare`, and `totalPurchaseCount` with `asinPurchaseShare` (field names also in sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14). Its `asin` option has "a 200 character limit". [official] (Analytics Reports)
  - Gap fill: the Seller Central help page for the dashboard says the search queries "includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers. It excludes traffic from widgets on the search page such as "Top Rated " or "New Arrivals."" The totals a competitor's share is read against therefore include sponsored placements. The SQP schema's `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime` say a WEEK request "must be a Sunday" / "must be a Saturday" "or else a fatal error returns". [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5 r0, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, read through the help hub's GetHelpTopic content call (route in method/help-page-access.md), 2026-10-14, text saved as capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
  - Reconciliation: the same page has a dashboard-only competitor view. "The Query Detail view provides visibility into the performance of the top 10 ASINs for the selected search query from the Search Query Performance dashboard in comparison to your selected ASIN. The top performance is selected based on the Query ASIN score, calculated using a combination of search funnel impressions, clicks, cart adds, and buys." The page also warns: "we do not recommend comparing the metric names of data across different dashboards." The SQP report schema read for this file has no list of other ASINs (its per-row data is the requested `asin`, the query, and the `total*` / `asin*` figures), so that view is Seller Central only; cx gets it only from the seller. The "top 10" is a figure for human readers. [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, capture G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5.txt; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, re-read 2026-10-14)
- The Market Basket Analysis report (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_MARKET_BASKET_REPORT`) lists items "most commonly purchased in combination with" the seller's items, and "a `purchasedWithAsin` property might or might not be an ASIN in the selling partner's catalog". [official] (Analytics Reports) Gap fill, read at the source:
  - The schema defines `purchasedWithAsin` as "a product that was purchased in the same customer order as the asin", `purchasedWithRank` as "The relative frequency of the purchasedWithAsin and the asin having been purchased together. Rank=1 means purchasedWithAsin is the most common product purchased in the same customer order as asin", and `combinationPct` as the "Percentage of customer orders that contain both the selling partner's product and the purchasedWithAsin in comparison to the total number of customer orders that contained at least two different items including the selling partner's product". Periods are DAY, WEEK, MONTH and QUARTER, and "Requests can span multiple reporting periods" (unlike the Search Terms report). [official] (sellingPartnerMarketBasketAnalysisReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerMarketBasketAnalysisReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
  - The report "can only be requested"; it takes `reportPeriod` and no ASIN option; it needs the Brand Analytics role and Brand Registry. [official] (Analytics Reports, re-read 2026-10-14)
  - The help page shows "the top three products that wre [sic] most frequently bought at the same time as the Brand Representative's products". Other brands' products appear "only ... if they are one of the top three products bought at the same time". A Display menu offers "Same brand combination only" and "Same brand combination excluded", and the page says: "These views make it easy to identify bundling opportunities and determine directly competing products." "The report retains three years of data." [official] (Market Basket Analysis dashboard, GQRPYRECLEPVTRAN v5 r1, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GQRPYRECLEPVTRAN, read through GetHelpTopic 2026-10-14)
  - The Brand Analytics blog frames the same dashboard as bundling and cross-marketing: "Create Virtual Bundles to increase average order size", "Identify cross-marketing opportunities to promote related products", with the example of baby bottles bought with "baby bottle brushes from other sellers". [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business, Oct 8, 2025, re-read 2026-10-14)
  - So Amazon's own two pages pull two ways. The blog and the schema describe complements (bought in the same order). The help page says the other-brand view helps "determine directly competing products", and does not say how. Same-order co-purchase is therefore not, on Amazon's own word, proof that an ASIN is a complement; nor is it proof that it is a substitute. The earlier draft's "These are complements, not substitutes" is withdrawn as stated too strongly.
- The Repeat Purchase report (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_REPEAT_PURCHASE_REPORT`) names no other brand's ASIN. Its schema covers "the quantity of repeated purchases of the selling partner's items", with `orders`, `uniqueCustomers`, `repeatCustomersPctTotal`, `repeatPurchaseRevenue` ("Returns are not reflected") and `repeatPurchaseRevenuePctTotal`, by `asin` "in the selling partner's catalog"; periods WEEK, MONTH, QUARTER, spanning allowed. The help page: "The report shows all of the products associated with brands that have successfully registered with the Amazon Brand Registry", a repeat purchase is counted only "within the selected reporting time range, where the sale from the first order for that brand or ASIN is excluded", and returns and cancellations "are not considered". It is no evidence of who the substitutes or complements are; at most it describes the seller's own products' repurchase. [official] (sellingPartnerRepeatPurchaseReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerRepeatPurchaseReport.json; Repeat Purchase Behavior report, GVV2TFEFUDRK55S7 v5 r0, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GVV2TFEFUDRK55S7, read through GetHelpTopic; Analytics Reports; all read 2026-10-14)
- Two reports practitioners still cite are gone. `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_ALTERNATE_PURCHASE_REPORT` and `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_ITEM_COMPARISON_REPORT` were deprecated April 13, 2022 and removed June 30, 2022. [official] (SP-API Deprecation Schedule, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-deprecations, read 2026-10-14)

**Amazon's APIs return a competitor's catalogue page and offers, but no history.**
- `getCatalogItem` (Catalog Items v2022-04-01) takes any `asin` and an `includedData` list: `attributes`, `classifications`, `dimensions`, `identifiers`, `images`, `productTypes`, `relationships`, `salesRanks`, `summaries`, `vendorDetails`.
  - `salesRanks` carries `classificationRanks` and `displayGroupRanks`.
  - `relationships` carries `parentAsins`, `childAsins` and the `variationTheme`.
  - Attributes "conform to the related Amazon product type definitions".
  - The role is Product Listing.

  [official] (Catalog Items API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/catalog-items-api, and catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/catalog-items-api-model/catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, both read 2026-10-14)
- `getCompetitiveSummary` (Product Pricing v2022-05-01) accepts `includedData` values `featuredBuyingOptions`, `referencePrices`, `lowestPricedOffers` and `similarItems`, batched; the model caps a batch at 20 requests. [official] (productPricing_2022-05-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/product-pricing-api-model/productPricing_2022-05-01.json, read 2026-10-14)
  - `similarItems` "Provides a list of up to 20 ASINs which are similar to the item". The release note says it is there "to identify comparable products for a given ASIN and Amazon store". It was added in the April 1, 2026 notes. [official] (getCompetitiveSummary reference, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/getcompetitivesummary, and SP-API Release Notes, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes, read 2026-10-14)
  - Since the July 29, 2026 release, offers can carry a `promotions` array of type `COUPON`, `DEAL` ("for example, Lightning Deal or Best Deal") or `BASKET_BUILDING`. [official] (Release Notes; PromotionType in the model)
  - Gap fill, what the reply holds for any ASIN asked about (productPricing_2022-05-01.json, re-read 2026-10-14):
    - `featuredBuyingOptions`: "the current segmented featured offer information for the item". `buyingOptionType` "currently supports `NEW`". Each `segmentedFeaturedOffers` entry is an offer plus `featuredOfferSegments`, where a segment is "a group of regional contexts that all have the same featured offer", keyed by `customerMembership` (`PRIME`, `NON_PRIME`, `DEFAULT` "whose Prime membership status is unknown") and `segmentDetails.glanceViewWeightPercentage`, "the glance views for this segment as a percentage of total glance views across all segments for the ASIN". A competitor's featured offer is therefore not one price: it can differ by Prime status and region. Amazon's help page says the same in words: "customers may see different Featured Offers when shopping from different shipping locations or when enrolled in a Prime membership", and "the Featured Offer is determined separately for each variation" (Becoming the Featured Offer, G201687550 v20, capture capture `G201687550-v20`, read 2026-10-14). The older Featured Offer page G37911 v19 now reads only "This page has been removed", and Featured Offer eligibility G200418100 v23 says "As of September 2026, Amazon no longer maintains a seller eligibility requirement" (captures G37911-v19.txt, G200418100-v23.txt).
    - `lowestPricedOffers`: "A list of up to 20 lowest priced offers" per `lowestPricedOffersInput`; up to 5 inputs per request; `itemCondition` defaults to `New`; `offerType` has the single value `Consumer`.
    - Each offer carries `sellerId`, `condition`, `fulfillmentType` (`AFN` / `MFN`), `listingPrice` ("This doesn't include shipping, points, or applicable promotions"), `shippingOptions`, `primeDetails.eligibility` (`NATIONAL`, `REGIONAL`, `NONE`) and `promotions` (each with `promotionSavings.discountedPrice`, "The final price after the promotion is applied"). A comparison of shelf prices must add shipping and subtract promotions, or say it did not.
    - `referencePrices`: named prices "e.g., `CompetitivePriceThreshold`, `WasPrice`, `CompetitivePrice`". The Pricing FAQ says the Competitive Price Threshold is "based on competitive prices from other retailers (excluding other Amazon sellers)".
    - The FAQ says the operation "builds upon the `getItemOffers` operation by providing more details about competitive pricing for a given ASIN". Neither the FAQ, the use-case page nor the model says whether the reply is complete for an ASIN on which the caller has no offer (Open questions). The role is Pricing (Product Pricing API page).

    [official] (productPricing_2022-05-01.json; Product Pricing API and Notifications FAQ, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/pricing-faq; Product Pricing API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/product-pricing-api; all read 2026-10-14)

**Amazon's keyword catalogue search is a candidate source with fixed caps, not a ranking (gap fill).**
- `searchCatalogItems` (Catalog Items v2022-04-01): "Search for a list of Amazon catalog items and item-related information. You can search by identifier or by keywords."
  - `keywords` is "A comma-delimited list of keywords" (the model caps the list at 20 entries); `identifiers` and `keywords` cannot be sent together; `brandNames` and `classificationIds` "limit the search in queries based on `keywords`"; `marketplaceIds` takes one value.
  - `pageSize` has a maximum of 20 and a default of 10; further pages come by `pageToken` from `pagination.nextToken`.
  - `numberOfResults`: "For searches that are based on `keywords`, `numberOfResults` is the estimated total number of Amazon catalog items that are matched by the search query. Only results up to the page count limit are returned per request regardless of the number found." Then: "**Note:** The maximum number of items (ASINs) that can be returned and paged through is 1,000."
  - `refinements` returns `brands` and `classifications`, each with "The estimated number of results that would be returned if you refine your search by" that brand or classification.
  - `includedData` takes the same ten values as `getCatalogItem`, default `summaries`.

  [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; searchCatalogItems, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/searchcatalogitems; Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 Reference, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/catalog-items-api-v2022-04-01-reference; all read 2026-10-14)
- **Order.** No Amazon documentation page states how keyword results are ordered. In the public issue "searchCatalogItems sorting?" (amzn/selling-partner-api-models #1425, opened 2021-06-04), the replier chapmanjw wrote on 2021-07-20 that "The new Catalog Search API sorts results based on popularity/relevancy", and on 2021-07-21 that it "is focused on "sellable" products as opposed to "buyable" products (where search relevancy is based on seller popularity instead of buyer popularity)" and that "we would not be able to replicate the search behavior of the retail websites exactly". A developer (ellis, 2021-09-24) relayed a support-case answer: it "presents ASINs in the same order as you would see them on the Seller Central product search page. These results can include ASINs that might be very relevant to seller's but not at all relevant to a buyer." GitHub shows chapmanjw's association as NONE, so the replies are Amazon's only by their content, and they are five years old. [official, as relayed in a public issue] (searchCatalogItems sorting? #1425, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/issues/1425, read 2026-10-14)
- **Rates disagree across Amazon's own pages.** The model and the operation page show 2 requests per second, burst 2, for `searchCatalogItems`; the "Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 Reference" page shows 5 and 5 for both `searchCatalogItems` and `getCatalogItem`. All three say the figure is a default and that "The `x-amzn-RateLimit-Limit` response header contains the usage plan rate limits for the operation, when available." The live figure is the header, which is door.md's. [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; searchCatalogItems; Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 Reference; read 2026-10-14)
- `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` fires on "a change to any of the top 20 offers, by condition", on a Buy Box winner or price change, or on an external price change. However: "You will only receive `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` notifications for items for which you have active offers." It is no feed for a competitor's own ASIN. [official] (Notification Type Values, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, read 2026-10-14)
- `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` fires on "a change to the title, description, bullet points, or images, for any ASIN that the selling partner has a brand relationship with". That covers our own brand's ASINs, not competitors'. [official] (Notification Type Values)
- The Data Kiosk Seller Analytics dataset groups by the seller's own `asin`, `parentAsin`, `brand` and similar keys. No competitor metric was found in its guide. [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide, read 2026-10-14)
- Rates are Amazon's per-operation usage plans, printed on the reference pages and in the models, and the pages do not all agree: the Catalog model prints `getCatalogItem` at 2 per second with a burst of 2 while the "Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 Reference" page prints 5 and 5 (bullet above); the Pricing model and reference print `getCompetitiveSummary` at 0.033 per second with a burst of 1. These figures are for human readers; the binding value is the `x-amzn-RateLimit-Limit` header of the live reply. All are stated as defaults: selling partners with higher demand "might have higher rate and burst values" (Catalog model) or "may receive higher rate and burst values" (Pricing reference). Treat them as door.md's, not the playbook's. [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; getCompetitiveSummary reference; productPricing_2022-05-01.json)

**Since 2026-07-27, what a competitor submits as a title may show split across the title and Item Highlights.**
- "This change is effective starting July 27, 2026", for non-media product types submitted through the Listings Items API or `JSON_LISTINGS_FEED`. "This is a gradual rollout. This change does not affect your current listings unless you submit an update."
- `title_differentiation` (Item Highlights) is "Up to 125 characters" beside `item_name` "Up to 75 characters", and "Item Highlights appear below titles in search results and on product detail pages. They are fully searchable."

At run time the product-type schema holds the limits. [official] (Submit modular titles, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/submit-modular-titles, page updated 2026-09-10, read 2026-10-14)

### Guidance: Amazon's recommendations and descriptions

- **Best Sellers Rank.**
  - "Amazon calculates Best Sellers Ranks using sales volume data."
  - "Both recent sales and all-time sales factor into a BSR, though recent sales count more than older sales."
  - "BSRs aren't influenced by page views or customer reviews."
  - "A product can have more than one BSR number if it falls under multiple categories."
  - "A sales rank doesn't necessarily indicate product quality."
  - Movers & Shakers "showcase the biggest gainers in sales rank over the past 24 hours."

  No update schedule is given beyond "frequently updated". [official] (Guide to Amazon sales rank: Best Sellers Rank (BSR), https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-best-sellers-rank, published Sep 3, 2025, read 2026-10-14)
- **Sales Rank FAQ (Seller Central help, reconciliation).** Read from the capture capture `G202059240-v2` (Sales Rank FAQ, G202059240 v2 r0, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202059240, read through GetHelpTopic 2026-10-14):
  - "Sales rank is calculated based on all-time sales of an ASIN/Product where recent sales are weighted more than older sales."
  - "Sales ranks are relative. While your product has had a lot of sales, there may have been other products in the same category that might also have seen an increase."
  - "Sales rank is generated based on order data and product reviews are not taken into consideration."
  - Timing: "While we update best sellers list every hour, it can take up to 12 hours for an order to show the sales rank." (Figures for human readers.)
  - Variations: "In some stores, we add up all the sales for a child ASIN and apply it to the parent ASIN. In these stores, parent ASINs and child ASINs will have the same rank. In other stores, we count the orders for each child ASIN separately and generate sales rank for each child ASIN."
  - "Sales ranks are displayed for an ASIN with a sale record and if it has been assigned to the featured categories."

  [official] For a competitor watch: a rank is relative to the category's movement, and whether a competitor's child and parent share one rank depends on the store, so a rank comparison between a parent and a child, or across stores, is not like with like.
- **Top Search Terms dashboard.** It shows "the product titles for the three top-clicked products for each search term, along with click shares and conversion shares". Search Query Performance "provides a breakdown of your brand's share of performance compared to overall query performance". Market Basket shows "the top three products customers most frequently purchase with the products you offer". Access: Brand Registry and "a Professional selling account". [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/brand-analytics, Oct 8, 2025; Amazon Brand Analytics, https://sell.amazon.com/tools/amazon-brand-analytics; both read 2026-10-14)
- **Amazon's own grouping of a competitor set: the Product Opportunity Explorer "niche".**
  - "A niche is a collection of customer search terms and products that represent a specific customer need."
  - Products are included if they "cumulatively received 80% of clicks from those search terms".
  - "Niche metrics are refreshed at the beginning of each week, and new niches are created at the start of each month".
  - "search terms and products can exist in multiple niches".

  The tool page says it is only a guide. No SP-API operation for it appears in the SP-API page index. [official] (Seller Forums "All about niches", post by Danika_Amazon — an "_Amazon" staff handle; the page shows it "updated by moderator 4 years ago", so the wording dates from about 2022 — https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/681c4de8-ceaa-4042-b41f-dedd05662535; Get product ideas with Product Opportunity Explorer, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/product-opportunity-explorer, Apr 10, 2023; Product Opportunity Explorer, https://sell.amazon.com/tools/product-opportunity-explorer; all read 2026-10-14)
- **Read competitors' listing text.** "Analyze competitor listings by reviewing the titles, bullet points, and descriptions on product listings for best-selling products like yours". [official] (Improve product visibility through effective Amazon keyword research, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-keyword-research, Oct 1, 2024, read 2026-10-14)
- **Promotions a competitor can run, and where shoppers see them.** [official] (Guide to running Amazon seller promotions, deals, coupons, and discounts, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/seller-promotions, May 27, 2026, read 2026-10-14)
  - "A Lightning Deal can run from 4 to 12 hours, as determined by Amazon"; a Best Deal "for a select number of days".

  - "Keepa Time minutes is the time format used for all timestamps of the product object"; to convert, "(keepaTime + 21564000) * 60000" gives Unix milliseconds.

- **Content fields carry current values only.**
  - `title` comes with this caution: "Descriptive content that does not fit into the title is published separately in itemHighlights, so read both fields".
  - `itemHighlights` is "Amazon's Item Highlights", "searchable", displayed "together with the title in search results and on the product detail page".

Keepa's Java client says 2000 for the same field and adds "This limitation may change in the future without prior notice" (Product.java, re-read 2026-10-14). Keepa's two documents disagree; the live answer is the length of the string a read returns, and a description at exactly either length is treated as possibly cut.

Keepa's two documents disagree (4000 against 50), and neither is dated, so the earlier draft's "the documentation page is newer" is withdrawn as unproven.   - 0 AMAZON, 1 NEW, 3 SALES (rank), 4 LISTPRICE.

## What practitioners add

- **Choose by the shopper's cross-shop, not by category.** Tanveer Abbas (Ecombrainly, "Amazon Competitor Analysis: A Step-by-Step Guide for Sellers", updated July 15, 2026) [opinion] (https://ecombrainly.com/amazon-competitor-analysis/, read 2026-10-14):
  - Search your 3 to 5 most important keywords, mark "7 to 10 relevant competitors", then keep a tracking list of "3 to 5 competitors" that are "nearly identical to yours in design, pack size, flavour, and price".
  - Only compare what a shopper would cross-shop.
  - Skip dominant giants: "A brand with 40,000 reviews isn't your competitor."
  - Cadence: weekly Keepa alerts on price moves and stock-outs; a monthly "Top Search Terms click-share check"; quarterly, re-check "whether both lists still hold the right ASINs".
- **Page one is the set.** Emplicit ("Ultimate Guide to Competitor Analysis for Amazon SEO", Feb 25, 2026, no author shown) [opinion] (https://emplicit.co/ultimate-competitor-analysis-amazon-seo-guide/, read 2026-10-14):
  - "Pay attention to the top 10–20 products on the first page".
  - Watch BSR, review count and its speed, price, stock, ads, share of shelf, Q&A and low-star reviews.
  - Price daily, keyword lists every 2 to 4 weeks, competitors weekly, a full review quarterly.
- **Keep the set stable.** Srushti P. Borle (SalesDuo, "Amazon Share of Voice: How to Measure, Analyze, and Improve Visibility", updated Aug 26, 2026) [opinion] (https://salesduo.com/blog/amazon-share-of-voice/, read 2026-10-14):
  - Choose "brands and ASINs competing for the same customer and search intent".
  - "keep that competitor set as stable as possible so changes in SOV reflect marketplace movement rather than changes in who you are measuring against" (wording re-read 2026-10-14; the earlier draft's "rather than a shifting comparison group" is not on the page).
  - "Record the keyword set, competitor set, organic and sponsored placements included, ranking depth, weighting method, and reporting period."
- **Analyse listings, not companies.** Courtney Billions (Helium 10, "How to Conduct an Amazon Competitor Analysis", Jan 18, 2023, modified June 5, 2024): "Focus on analyzing a specific product listing; not the competitor as a whole." The article names no selection criteria and no cadence. [opinion] (https://www.helium10.com/blog/how-to-conduct-an-amazon-competitor-analysis-helium-10/, read 2026-10-14)
- **Share of voice can be linear or weighted.** Lena Einschütz (eComEngine, "Share of Voice: What It Is and How to Get More", May 16, 2023, updated Jan 4, 2024): a linear count of placements, or weighting where "upper placements would therefore have a 'higher' value". [opinion] (https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/share-of-voice, read 2026-10-14)
- **"Bought in past month" as competitor sales data.** Juozas Kaziukėnas (Marketplace Pulse, "Amazon Made Sales Data Public", Mar 17, 2023), at launch:
  - It is "rounded to hundreds, thousands, or tens of thousands and is capped at one hundred thousand".

  - "The data is not yet available to sellers through Seller Central or APIs."

**Where they disagree:**
- **Set size.** Ecombrainly tracks 3 to 5; Emplicit watches 10 to 20.
- **Giants.** Ecombrainly excludes them; Emplicit's page-one rule includes them.
- **Changing the set.** SalesDuo wants it stable; Ecombrainly re-picks quarterly. The two can both hold if the set is versioned: re-pick on a schedule, but never inside an observation window.

## Published research

- **McAuley, Pandey and Leskovec, "Inferring Networks of Substitutable and Complementary Products", KDD 2015, pp. 785–794 (DOI 10.1145/2783258.2783381).** [research] (arXiv 1506.08839 and its PDF; Crossref metadata; read 2026-10-14)
  - The authors crawled four Amazon graphs ("Users who viewed x also viewed y", "viewed x eventually bought y", "bought x also bought y", "frequently bought x and y together"). "We refer to edges of type 1 and 2 as substitutes and edges of type 3 or 4 as complements": a labelling choice for ground truth, not a finding.
  - In a human evaluation on "a random sample of 200 Clothing items", judges compared the authors' model's recommendations with Amazon's; "Amazon's 'also viewed' links generate preferable substitutes, indicating that large volumes of browsing data yield acceptable substitute relationships with relatively little noise." For complements, the authors' own links were preferred over Amazon's "also bought".
  - Transfer: co-viewing is a reasonable signal for a substitute (competitor) set; that co-purchase data marks complements is the paper's working definition, consistent with Amazon's own Market Basket description, rather than something the paper proved. One category, one crawl (pre-2015).
- **Kim, Albuquerque and Bronnenberg, "Online Demand Under Limited Consumer Search", Marketing Science 29(6), 2010, pp. 1001–1023.** [research] (Tilburg University research record; read 2026-10-14)
  - Using Amazon.com aggregate search data for camcorders, consumer search "is typically limited to 10–15 choice options".
  - Transfer: a shopper weighs a small set, so a competitor set of roughly that order is behaviourally plausible. It does not fix a number for other categories.
- **Kim, Albuquerque and Bronnenberg, "Mapping Online Consumer Search", Journal of Marketing Research 48(1), 2011, pp. 13–27 (Crossref; working paper SSRN 1432292).** [research] (HKUST Business Insight summary; Crossref; read 2026-10-14)
  - From products "searched together" on Amazon.com for 62 digital camcorders (four brands, three media formats, a year of data from June 2006, per the summary), the authors built search maps.
  - Per the HKUST summary, "consumer searches tended to be guided first by format, second by price, and finally by brand." (The summary, not the paper, was read.)
  - Transfer: the competitor set should be cut by use or format and price band before brand. One category.
- **Ringel and Skiera, "Visualizing Asymmetric Competition Among More Than 1,000 Products Using Big Search Data", Marketing Science 35(3), 2016, pp. 511–534.** [research] (abstract via Crossref; the INFORMS page returned 403; read 2026-10-14)
  - They derived consideration sets from co-search on a price-comparison site, in one application: the LED-TV market, 1,124 products and 56 brands.
  - "reduction of the analysis to smaller representative product sets can obscure important information".
  - Competition is asymmetric: A may compete with B more than B with A (the seat's gloss of the abstract's "asymmetric competition"; the paper body was not read).
  - Their price-comparison search data had "higher external validity than search data from Google and Amazon".
  - Transfer: a small hand-picked set is a lens, not the market. The fact that a competitor counts us as their rival does not mean we count them as ours.
- **Chen, Mislove and Wilson, "An Empirical Analysis of Algorithmic Pricing on Amazon Marketplace", WWW 2016, pp. 1339–1349.** [research] (PDF at mislove.org; read 2026-10-14)
  - Over four months of data on all sellers of 1,641 best-seller products, they identified 543 sellers likely using algorithmic pricing.
  - In a pilot that crawled 5 best-seller products once a minute for 3 days, "40% of price changes occur within a minute of the previous price change, with a long tail of update times". The figure is from those 5 products, not the full sample.
  - The lowest price and the Buy Box on products with algorithmic sellers are "significantly more volatile than for products without any algorithmic sellers".
  - Transfer: on contested offers, a once-a-day snapshot of price can miss intraday movement. Read price over a window from history, not from two snapshots. Data from 2015, best-seller products only, and mostly about multi-seller offer competition, which matters less for a brand's own single-seller ASIN.
- **Chevalier and Goolsbee, "Measuring Prices and Price Competition Online: Amazon.com and BarnesandNoble.com", Quantitative Marketing and Economics 1(2), 2003, pp. 203–222.** [research] (NBER w9085 abstract; Crossref; read 2026-10-14)
  - They used "publicly available data on the sales ranks of about 20,000 books to derive quantity proxies".
  - Transfer: rank-to-quantity mapping is an established academic method. It is a model, and books in 2001 are not today's categories.
- **Brodersen, Gallusser, Koehler, Remy and Scott, "Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models", Annals of Applied Statistics 9(1), 2015, pp. 247–274.** [research] (arXiv 1506.00356 and PDF; read 2026-10-14)
  - Control series are valid "As long as the control series received no intervention themselves".
  - "This approach assumes that covariates are unaffected by the effects of treatment", because spill-over into controls biases the estimate.
  - Transfer: this is the formal version of effect-review's "subtract the competitor set's movement". Direct competitors compete for the same clicks, so a change to our listing moves them too, and the method's assumption is broken by construction (see the seat's note on direction).
- **Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller, "Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California's Tobacco Control Program", Journal of the American Statistical Association 105(490), June 2010, pp. 493–505 (DOI 10.1198/jasa.2009.ap08746).** [research] (author PDF at economics.mit.edu; Crossref; read 2026-10-14; gap fill)
  - The method assumes "no interference between units": "we assume that outcomes of the untreated units are not affected by the intervention implemented in the treated unit."
  - Anticipation: "interventions may have an impact prior to their implementation (e.g., via anticipation effects). In those cases, T0 could be redefined to be the first period in which the outcome may possibly react to the intervention."
  - The comparison is "a weighted average of potential control states, with weights chosen so that the resulting synthetic California best reproduces the values of a set of predictors of cigarette consumption in California before the passage of Proposition 99": the comparison is fixed from pre-period data.
  - Donor pool: "we discard from the donor pool states that adopted some other large-scale tobacco control program during our sample period", and states with large tax rises.
  - Their own interference example is a rival's reaction: "in response to Proposition 99 the tobacco industry could have diverted funds from planned advertising campaigns in other states to California. In both cases, interference would likely cause lower levels of smoking in the control states, artificially reducing the magnitude of our estimate". They judged the violations "do not appear to be severe".
  - Transfer: close. Our listing change is the intervention, the competitor set is the donor pool, and a rival that moves price, coupon or ad money in response plays the tobacco industry's part. The paper's remedies are to exclude units touched by other interventions and to fix the comparison on pre-period data. For a control that reacts afterwards it offers no correction, only a statement of the bias's direction.
- **Abadie, "Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects", Journal of Economic Literature 59(2), June 2021, pp. 391–425 (DOI 10.1257/jel.20191450).** [research] Only the abstract was read (AEA article page; Crossref; Semantic Scholar): practical guidance on "the settings where synthetic controls provide reliable estimates and those where they may fail". The full text was unreachable: the AEA PDF returned 403, and the open MIT DSpace copy (handle 1721.1/144417) returned HTTP 429 with a 900-second retry. Its interference and donor-pool conditions are therefore not cited from it here (Open questions).
- **Angrist and Pischke, *Mostly Harmless Econometrics*, Princeton University Press, 2009, §3.2.3 "Bad Control".** [research] The book itself was not reached: the publisher page returned 403 to a plain fetch and lists only second-level contents, and no open copy was found. Its p. 64 passage is read as quoted by Cinelli, Forney and Pearl (below): "Bad controls are variables that are themselves outcome variables in the notional experiment at hand. That is, bad controls might just as well be dependent variables too. Good controls are variables that we can think of having been fixed at the time the regressor of interest was determined." Transfer: a competitor's post-change price or sales is partly an outcome of our change, so in this sense it is a bad control, while its pre-change level is a good one. This is a secondary quotation, not read in the book.
- **Cinelli, Forney and Pearl, "A Crash Course in Good and Bad Controls", Sociological Methods & Research 53(3), pp. 1071–1104 (online 2022-05-20; UCLA technical report R-493, revised March 2022).** [research] (technical-report PDF; Crossref; read 2026-10-14)
  - "'good controls' are defined as variables that are thought to be unaffected by the treatment, whereas 'bad controls' are variables that could be in principle affected by the treatment."
  - Overcontrol: "Controlling for Z will block the very effect we want to estimate (the total effect of X on Y), thus biasing our estimates (this is usually known as "overcontrol bias")."
  - They also say "not all "post-treatment" variables are inherently bad controls". The test is whether the treatment affects the variable, not timing alone.
  - Transfer: the paper is about regression adjustment, not the subtraction of a comparison series. The shared point is that adjusting by something the treatment moved biases the estimate.
- **Johari, Li, Liskovich and Weintraub, "Experimental Design in Two-Sided Platforms: An Analysis of Bias", Management Science 68(10), 2022, pp. 7069–7089 (arXiv 2002.05670).** [research] (arXiv abstract; Crossref; read 2026-10-14)
  - "Many of these experiments exhibit interference, where an intervention applied to one market participant influences the behavior of another participant. This interference leads to biased estimates of the treatment effect of the intervention."
  - Listing-side randomization is unbiased in highly supply-constrained markets and biased in highly demand-constrained ones; customer-side randomization is the reverse.
  - Transfer: when listings compete for the same buyers, an intervention on one affects the others. This is the formal statement for marketplaces. The model is a stylised market and concerns randomised experiments, not Amazon search or a single before/after change.
- **Chen, Mislove and Wilson 2016, re-read for reaction (gap fill).** Their detection method rests on the hypothesis that "sellers using algorithmic pricing are likely to base their prices at least partially on the prices of other sellers". It uses "three target prices for each product: lowest price, Amazon's price, and the second lowest price", chosen by "examining popular repricing software". The tracking they measured is between offers on the same product. Transfer: the paper shows fast, automatic price reaction among the sellers of one ASIN. It does not show a rival brand's ASIN repricing in response to our listing. Amazon's own Automate Pricing sales-based rule (above) is a mechanism by which that cross-ASIN reaction could happen; that it does happen is unmeasured. [research] (PDF at mislove.org, re-read 2026-10-14)
- **Sorokina and Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The joy of ranking products", SIGIR 2016.** [research] (Amazon Science publication page; read 2026-10-14) The paper describes "a general machine learning framework used for ranking within categories". Its transfer is thin: it supports reading competition per search context rather than as one global list.

## Stable enough for a skill

- A competitor set is a chosen, written list of ASINs, picked for the same shopper need (same search terms, same use or format, same price band), and recorded with the date it was chosen. Sources: Product Opportunity Explorer niche definition; SalesDuo; Ecombrainly; Kim et al. 2011.
- Amazon's own evidence of who wins a search term is Brand Analytics: the Search Terms report's top clicked ASINs with click and conversion share, and Search Query Performance's totals beside the brand's share. Both need Brand Registry and the Brand Analytics role. Source: Analytics Reports.
- Market Basket names ASINs bought in the same customer order as the seller's own. Same-order co-purchase alone settles neither "complement" nor "competitor": Amazon's blog reads the dashboard as bundling and cross-marketing, while its help page says the other-brand view helps "determine directly competing products". Sources: sellingPartnerMarketBasketAnalysisReport.json; Market Basket Analysis dashboard (help); Amazon Brand Analytics blog; McAuley et al. 2015 (co-purchase taken as complement by definition, not by finding).
- The Repeat Purchase report covers only the seller's own brand-registered products and names no other ASIN. Sources: sellingPartnerRepeatPurchaseReport.json; Repeat Purchase Behavior report (help).
- Keyword `searchCatalogItems` returns a capped number of ASINs in total and per page (both caps are in the Catalog Items model and are read from it at run time), with an estimated `numberOfResults`; Amazon documents no order for the results, and its 2021 replies describe a seller-oriented ("sellable") order, not the shopper's results page. Sources: catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 Reference; searchCatalogItems sorting? #1425.
- The SQP dashboard's Query Detail view (top ASINs for a query beside the seller's ASIN) is Seller Central only; the SQP report schema carries no other ASIN. Sources: Search Query Performance dashboard (G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5); sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json.
- Search-term impression share and rank in the Ads API are account-wide per search term and cannot be tied to an ASIN; top-of-search impression share is a campaign figure, not a search-term figure. Sources: Search (Impression share); Reporting columns (version 3).
- Deal and coupon eligibility have a seller floor and a product floor, and Amazon's blog and help pages word them differently; eligibility for a given ASIN is read from the seller's Deals dashboard or coupon flow, and a competitor's is not readable. Sources: Guide to running Amazon seller promotions; Amazon deals (G202111490 v25); Coupon eligibility criteria (GJHCPEFJ5JJQD52D v14).
- A `getCompetitiveSummary` featured offer is per segment (Prime, non-Prime, unknown; by region), and an offer's `listingPrice` excludes shipping, points and promotions. Source: productPricing_2022-05-01.json.
- Amazon sells sellers a repricer that reacts 24/7, including on the seller's own sales volume, and names targeting competitors' products and their branded keywords as ordinary ad tactics. A competitor's price, promotion or ad pressure can therefore move in response to our change. Sources: Automate Pricing; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products.
- Brand Analytics "Item Comparison" and "Alternate Purchase" reports no longer exist. Source: SP-API Deprecation Schedule.
- Amazon's `similarItems` in `getCompetitiveSummary` is an Amazon-side list of comparable ASINs for a given ASIN. Sources: getCompetitiveSummary reference; Release Notes.
- Neither the Catalog Items model nor Keepa's product object has a history field for a listing's title, bullets, description, images or A+ (Keepa says of `parentTitle` "there is no history for this field"). Amazon's `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` notification covers only ASINs the seller has a brand relationship with. A change in a competitor's content therefore exists only as a difference between two dated copies someone kept. Sources: Keepa Product Object; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Notification Type Values.
- Keepa's history arrays are change-only, and Keepa may add csv slots without notice. Read slots by name from Keepa's documentation, never by an assumed array length. Source: Keepa Product Object.
- Keepa timestamps are Keepa minutes; the documented conversion is `(keepaTime + 21564000) * 60000` milliseconds. Sources: Keepa Product Object; KeepaTime.java.
- A competitor's visible title includes Item Highlights, which are searchable and shown under the title. A title diff therefore compares `title` plus `itemHighlights`. Sources: Submit modular titles; Keepa Product Object.
- Since the modular-title change, when a seller submits a title over the `item_name` limit, Amazon may split it across `item_name` and Item Highlights, so the visible split is Amazon's reconciliation, not necessarily the competitor's wording choice. Source: Submit modular titles.
- Amazon also applies AI-recommended titles to non-compliant over-long titles that nobody resubmitted, gradually, under a rollout it announced in 2026, so a competitor's title change may be Amazon's, not the competitor's. Whether the rollout is still running is read from Amazon's announcements, not assumed. Sources: Seller Forums News_Amazon posts "Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27" and "Answers to your product title update questions".
- BSR reflects sales volume, weighted toward recent sales, per category, and is relative to the other products in that category. It does not reflect page views or reviews, and it is not a unit count. Whether a parent and its children share one rank depends on the store, so ranks are compared only at the same level in the same store. Sources: Guide to Amazon sales rank; Sales Rank FAQ (G202059240 v2); Keepa Statistics Object.
- "Bought in past month" is a bracketed figure, variation-specific and often absent. An absent value is not zero sales. Sources: Keepa Product Object; Seller Forums staff reply.
- Lightning Deals, Best Deals, coupons and Prime-targeted discounts show in search results and on detail pages, so a competitor promotion is a click-through and conversion confounder. Source: Guide to running Amazon seller promotions.
- `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` covers only items the seller has an active offer on; it is not a competitor-ASIN feed. Source: Notification Type Values.
- When the competitor set is used as a control, its own movement may be caused by our change, because it competes for the same shoppers. Source: Brodersen et al. 2015.
- A comparison group is valid only for units the intervention did not affect; it is chosen and weighted on pre-intervention data, and units hit by another intervention in the window are left out. Sources: Abadie, Diamond and Hainmueller 2010; Brodersen et al. 2015.
- A variable the treatment may have moved is a bad control; its value fixed before the treatment is not. Sources: Cinelli, Forney and Pearl 2022 (quoting Angrist and Pischke 2009, p. 64).

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- **Direction of the bias** [seat]. Brodersen et al.'s spill-over example biases the estimate toward zero, because the campaign lifted the controls. Between direct competitors the spill-over is negative: our gain is partly their loss. Subtracting their movement then *inflates* our credited effect. A less-contaminated comparison is a market-level series: SQP total query volume, or the rank movement of category products outside the set. This is reasoning, not a source.
- **Size** [seat]. Two tiers would reconcile the practitioners: a short list (about 3 to 8) cross-shopped at the same price and use, watched daily; and a wider ring (the Search Terms top clicked ASINs for the core terms), refreshed when Amazon's weekly period closes. Kim et al.'s 10–15 options per shopper is consistent with this, not proof of it.
- **Cadence** [seat]. One typed read a day per competitor is enough for content diffs, because Keepa's history arrays carry the intraday price and rank between reads. Content (title, bullets, images) has no history, so a change that is made and reverted within a day is invisible. Accept that.
- **Asymmetry** [seat]. Following Ringel and Skiera, a competitor that lists us among its rivals is not necessarily one of ours. The playbook could let the seller mark "they watch us" separately from "we watch them".
- **Brand-registered or not** [seat]. Without Brand Registry, the set has no Amazon-side evidence and rests on Keepa, `similarItems` and the seller's word. The skill should say which kind of set it is.
- **A+ watch cost** [seat]. Detecting competitor A+ changes needs a raw read with `aplus=1` plus `offers` for freshness. Whether that cost is worth it for a daily watch is a seller choice for the standing instruction, not a playbook default.
- **Direction of a reacting control** [seat, gap fill]. If a rival cuts price because our listing improved, its sales hold up or rise, so subtracting its movement shrinks our credited gain, and the improvement is hidden. If a rival does nothing and simply loses the shoppers we won, subtracting its fall inflates our gain (the note above). The two biases run in opposite directions. That is why P-competitor-set-26 withholds a "held" verdict rather than correcting by a guessed amount.
- **A market-level series as a second comparison** [seat, gap fill]. SQP's query totals (organic plus Sponsored Products, per the help page) are less contaminated by any one rival's reaction than a five-ASIN set, though our own change also moves them a little. Reading both, and saying when they disagree, is the seat's suggestion, not a source's.
- **Market Basket as a substitute signal** [seat, gap fill]. Shoppers who buy two sizes, colours or brands of the same thing in one order (to compare, or for two users) make substitutes show up as co-purchases. That may be what Amazon's help page means by "directly competing products". It is a guess and is left to Open question 12.
- **Repeat share and listing sensitivity** [seat, gap fill]. A product whose sales are mostly repeat purchases may respond less to a listing change, because returning buyers reorder without reading the page. If so, effect-review would expect a smaller effect on such products. This is reasoning, not a finding; no source read tests it.
