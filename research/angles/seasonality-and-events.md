# Seasonality, major sales and event windows

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage:** none of its own. Seasonality and sale events act on all three stages at once, and they act on the competitor set at the same moment. Exposure moves because shoppers search differently: Amazon's own search ranks with seasonal features. Click-through moves because deal badges, strike-through prices and coupons appear in search results. Conversion moves because shoppers hold back before an event, buy during it and pull back after it. In this package the angle is a **confounder for every judgement**, not a lever.

**Skills it feeds:**
- `effect-review` (mainly): which windows are clean; what counts as "a major sale"; how to get the event dates at run time rather than from memory; what to read so the seller's own and the competitors' promotions are named as confounders; when the honest verdict is "cannot tell".
- `listing-review`: the trigger family "demand change: seasonal rise and fall; before and after a major sale". A drop in sessions entering an off-season, or the week after an event, is not a listing defect until it has been checked against query volume and the seller's own history for the same weeks.
- `listing-change-log`: each change record carries the event context of its two windows. That covers which announced events overlap, which of the seller's own promotions ran (with their ids) and which competitor deals were seen.
- `/listing-watch` (the standing-instruction template): what cx does in an event window when the seller's instruction allows it to write.
- The report script: the read operations that date events and promotions (Sales `getOrderMetrics`, the promotion and coupon performance reports, Promotions API `searchPromotions`, Data Kiosk promotion fields).

**Why a senior operator cares.** Event weeks are the largest movements most listings ever see, and their dates move from year to year. Prime Day ran in July 2025 over four days and in June 2026 over four days. Prime Big Deal Days ran on 7–8 October 2025 and 6–7 October 2026. A seller's 30-day baseline pulled on 2026-10-13 already contains Prime Big Deal Days 2026. That is what day 1 of the beta run pulled: `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT` for 2026-09-13 to 2026-10-13. The day-2 proposal then put a 10% coupon (row 8) into one package of 13 changes, six of them text attributes, beside images, A+ and ads, and named no event (`office-mailbox/from-engineering/20261013-0904-…day-1.md`, item 2; `…20261014-0921-…day-2.md`, item D and the proposal quoted whole). An operator reads every number through the calendar first. cx did not.

### B. Deals, coupons and discounts: eligibility, visibility, timing

RULES (enforced by Amazon when a seller creates the promotion; Amazon's thresholds can change):
- "You'll need a Professional selling plan and an overall rating of at least four stars to create deals." "Only select products are eligible for Lightning Deals." Coupons: "a Professional selling plan and an overall rating of at least 3.5 stars". The page does not say whether "overall rating" is the seller's feedback rating or the product's star rating; the help pages below do (deals: 4 stars is the product's floor; coupons: 3.5 is the seller's feedback rating). [official] (Guide to running Amazon seller promotions, deals, coupons, and discounts, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/seller-promotions, dated May 27, 2026, read 2026-10-14)
- **Gap fill: the floors are two, one for the seller and one for the product, and they differ by promotion type** (Seller Central help read through the help hub's content call, see Pages read):
  - Deals: "To be eligible for deals, you must be a Professional seller with an overall rating of at least 3.5 stars." Products "must have a sales history on Amazon and at least a 4-star rating", only new condition, a "validated reference price", and for clothing and shoes "at least 65% of variations should be included". "Amazon Deals is an invite-only program." "Eligible ASINs are refreshed every week". The criteria are "subject to change based on country and time of year". [official] (Amazon deals, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202111490, version 25, read 2026-10-14)
  - Coupons: "a Professional seller with at least a 3.5 Seller feedback rating"; sellers with no feedback yet are eligible. Products with 1–4 reviews need "an average rating of at least 2.5 stars", with 5+ reviews "at least 3 stars"; new products with no reviews are eligible; the discount "must be at least 5% below the Was Price or List Price". [official] (Coupon eligibility criteria, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GJHCPEFJ5JJQD52D, version 14, read 2026-10-14)
  - Price discounts during events: "your seller feedback rating must be at least 3.5"; "Products must have a four-star rating", but "Products with no ratings are eligible". In the US, price discounts during events "such as Prime Day and Prime Big Deal Days are subject to fees"; on non-event days they are not. [official] (Price discounts and events, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYM7CTJPXEJBFBXH, version 6, read 2026-10-14)
  - So the May 2026 blog's "overall rating of at least four stars to create deals" is the product floor, and the seller floor is 3.5. The blog's coupon "overall rating of at least 3.5 stars" is the seller's feedback rating (Coupon eligibility criteria). The practitioners' product "3 stars" for deals (2023) is out of date; 3 stars is today's coupon floor for products with 5+ reviews.
  - **Amazon's own pages disagree on the coupon product floor for 1–4 reviews.** Coupon eligibility criteria (GJHCPEFJ5JJQD52D v14) says 2.5 stars. Understanding coupon errors (GLBL4T5C9UAB9T5P, as read and quoted in angles/price-position.md, which has no capture of it) gives "a minimum star rating of 1 for products with one to four reviews and 3 for products with five or more reviews". This file picks neither. The binding floor for a given ASIN is what the coupon creation flow in Seller Central reports at run time, as the seller relays it.
  - The help-page texts above are the captures saved on 2026-10-14 under the office's capture folder (outside this repository) (G202111490-v25.txt "Amazon deals"; GJHCPEFJ5JJQD52D-v14.txt "Coupon eligibility criteria"). Price discounts and events (GYM7CTJPXEJBFBXH v6) has no capture there; its quotes rest on this file's gap-fill read and same-day re-check.
- Durations: "A Lightning Deal can run from 4 to 12 hours, as determined by Amazon". "A Best Deal can run for a select number of days". "All coupons expire after 90 days". Price discounts run "for up to 30 days on a limited number of units". [official] (same page)
- Price discounts are validated before they run: "You'll have to submit your discount for validation." "Price discounts for products with a validated reference price appear with a strike-through price in search results and on product detail pages." "Not all SKUs are eligible for discounts." For coupons the page says only "There are certain requirements for product eligibility too." [official] (same page; Ready, set, sell! Prepare for Prime Day 2026, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/prime-day-tips, May 19, 2026, read 2026-10-14)

GUIDANCE:
- **Where shoppers see promotions:** "Customers will see your deals on product detail pages, in search results" and on the Deals page. Coupons also appear in search results. A promotion therefore acts on click-through and on conversion at the same moment. [official] (Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…)
- **Event deals have their own submission windows, and those windows live in Seller Central:** "To have your recommended deals considered for Prime Day, watch for submission timelines in Seller Central." "Self-service options like coupons and discounts can be set up closer to event dates." The words "submission timelines in Seller Central", the inventory "needed date" and the button "Get the Prime Day readiness playbook" all link to the same Seller Central page, `GM2ADTH3XH3A5N57`. A plain fetch of that page serves only the Seller Central shell; its text, "Prime Day readiness playbook" version 4, was read on 2026-10-14 and is saved at capture `GM2ADTH3XH3A5N57-v4` (section E quotes it). [official] (Prime Day | Sell on Amazon, https://sell.amazon.com/prime-day, read 2026-10-14)
- **Amazon's own holiday timeline puts listing work weeks before the peak and promotions after it.** Under "4-6 weeks before": "Fix "no listing" errors", "Enhance listings with A+ Content", "A/B test your listing content". Under "3 weeks before": "Create a coupon", "Create a promotion", "Create a Prime Exclusive Discount". The page gives no "during" or "after" phase. [official] (Amazon FBA holiday guide | Sell on Amazon, https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon/holiday, read 2026-10-14)
- **Content moderation takes time:** allow "at least one week for the moderation of your content". The context on the page is the Prime Day version of a brand Store, which should be submitted "for approval at least a week before Prime Day". [official] (Ready, set, sell! Prepare for Prime Day 2026)
- **Discount stacking:** "Avoid unintended 'discount stacking.' This happens when you add a coupon to a product that already has an active discount. This can lead to extreme discounts." [official] (Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…, May 27, 2026). The 2024 holiday post adds the remedy: "only putting coupons on products that don't have a deal coming up". [official] (7 tips to reach shoppers and promote your brand this holiday season, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/7-tips-promote-brand-holiday-season, July 5, 2024, read 2026-10-14)
- **No Amazon page read here says "do not change a listing during an event"** or says what happens to an active deal when the listing or price is edited. The roadmap rule "no change and no verdict during a major sale" therefore has no Amazon RULE behind it. Its basis is Amazon's timeline guidance above, the practitioners in the next section and the experimental method in Published research. (Gap fill: Seller Central's deals page does say, for a deal in Upcoming status, "avoid making major changes to the ASINs or parent ASIN participating in the deal". It names price edits, closing listings and new child ASINs, and says what each does to the deal. See section E.)

### C. Where the data that dates and sizes an event lives (operations and fields; the figures are Amazon's and can change)

- **Sales API `getOrderMetrics`** gives the seller's own order series for one ASIN or SKU. `granularity` can be Hour through Total. Hour works only when "the interval specified is less than or equal to 30 days from now"; other granularities allow an interval "less or equal to 2 years from now". `granularityTimeZone` is "Required when specifying a granularity value greater than Hour". `firstDayOfWeek` is Monday or Sunday. It returns `unitCount`, `orderItemCount`, `orderCount`, `averageUnitPrice` and `totalSales`. Default rate 0.5/s, burst 15. [official] (sales.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/sales-api-model/sales.json, read 2026-10-14). An optional `amazonProgram` parameter was added May 28, 2025. [official] (SP-API Release Notes, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes, read to character 100,000)
- **Data Kiosk seller analytics dataset**: grouping by `DAY`, `WEEK` or `MONTH`. The `startDate` can be no more than two years ago. "All metrics in the seller analytics dataset are ready after 34 hours", and a day is "generally available at 10 AM local time two days later". Promotion fields live under `metrics.metrics.promotion`: `promotionStartDate`, `promotionEndDate`, `promotionType`, `promotionStatus`, `dealId`, `dealTitle`, `promotionGlanceViewCnt`, `promotionConversionRate`, `promotionOrderedUnitsWithRevenue`, `promotionDiscountPct`, `dealOurPrice`. Return data "is computed with a delay of about 45 days". [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide, read 2026-10-14)
- **`GET_PROMOTION_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`**: sellers with the Selling Partner Insights role. Sellers get Best Deal and Lightning Deal data. `reportOptions` `promotionStartDateFrom`/`To` are required. Start dates go up to two years back. "If a selected promotion is in progress when you request a report, the report will contain cumulative data for the promotion up until the day prior to your report request" (the coupon report says the same of coupons). Fields include `type`, `status`, `glanceViews`, `unitsSold`, `revenue`, `startDateTime`, `endDateTime`, and per-ASIN `includedProducts`. **`GET_COUPON_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`** (seller schema): Brand Analytics role; `couponStartDateFrom`/`To`; `clips`, `redemptions`, `startDateTime`, `endDateTime`, `coupons.asins`. [official] (Performance Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-performance, read 2026-10-14)
- **Promotions API v2025-12-01**, launched August 26, 2026 "to search, retrieve, and manage promotions". Availability: Sellers. Roles: Pricing or Product Listing. Operations: `searchPromotions`, `getPromotion`, `getSelection`. Promotion types are `BASKET_BUILDING`, `DEAL`, `PRICE_DISCOUNT` and `COUPON`. The `statuses` filter takes `PROCESSING`, `UPCOMING`, `RUNNING`, `EXPIRED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLING` and `CANCELLED`. `schedule.eventId` is "An event identifier that associates the promotion with a specific event. For event promotions, this field contains the event name when available (for example, `Prime Day`). For historic event promotions, this field may contain `EVENT` as a placeholder." The model's own field example is `Prime Day`, while its response examples show `PRIME_DAY_2025`. The Promotions API page's version table reads "Legacy versions: None" and "Sandbox: Static" (gap fill correction: the earlier "Sandbox: None" misread the legacy-versions column; see Verification). `PRIME_EXCLUSIVE` is a `PROGRAM` customer segment, not a type. Default rates are 0.1/s, burst 4 for `searchPromotions` and 2 for `getPromotion` and `getSelection`. [official] (Promotions API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/promotions-api; Search for promotions, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/search-promotions; promotions_2025-12-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/promotions-api-model/promotions_2025-12-01.json, first 100,000 of 118,814 characters; SP-API Release Notes; all read 2026-10-14)
- **Product Pricing `getCompetitiveSummary`**: a `promotions` array was added July 29, 2026. `PromotionType` is "COUPON", "DEAL: The offer has a live deal (for example, Lightning Deal or Best Deal)" or "BASKET_BUILDING", with no event field. The model describes `referencePrices` names as "e.g., `CompetitivePriceThreshold`, `WasPrice`, `CompetitivePrice`", but the release notes' deprecation list says `CompetitivePriceThreshold` "will be removed on September 30, 2025", so its presence today is unconfirmed. `WasPrice` "is the 90-day median price that customers pay for the product on Amazon" (release note, November 12, 2024). This is the competitor-side live flag; it has no history. [official] (productPricing_2022-05-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/product-pricing-api-model/productPricing_2022-05-01.json, first 100,000 of 105,753 characters; SP-API Release Notes; read 2026-10-14)
- **Brand Analytics demand series**:
  - Search Query Performance has periods `WEEK`, `MONTH` and `QUARTER`. Its fields include `searchQueryVolume`. "Requests cannot span multiple periods." The page also lists, under "Maximum reporting periods", "MONTH: 17 months"; section G records how that line conflicts with the one before it.
  - The Search Terms report has periods `DAY`, `WEEK`, `MONTH` and `QUARTER`, and a field `searchFrequencyRank`.
  - Search Query Performance: "Sellers who have the Brand Analytics Selling Partner API role and are registered in Amazon Brand Registry". Search Terms: sellers and vendors who have the role, "are registered in Amazon Brand Registry, and are a brand representative".
  - [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
- **Amazon Ads API: a machine-readable event calendar.**
  - `POST /sp/v1/events` (`SPGetAllRuleEvents`): "Gets all special individual and grouped events with suggested date range in advertiser's marketplace." It returns `events[]` (`eventId`, `eventName`, `startDate`, `endDate`) and `groupedEvents[]`.
  - `POST /sp/campaigns/budgetRules/recommendations`: "special events with date range and suggested budget increase" (`suggestedBudgetIncreasePercent`).
  - These need Ads API authorization, which is not on the route today.
  - [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json, the file the Ads API reference loads, read 2026-10-14)
- **Best Sellers Rank reacts to sales, so it spikes in events:** "Both recent sales and all-time sales factor into a BSR, though recent sales count more than older sales"; "BSRs aren't influenced by page views or customer reviews". [official] (Guide to Amazon sales rank: Best Sellers Rank (BSR), https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-best-sellers-rank, Sep 3, 2025, read 2026-10-14)
- **Manage Your Experiments** splits traffic at random ("customers that view your product listing are randomly split into two groups"). The page says nothing about running across holidays or events. [official] (Optimize product content and increase sales with A/B testing, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/manage-your-experiments, Mar 12, 2024, read 2026-10-14)

### D. Keepa (supplier documentation, the second source on the route)

- `deals`: "provides metadata for active deals associated with the product's buy box". It is "Only updated when the _offers_ parameter is used".
  - `dealType` is "The type of the deal badge. New deal types can be added at any time". The values listed in the source include `PRIME_DAY`, `PRIME_DAY_EARLY`, `EARLY_ACCESS_WITH_PRIME`, `PRIME_EXCLUSIVE`, `LIMITED_TIME_DEAL`, `SPECIAL_EVENT_SALE`, `CLEARANCE_NO_RETURNS`, `GENERIC_OFFER_PROMO` and `UNKNOWN`; the full current list also has `SELLING_FAST`, `PRIME_SELLING_FAST`, `COUNTDOWN_ENDS_IN` and `APP_ONLY`. `percentClaimed` (0–100) is filled for Lightning Deals (`dealType == LIMITED_TIME_DEAL`).

  - `startTime`/`endTime` are in Keepa Time minutes and are "not available for all deal types".
  - [official — Keepa] (Product Object — Keepa API Documentation, https://keepa.com/api-docs/product-object.html; Product.java, https://github.com/keepacom/api_backend/blob/master/src/main/java/com/keepa/api/backend/structs/Product.java; both read 2026-10-14). [official — Keepa] (same)

### E. Lead times that decide when a change must be made around an event (gap fill)

A change can be judged only if it is live before its observation window opens and the window closes before an event's run-up. Amazon states the lead times below, each on its own page. The figures are for human readers and Amazon restates them; a skill points to the page, not to the number. The figures also differ by path and by page across this research (other files record read-back, notification, bulk and indexing figures); none of them is an effect-window rule, and the live date of a change is the date cx reads it back (method/funnel-and-search.md P-15). Seller Central help texts quoted in sections E and F are the captures saved on 2026-10-14 under the office's capture folder (outside this repository)<id>-v<version>.txt, except Troubleshoot suppressed and active deals (GKN9A84DGTYWYHWY), Prime Exclusive Deals (GFXXXKMMQE9WLVNZ) and Subscribe & Save (G201620110), which have no capture; their quotes rest on this file's gap-fill read and same-day re-check.

**From content change to live**
- **Any listing change, to live:** "It can take up to 72 hours for changes to new or existing ASINs to go live. Latency can be slower or faster, depending on the website traffic volumes and other technical factors." Fixed search-suppressed listings "may become searchable within 72 hours". GUIDANCE: a stated expectation, not an enforced limit. [official] (Amazon search glossary, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV, version 2, read 2026-10-14). The "traffic volumes" clause matters here. Event weeks are Amazon's highest-traffic weeks, so the stated latency is least reliable exactly then [seat].
- **Title and item highlights, to display:** "the change is accepted immediately but can take 24 to 48 hours to be processed and displayed on Amazon stores, including search results and the product detail page". "If your change doesn't appear after 48 hours, you can edit and resubmit it". [official] (Product title requirements and guidelines, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC, version 21, read 2026-10-14). This settles the cross-file doubt: the 24–48-hour figure is on Amazon's own title page, not only in forum posts.
- **A+ content, to live:** "Review can take up to seven business days (longer during periods of high volume)". "After the A+ Content has been approved, it can take up to 24 hours to be published". A rejected document that is resubmitted "will also take up to seven business days for review". [official] (Complete Guide to A+ Content for Brand Owners, US, version 1.1, "Last updated March 28, 2023", https://storage.pardot.com/229492/1680539631VYq9kP6F/AMZ_Guide_A__04b_US__1_.pdf, read 2026-10-14). Seven business days plus a day to publish is about ten calendar days, and a rejection doubles it [seat arithmetic].
- **Brand Store for Prime Day:** submit "at least a week before Prime Day", allowing "at least one week for the moderation of your content" (section B; Ready, set, sell! Prepare for Prime Day 2026).
- **Amazon's own title rewrites:** "When changes are made to your listings, brand owners will have 14 days before implementation to review, modify, and approve AI-generated recommendations for titles and Item Highlights in Review Listings Changes." "After July 27, any titles still over 75 characters will be updated to the AI recommendation gradually." [official] (Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27, News_Amazon, Seller Forums, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/145b6d0f-999c-4555-896c-c694bda2e470, read 2026-10-14). A pending recommendation is therefore a scheduled Amazon-side change that can land up to 14 days later, including inside an event window. The post says nothing about pausing rewrites around events.

**Manage Your Experiments (randomized: the only design that survives an event)**
- **Duration and timing:**
  - "Experiments typically run for 8 to 10 weeks." "To significance" ends the experiment "whenever there are statistically relevant results—in as little as four weeks". "Eight weeks is the recommended length for a set duration experiment."
  - "the earliest possible start date might be several days in the future to allow time to confirm that all submitted content meets our guidelines."
  - Auto-publish: "Amazon will only publish the winning version if there is at least a 66% probability that it will perform better". It can be turned "on or off for any experiment in progress".
  - Once an experiment is in progress, "the content can no longer be revised".
  - [official] (Create a listing experiment, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYZYA77YZTWTF7YZ, version 13, read 2026-10-14)
- "Only one experiment can be run on any particular ASIN at a time". The split is by customer, not by time: "experiments are not rotating content over time". "Experiments do not impact search rankings". [official] (Manage Your Experiments, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GVP453K5XRBJS7Y9, version 7, read 2026-10-14)
- **What Amazon says about seasons and experiments:**
  - On the simplified title experiment: "We recommend that you opt out of title experiments during holiday seasons if you would like to add time-specific key words such as "Mother's Day" or "Valentine's Day."" [official] (Create a listing experiment)
  - Amazon suggests "testing during different seasonal periods to understand how different types of customers approach your product". [official] (Tips for experiments, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G2BNMJ8NFCGZQVFH, version 5, read 2026-10-14)
  - The projected one-year impact "doesn't take into account seasonality, price changes, or other factors". Results are "updated once a week" and use "a Bayesian approach". [official] (Experiment results, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GY6DCFA53HYDH79H, version 4, read 2026-10-14)
- No MYE page read says an experiment pauses, stops or is flagged when it spans an event. An 8-to-10-week experiment started in late August or September runs through Prime Big Deal Days. One started in October runs into Black Friday Week [seat arithmetic].

**Deals: submission, scheduling and the freeze Amazon does state**
- **RULE (scheduled deals):** "For deals in Upcoming status, avoid making major changes to the ASINs or parent ASIN participating in the deal." The page names three changes:
  - editing prices: "you may also be required to lower the deal price … to avoid deal suppression";
  - closing listings: "Doing so will cancel any deal currently scheduled for that ASIN. Canceled deals cannot be reactivated";
  - "creating or assigning new child ASINs to the variation family after the deal has been submitted", which makes the deal "revalidate" and "will cause the deal to become suppressed or canceled".

  [official] (Amazon deals, G202111490 v25). **This is the only Amazon text read that asks a seller to hold listing changes around a promotion.** It names price, status and the variation family. It does not name text, images or A+.
- **RULE (suppression deadline):** suppression errors "must be resolved 25 hours before the scheduled start time of the Deal to avoid cancellation". "If a deal is suppressed during its scheduled run time, the deal price may continue to display to customers." "Running a Deal does not guarantee that you will win the Featured offer." [official] (Troubleshoot suppressed and active deals, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GKN9A84DGTYWYHWY, version 18, read 2026-10-14)
- **Event deals:**
  - "check Upcoming events to see the events that are open for deal submissions".
  - "The date and time of your deal will be revealed in Seller Central at least one week before the deal is scheduled to run." If the deal is not selected for the event, "the start and end dates will reflect the week-long timeframe".
  - "Two weeks before your deal starts and while your deal is running, check for "Needs Attention" alerts".
  - "Peak event prices are excluded from the 30-day and 60-day lookback windows once the event ends".
  - [official] (Deals for events, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202183600, version 10, read 2026-10-14)
  - So a seller can submit a deal for an event and learn only a week ahead whether it runs, and when.
- **Prime Day 2026 timeline** (the readiness playbook, now read):
  - Deals: "March 24, 2026: Prime-Exclusive Best Deal and Lightning Deal submissions open". There is "$50 off the upfront fee per deal if you schedule … by April 30, 2026".
  - Price discounts: "April 6, 2026: Prime-Exclusive Price Discounts submissions open … and will close six hours before the end of the event".
  - Close: "June 9, 2026: Deal scheduling for Prime-Exclusive Best Deals and Lightning Deals for Prime Day closes. You may continue to submit Prime-Exclusive Price Discounts until 12-hours before the event end time and Coupons through the end of the event."
  - Coupons: submissions "will open after Prime Day dates have been announced".
  - Inventory arrival: "May 27, 2026" for AWD and for FBA "minimal shipment splits"; "June 5, 2026" for "Amazon-optimized shipment splits".
  - [official] (Prime Day readiness playbook, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GM2ADTH3XH3A5N57, version 4, read 2026-10-14)
  - The page gives two different closing times for Prime-Exclusive Price Discounts: six hours and 12 hours before the end.
- **Holiday 2026 timeline:**
  - Deal submission: "July 8 to September 8 for Prime Big Deal Days" and "July 8 to October 20 for Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday". The early-bird dates are August 5 and September 5.
  - Inventory for Black Friday and Cyber Monday: "October 14: AWD shipments"; "October 21" and "October 28" for the two FBA split options.
  - "Prime Big Deal Days promotional prices are excluded from the 30-day and 60-day lookback window". "There are no new eligibility requirements".
  - The post gives no event dates for Black Friday Week 2026. It says only that Prime Big Deal Days "returns at the same time as last year".
  - [official] (Holiday 2026: Same fees, same eligibility, earlier deadlines, News_Amazon, Seller Forums, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/3e31fbb7-04e0-4ed4-873e-f74b1052e2ff, read 2026-10-14; the page shows only "3 months ago")
- **Read together** [seat]: deal decisions close three to eight weeks before an event, while listing content has no Amazon deadline at all, only the lead times above. A seller can therefore be locked into an event deal and still be free to edit the listing. An edit that lands in the deal window is confounded by the deal.

### F. Other event-window confounders Amazon documents (gap fill)

- **Deal fees: the 2025 per-day model.**
  - The 2025 change: "Effective on June 2, 2025, deals and coupons fees in the Amazon US store will change as follows":
    - Best Deal and Lightning Deal up-front fees "will be reduced from $300/$150 per deal respectively to $70 each per day plus a variable fee of 1.0% of deal sales with a cap for the variable fee at $2,000 per deal";
    - coupons go "from $0.60 per unit sold to an up-front fee of $5 per coupon plus 2.5% of coupon sales";
    - for Prime Day 2025, deals stayed at "fixed fees of $1000 and $500", and PED fees went "from $50 to $100 per PED".

    [official] (Amazon is making it easier to try using deals and coupons to create additional demand for your products, News_Amazon, Seller Forums, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/c71e49a4-8f57-42ea-8d6a-accec5de1ebe, updated March 25, 2025, read 2026-10-14)
  - Today's help page:
    - Non-peak: "$70 per day, plus" "1.0% of sales (capped at $2,000)".
    - Peak: "The 2026 rates for Prime Day, Prime Big Deal Days, Black Friday and Cyber Monday" are "$100 per promotion" plus "1.5% of sales (capped at $5,000)".
    - Early-bird deadlines: "August 5, 2026" for Prime Big Deal Days and "September 5, 2026" for Black Friday and Cyber Monday.
    - "running a deal doesn't guarantee that you'll be the Featured Offer". "You'll be charged the full fee for any deal that is canceled while it is running."

    [official] (Understand deal fees, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202111590, version 27, read 2026-10-14)
  - These replace the 2023 practitioner fee figures in "What practitioners add". The per-day fee makes long non-peak deals cost more, so sellers may run shorter deals that fall wholly inside one window [seat].
- **Prime Exclusive Discounts and Prime Exclusive Deals.** A Prime-Exclusive Price Discount gets "event badging in search, on the detail page, and in cart", but "unlike deals, they will not be featured on the main event page". A campaign holds up to "500 SKUs". [official] (Prime Day readiness playbook). "Sellers cannot independently request Prime Exclusive eligibility for their ASINs." [official] (Prime Exclusive Deals, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GFXXXKMMQE9WLVNZ, version 1, read 2026-10-14). In the Promotions API a Prime-exclusive promotion is a `DEAL` or `PRICE_DISCOUNT` with the `PROGRAM` customer segment `PRIME_EXCLUSIVE` (section C).
- **Subscribe & Save.** "Seller coupons and promotional discounts are added to Subscribe & Save discounts. For example, if you run an Amazon Deal on a Subscribe & Save enrolled product, the program discount will apply in addition to the deal price." Deliveries may be paused when "There is a lower-priced offer available for the ASIN", and on stock-outs. [official] (Subscribe & Save, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201620110, version 29, read 2026-10-14). Subscriptions started during an event recur after it, so S&S orders carry the event's tail into later windows [seat].
- **Stock and the Prime badge.** "late arrivals may not be processed in time for Prime eligibility". "To avoid the low-inventory level fee, make sure you have 4-6 weeks of inventory available before your deal event. After the event, we recommend that you maintain at least 28 days of inventory for your post-deal sales." "If a promotion doesn't follow our product image requirements or isn't representative of the products within the promotion, the promotion may be canceled." [official] (Prime Day readiness playbook). The inventory-and-fulfillment angle owns stock-outs as a window-breaker.
- **Featured Offer during deals.** A deal does not secure the Featured Offer. If the deal is not visible, "check if a different seller is the Featured Offer". [official] (Troubleshoot suppressed and active deals; Understand deal fees). The price-position angle owns the Featured Offer as a confounder.
- **Seasonal content rules on the listing itself.**
  - Search terms must not hold "Temporary statements such as "new," or "on sale now"". The listed temporary words include "limited time", "on sale", "last chance", "this week (month, year)" and "today". Violations "may result in your ASIN being suppressed". [official] (Use search terms effectively, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G23501, version 14, read 2026-10-14)
  - Titles, descriptions, bullets and images must not carry "Time-sensitive information, such as dates of tours, seminars, or lectures" or "Adverts, promotional material". [official] (Product detail page rules, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640, version 21, read 2026-10-14)
  - A+ must not mention "time sensitive information such as now / new / latest / yet / on sale now" "or references to holidays", which "are permitted in the brand story section". [official] (A+ content guidelines, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GGW8U76SSNTRTBX7, version 8, read 2026-10-14)
  - Titles may hold occasion words such as "Mother's Day"; the MYE note above assumes they may.
  - So an "event edit" to search terms or A+ is text Amazon does not allow. A seasonal title edit is allowed, but it is itself a dated change, and removing it later is a second change [seat].
- **Reviews after an event.** The programmatic review request runs "within a time frame of five days after the `EarliestDeliveryDate` to 30 days after the `LatestDeliveryDate`". [official] (Set up the Solicitations API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/set-up-the-solicitations-api, read 2026-10-14). For coupons: "customer reviews that result from purchases with coupons may not always get the Amazon Verified Purchase badge". [official] (Coupon eligibility criteria). Reviews from event orders therefore arrive over the following one to five weeks, and the rating mix can move after the event with no listing change [seat].
- **Returns after the holiday peak.** Holiday purchases from November 1 to December 31 can be returned until January 31 (section A). Data Kiosk attributes returns "to the day the returned unit was shipped" and computes them "with a delay of about 45 days" (section C). A return wave that arrives in January is therefore written back onto November and December dates, and it is not complete in Data Kiosk until about mid-March [seat arithmetic].
- **Ad spend without the Ads API.** Data Kiosk's Economics dataset carries `ads: [AdSummary]`, "The advertising spend data for the product within the specified date range". [official] (analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql, read 2026-10-14). "The Vendor Analytics and Sales and Traffic datasets require the Brand Analytics role. The Economics dataset requires the Selling Partner Insights role." [official] (Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/changelog/correction-data-kiosk-role-requirements, read 2026-10-14). When that role is granted, event-week ad spend per product per day can be read on the SP-API route. Terms and clicks still need the Ads API; the paid-keywords angle owns the detail.
- **Non-US calendars.**
  - India: the Great Indian Festival 2025 "starts on September 23rd" "with 24-hour early access for Prime members" and "ends on 20 October", about four weeks. On the day read, the page said the 2026 edition "is live on Amazon.in". [official] (Amazon Great Indian Festival, https://www.aboutamazon.in/amazon-great-indian-festival; Amazon Great Indian Festival 2025 ends 20 October: Dates & more details, https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/retail/amazon-great-indian-festival-2025-dates; both read 2026-10-14)
  - UK: Black Friday Week 2025 ran "kicking off 20 November and running until 1 December", with "early deals on Everyday Essentials from 13 November". [official] (It's the Season for Savings: Amazon's Black Friday Week Offers Deep Discounts…, Amazon UK press release, November 13, 2025, https://press.aboutamazon.com/uk/news/retail/2025/11/its-the-season-for-savings-amazons-black-friday-week-offers-deep-discounts-on-customer-favourite-brands-from-20-november-until-1-december, read 2026-10-14)
  - Australia, Brazil, India and Japan had Prime Day 2026 later than the US (section A).
  - An event's length, run-up and month differ by store.
- **Non-Amazon seasonality.** Amazon tells deal sellers: "All products have an optimal time of year to sell to customers. Choose the week that best optimizes the seasonality of your product." [official] (Amazon deals, G202111490 v25). The playbook's Demand forecast tool "predicts your products' future demand for up to 40 weeks based on your previous orders, active deals, and seasonal patterns". It is in Seller Central only; no API for it was read. [official] (Prime Day readiness playbook). No Amazon page read lists the non-Amazon holidays (Mother's Day, back to school, Valentine's Day and so on) or their effect. They show in the seller's own series and in query volume.

### G. The promotion records, and what the year-over-year benchmarks align on (gap fill)

- **The Promotions API reads; it does not write.**
  - The model's description says it "enables selling partners to create and manage promotions". Version 2025-12-01, however, has three operations, all `GET`:
    - `searchPromotions` filters by `statuses`, `asins`, `skus`, `promotionTypes`, `startDateBefore`/`After`, `endDateBefore`/`After` and `updateDateAfter`/`Before`.
    - `getPromotion` returns one promotion.
    - `getSelection` returns "up to 100 product items" and "only supports items found in `SelectionType.ITEMS`".
  - A promotion carries:
    - `promotionId`;
    - `trackingId`, "to uniquely identify a promotion and track its performance";
    - `schedule` (`startDate`, `endDate`, `eventId`);
    - `issues`, "Promotion-level validation issues";
    - `latestRevision`, present "only when an edit is still processing … or was rejected";
    - `merchandising.displayOnWebsite`;
    - a `feeSnapshot` whose upfront-fee `frequency` is `ONE_TIME` or `DAILY`.
  - [official] (promotions_2025-12-01.json, read whole 2026-10-14)
  - Roles: Pricing or Product Listing; one of them is enough. Regions: NA, EU, FE. Sandbox: "Static". [official] (Promotions API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/promotions-api, read 2026-10-14)
- **The join to the performance reports.**
  - On August 26, 2026, Amazon "Added the `promotionsApiMappingId` field to the `GET_COUPON_PERFORMANCE_REPORT` and `GET_PROMOTION_PERFORMANCE_REPORT` report types". Sellers "can use this field to cross-reference performance report data with promotions managed through the Promotions API". [official] (SP-API Release Notes, August 26, 2026)
  - For sellers, the promotion report covers only "Best Deal and Lightning Deal"; price discounts and coupons are not in it. Its data "is updated on a daily basis".
  - Roles as the page states them: the promotion report is for "Sellers who have the Selling Partner Insights Selling Partner API role"; the seller coupon report needs Brand Analytics, with "Availability: Sellers". [official] (Performance Reports, re-read 2026-10-14)
  - So of the three reads, only the Promotions API lists a seller's price discounts.
- **Data Kiosk's promotion fields carry no mapping id.**
  - The `Promotion` group has:
    - `dealId` ("Deal identifier") and `dealTitle`;
    - `promotionType`, a free string ("Example values are Best Deal, Deal of the Day, Lightning Deal, Sales Discount");
    - `promotionStatus` ("approved, pending or rejected");
    - `promotionStartDate`/`EndDate`, typed `Date` but described as "date and time, in the local time zone for a store";
    - `promotionConversionRate`: "units sold, minus any cancelled orders, divided by the number of details page views for that promotion";
    - `promotionGlanceViewCnt`.
  - Each field needs the PAAM right `analytics_sales_and_traffic_view_pa` and carries `@resultRetention(duration: "P30D")`. [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, read 2026-10-14)
  - The guide calls the access level "Sales and traffic access", which "Grants the sales, traffic, promotion … metric groups". It says the promotion attributes "are not additive across periods". [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, re-read 2026-10-14)
  - The two vocabularies differ: Data Kiosk has "Lightning Deal" and "Sales Discount", the Promotions API has `DEAL` and `PRICE_DISCOUNT`. No page read says how `dealId` relates to `promotionId` or `promotionsApiMappingId`. Reconciliation is by ASIN and dates only.
- **What `yearOverYear` aligns on.**
  - "Every numeric metric can carry a `timeRangeBenchmarking` object".
  - At `WEEK`, `weekOverWeek` compares against "The preceding Sunday-to-Saturday week" and `yearOverYear` against "the same ISO week number in the prior year".
  - At `MONTH`, `yearOverYear` compares against "the same calendar month in the prior year".
  - "no benchmarking is available at `DAY` granularity".
  - [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
  - The benchmark is aligned on the calendar, never on events [seat arithmetic from the dates in section A]:
    - Prime Day 2025 (July 8–11) fell in ISO week 28 and Prime Day 2026 (June 23–26) in ISO week 26. Each of those weeks' `yearOverYear` therefore compares an event week with a non-event week, and the June and July 2026 `MONTH` benchmarks do the same.
    - Prime Big Deal Days 2025 and 2026 both fell in ISO week 41.
    - Black Friday 2025 and 2026 both fall in ISO week 48, but the Black Friday Week window starts on different dates (November 21, 2024; November 20, 2025).
  - ISO weeks run Monday to Sunday, while the dataset's weeks run Sunday to Saturday. The guide does not say how the two are matched.
- **Search Query Performance has no benchmark field.**
  - A same-weeks-last-year SQP comparison is a second request. Each request covers one period ("Requests cannot span multiple periods"), and a `WEEK` request must start on a Sunday and end on a Saturday.
  - Under "Maximum reporting periods" the page lists "MONTH: 17 months" and "N/A" for the other periods, described as "The maximum reporting periods that you can specify using the `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime` parameters". By that heading it bounds the span between the two dates, yet the same entry says "Requests cannot span multiple periods". **The page disagrees with itself**, and it gives no lookback line for SQP. It is not a stated retention, and it does not say how far back the data goes.
  - [official] (Analytics Reports, re-read 2026-10-14, reconciliation)
  - Amazon's seller blog says, in its Demographics section, "Data in Brand Analytics usually goes back three years and is generally available within three days". That is a general Brand Analytics statement, not the SQP report's. [official] (What is Amazon Brand Analytics? (H1 "Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business"), https://sell.amazon.com/blog/brand-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
  - What binds for a given same-period-last-year request is the request's own outcome at run time (a `DONE` or `FATAL` report; method/report-lifecycle.md owns the lifecycle), not either figure.
  - The SQP help page says the queries include "organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page" and exclude widgets such as "Top Rated" or "New Arrivals". [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, version 5, read 2026-10-14)

## What practitioners add

- **Before an event: hold-back and browsing; after it: frugality.** "Consumers may hold back on their spending ahead of Black Friday/Cyber Monday". Shoppers "may be operating with a browsing mentality", so click-through can rise while conversion lags. After the event, "Consumers tend to be more frugal", "Conversion rates will undoubtedly fall from their highs", and CPCs can stay "higher than usual" for a week or two. The basis is qualitative, from Perpetua's ad experience, with no dataset cited. [opinion] (Laura Conboy, Perpetua, "The impact of peak events on consumer purchasing habits", June 22, 2023, https://perpetua.io/blog-impact-of-peak-events-on-consumer-purchasing-behavior/)
- **Carts are filled before the event.** Shoppers "add items they think about buying to the shopping cart a few days before the event". On moving dates: "The movement of Prime Day to different dates depending on when it benefits Amazon." [opinion] (Juozas Kaziukėnas, Marketplace Pulse, "Prime Day's Growth Is Stalling", June 28, 2021, https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/prime-days-growth-is-stalling)
- **The Cyber Five shape.** Deals rose in "the week preceding Thanksgiving". "Once Cyber Monday was over, the number of deals dropped to the lowest point in seven days", and Cyber Five "marked the start of holiday shopping". The measure was active deals counted, not sales. [opinion] (Juozas Kaziukėnas, Marketplace Pulse, "The Shape of Cyber Five 2019 on Amazon", Dec 4, 2019, https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/the-shape-of-cyber-five-2019-on-amazon)
- **No major rewrites three weeks out; traffic continues after.** Three weeks before Prime Day 2025: "This is not the time for major rewrites", though smaller changes such as tightening titles and testing hero images are still suggested. After the event: "Expect continued traffic through July 14–18", and "the real gains often extend beyond it". [opinion] (Nhan Dinh, Seller Labs, "Amazon Prime Day 2025 Is Official: Your 3-Week Countdown Prep Plan", June 18, 2025, https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/amazon-prime-day-2025-final-seller-checklist/)
- **Lock listing plans three to four weeks out.** "3–4 Weeks Before Prime Day: Lock Inventory and Listing Plans"; that step's own list includes "Update listing content", and the next step, "1–2 Weeks Before Prime Day: Activate Monitoring", has no content work. During the event the page lists "Listing suppression or content changes" among the things to monitor. [opinion] (Bob Norberg, eComEngine, "Prime Day Is in June: The Last-Mile Readiness Plan Amazon Sellers Need Now", June 8, 2026, updated June 11, 2026, https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/prime-day-readiness-plan-amazon-sellers)
- **Judge the event against "your normal baseline"**, and against "historical promotional periods". No window is defined. [opinion] (Bob Norberg, eComEngine, "The Prime Day Profit Scorecard: 10 Metrics Amazon Sellers Should Watch Daily", June 24, 2026, https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/prime-day-metrics-amazon-sellers)
- **The deal halo.** "Amazon's promotional materials for Lightning Deals note that many sellers see a bump in sales that lasts beyond the deal itself and extends to other products" — eComEngine relaying Amazon, not its own measurement; the Amazon material itself was not read. Event Lightning Deals "will generally incur higher fees". The article is dated 2021. [opinion] (Kristen Hicks, eComEngine, "Amazon Lightning Deals: Everything You Need to Know", updated Oct 19, 2021, https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/amazon-lightning-deals)
- **Deal eligibility as practitioners state it (older than Amazon's 2026 page):** "a rating of at least 3 stars" for the product, a "minimum 3.5-star Seller rating overall", "Prime-eligible in all regions", "an Amazon sales history" and "at least a 15% discount". Fees: $150 typical, $300–$500 at major events (2023 figures, superseded by Amazon's own fee page; section F). Approved deals show their time "one week before". [opinion] (Brian Connolly, Jungle Scout, "Amazon Lightning Deals for FBA Sellers: Are They Worth It?", May 9, 2023, https://www.junglescout.com/blog/amazon-lightning-deals/). Helium 10 gives the same 3-star (product) and 3.5-star (seller) figures, adds "at least five seller feedback ratings per month", and quotes Amazon ("According to Amazon") that "Eligible ASINs are refreshed every week". [opinion] (Helium 10 Team, "How to Use Amazon Lightning Deals: More Than Just a Quick Buck", published Dec 29, 2020, modified Nov 21, 2023, https://www.helium10.com/blog/amazon-lightning-deals/, canonical https://www.helium10.com/blog/how-to-use-amazon-lightning-deals/)
- **A Prime Day price rule (unverified):** "the offer you give must be the lowest selling price since the beginning of the year". The same page says "In general, Amazon allows Prime Day deal submissions in March-April." [opinion] (Arishekar N, SellerApp, "Ultimate Guide to Amazon Lightning Deals And Its Benefits", first published Aug 13, 2021, modified Oct 10, 2026, https://www.sellerapp.com/blog/amazon-lightning-deals/). No Amazon page read here states either.
- **Event deadlines fall about two months ahead.** For the October 8–9, 2024 event, "it's now too late to set up a special 'Prime Fall Deal Event' deal in Seller Central (the deadline was August 11)", and "Amazon states that you must have your inventory arrive at fulfillment centers by September 13" for the Prime badge. [opinion] (Brian Connolly, Jungle Scout, "How Sellers Can Prepare for Amazon's Prime Big Deal Days 2024", Aug 26, 2024, https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-prime-day/)

## Published research

- [research] **Ron Kohavi, Roger Longbotham, Dan Sommerfield, Randal M. Henne, "Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide", *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 18:140–181, 2009 (doi 10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1).**
  - Under "Beware of day of week effects", the authors "strongly recommend running experiments for at least a week or two, then continuing by multiples of a week". They add: "This lesson can be generalized to other time-related events, such as holidays and seasons". In context the lesson is that users on different days "represent different segments" to be analyzed separately; the paper does not prescribe excluding holidays.
  - Randomized assignment is what "neutralize[s] confounding variables by distributing them equally".
  - **Transfer:** high for the method, by contrast. A listing before/after comparison has no concurrent randomized control, so an event inside either window is not neutralized. Windows should be whole weeks.
- [research] **Kay H. Brodersen, Fabian Gallusser, Jim Koehler, Nicolas Remy, Steven L. Scott, "Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models", *Annals of Applied Statistics* 9(1):247–274, 2015 (arXiv 1506.00356).**
  - The counterfactual is built from control series, "a synthetic control that would have occurred had no intervention taken place".
  - **Transfer:** moderate. It supports subtracting the competitor set's same-period movement. During a sitewide event, though, the controls are hit by the same event in different ways: some competitors run deals and some do not. That weakens them as a counterfactual. The page read did not state the paper's control assumptions.
- [research] **Haode Yang, Parth Gupta, Roberto Fernandez Galan, Dan Bu, Dongmei Jia, "Seasonal relevance in e-commerce search", CIKM 2021 (Amazon Science).**
  - "39% queries are highly seasonally relevant to the time of search".
  - Adding seasonal relevance features in an online A/B test "results in 2.20% higher purchases".
  - **Transfer:** direct for the exposure stage. Amazon's ranking itself shifts with season, so impressions can move with no change to the listing. The figures belong to 2021 and to Amazon's system of that time.
- [research] **Malcolm Wolff, Kin G. Olivares, Boris Oreshkin, Sunny Ruan, Sitan Yang, Abhinav Katoch, Shankar Ramasubramanian, Youxin Zhang, Michael Mahoney, Dmitry Efimov, Vincent Quenneville-Belair, "SPADE: Split peak attention decomposition", NeurIPS 2024 Workshop on Time Series in the Age of Large Models (Amazon Science).**
  - Peak events, "special periods such as promotions and holidays", "create significant spikes in demand followed by demand ramp down periods".
  - Forecasting models "overreact to demand peaks by carrying over the elevated PE demand into subsequent Post-Peak-Event (PPE) periods".
  - **Transfer:** high for the window rule. The weeks after an event are a ramp-down, not a baseline. A naive before/after makes the same error the paper corrects.
- [research] **Hanyu Zhang, Chuck Arvin, Dmitry Efimov, Michael Mahoney, Dominique Perrault-Joncas, Shankar Ramasubramanian, Andrew Wilson, Malcolm Wolff, "LLMForecaster: Improving seasonal event forecasts with unstructured textual data", NeurIPS 2024 Workshop on Time Series in the Age of Large Models (Amazon Science):** models can "fail to anticipate seasonal surges in customer demand" around "major exogenous events like holidays". **Zexing Xu, Linjun Zhang, Sitan Yang, Nan Jiang, "Peak period demand forecasting with proxy data: GNN-enhanced meta-learning", WACV 2024 Workshop on Physical Retail AI (Amazon Science):** "the limited availability of historical data from these peak periods poses a significant challenge". **Transfer:** a seller has one or two past instances of each event, which is too few to model an event's lift. That argues for excluding event windows rather than adjusting for them.
- [research] **Gap fill: within-month consumption cycles.**
  - **Melvin Stephens Jr., "'3rd of tha Month': Do Social Security Recipients Smooth Consumption Between Checks?", *American Economic Review* 93(1):406–422, 2003** (doi 10.1257/000282803321455386; NBER Working Paper 9135, August 2002; the abstract was read on the NBER page and the record on Crossref). Using daily diary data from the Consumer Expenditure Survey, "both the dollar amount and probability of expenditures increase immediately following the receipt of this check", mainly "amongst households for whom Social Security is the primary source of income". The magnitudes "are relatively small".
  - **Justine Hastings, Ebonya Washington, "The First of the Month Effect: Consumer Behavior and Store Responses", *American Economic Journal: Economic Policy* 2(2):142–162, 2010** (doi 10.1257/pol.2.2.142; the abstract was read on Crossref). "benefit recipients decrease expenditures on, and consumption of, food throughout the benefit month". The decrease is "largely driven by reductions in quantity, not quality", and grocery "prices … vary pro-cyclically with demand".
  - **Michael Gelman, Shachar Kariv, Matthew D. Shapiro, Dan Silverman, Steven Tadelis, "Harnessing naturally occurring data to measure the response of spending to income", *Science* 345(6193):212–215, 2014** (doi 10.1126/science.1247727; the editor's summary was read on Crossref). From 60 million transactions by 75,000 people: people "go on a mini–spending spree after they get their paychecks or pensions". This is "mostly explained by the convenience of linking regular payments, such as rent and utilities, to regular income". "cash-strapped people are more likely to increase their spending in response to receiving income."
  - **Transfer:** low to moderate, and narrow. These are US spending data on groceries, restaurants and household payments, not Amazon listings. They establish that spending can follow the pay and benefit calendar, most for low-income households. They do not show the size of a month-start effect on an Amazon ASIN. A two-week window that holds a month start, compared with one that does not, can differ for that reason alone, most plausibly in low-price consumables. The seller's own daily series is the test (Open question 16).
- [research] **Gap fill: event-aligned modelling.** **Sean J. Taylor, Benjamin Letham, "Forecasting at Scale", *The American Statistician* 72(1):37–45, 2018** (doi 10.1080/00031305.2017.1380080). Only the bibliographic record was read; the preprint answered 403. The method's documentation, which was read, models each event from a table with "a row for each occurrence of the holiday", which "must include all occurrences of the holiday, both in the past … and in the future". `lower_window` and `upper_window` "extend the holiday out to" days around it, for example Christmas Eve. [opinion: software documentation] (Seasonality, Holiday Effects, And Regressors | Prophet, https://facebook.github.io/prophet/docs/seasonality,_holiday_effects,_and_regressors.html, read 2026-10-14). **Transfer:** high for the design idea. An event's effect is placed by its own dates and a window of days around them, not by the calendar week. This is the event-aligned comparison the Data Kiosk benchmark lacks. No finding of the paper itself is claimed.
- **Not read (unreachable), so no finding is claimed:** the marketing-science literature on pre- and post-promotion dips (van Heerde, Leeflang & Wittink, *Journal of Marketing Research* 2000; Macé & Neslin, *JMR* 2004). The publisher pages returned 403 and the repository pages 404.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Amazon's sale events change dates, length and even month from year to year, and dates can differ by country. Event dates are read at run time, never remembered. (The history of Prime Day; Prime Day 2026 and Prime Big Deal Days 2026 pages.)
- Deals and coupons show in search results and on detail pages, so a promotion, the seller's own or a competitor's, moves click-through and conversion together. (Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…)
- Best Sellers Rank weighs recent sales more, so an event moves rank without any listing change. (Guide to Amazon sales rank.)
- Amazon search uses seasonality in ranking, so exposure can move with the calendar. (Seasonal relevance in e-commerce search.)
- Demand spikes in an event and ramps down after it, so the weeks after an event are not a baseline. (SPADE.)
- Day-of-week effects exist, so windows are whole weeks. Holidays and seasons are the same kind of time effect. (Kohavi et al. 2009.)
- Amazon's own guidance schedules listing-content work weeks before a peak and promotion setup after it. Amazon publishes no rule forbidding edits during an event in any page read. (Amazon FBA holiday guide.)
- Event deal submission windows and eligibility thresholds are Amazon's to state. They live in Seller Central and on Amazon's promotion pages, not in a skill. (Prime Day | Sell on Amazon; Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…)
- Keepa's `/deal` endpoint lists price and rank changes. Amazon deal badges are in the product's `deals` field and the Lightning Deal and Prime-exclusive price slots. (DealRequest.java; Product Object.)
- Return figures for holiday purchases keep arriving until Amazon's extended return date, and Amazon's own return data lags weeks. (Holiday return policy 2025; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide.)
- Gap fill:
  - A listing change is not live the moment it is accepted. Amazon states propagation times on its help pages, and says latency varies with "website traffic volumes". A change's live date is the date it is read back as live. (Amazon search glossary; Product title requirements and guidelines.)
  - A+ content passes a moderation queue before it publishes. Amazon says that queue is "longer during periods of high volume". (Complete Guide to A+ Content for Brand Owners.)
  - Amazon may apply its own title recommendation after a brand owner's review window, so a listing can change with no seller write. (Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27.)
  - For a deal in Upcoming status, Amazon asks sellers to avoid major changes to the participating ASINs. A price edit can suppress the deal, closing the listing cancels it, and new child ASINs make it revalidate. (Amazon deals.)
  - Event deals are decided weeks before the event. Amazon reveals a deal's run time in Seller Central ahead of the run, by a lead the page states, and a deal submitted for an event may run in a non-event week instead. (Deals for events.)
  - Deal, coupon and event price-discount eligibility each set two floors, the seller's feedback rating and the product's star rating, and the values differ by promotion type. Amazon's own pages can disagree on a value (the coupon floor), so the creation flow's answer binds. (Amazon deals; Coupon eligibility criteria; Price discounts and events; Understanding coupon errors as read in angles/price-position.md.)
  - Running a deal does not secure the Featured Offer. (Understand deal fees; Troubleshoot suppressed and active deals.)
  - Promotion discounts add to Subscribe & Save discounts. (Subscribe & Save.)
  - Amazon does not allow temporary or promotional words in search terms, nor holiday references in A+ outside the Brand Story. (Use search terms effectively; A+ content guidelines; Product detail page rules.)
  - Manage Your Experiments randomizes by customer, runs one experiment per ASIN, and does not say it pauses for events. Amazon's one-year projection from a result ignores seasonality. (Manage Your Experiments; Experiment results; Create a listing experiment.)
  - The Promotions API (2025-12-01) only reads promotions. The promotion and coupon performance reports join to it through `promotionsApiMappingId`. Data Kiosk's promotion fields have no such id. (promotions_2025-12-01.json; SP-API Release Notes; Performance Reports; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql.)
  - Data Kiosk's `yearOverYear` compares the same ISO week number or the same calendar month of the prior year. It is aligned on the calendar, not on events, and is not offered at day granularity. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide.)
  - Search Query Performance has no benchmark field. Each request covers one period, and a week runs Sunday to Saturday. The page does not state how far back SQP data goes; a request's outcome does. (Analytics Reports.)
  - Event length and run-up differ by marketplace. (Amazon Great Indian Festival pages; Amazon UK Black Friday Week press release; Prime Day 2026 page.)

## Positions

1. **P-seasonality-and-events-1**: An effect review gives no verdict, neither held nor not held, when either window overlaps an announced major sale, its early-deals run-up or the ramp-down weeks after it. It says "cannot tell" and names the event and its dates. — Controlled experiments on the web (Kohavi et al. 2009); SPADE; The impact of peak events on consumer purchasing habits (Perpetua); Amazon Prime Big Deal Days: Everything you need to know.
2. **P-seasonality-and-events-2**: cx reads event dates at run time from Amazon's announcement pages for the seller's marketplace. When the Ads source exists, it also reads the Ads API events list. It never uses a remembered calendar, because Amazon moves the month, the length and the per-country dates. — The history of Prime Day; When is Amazon Prime Day 2026?; Prime Big Deal Days is back October 6-7; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`/sp/v1/events`).
3. **P-seasonality-and-events-3**: "A major sale" is defined from evidence, not from a list. It is either a sitewide event Amazon announced for the marketplace, or in-window signs such as Keepa `deals` with an event `dealType` (for example `PRIME_DAY`, `PRIME_DAY_EARLY`, `SPECIAL_EVENT_SALE`) across the seller's or the competitors' ASINs. Because Keepa adds new types at any time, cx reads the badge text rather than matching a fixed list. — Product Object (Keepa); Product.java; Amazon Prime Big Deal Days: Everything you need to know.
4. **P-seasonality-and-events-4**: cx does not propose a listing change whose baseline or observation window would include an announced major sale. It tells the seller when the next clean window opens, because Amazon's own timeline puts listing-content work weeks before a peak and practitioners lock listing plans three to four weeks out. — Amazon FBA holiday guide; Prime Day Is in June: The Last-Mile Readiness Plan (eComEngine); Amazon Prime Day 2025 Is Official: Your 3-Week Countdown Prep Plan (Seller Labs).
5. **P-seasonality-and-events-5**: Baseline and observation windows are whole weeks with the same weekdays, and when a window touches a seasonal swing the seller's same weeks last year are read alongside. — Controlled experiments on the web (Kohavi et al. 2009); sales.json (`getOrderMetrics`, two-year interval).
6. **P-seasonality-and-events-6**: Every change record names the seller's own promotions that ran in either window, read from the Promotions API, the promotion and coupon performance reports or the Data Kiosk promotion fields, because deals and coupons show in search results and on detail pages and so move click-through and conversion. — Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…; Promotions API; Performance Reports; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide.
7. **P-seasonality-and-events-7**: cx subtracts the competitor set's same-period movement before reading its own. When competitors ran deals in the window (Keepa `deals`, `csv[8]`, `csv[33]`, `couponHistory`; `getCompetitiveSummary` `promotions`), it says the comparison is weakened rather than treating it as a clean control. — Inferring causal impact… (Brodersen et al. 2015); Product Object (Keepa); productPricing_2022-05-01.json.
8. **P-seasonality-and-events-8**: cx does not use Keepa's `/deal` endpoint as evidence of an Amazon promotion, because it lists price and rank changes. Amazon deal badges come from the product's `deals` field and its Lightning Deal and Prime-exclusive price slots. — DealRequest.java; Product Object (Keepa).
9. **P-seasonality-and-events-9**: A rank jump or a `monthlySold` jump during an event is read as event demand, not as a listing effect, because Best Sellers Rank weighs recent sales. — Guide to Amazon sales rank: Best Sellers Rank (BSR); Product Object (Keepa).
10. **P-seasonality-and-events-10**: Before calling an impression or session drop a listing problem, cx checks query volume for the core queries over the same weeks (Search Query Performance `searchQueryVolume`, Search Terms `searchFrequencyRank`), because Amazon search itself ranks with seasonality. — Seasonal relevance in e-commerce search (Yang et al. 2021); Analytics Reports.
11. **P-seasonality-and-events-11**: cx states deal-eligibility thresholds and event submission deadlines only from Amazon's page, naming the page and the version recorded in the tree, or from the seller's Seller Central copy; never from memory. A rating drop or a missed deadline is a reason a planned event deal may not run. — Guide to running Amazon seller promotions…; Prime Day | Sell on Amazon.
12. **P-seasonality-and-events-12**: cx does not assume a post-event halo or a post-event dip. It reads the seller's own daily orders around the last events and lets that shape set how long the after-event exclusion lasts for that ASIN. — The impact of peak events… (Perpetua); Amazon Prime Day 2025 Is Official… (Seller Labs); Amazon Lightning Deals: Everything You Need to Know (eComEngine); SPADE.
13. **P-seasonality-and-events-13**: A returns-targeted verdict whose window includes holiday purchases waits until Amazon's extended return date has passed and Amazon's return data has caught up. — Amazon's holiday return policy 2025; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide.
14. **P-seasonality-and-events-14**: When a standing instruction lets cx write and an event window is open, cx's report says the change's effect cannot be judged until the window clears, and the change log records the event beside the change. — Controlled experiments on the web (Kohavi et al. 2009); SPADE.

15. **P-seasonality-and-events-15**: cx proposes a listing change before an event only when the change can be live and its whole observation window can close before the event's early-deals run-up begins. For planning it allows the propagation time Amazon states on its search glossary page at the time, which Amazon says can be slower at high traffic; the window itself opens only on the date cx reads the change back as live.
