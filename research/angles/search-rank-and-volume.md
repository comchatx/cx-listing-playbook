# Search rank and search volume (third-party; no Amazon API)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage: exposure.** This angle covers two questions a seller asks before any listing change and after it:

1. "Where do we rank for keyword X?" (organic search position)
2. "How many people search keyword X?" (search volume)

Both measure the exposure stage of the funnel: whether the listing is shown, and how big the pool of searches is. Amazon does not answer either question the way sellers ask it. It gives no keyword-position figure through any API. It gives a search count only for queries tied to the seller's own brand (Search Query Performance). Its broader demand signal is a *rank of searches* (Search Frequency Rank), not a count. The figures sellers usually quote ("rank #7", "12,400 searches a month") come from third-party tools that collect search pages and model volume, and their methods are not published.

**Skills it feeds:**
- `listing-review`: which source answers an exposure question, and what to say when the seller asks for rank.
- `effect-review`: what the exposure-stage metric of a change is, and why a third-party rank is a weak verdict basis.
- `listing-change-log`: which exposure baseline gets recorded before a change.
- `/listing-watch`: what a seller's standing instruction may ask to be watched.

**Why a senior operator cares.** Rank and volume are the numbers agencies report most often. They are also the numbers most often misread:
- a rank that moved because the checker's ZIP code or hour differed;
- a "search volume" from one tool that is half of another tool's;
- an organic position that sits below four ads;
- an "indexing" check that proves less than it seems.

A playbook that leaves out the difference between Amazon's measured exposure (impressions per query) and a tool's scraped position misses the most common error in listing reviews. That difference has to be stated plainly.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. What Amazon offers for search demand

**A1. Search Query Performance (SQP) report.**
- **What it is.** The SP-API report type `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_QUERY_PERFORMANCE_REPORT` "Provides overall query performance, such as impressions, clicks, cart adds, and purchases for a list of Amazon Standard Identification Numbers (ASINs) and date range that you specify." [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
- **RULE: who can request it.** "Sellers who have the Brand Analytics Selling Partner API role and are registered in Amazon Brand Registry." It "can only be requested", not scheduled. [official] (same page)
- **RULE: periods.** Periods are `WEEK`, `MONTH` and `QUARTER`, and "Requests cannot span multiple periods". "`dataStartTime` must be a Sunday and `dataEndTime` must be a Saturday when `reportPeriod=WEEK`." The `asin` option is "A space-separated list of ASINs … There is a 200 character limit." [official] (same page, `updatedAt` 2026-10-06; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
- **RULE: who sees the dashboard.** The Seller Central help page says "Brand Analytics is only available to sellers who have a Brand Representative role assigned to a brand in Brand Registry". [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, read 2026-10-14 through the help hub content call; text in capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`)
- **Field definitions** [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14). These are the volume and exposure figures Amazon itself defines:
  - `searchQuery`: "A buying customer's search query that is relevant to your branded catalog."
  - `searchQueryVolume`: "The number of times customers search for a query at a select time period." **This is Amazon's only per-query search count**, and it exists only for queries relevant to the seller's branded catalog.
  - `searchQueryScore`: "The rank for a search query in comparison to other queries for the ASIN. The ranking mechanism measures overall performance (the highest rank is 1)." This is a performance order within the ASIN's queries, not a search position.
  - `totalQueryImpressionCount`: "The total number of impressions that originate from the search results page for all ASINs with the current query."
  - `asinImpressionCount`: "The total number of impressions that originate from the search results page for the current ASIN with the current query."
  - `asinImpressionShare`: "The fraction of impressions for the ASIN in comparison to the total impression count for the query."
  - `totalClickRate`: "For a search query, the number of clicks divided by the search query volume."
  - The schema does not say whether the impressions are organic, sponsored, or both.
- **What the impressions include, per the dashboard help page.** "The search queries are selected based on their overall performance in search results, and includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers. It excludes traffic from widgets on the search page such as "Top Rated " or "New Arrivals."" And: "An impression is counted every time the ASIN is displayed in the search results page." The page names Sponsored Products only; it says nothing of Sponsored Brands. It states no launch date, week definition, retention or ASIN-view query cap. [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5, read 2026-10-14; capture in the office's capture folder (outside this repository))
- **Views and limits on the help page.** Brand view: "a maximum of 1000 query results". Query Detail view: "the top 10 ASINs for the selected search query". Amazon warns: "we do not recommend comparing the metric names of data across different dashboards." [official] (same page)
- **Launch.** The dashboard launched in Seller Central before the API: News_Amazon's post "We have launched two new Brand Analytics search dashboards, Search Catalog Performance and Search Query Performance" carries the post timestamp (`datetime`) 2022-04-14; the page shows only "5 years ago". The "New ASIN view" post carries 2022-09-08. [official] (New Brand Analytics search dashboards are now available, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1afe683eb0acb90bb31abdc34950ef74; Brands: New ASIN view in Brand Analytics Search Query Performance dashboard, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/5b5759d6f424964254d38fd09bd03bd1; both read 2026-10-14)
- **Release history.** The report entered the SP-API on the release notes' February 26, 2025 entry ("Added two new Analytics Reports: Search Catalog Performance Report … Search Query Performance Report"). The space-separated ASIN list came in the June 25, 2025 entry. [official] (SP-API Release Notes, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes, read 2026-10-14)
- **A figure that lives on the page and conflicts with another line on it.** The page lists "Maximum reporting periods: The maximum reporting periods that you can specify using the `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime` parameters, by `reportPeriod` type", then "MONTH: 17 months", with DAY, WEEK, QUARTER and YEAR as "N/A". ### B. What Amazon does not offer: an organic rank API

The only "rank" in Catalog Items is `salesRanks`, defined as "Sales rank of an Amazon catalog item", by classification or display group. That is category best-seller rank, not keyword position. [official documents; the search is the seat's]

### C. Rules on automated access to Amazon's pages

- Agents must not conceal that they are agents, "such as by (a) mimicking the speed or pattern of human keystrokes, page navigation, or other interactions or (b) completing or circumventing CAPTCHAs".
- Amazon "may limit, including by technical measures, whether and how any Agent accesses" its services. [official] (Conditions of Use, read 2026-10-14)
- An AI assistant that opened amazon.com search pages for a seller would fall under these terms. [seat]

## What practitioners add

**Vendors' volume accuracy claims contradict each other.** No independent test was found.
- **Jungle Scout (Brian Connolly, "Jungle Scout vs. Helium 10", Mar 21, 2024)** [opinion] compared 22 of its own brand's keywords with SQP volume. It reports Keyword Scout at "10.93%" average difference and Helium 10 Magnet at "-58.38%".
- **Helium 10 (Bradley Sutton, "Helium 10 vs. Jungle Scout Accuracy: Amazon Search Volume", Aug 23, 2024)** [opinion] used 31 keywords from one brand. It scored *rank-order* agreement: Helium 10 93.5% against Brand Analytics SFR, Jungle Scout 41.9% against SQP.
  - Sutton says "Brand analytics is based on "normalized searches."" and that "Helium 10 is based on normalized searches", while "Search Query Performance and Jungle Scout are basing their numbers on denormalized searches." He gives no formal definition, only an example: a shopper who repeats the keyword ("5 hours later searches that same keyword") counts as "ONE search" normalized and as several denormalized. On that argument the two can't be compared directly.
  - He calls the ranking "the arguably more important component of the 'Search Frequency Rank.'"
  - He also reports a weekly-data replication in which Jungle Scout scored 35.4% (11 of 31).
- The two tests use different ground truths and different scoring. Each vendor wins its own test.
- **The seat's reading:** a third-party volume can sit tens of percent from SQP for the same query. Which way it errs depends on the vendor. [seat]

**What the vendors say about their own methods.**
- **Jungle Scout support, "The Science Behind Keyword Scout"** (updated 2025-01-30) [opinion]: "a proprietary algorithm combined with historical and live data straight from Amazon". It says competitors estimate "using Google or Bing data". No method is published.
- **Jungle Scout support, "Keyword Rank information"** (updated 2025-02-11) [opinion]:
  - Exact search volume is "the number of times this exact keyword has been searched for on Amazon (does not include any plurals or misspellings)". Broad volume includes "plurals, misspellings or synonyms".
  - "Since keyword rankings and BSR can change several times throughout the day … it is possible to spot small differences when comparing the information in the Rank Tracker with the current rankings on Amazon."
- **Helium 10, Carrie Miller, "See the History of Search Frequency Rank for Any Keyword"** (Jun 7, 2023) [opinion]: SFR ranks terms "from most searched to least searched" across a list "often numbering from one to two million". Helium 10 uses it to check its own estimates and claims alignment "with about 98% accuracy". The method is not shown.
- **Helium 10 KB, "Under Which Circumstances Might Cerebro (Plus Magnet) Not Find Keyword Data?"** (updated 2026-01-06) [opinion]: a missing keyword "may have extremely low search volume"; the database grows "over time". Long-tail terms are thin in tool databases.
- **Helium 10 KB, "Why Does the Historical Search Volume Graph Sometimes Contain Gaps?"** (updated 2025-11-14) [opinion]: gaps appear while "the system processes and updates"; data "is typically refreshed by the end of the week".

**Rank readings vary with time and place, by the vendors' own account.**
- **Helium 10 KB, "Cerebro PRO Training Video: Ranking Differences in Cerebro vs. Keyword Tracker"** (updated 2024-03-22) [opinion]: the same product shows organic rank 16 in one Helium 10 tool and 31 in another, and "They both are" correct. Cerebro's rank was taken "within the last day, seven days, or 30 days"; Keyword Tracker's "hourly or daily, depending on which subscription plan".
- **Helium 10 KB, "Why Isn't My Product Indexing for a Specific Keyword?"** (updated 2023-12-24) [opinion]: "Amazon does not always produce the same results in an identical search for every geographical region. Simply changing the Deliver to address can completely change which products appear in which order".
- **SmartScout** (the URL `smartscout-data-collection-estimation-process` serves a landing page headed "Choose SmartScout for Wholesale and Online Arbitrage on Amazon", no author or date) [opinion]: "Keyword Map: See exactly how your products rank for a keyword across the United States". The page describes no collection or estimation method and makes no accuracy claim; it links a "SmartScout Accuracy" article that was not opened. That a location-by-location rank map is sold is consistent with rank varying by location. [seat]
- **DataDive KB, "Rank Radar"** (updated 2026-09-17) [opinion]: a keyword tracker that "updates daily" and "combines organic rank tracking with PPC data". It "now includes the ability to track keyword rankings for each variation of a product". Its performance section's averages use, in DataDive's own words, "a 7‑day average to smooth fluctuations."
- **DataDive KB, "What are Impression Share and Impression Rank in Rank Radar?"** (updated 2024-08-12) [opinion]: those metrics "are for Sponsored Products PPC Impressions". "Keep in mind that while the ad appeared, it might not have been visible to shoppers if it was below the fold."
- **DataDive KB, "Master Keyword List"** (updated 2026-07-22) [opinion]: the list shows every keyword the niche's competitors rank for, each competitor's organic rank and sponsored rank, search volume, and "Relevancy %" = "(# of competitors ranking for the keyword ÷ total competitors in the niche) × 100"; by default it keeps keywords with "30%+ relevancy". **What SQP counts, per practitioners.**
- **Perpetua, Martin Saunders, "Amazon Search Query Performance Report: tapping into the superpowers of search query data"** (Oct 16, 2024) [opinion]:
  - SQP impressions "include both organic and Sponsored Products ads", "exclude ASINs that appear in sections of the search results like "Editorial recommendations,"" and the "Top rated" / "Highly rated" widgets, and count a product "regardless of whether the product is in the scrollable view or below the fold." Sponsored Brands is not mentioned.
  - Query volume, per Perpetua citing Amazon, "includes all queries issued within a day" — the same query repeated by one session within 24 hours counts each time — and "clicking on the next page of search results will create additional search volume." This agrees with Sutton's "denormalized" reading of SQP.
  - Brand view shows "the top 1,000 queries", and the ASIN view "the top 100 queries per ASIN".
  - Attribution uses a 24-hour window; "you cannot isolate and understand individual competitors in your market niche".
  - **Disagreement with Amazon:** Perpetua's table defines Search Query Volume as "The number of times this query was shown". Amazon's schema says "The number of times customers search for a query". The schema wins.
  - **Agreement with Amazon:** Perpetua's organic-plus-Sponsored-Products reading and widget exclusions match Amazon's own dashboard help page (G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5, §A1). Its ASIN-view "top 100 queries per ASIN" is not on that page.
- **Amalytix, Trutz Fries, "Amazon Search Query Performance Report: Complete Guide"** (Jul 27, 2023) [opinion]: "only search terms for your own brand are included".

**Indexing check (searching keyword + ASIN) is a practitioner method.**

It says nothing about where the ASIN ranks for the plain keyword. Amazon's staff draws the same line: "keyword indexing issue or a search ranking challenge" (§D). [seat]
- The Helium 10 article suggests fixing non-indexing by repeating the keyword ("Use the keyword multiple times"). That runs against Amazon's title rule: "Titles must not contain the same word more than twice" (Product title requirements and guidelines, GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC v21, capture in the office's capture folder (outside this repository); `elements/title.md` owns the rule). ## Published research

1. **Daria Sorokina, Erick Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products", SIGIR 2016** (two-page talk abstract; amazon.science page) [research].
   - Finding: "Amazon Search powers the majority of Amazon's sales". The talk describes "a general machine learning framework used for ranking within categories" and "blending separate rankings in All Product Search", replacing earlier hand-tuned ranking functions. A 2016 description; the system has surely changed since.
   - Transfer: confirms that search exposure is the main path to sales and that ranking is learned, not rule-based. It gives no factor weights; nothing about "what moves rank" can be cited from it.
2. **Chiara Farronato, Andrey Fradkin, Alexander MacKay, "Self-Preferencing at Amazon: Evidence from Search Rankings", AEA Papers and Proceedings 113 (2023) 239–243; NBER WP 30894** [research].
   - Finding: from a browser-extension consumer panel, "Amazon branded products are indeed ranked higher than observably similar products". The boost is "30% to 60% of the prominence granted to sponsored products".
   - Transfer: rank depends on more than listing content and sales. Observably similar products do not get equal positions, so position gaps are not all listing-attributable.
3. **Raluca M. Ursu, "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions", Marketing Science, July 2018** (read via the INFORMS press release; the journal page refused the fetch) [research].
   - Finding: Ursu "analyzed rankings and search data from a field experiment at Expedia"; rankings "have a causal effect on what consumers search" but "do not necessarily affect purchase decisions directly". The release does not say what the experiment randomised; that detail is in the paper, which was not read.
   - Transfer: a hotel platform, not Amazon. Still, it supports treating rank as an exposure/click lever, judged on impressions and clicks, not on conversion.
4. **Abhisek Dash, Saptarshi Ghosh, Animesh Mukherjee, Abhijnan Chakraborty, Krishna P. Gummadi, "Sponsored is the New Organic: Implications of Sponsored Results on Quality of Search Results in the Amazon Marketplace", AIES 2024** [research].
   - Findings: 4,800 simulated searches in four Amazon stores. "Items with poor organic ranks (beyond 100th position) appear as sponsored results even before the top organic results on the first page". In the default "All categories" setting "only 306 organic results are shown for a given query".
   - Method: the authors collected the result pages with their own web scrapers in Amazon.com, .in, .de and .fr. They note "The search algorithms are likely to tailor results based on several signals, including the geographical location, consumer browsing history". They limited this with incognito Firefox browsers, the same time frame, the same delivery address and account / IP "as similar as possible". To see organic positions beyond 306 they searched in a category-refined setting, not the default.
   - Transfer: direct. Measuring organic rank needs a controlled location and context, and a paid slot can place an item far above its organic position.
5. **Rufus Rock, Ilan Strauss, Tim O'Reilly, Mariana Mazzucato, "Behind the clicks: Can Amazon allocate user attention as it pleases?", Information Economics and Policy 69 (2024)** (abstract on IDEAS/RePEc) [research].
   - Finding: over 2,000 Amazon queries, position adjusted for on-screen prominence "strongly shapes whether a user clicks". In the top five, typically four ads, users become less sensitive to relevance and price.
   - Transfer: supports the exposure → click link and the confound from ads at the top. The data source could not be read (working-paper page refused).
6. **Hannak, Soeller, Lazer, Mislove, Wilson, "Measuring Price Discrimination and Steering on E-commerce Web Sites", IMC 2014.** Only the conference slides opened. No Amazon-specific finding was extracted, and none is claimed here.

None of these studies measures how accurate third-party search-volume estimates are. No such study was found.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Amazon gives no keyword search position through the SP-API. The only rank in Catalog Items is category sales rank (`salesRanks`). (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; SP-API models)
- The order of `searchCatalogItems` keyword results is not the shopper's search order. (searchCatalogItems sorting?, Amazon staff replies)
- Amazon's only per-query search count is SQP `searchQueryVolume`. It covers queries relevant to the seller's branded catalog, for a Brand Registry seller with the Brand Analytics role. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Analytics Reports)
- Search Frequency Rank is a rank of terms within a department and period, not a count. (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json)
- SQP's exposure figures for an ASIN are `asinImpressionCount` and `asinImpressionShare` of `totalQueryImpressionCount`, counted from the search results page. They include organic and Sponsored Products results from the search results page and exclude search-page widgets; the schema itself does not split the two, so no SQP figure separates organic from paid exposure. (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json)
- Data Kiosk's seller analytics dataset gives the seller's own impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases per search keyword (`search.query`), aggregated by a granularity the query names. It has no market total, no volume and no position, and a null in a keyword row means "not available at this granularity", not zero. Which role opens it is not stated consistently on Amazon's pages; the `createQuery` result for the seller is the answer. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Data Kiosk API; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements)
- Amazon Ads impression share and impression rank count ad impressions among advertisers, not organic placement. Search-term impression share and rank are account-wide per search term, not tied to one ASIN. (Search term impression share report for Sponsored Products; Introducing the search term impression rank report; Top-of-search impression share; Search (Impression share) | Amazon Ads API; Sponsored Products keyword recommendations overview)
- Amazon's Conditions of Use exclude "data mining, robots, or similar data gathering and extraction tools". Agents must identify themselves and must not imitate human pacing or solve CAPTCHAs. (Conditions of Use)
- Third-party rank and volume figures are vendor estimates with unpublished methods, and vendors' own accuracy claims contradict each other. (Jungle Scout vs. Helium 10; Helium 10 vs. Jungle Scout Accuracy)
- An observed organic position depends on delivery location and time of check. (Helium 10 KB "Why Isn't My Product Indexing…"; Helium 10 KB "Ranking Differences…"; Dash et al., AIES 2024)
- Amazon staff separate "keyword indexing" from "search ranking". Amazon names the causes of an unsearchable ASIN (parent ASIN, no browse node, no buyable offer, future launch date, search suppression or detail page removed) and a Seller Central tool that diagnoses one ASIN against one search URL; it publishes no per-keyword indexing check and no API for either. (The listing information has not been indexed by Amazon; My ASIN is unsearchable, GHGHDMU4X5K9QGCV; Amazon search glossary, GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV)
- How long a change takes to reach search is stated differently on different Amazon pages and for different changes, and Amazon says it varies; it is never a fixed wait in a skill. (Amazon search glossary; My ASIN is unsearchable; Product title requirements and guidelines)

## Open questions

1. **AUP 4.3 and third-party tools.** The product is an SP-API solution provider. Does a cx skill that *recommends* a rank tracker or volume estimator, or reads a seller-provided export from one, fall under "use, offer or promote external (non-Amazon) data services that vend information or data retrieved from Amazon's websites"? The same question arguably reaches Keepa. **Settled by:** Jack's ruling, or Amazon Developer Support / the solution-provider agreement, read in full. Until then the seat would have skills accept a seller's file without naming or recommending a vendor.
2. **Is `searchQueryVolume` normalized?** *Half settled at reconciliation:* the SQP help page (G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5, read through the content call) says SQP includes "organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page" and excludes widgets, so the sponsored question is answered (Sponsored Brands is not named). Still open: whether a repeated search counts once (Sutton says Brand Analytics normalizes; Perpetua, citing Amazon, says repeats within a day count). **Settled by:** Amazon's Metric Glossary inside the dashboard (not captured).
3. **Ads API impression share.** *Settled at reconciliation from Amazon's reference pages:* v3 reporting offers `topOfSearchImpressionShare` for `spCampaigns`, `sbCampaigns` and `sbTargeting` (the Targeting reports page, per `angles/paid-keywords.md`, adds `spTargeting`) and no search-term share column; v1 reporting offers `metric.impressionShare` and `metric.impressionShareRank` per search term for SP and SB; keyword recommendations v4 return search-term impression share and rank. Left open: whether the seller's Ads account returns them; settled by the first authorized Ads API call when the Ads source is built.
4. **The "MONTH: 17 months" figure.** The page says it bounds the span between `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime`, which contradicts "Requests cannot span multiple periods" on the same page, and the page gives SQP no lookback line. How far back does SQP data go? **Settled by:** the first real run asking for a month older than 17 months (door.md's to record), or a docs correction.
5. **Data Kiosk keyword scope and role.** Is there a cap on the number of keywords per ASIN in `search.query`, and which role opens the search group? The guide names an access level, not a role or Brand Registry; the Data Kiosk API page lists Brand Analytics for every operation; the correction note does not name the seller analytics dataset. **Settled by:** the first Data Kiosk query on the beta store (door.md).
6. **POE access.** Is there any API access to POE search volume (none found in models or report types)? Has POE's keyword-level volume survived its AI redesign? **Settled by:** a signed-in look at POE.
7. **Conversational queries.** Does Amazon count conversational (Alexa for Shopping) queries, or Sponsored Products prompt impressions, in SQP `searchQueryVolume` and impressions? No Amazon page read says; the SQP help page speaks only of "the search results page". **Settled by:** an Amazon announcement or help-page change.
8. **Independent accuracy test.** No independent test of third-party volume or rank accuracy was found. The web-search budget ran out before a last pass on academic sources. **Settled by:** a later search session, or the seller's own comparison of a tool export with SQP for the same week.

## Positions

- **P-search-rank-and-volume-1.** When a seller asks for keyword rank, cx says Amazon gives no keyword position through its API. It offers Amazon's measured exposure for that query instead: SQP impressions and impression share, or Data Kiosk keyword impressions (`search.query`). (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json and the SP-API `models/` tree; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-2.** cx never reports an ASIN's position in `searchCatalogItems`, Keepa `/search` or the Creators API `SearchItems` results as its search rank. Keepa's claim that its product search follows Amazon's non-sponsored order is said, where used, as Keepa's claim. (searchCatalogItems sorting?; Creators API SearchItems; Keepa Request.java; Product Search — Keepa API Documentation)
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-3.** cx calls `searchQueryVolume` Amazon's count of searches for a query relevant to the seller's brand. It calls `searchFrequencyRank` a rank and never converts a rank into a count. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json)
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-4.** For a change aimed at exposure, the metric is the targeted queries' `asinImpressionShare` with `searchQueryVolume` beside it, read for the same period type before and after. Share is the ASIN's fraction of that query's total impressions, so movement in the query's total impressions is already in the denominator (competitors' ad spend on the query is not). (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json)
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-5.** cx treats ad spend, bids and placement changes in the same window as a confounder of SQP impression figures, because SQP counts organic and Sponsored Products impressions from the search results page together and no SQP field splits them. (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Perpetua, Martin Saunders [opinion])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-6.** cx reads Amazon Ads impression share or impression rank as ad-auction exposure, never as organic rank, and reads search-term share and rank as account-wide per term, never as one ASIN's. (Search term impression share report for Sponsored Products; Introducing the search term impression rank report; Top-of-search impression share; Search (Impression share) | Amazon Ads API; Sponsored Products keyword recommendations overview; DataDive KB [opinion])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-7.** cx does not load Amazon search pages to read rank or check indexing. Amazon's Conditions of Use exclude robots and data-extraction tools and bind agents to Agent Terms; the product's own rule bars scraping from platform IPs. (Conditions of Use; robots.txt; docs/PRODUCT.md §7 [internal])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-8.** When rank or third-party volume is wanted, cx asks the seller for an export from their own tool. It records the vendor, export date and, where shown, the location used, and labels every figure from it as a third-party estimate. (Jungle Scout vs. Helium 10 [opinion]; Helium 10 vs. Jungle Scout Accuracy [opinion]; Jungle Scout "Keyword Rank information" [opinion])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-9.** cx does not compare two rank readings taken at different times, by different tools or from different delivery locations as a change in rank. (Helium 10 KB "Ranking Differences in Cerebro vs. Keyword Tracker" [opinion]; Helium 10 KB "Why Isn't My Product Indexing…" [opinion]; Dash et al., AIES 2024 [research])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-10.** cx treats "indexed for keyword X" as a different claim from "ranks for keyword X". It counts non-zero `asinImpressionCount` for a query in SQP as Amazon's own evidence that the ASIN was shown for that query, and does not read a query's absence from SQP as proof the ASIN was not shown, since SQP lists only the top queries selected "based on their overall performance in search results", with a capped list (the cap is Amazon's to state and change; practitioners report one for the ASIN view). (The listing information has not been indexed by Amazon, Lola_Amazon; Helium 10 KB Index Checker [opinion]; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP; Perpetua, Martin Saunders [opinion])
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-11.** Before reading an exposure drop as listing-caused, cx checks the causes Amazon names for an unsearchable ASIN: search suppression, browse-node assignment, a buyable offer, the launch date, and a parent ASIN in place of a child. It starts the observation window only once the change is read back as live, not after an assumed wait, because Amazon's pages state different latencies for different changes and say latency varies. (My ASIN is unsearchable, GHGHDMU4X5K9QGCV; Amazon search glossary, GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV; Why Isn't My Listing Showing in Search Results? A Complete Guide, Manny_Amazon; Product title requirements and guidelines, GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC)
- **P-search-rank-and-volume-12.** cx tells a seller without Brand Registry and the Brand Analytics role that Amazon's Brand Analytics search reports (SQP, Search Terms, Search Catalog Performance) are not open to them. Data Kiosk's keyword-level search group is checked per field at query creation, and Amazon's pages do not agree on which role opens it, so cx tries it and reports the `createQuery` result rather than ruling it out. (Analytics Reports; Amazon Brand Analytics; Search Query Performance dashboard; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Data Kiosk API; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements)

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- A third-party volume figure can be expected to sit tens of percent from SQP for the same query, with the direction depending on the vendor. This is inferred from two vendor-run tests, not measured independently.
- An "organic rank" reported by a tool rarely equals what the shopper sees first, because ads and widgets occupy the top. The 2019–2022 layout figures should not be quoted as current.
- Daily Data Kiosk keyword impressions may make a shorter exposure observation window feasible than weekly SQP. That needs the first real Data Kiosk run before any skill says so.
- Conversational search may shrink the share of demand that appears as a query in SQP. If it does, a falling `searchQueryVolume` would not by itself prove falling demand.
- Practitioner advice to repeat a keyword to get indexed conflicts with Amazon's title rule against repeated words. The skill should not carry that advice.
