# Competitor discovery and new entrants

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage:** exposure and click-through. A competitor matters to a listing when it takes impressions on the terms the listing depends on (exposure), or takes the click on a results page where both appear (click-through). This angle does not cover what competitors charge or how their pages convert. Those belong to the competitor-monitoring and price angles. This angle covers who the competitors are, how Amazon's own surfaces name them, how a new one is spotted, and how a variation family is taken over.

**Skills it feeds:**
- `listing-review`: trigger family 1 (competitor moves). The top results on a core term change hands. A new listing climbs. A variation family changes shape. This angle says which source names "who is on my terms" and which only looks as if it does.
- `effect-review`: the competitor set is the control. Its same-period movement is subtracted before the listing's own change is judged. A set picked carelessly, or one that silently gains a new entrant during the observation window, gives a wrong verdict. A new entrant on the core terms during the window is a confounder.
- `/listing-watch` (the standing-instruction template): which competitor signals the seller wants cx to watch, and how often.

**Why a senior operator cares.** The competitor set is the denominator of every "the market moved" sentence. On day 1 of the beta run, cx "picked three of five competitors before knowing what they were" (`office-mailbox/from-engineering/20261013-0904-beta-browser-listing-run-day-1.md`, item 3). On day 2, a fresh Keepa read overwrote the previous day's `product.json`, so "yesterday's market" came from chat text and history arrays rather than from a kept file (`20261014-0921-beta-browser-listing-run-day-2.md`, lesson 6). A new entrant can only be seen against a kept baseline. [seat]

**A note on tags.** Keepa's own API documentation is cited as "[official — Keepa]". It is Keepa's word about Keepa's own fields and endpoints. It is never Amazon's word about Amazon's ranking.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. Best Sellers Rank and the discovery lists (descriptions, not enforced rules)

- **What BSR is built from.** Amazon says "The Amazon Best Sellers calculation is based on Amazon sales, and is updated frequently to reflect recent and historical sales of every item sold on Amazon." It also says the overall list "doesn't always indicate how well an item is selling in relation to similar items", and "We choose a few of the most popular subcategories in which the item has a high ranking in relation to other items in that subcategory, and showcase the item's rank on the product page." [official] (Amazon Best Sellers Rank – Amazon Customer Service, https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GGGMZK378RQPATDJ, read 2026-10-14)
- Amazon's seller guide says "Both recent sales and all-time sales factor into a BSR, though recent sales count more than older sales." It also says "BSRs aren't influenced by page views or customer reviews", "A product can have more than one BSR number if it falls under multiple categories, and the BSR can be different for each", "Rankings can also vary depending on the Amazon store", and "A top BSR doesn't always equal a high organic search ranking". The guide states the badge rule as "The best seller badge appears on detail pages for products that meet an ordering threshold and rank #1 in a category with at least 100 products" (the ordering threshold is not given). Published Sep 3, 2025. [official] (Guide to Amazon sales rank: Best Sellers Rank (BSR), https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-best-sellers-rank, read 2026-10-14)
- **The list headers, verbatim:**
  - Best Sellers: "Our most popular products based on sales. Updated frequently."
  - New Releases: "Our best-selling new and future releases. Updated frequently."
  - Movers & Shakers: "Our biggest gainers in sales rank over the past 24 hours. Updated frequently." Its "About" box defines the figure: "Movers & Shakers identifies the biggest gainers in sales rank compared to twenty-four hours ago. For example, if a music item has a current sales rank of 10, but was ranked 30 twenty-four hours ago, its Movers & Shakers increase would be 200%."
  - Most Wished For: "Our products most often added to Wishlists and Registries. Updated daily."
  [official] (Amazon.com Best Sellers / New Releases / Movers & Shakers / Most Wished For, https://www.amazon.com/gp/bestsellers, /gp/new-releases, /gp/movers-and-shakers, /gp/most-wished-for, read 2026-10-14)
- **"Hourly" comes from Amazon staff in the Seller Forums, not from a help page.** Rose_Amazon wrote on 2023-05-30: "While we update best sellers list every hour, it can take up to 12 hours for an order to show the sales rank. In some cases not seeing a sales rank can be because of filtering rules applied during sales rank generation." [official, forum staff reply] (Best Seller Rank no updated, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/d46bca47-c6e8-4552-9e73-321d16a16bbe, read 2026-10-14)
- Glenn_Amazon wrote, in a thread shown as "3 years ago": "Sales rank is calculated based on all-time sales of an ASIN/Product where recent sales are weighted more than older sales." [official, forum staff reply] (What calculation method do you think Amazon relies on to determine the ranking of a product?, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/322f2260-dac0-46fa-a1b7-5b1415472d0a, read 2026-10-14)
- **The New Release badge.** Michelle_Amazon wrote in June 2024: "The badge is earned within the first 90 days of launching your listing, not 30." She added that it is "awarded to products with a high level of sales demonstrated during the first three months after listing", and that "Amazon updates the recipient of its 'New Releases' badge hourly." [official, forum staff reply] (The #1 New Release Badge Requirements, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/84890c9f-d38a-42dc-a9f0-39c69310bf72, read 2026-10-14)
- **The Seller Central Sales Rank FAQ (G202059240, version 2) was read on 2026-10-14 from its capture** (capture `G202059240-v2`, taken through the help hub content call; see `method/help-page-access.md`). A plain fetch of the page URL returns only a shell; the capture holds the article. Its text: [official] (Sales Rank FAQ, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202059240, version 2, read 2026-10-14)
  - "Sales rank is calculated based on all-time sales of an ASIN/Product where recent sales are weighted more than older sales." "Sales ranks are relative." "Sales rank is generated based on order data and product reviews are not taken into consideration."
  - "Sales ranks are displayed for an ASIN with a sale record and if it has been assigned to the featured categories. Sales ranks are not generated for all categories". "While we update best sellers list every hour, it can take up to 12 hours for an order to show the sales rank." This is the same sentence Rose_Amazon posted above; the forum reply quotes the help page.
  - Variations: "In some stores, we add up all the sales for a child ASIN and apply it to the parent ASIN. In these stores, parent ASINs and child ASINs will have the same rank. In other stores, we count the orders for each child ASIN separately and generate sales rank for each child ASIN." Which applies "depends on the Website Display Group (wdg) of the parent ASIN".
  - Best seller badge: "assigned to those ASIN's that are featured as #1 in a featured category with at least 100 products. In addition to this, there are ordering thresholds that are placed on ASINs. These are different in different marketplaces." No badge is shown "for stores where orders of the child ASINs are assigned to the parent". "We keep revisiting and experimenting with this criteria".
  - New release badge: "provided to ASINs that are #1 best sellers among the new releases in a category. The definition of new release varies from category to category." Michelle_Amazon's "90 days" (above) is a staff forum figure; the help page does not give a fixed window.
- The Seller Central form of GGGMZK378RQPATDJ has no capture under the office's capture folder (outside this repository); its amazon.com Customer Service form is the page quoted at the top of this section.

### B. Brand Analytics: who gets the clicks on a term

RULES (enforced by the API):
- `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_TERMS_REPORT` "shares data on the top-3 clicked ASINs by search keyword and department for a marketplace". Its periods are `DAY`, `WEEK`, `MONTH` and `QUARTER`, and "Requests cannot span multiple periods". For `WEEK`, `dataStartTime` "must be a Sunday" and `dataEndTime` "must be a Saturday". [official] (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, read 2026-10-14; repository head `bad4c9b6`, 2026-10-13)
- The row fields are `departmentName`, `searchTerm`, `searchFrequencyRank` ("relative ranking of the searchTerm within a departmentName based on frequency of searches"), `clickedAsin`, `clickShareRank`, `clickShare` ("The fraction of clicks that the product received compared to the total clicks for the searchTerm within the departmentName") and `conversionShare` ("The fraction of conversions that the product achieved in comparison to the total conversions"). The schema's worked example is 13 of 98 conversions = 0.1327. [official] (same)
- **Who may request it.** The requester needs the Brand Analytics role and registration in Brand Registry, and must be a brand representative. The page says "This report is limited to approximately 12 million items". The report can be requested but not scheduled. The page's own wording is "most-clicked ASINs"; it does not say "top three". The schema does. [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
- **The dashboard shows more than the report.** Amazon says the Top Search Terms dashboard shows, as a list, "Search frequency rank for top search terms", "Top-clicked brands" and "Top-clicked categories", and adds "You can also see the product titles for the three top-clicked products for each search term". The same post says "Amazon Brand Analytics is available to brands enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry". Dated Oct 8, 2025. The SP-API schema has no brand or category column; that observation is the seat's. [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/brand-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
- **Search Query Performance gives each query's market totals beside the ASIN's share.** Examples are `totalQueryImpressionCount` ("all ASINs with the current query"), `asinImpressionShare`, `totalClickCount`, `asinClickShare`, `totalPurchaseCount` and `asinPurchaseShare`, plus `searchQueryVolume`. It names no other ASIN. Its periods follow the same calendar rule as the Search Terms report: for `WEEK`, `dataStartTime` "must be a Sunday" and `dataEndTime` "must be a Saturday", "or else a fatal error returns". [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, same repository, read 2026-10-14)
- **What the SQP totals contain.** The schema defines `totalQueryImpressionCount` as "The total number of impressions that originate from the search results page for all ASINs with the current query"; it does not mention sponsored results. The Seller Central dashboard page says the queries "are selected based on their overall performance in search results, and includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers. It excludes traffic from widgets on the search page such as "Top Rated " or "New Arrivals."" So a share loss in SQP can come from a competitor's Sponsored Products impressions as well as from its organic placement. [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, version 5, read 2026-10-14 from its capture capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`)
- **The dashboard names competitors; the report does not.** The same page: "The Query Detail view provides visibility into the performance of the top 10 ASINs for the selected search query from the Search Query Performance dashboard in comparison to your selected ASIN. The top performance is selected based on the Query ASIN score, calculated using a combination of search funnel impressions, clicks, cart adds, and buys." No SP-API field carries that view; it reaches cx only as a file the seller exports. [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, as above) [seat] for the absence in the schema.
- **No competitor identities in the seller analytics dataset.** Data Kiosk's dataset carries `search.query` metrics: impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases "WithPrice". These are the seller's own. No competitor ASIN is named. [official] (seller-analytics-dataset-guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide.md, read 2026-10-14)

- **How niches are formed.** "We create niches by grouping search terms based on the products customers view or purchase after searching." The tool covers "demand and purchasing behavior, competition and saturation, search terms and volume". It sits in Seller Central under Growth. Amazon cautions: "The tool is only a guide and should not be a substitute for your own judgment about demand for your products". [official] (Product Opportunity Explorer | Sell on Amazon, https://sell.amazon.com/tools/product-opportunity-explorer?lang=en-US, read 2026-10-14)

[official] (Customer Feedback API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/customer-feedback-api, read 2026-10-14)

### D. Catalog Items `searchCatalogItems` (2022-04-01)

RULES (the API's contract):
- **The request.** `GET /catalog/2022-04-01/items`.
  - `keywords`: "A comma-delimited list of keywords", up to 20. It cannot be combined with `identifiers`.
  - `brandNames` and `classificationIds` limit keyword searches. `keywordsLocale` sets the keywords' language.
  - `pageSize`: default 10, maximum 20.
  - `includedData`: may add `salesRanks`, `relationships`, `summaries` and the other datasets.
  [official] (Catalog Items API v2022-04-01 reference, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/catalog-items-api-v2022-04-01-reference, read 2026-10-14)
- **What comes back.** "The maximum number of items (ASINs) that can be returned and paged through is 1,000." For keyword searches `numberOfResults` "is the estimated total number". `refinements` lists `brands` and `classifications` with a count for each. [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Search catalog items, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/search-catalog-items, read 2026-10-14)
- **The rate limit: Amazon's pages disagree.** The reference page's table shows 5 requests a second, burst 5. The rate-limits page and the model show 2 and 2. The rate-limits page adds "500 per application" and "Keyword-based searches: 50 requests per application per second". The live figure is the `x-amzn-RateLimit-Limit` header. [official] (Catalog Items API Rate Limits, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/catalog-items-api-rate-limits; reference page; model, read 2026-10-14)
- **Ranking order: Amazon documents none.** Neither the reference nor the tutorial says how keyword results are ordered.
  - On GitHub, the commenter `chapmanjw` speaks for the Catalog team ("my team"). In 2021 he wrote that the search "sorts results based on popularity/relevancy". He added that it is "focused on 'sellable' products as opposed to 'buyable' products (where search relevancy is based on seller popularity instead of buyer popularity)", and that "we would not be able to replicate the search behavior of the retail websites exactly".
  - In 2022 he wrote that keyword results come from "matching some or all of the keywords".
  - In #1425 (2021-09-24), the developer `ellis` quoted an answer from an Amazon support case: searchCatalogItems "is intended to be seller oriented and presents ASINs in the same order as you would see them on the Seller Central product search page. These results can include ASINs that might be very relevant to seller's but not at all relevant to a buyer."
  [official, GitHub staff replies (chapmanjw 2021-07-20/21/22 in #1425; 2022-07-19 in #2998); the support quote is relayed by a developer in #1425, not in #2998] (searchCatalogItems sorting? #1425; [BUG] searchCatalogItems does not return the search identifiers or keywords in the result #2998, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/issues/1425 and /issues/2998, read 2026-10-14)
- **What the fields mean.**
  - `summaries.releaseDate` is "The earliest date on which the Amazon catalog item can be shipped to customers". It is not the date the item was first listed.
  - `relationships` gives `childAsins`, `parentAsins` and `variationTheme`.
  - `salesRanks` gives `classificationRanks` and `displayGroupRanks`, each with `title`, `link` and `rank`.
  [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, read 2026-10-14)

### E. Variation families: what may be grouped, and what changed

- **GUIDANCE.** Variations are "sets of products that are related to each other by color, size, or other attributes". They "should be the same type, brand, and style". Functionally different products "should be listed individually, on separate product detail pages". A newer version of a product needs "a new listing for the product rather than listing it as a variation of the older product". Dated Dec 31, 2024. [official] (How to create Amazon product variations, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-variation-listing, read 2026-10-14)
- **RULE (Customer Product Reviews policy).** Among listed violations: "A seller creates a variation relationship between products with the aim of manipulating reviews and boosting a product's star rating via review aggregation." This was quoted from the policy by Amazon staff (Emet_Amazon, 2025-05-11). The Seller Central policy page itself was not reachable without sign-in. [official, forum staff post] (Communication and Customer Product Review policies and violations, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/fd158c27-2dea-4652-9727-7836aaf5c0f8, read 2026-10-14)
- **RULE (announced change to review sharing).** "Starting February 12, 2026 … reviews will only be shared between variations with minor differences that don't affect functionality. Reviews will no longer appear on variations with significant differences". [official] (Changes to review sharing across product variations starting Feb 12, News_Amazon, posted 2026-01-07, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a572986d-9bb1-4e91-a53f-41c575874dd7, read 2026-10-14)
  - Sharing continues for color or pattern, same-function size, pack size or quantity, secondary scent, and model fitment.
  - Rollout was "gradually by product category between February 12, 2026, and May 31, 2026", with email "30 days before any changes affect your products".
  - The help page now carries the same rule: "Reviews are only shared when product variations have minor differences that don't impact the product's core functionality", with lists of shared and not-shared differences, and "If you have to update your variation themes after the change takes effect, reviews will be re-shared for eligible products." [official] (Guidelines for reviews shared across product variations, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G74DLBUEHT5GY88C, version 3, read 2026-10-14 from its capture capture `G74DLBUEHT5GY88C-v3`)
- **RULE (variation themes), as first announced and then revised.** The theme list changes by announcement, so no date or scope below is a standing rule.
  - First post: "Between September 2, 2025, and November 30, 2025, we will remove variation themes from our product templates that aren't relevant or frequently used". Impacted themes were marked "Deprecated: Do Not Use". "If you try to update a listing with a deprecated variation theme, you'll receive an error message that states, "the value specified is invalid" and the update will not be successful." [official] (Removal of irrelevant variation themes will start September 2, News_Amazon, 2025-08-19, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/29b9e0b4-2b4d-4caa-b4af-83b57c47862d, read 2026-10-14)
  - The update narrowed it: "We've revised the list to only remove variation themes that had no sales in the past 12 months", "we won't remove any versions of critical variation themes like size, color, and style", "Your existing variation families will continue to operate normally, with no disruption to sales", and "Child ASINs will stay active even if you choose to update your variation themes." The update gives no removal date and does not repeat the error text. [official] (Update to irrelevant or redundant variation theme removal, News_Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0a027b19-0e4f-4525-9535-13aefae92cda, read 2026-10-14)
  - Whether the deprecated-theme error still applies to themes kept after the revision is not stated. Which theme values are live for a product type is read at run time from `getDefinitionsProductType` (values flagged `enumDeprecated` are not to be used), as `elements/variations.md` records. [seat]
- **RULE (who may change a family).** The Variation relationship FAQ answers "A competitor added their product to my variation family, what can I do?": "If you believe a bad actor has manipulated a variation family (such as adding an invalid product or removing a valid product), contact Selling Partner Support with specific details about the issue." Families created by another brand owner: "Work directly with the original brand owner". It also says "Product detail pages are tied to ASINs, not to individual sellers", so another seller's valid children appear on the same page. [official] (Variation relationship FAQ, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201951410, version 16, read 2026-10-14 from its capture capture `G201951410-v16`)
- **What Amazon will notify, and what it will not.**
  - `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` fires "whenever there is a change to the title, description, bullet points, or images, for any ASIN that the selling partner has a brand relationship with". It is delivered through EventBridge.
  - `ITEM_PRODUCT_TYPE_CHANGE` covers a product-type change on a brand-owned item.
  - `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` fires on a change to "any of the top 20 offers" or the Buy Box. It is delivered through SQS.
  - `LISTINGS_ITEM_STATUS_CHANGE`, `LISTINGS_ITEM_ISSUES_CHANGE` and `LISTINGS_ITEM_MFN_QUANTITY_CHANGE` exist but concern the seller's own listings items; none of them, as described, names a relationship or variation change.
  - No listed notification names a change to a variation family; the word "variation" does not appear in the notification descriptions read. [official] (Notification Type Values, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values.md, updated 2026-09-30, read 2026-10-14)

### G. Keepa (Keepa's word about Keepa's endpoints)

- **Best Sellers (`/bestsellers`).** "Retrieve an ASIN list of the most popular products based on sales in a specific category or product group". [official — Keepa] (Best Sellers — Keepa API Documentation, https://keepa.com/api-docs/best-sellers.html, read 2026-10-14)
  - Lists are built from sales rank. "Lists are usually updated hourly" and are cached up to an hour.
  - Root lists hold up to 500,000 ASINs and sub-category lists up to 10,000.
  - `range` is `0` (current rank) or a 30-, 90- or 180-day average. `month` and `year` give a past month's list from the last 36 months. `variations=0` (the default) returns one variation per parent. `sublist` ranks by sub-category rank.
  - Products without an accessible sales rank are excluded. "Keepa cannot always correctly identify the sales rank reference category".
  - The Java client says the list "starts with the best selling product (lowest sales rank)". (BestSellers.java, https://github.com/keepacom/api_backend, head `683bfa83`, 2026-07-25)
- **Product Finder (`/query`).** "Search for products in the Keepa database that match your specified criteria". It returns ASIN lists. It "may not find all products on Amazon that match your query". Unless `lastUpdate` is set, it covers only products updated in the last six months. [official — Keepa] (Product Finder — Keepa API Documentation, https://keepa.com/api-docs/product-finder.html; ProductFinderRequest.java, read 2026-10-14)
  - Filters include `listedSince_gte/_lte` ("as far as Keepa can determine it"), `trackingSince`, `salesRankDrops30/90/180/365`, and the `delta…`/`deltaPercent…` families on `COUNT_REVIEWS` and `SALES`.
  - Further filters: `monthlySold` and `deltaPercent90_monthlySold`, `title` (the title "must contain the specified string's keywords", up to 50, case-insensitive, "Partial keyword matches are not supported"), `brand`, `categories_include`, `hasParentASIN`, `historicalParentASIN`, `singleVariation` and `variationCount`.
  - Up to 10,000 ASINs per query. Sort on up to three fields; the default is current sales rank, ascending.
  - With `stats=1` it adds a Search Insights summary of the result set: `brandCount`, `topBrandsWithCounts`, `sellerCount`, `avgReviewCount` and others. That summary has no new-product count. (Search Insights Object, https://keepa.com/api-docs/search-insights-object.html)
- **Product Search (`/search?type=product`): Keepa's two documents disagree on the count, and only one states an order.**
  - The web page: "Search for Amazon products using keywords, returning up to 20 results per search term." "The results are in the same order as a search on Amazon, excluding sponsored content." It mentions no `page` parameter. [official — Keepa] (Product Search — Keepa API Documentation, https://keepa.com/api-docs/product-search.html, read 2026-10-14)
  - The Java client: "Search for Amazon products using keywords with a maximum of 50 results per search term." Its `page` parameter takes "Valid values 0 - 9. Each search result page provides up to 10 results", and "When not using the page parameter the first 40 results will be returned." It says nothing about order. [official — Keepa] (Request.java, https://github.com/keepacom/api_backend/blob/master/src/main/java/com/keepa/api/backend/structs/Request.java, read 2026-10-14)
  - So the result count is whatever a call returns, and the order is Keepa's claim on its web page alone. Neither document is Amazon's word, and the claim has not been checked against an Amazon results page (open question 8). `angles/search-rank-and-volume.md` reads only the Java client and so says the order is undocumented; the web page does state it, as Keepa's claim. [seat]
- **Product object fields for spotting entrants and takeovers.** [official — Keepa] (Product Object — Keepa API Documentation, https://keepa.com/api-docs/product-object.html; Product.java, read 2026-10-14)
  - `listedSince`: "the time the item was first listed on Amazon". The web page says it "is only available for some products. If not available, the field has the value 0 or -1"; the Java client says the value is 0 and that it "is updated in conjunction with the offers request" (Product.java only; the web page does not say this).
  - `trackingSince`: when Keepa started tracking the product.
  - `parentAsin`, and `parentAsinHistory` ("[Keepa time in minutes, previous parent ASIN, ...]"; the timestamp marks when the previous parent "ceased to be valid"; "null" (Java) / "undefined" (web) "if the parentAsin field never changed"). The web page adds: "We started tracking parentAsin changes on March 18th 2024." A family change before that date is not in the history.
  - `variations`: parents only. The Java client says "Contains the dimension attributes for up to 50 variations of this product"; the web page says "Contains up to 4000 variations of this product". The two disagree. Amazon's own display limit is a separate figure: "variation families with more than 4,000 child ASINs cannot be displayed on the detail page" (Variation relationship FAQ, G201951410 v16, capture). Whether Keepa's array is cut at 50 is settled by counting one (open question 6).
  - `csv[16]` (`RATING`) and `csv[17]` (`COUNT_REVIEWS`): the web page calls slot 17 "The product’s rating count history"; the Java client calls the same slot "The product's review count history". The enum name says reviews, the web page says ratings. [official — Keepa] (Product Object; Product.java)
  - `reviews`: "variation specific review and rating counts histories". Keepa adds, on both the web page and Product.java: "The ratingCount history has not been updated since April 9th 2025 as that data point was removed by Amazon." Keepa states that freeze for the variation-specific `reviews.ratingCount` only. Whether `csv[17]` kept updating after that date, and whether it now counts ratings or written reviews, Keepa does not say (open question 10).
  - `monthlySold`: "not an estimate", "variation specific", "at least".
  - `frequentlyBoughtTogether`.
- **Token costs and admitted parameters live in the keepa skill's `references/raw-routes.md`, not here.** Its route table names `category` and optional `range` for `/bestsellers`. It does not list Keepa's `month`, `year`, `variations` or `sublist`. [seat]

## What practitioners add

- **Top Search Terms as a competitor map.** Bradley Sutton (Helium 10, Serious Sellers Podcast #523, "Using Amazon Brand Analytics Data Like You Never Have Before!", 2024-01-02) uses it in three ways:
  - Find the terms where a competitor is "one of the top three clicked products in all of Amazon for that keyword".
  - Read low top-three click share as a term that is "wide open".
  - Read conversion share above click share as converting better than the average result.
  [opinion] (https://www.helium10.com/podcast/using-amazon-brand-analytics-data-like-you-never-have-before/)
- **Tool vendors treat the competitor set as moving, not frozen.** Helium 10 Team ("How Sellers Can Scale, Grow, and Win With a Vital New Tool: Market Tracker 360", 2022-10-06): a market is seeded with "up to 15 ASINS or keywords". In dynamic mode it shows "Which competitors are entering or leaving your market". [opinion] (https://www.helium10.com/blog/market-tracker-360/)
- Jungle Scout's Competitive Intelligence page (undated, footer © 2026) offers "up to 200 ASINs", market share, keyword "share of voice" and keyword position tracking. It says nothing about new-entrant detection or BSR. [opinion] (https://www.junglescout.com/features/amazon-competitive-intelligence-tool/)
- **Who counts as a competitor.** SellerSprite Success Team ("How to Choose the Right Competitors for Reverse ASIN Lookup", 2026-03-25) recommends 3–10 ASINs across "peers, aspirational brands, and lower-authority sellers". [opinion] (https://www.sellersprite.com/en/blog/choose-right-competitors-reverse-asin)
  - Include: "Only include ASINs with matching core functionality"; "Stay within ±20% of your price unless testing market tiers"; match specs, audience and fulfilment.
  - Exclude: "Category giants / household brands (they rank for everything)", bundles, "Sponsored-heavy listings (ads may mask true organic intent)", mismatched variations, and "Listings with temporary advantages (deep coupons, outlier promos)".
  - For validating a new product idea, include "new entrants".
  - This is the only practitioner source read that defines a competitor by fit, not by rank. Helium 10 and Jungle Scout seed sets from keywords or ASINs and do not state an exclusion rule.
- **How often the top changes hands.** Ben Donovan (Marketplace Pulse, "China Won Amazon's Top 10,000, America Kept Its Top 100", 2026-07-09) reports on the top 10,000 sellers: "68.6% of today's top sellers held the position a year ago", and "6.3% arrived within the last eighteen months". He names "AI tooling that erased the listing-quality gap once protecting domestic sellers" as a driver. These are seller-level figures, not ASIN-level. [opinion] (https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/china-won-amazons-top-10000-america-kept-its-top-100)
- **Merged reviews as a takeover tactic.** [opinion]
  - Hannah Walsh (Which?, "Exposed: the tricks sellers use to post fake reviews on Amazon", 2019-07-05) documented listings merged to move reviews, including "a smartwatch with 938 reviews dating back to 2011, despite being first listed for sale in January 2019". She also found "dozens, or in some cases hundreds, of spurious product variations". (https://www.which.co.uk/news/2019/07/exposed-the-tricks-sellers-use-to-post-fake-reviews-on-amazon/)
  - My Amazon Guy ("Merged Vine Reviews Targeted as Amazon Tightens Rules"; no byline and no publication date shown, metadata `date_modified` 2025-07-03), relaying Or Shamosh's LinkedIn post: merging Vine reviews into another ASIN "is now being flagged as review manipulation". Reported consequences are suppression, unmerging, and reviews "wiped out without prior notice". (https://myamazonguy.com/news/merged-vine-reviews/)
- **Practitioners disagree on a "honeymoon period" for new listings.** Code3 ("Honeymoon Period Decoded: The Truth Behind Amazon's Cold Start Algorithm", no author or date shown, © 2024) calls the belief that new products automatically get more visibility for 30 days "only partially true". It describes priors from new-item-setup attributes and "posterior estimation values" from five days after a product becomes purchasable. It cites "the Cold Start Patents" without numbers. [opinion] (https://code3.com/resources/honeymoon-period-decoded-the-truth-behind-amazons-cold-start-algorithm/)
  - Other vendors' honeymoon pages surfaced in search (SellerSprite, My Amazon Guy) and were not opened.
  - Amazon has published research on cold start (next section) but no seller-facing statement.
- **POE launch metrics, second-hand.** Becky Trowbridge (eComEngine, "Understand Demand with Amazon Product Opportunity Explorer", 2021-11-11, updated 2024-06-11) lists Insights-tab metrics: "Number of new products launched (past 180 days)", "Number of successful launches (past 180 days)", and "Click share for the top five products (past 360 days)". ## Published research

- **Amazon search deliberately gives new products exposure.** Han, Castells, Gupta, Xu, Salaka, "Addressing cold start in product search via empirical bayes", CIKM 2022 (Amazon Science). Topical relevance and behavioural popularity bias ranking against new products. The paper uses non-behavioural signals as priors for engagement. An online A/B test on 50 million queries showed new-product impressions up 13.53% and new-product purchases up 11.14%, with overall purchases up 0.08%. [research] (https://www.amazon.science/publications/addressing-cold-start-in-product-search-via-empirical-bayes)
  - Transfer: high. It is Amazon's own search. It shows a mechanism that can put a new entrant on a core term before it has sales history. The paper does not say this exact model runs today.
- **New items borrow the signals of their substitutes.** Dong, Momma, Wang, Gao, Sun, "Behavioral feature boosting via substitute relationships for e-commerce search", SIGIR 2026 (Amazon Science; arXiv 2602.14502, 2026-02-16). The method pools clicks, add-to-carts, purchases and ratings of substitutes to give new items a "warm start". The paper says the model "has been launched in production and has served customers since 2025". [research] (https://www.amazon.science/publications/behavioral-feature-boosting-via-substitute-relationships-for-e-commerce-search; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14502)
  - Transfer: high for the claim that a substitute-like entrant can rank early. The paper gives no seller-visible signal for it.
- **What a fake-review launch looks like, and that it fades.** He, Hollenbeck, Proserpio, "Exploiting Social Media for Fake Reviews: Evidence from Amazon and Facebook", ACM SIGecom Exchanges 19(2), November 2021, an overview of "The Market for Fake Reviews" (the overview cites its EC 2021 version; the journal version is Marketing Science 41(5), 896–921, 2022, per the SSRN listing as shown in a search result — the SSRN page itself returned 403). The full journal article was not opened. [research] (https://www.sigecom.org/exchanges/volume_19/2/HE.pdf)
  - Sample: "approximately 1,500 products observed soliciting fake reviews".
  - While buying, weekly reviews were "nearly doubling", sales rank showed "a large and immediate decrease", and search position improved and "remains virtually constant for several weeks". That the rank series came from Keepa is the seat's inference from figure file names embedded in the PDF (`keepa-sales-rank-…`); the text does not say so. [seat]
  - After buying stopped, ratings fell "from 4.4 stars to 4.1 stars eight weeks later", one-star share rose "particularly for young products", and sales declined.
  - Transfer: direct, since it is Amazon data. A young listing with a review spike and a rank climb is consistent with manipulation, and the effect tends to reverse within weeks. The study does not let anyone identify a given listing as fake.
- **Rank is a log-scale proxy for quantity.** Austan Goolsbee and Judith Chevalier (the working paper's author order), "Measuring Prices and Price Competition Online: Amazon and Barnes and Noble", NBER WP 9085 (July 2002); the NBER page gives the published version as Quantitative Marketing and Economics 1(2), June 2003. They translate sales rank into quantity under a Pareto assumption, `ln(Rank − 1) = c − θ ln(Sales)`, for about 20,000 books. The paper reports Amazon's 2001 statement that books in the top 10,000 were ranked on the last 24 hours and updated hourly, those ranked 10,001–100,000 daily, and those beyond monthly. [research] (https://www.nber.org/papers/w9085)
  - Transfer: the log relation is a modelling convention that has been used widely since. The 2001 cadence is history about books and is not today's rule.
- **Rankings move clicks more than purchases.** Ursu, "The Power of Rankings", Marketing Science 37(4), 2018. The abstract says the study uses "the first data set with experimental variation in the ranking from a field experiment at Expedia"; it does not say how the variation was assigned, so this file does not call it randomised. Finding: rankings "affect what consumers search, but conditional on search, do not affect purchases". [research] (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v37y2018i4p530-552.html)
  - Transfer: moderate. It is not Amazon. It supports reading a new entrant ranked above you on a term first as a click-share loss.
- **Amazon itself enters successful product spaces.** Zhu and Liu, "Competing with complementors: An empirical look at Amazon.com", Strategic Management Journal 39(10), 2018: "Amazon is more likely to target successful product spaces", and entry "discourages affected third‐party sellers". [research] (https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/06b55865-1bab-4ccb-95c6-a17b6ff9100a)
  - Transfer: the sample period predates today's catalogue. It supports counting Amazon's own offer as a possible entrant.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Best Sellers Rank is computed from Amazon sales, recent and historical, within a category. It does not count page views or reviews, and a product can hold a different rank in each category it sits in. (Amazon Best Sellers Rank – Customer Service; Guide to Amazon sales rank)
- A top sales rank is not a top search position. A competitor on the Best Sellers list may not be a competitor on the listing's terms. (Guide to Amazon sales rank)
- Movers & Shakers measures rank gain against 24 hours earlier, so it flags change, not size. (Amazon.com Movers & Shakers)
- Amazon's Sales Rank FAQ states the best-seller list refresh interval and an order-to-rank lag; they are figures Amazon can change, so a skill cites the page rather than the numbers. Whether a child ASIN carries its own rank or its parent's depends on the store and the parent's website display group. Reviews do not enter sales rank. (Sales Rank FAQ, G202059240)
- The Search Terms report names, per term and department, the most-clicked ASINs with their click share and conversion share. It is the only SP-API report that names other sellers' ASINs on a term; the Search Query Performance dashboard's Query Detail view also names leading ASINs, but only in Seller Central. It needs Brand Registry, a brand representative and the Brand Analytics role. A request covers exactly one period. (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json; Analytics Reports)
- Search Query Performance gives each query's market totals beside the ASIN's own share. A share drop while the totals hold means someone else took share; a drop in the totals means demand moved. Amazon's dashboard page says the data covers organic and Sponsored Products results, so the share taken may be paid. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP)
- Keyword `searchCatalogItems` is a catalogue lookup ordered for sellers, not the buyer's results page. It is a source of candidates, and of brand and classification counts, not of who ranks on a term. (Issue #1425; Issue #2998; Catalog Items reference)
- Catalog `summaries.releaseDate` is a ship date, not a listing date. (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json)
- Product Opportunity Explorer's niche, search-volume and launch metrics have no SP-API route. The Customer Feedback API carries review and return topics only. (Discussion #3373; Customer Feedback API)
- Grouping products into a variation to pool reviews violates Amazon's review policy. Since the 2026 review-sharing change, functionally different variations no longer share reviews. (Communication and Customer Product Review policies and violations; Changes to review sharing across product variations starting Feb 12)
- No SP-API notification announces a change to a variation family. A takeover is found by reading the family. Amazon's route for a competitor that added a product to the seller's family is Selling Partner Support. (Notification Type Values; Variation relationship FAQ, G201951410)
- Amazon changes the list of valid variation themes by announcement; the live theme values for a product type are read from the schema, not from a dated announcement. (Removal of irrelevant variation themes will start September 2; Update to irrelevant or redundant variation theme removal)
- Keepa's Best Sellers list is built from Keepa's sales-rank data. It drops products without an accessible rank and by default returns one variation per parent. (Best Sellers — Keepa API Documentation)
- Keepa's Product Finder searches Keepa's database, not Amazon's. Its `listedSince` is Keepa's best determination and is 0 (or, per the web page, -1) when unknown. (Product Finder; Product Object — Keepa API Documentation)
- Keepa's product search web page claims Amazon's order without sponsored results. That is Keepa's claim, not Amazon's, and Keepa's own documents disagree on how many results a term returns. A position in it is never reported as the listing's search rank. (Product Search — Keepa API Documentation; Request.java)
- Keepa's `parentAsinHistory` records each earlier parent of a child ASIN, and is empty (null/undefined) if the parent never changed. Keepa began tracking parent changes on a fixed date (stated on its page), so an earlier family change is invisible to it. (Product Object — Keepa API Documentation; Product.java)

## Positions

1. **P-competitor-discovery-1.** A competitor is an ASIN that shares the listing's core terms and its function and price band; a Best Sellers neighbour that does not share the terms is not in the control set. (Guide to Amazon sales rank — "A top BSR doesn't always equal a high organic search ranking"; How to Choose the Right Competitors for Reverse ASIN Lookup)
2. **P-competitor-discovery-2.** Where the seller is a registered brand representative, the Search Terms report's most-clicked ASINs on the core terms are the first source for who is on those terms, with click share and conversion share as the measure. (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json; Analytics Reports; Serious Sellers Podcast #523)
3. **P-competitor-discovery-3.** A drop in the listing's share in Search Query Performance while the query totals hold is read as share taken by others; a drop in the totals is read as demand. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json)
4. **P-competitor-discovery-4.** Keyword `searchCatalogItems` results are candidates to check, never a ranking of who wins a term. (searchCatalogItems sorting? #1425; issue #2998)
5. **P-competitor-discovery-5.** Who appears on a term, and in what order, may be read from Keepa's product search only as Keepa's claim of Amazon's non-sponsored order, said as such; the number of results is whatever the call returns, and a position in it is never reported as the listing's search rank. (Product Search — Keepa API Documentation; Request.java; P-search-rank-and-volume-2)
6. **P-competitor-discovery-6.** A listing's age is Keepa's `listedSince`, read as "first listed as far as Keepa can tell". Its absence (0 or -1) is unknown, not new, and Catalog `releaseDate` is never used as a listing date. (Product Object — Keepa API Documentation; Product.java; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json)
7. **P-competitor-discovery-7.** A new entrant matters to a listing when it shares the listing's core terms and shows on them: in the most-clicked ASINs, in Keepa's claimed order for the term, or as a share loss in Search Query Performance (which may be organic or Sponsored Products). A climb on the Best Sellers list alone does not count. (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json; Product Search — Keepa; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard; Guide to Amazon sales rank)
8. **P-competitor-discovery-8.** Amazon's search gives new products deliberate exposure, so a new entrant can appear on a core term before it has a sales history. Its first weeks are watched, not extrapolated. (Addressing cold start in product search via empirical bayes; Behavioral feature boosting via substitute relationships)
9. **P-competitor-discovery-9.** A young listing whose review count jumps while its rank climbs is flagged as possibly bought and re-checked weeks later, never treated as a settled threat on first sight. A Keepa count series is used for this only after its last timestamp shows it is still updating. (Exploiting Social Media for Fake Reviews; Communication and Customer Product Review policies and violations; Product Object — Keepa API Documentation)
10. **P-competitor-discovery-10.** Variation takeover is checked by reading the family: Catalog `relationships` on the seller's parent and children, and Keepa `parentAsinHistory` (which reaches back only to Keepa's stated tracking start). Amazon sends no notification for a family change. A competitor's product added to the seller's family is reported with Amazon's named route, Selling Partner Support. (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Product Object — Keepa API Documentation; Notification Type Values; Variation relationship FAQ)
11. **P-competitor-discovery-11.** An ASIN in a family that is functionally different from its siblings is named as a possible policy breach, and as a listing whose shared reviews may stop showing since the 2026 change. (Communication and Customer Product Review policies and violations; Changes to review sharing across product variations starting Feb 12; How to create Amazon product variations)
12. **P-competitor-discovery-12.** Rank movement is read as a ratio or in logs, never as unit sales. The 24-hour Movers & Shakers gain flags change, not size. (Measuring Prices and Price Competition Online; Amazon.com Movers & Shakers; keepa skill product-file.md)
13. **P-competitor-discovery-13.** The competitor set is written down before a change and frozen for its observation window. An entrant that arrives on the core terms during the window is recorded as a confounder, not added silently to the control. (Market Tracker 360 — sets that move; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json)
14. **P-competitor-discovery-14.** Product Opportunity Explorer niche and launch metrics, the Top Search Terms dashboard's brand and category columns, and the Search Query Performance Query Detail view's leading ASINs, reach cx only as files the seller puts in the tree. (Discussion #3373; Customer Feedback API; Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business; sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard)
15. **P-competitor-discovery-15.** Amazon's own offer appearing on a term's leading products is treated as a new entrant like any other. (Competing with complementors; product-file.md `availabilityAmazon`)

**The seat's own (not for a skill)**
- The Search Terms file should be filtered by term while streaming rather than loaded whole. This is inferred from "approximately 12 million items" and has not been measured.
- Comparing a listing's `listedSince` with the first point of its `csv[17]` count history may expose merged reviews: ratings older than the listing. This is suggested by Which?'s 938-reviews-before-listing case and has not been tested on Keepa data. The first point does not depend on whether the series stopped in 2025 (open question 10), but Keepa's own documents differ on whether the slot counts ratings or reviews.
- A mismatch between the Listings and Catalog relationships may flag an outside change to a family (open question 5).
- `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` and `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` would make watching push-based, but the box has no SQS or EventBridge destination. For now that belongs to the heartbeat, not to notifications.
- Ads `/sp/targets/products/recommendations` is the only Amazon list of related ASINs built from co-view and co-purchase signals. It becomes a discovery source once the Ads source exists.
