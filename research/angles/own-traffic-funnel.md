# The listing's own traffic funnel (sessions, page views, Buy Box %, unit session %, orders)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage:** all three. This angle does not serve one stage. It measures the funnel the other angles act on. It covers the seller's own detail-page traffic (sessions, page views), whether the seller's offer was the one shown (Featured Offer / "Buy Box" percentage), and the step from visit to purchase (unit session percentage, units, ordered product sales). It also covers where the stage *before* the detail page lives, which is search impressions and clicks. Amazon keeps those in different files from sessions.

**Skills it feeds:**
- `listing-review`: locating the stage that dropped. This needs the right figure from the right file, read the right way.
- `effect-review`: the metric of the targeted stage plus orders, read over a baseline window and an observation window. This needs per-ASIN, per-day series and knowledge of when a day's figure is final.
- The report script: create, poll and download `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT`, or the Data Kiosk equivalent.

**Why a senior operator cares.** Almost every listing judgement goes through these figures, and each has a trap. Sessions are de-duplicated over 24 hours and page views are not. "Conversion" means at least three different ratios across Amazon's files. The Business Reports conversion counts units, not orders. Featured Offer % is a share of page views, so a lost offer shows up as falling conversion. Search impressions are not in the sales-and-traffic report at all. Amazon's own files disagree with each other on denominators and on how they snap weeks. On day 1 of the beta run, cx stated a conversion figure as sessions divided by orders, which is the division upside down (`office-mailbox/from-engineering/20261013-0904-beta-browser-listing-run-day-1.md`, finding 7). That is the failure this angle exists to prevent.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. The SP-API sales-and-traffic report (`GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT`)

RULES (enforced by the API):
- The report sits under the page's heading "Seller retail analytics reports", with the display name "Sales and Traffic Business Report". It is listed with "Availability: Sellers" and the **Brand Analytics** role, "can be requested or scheduled", and returns JSON. No Brand Registry condition is stated for this report, while the Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance sections on the same page state one. [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics — developer-docs.amazon.com answers 301 to this host — read 2026-10-14)
- `reportOptions`: `dateGranularity` is `DAY`, `WEEK` or `MONTH` (default `DAY`). `asinGranularity` is `PARENT`, `CHILD` or `SKU` (default `PARENT`). [official] (Analytics Reports; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
- **There are two sections and no ASIN-by-day table.** `salesAndTrafficByDate` is "aggregated by date (across the seller's entire catalog of items)". `salesAndTrafficByAsin` is "aggregated by ASIN" over the requested range. `dateGranularity` shapes only the by-date section and `asinGranularity` only the by-ASIN section. The schema says so field by field: "the salesAndTrafficByDate section of this report will default to DAY granularity" and "the salesAndTrafficByAsin section of this report will default to PARENT granularity". [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- **Dates.** On `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime`, "the time component is ignored". A start date "more than two years ago" means "the report will be cancelled". For `WEEK` and `MONTH`, dates are **expanded outward** to Sunday–Saturday or to the whole calendar month (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json). If the expanded end date is in the future, "the report will contain partial data for the most recent report period" (Analytics Reports, which words the two-year rule as "must not be more than two years before the date of the request… the request will be cancelled"). [official]
- One marketplace per report: "Specifying multiple marketplaces will result in failure to generate the report." [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- Request rate: "You can request this report up to three times every five minutes". On HTTP 429, "Wait one minute before retrying your request", then double the wait. The page also advises caching, 7–30-day ranges and off-peak scheduling. [official] (Analytics Reports). These figures are door.md's to carry, not this package's.

Field DEFINITIONS (Amazon's words, from the schema; the field names are stable, the definitions are text Amazon can revise):
- **Sessions**: "visits to your Amazon.com pages using browser or mobile app by any user. All activity within a 24-hour period is considered a session… if any user visits your pages multiple times… within a 24 hour period it is counted as a single session." The report splits sessions into `browserSessions` and `mobileAppSessions`. [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- **Page views**: "the number of times any user visited your Amazon.com pages using browser or mobile app". They are not de-duplicated, and are split into `browserPageViews` and `mobileAppPageViews`. [official] (same)
- **Buy Box percentage** (`buyBoxPercentage`): "The percentage of page views where the buy box (the add to shopping cart link) appeared on the page for customers to add your product to their cart." The denominator is **page views**. [official] (same)
- **Unit session percentage**: "how many units were purchased relative to the number of people who viewed the products, calculated by dividing unitsOrdered by sessions" (by-ASIN wording). The numerator is **units**, not orders. [official] (same)
- **Order item session percentage** (`orderItemSessionPercentage`) exists **only in the by-date section**. It is "how many order items were generated relative to the number of people who viewed the products". [official] (same)
- **Session percentage / page views percentage** (by ASIN only): an ASIN's share of the account's sessions or page views, for example "relative to the total number of sessions for all products". [official] (same)
- **Units ordered, total order items, ordered product sales.** The schema separates orders, order items and units: "An order containing 2 copies of book A and 3 copies of book B; The number of orders is 1, the number of order items is 2…". `orderedProductSales` is "calculated by multiplying the price of products and the number of units sold". [official] (same)
- **B2B.** Each B2B field "is only populated when the seller is a B2B seller". The B2C/B2B label convention is stated on Amazon's blog: labels without "B2B" "include both B2B and B2C data". [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; Get actionable B2B insights with Amazon business reports, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-business-reports, Mickey Toogood, 2025-08-05, read 2026-10-14)
- **Fields only in the by-date (whole-catalogue) section:** `unitsRefunded`, `refundRate`, `claimsGranted`, `claimsAmount`, `shippedProductSales`, `unitsShipped`, `ordersShipped`, `averageOfferCount`, `averageParentItems`, `feedbackReceived`, `negativeFeedbackReceived`, `receivedNegativeFeedbackRate`, `averageSellingPrice`, `averageSalesPerOrderItem`, `averageUnitsPerOrderItem` and `orderItemSessionPercentage`. The by-ASIN sales block has only units, order items and ordered product sales, each with its B2B variant. [official] (same)
- **Two wording slips in Amazon's schema**, recorded so nobody builds on them. `refundRate` is described as "calculated by dividing unitsOrdered by unitsRefunded" (read literally, that is the inverse of a rate). `browserPageViewsPercentage` is described as relative to "the total number of mobile page views"; the `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24` version of the same field says "relative to the total number of browser page views", and the same dataset defines `refundRate` as "dividing unitsRefunded by unitsOrdered". [official] text; [seat] that these are slips.

### B. Data Kiosk: the per-ASIN, per-day series

RULES:
- Every Data Kiosk operation lists the **Brand Analytics** role. "Each field available with Data Kiosk requires a certain role", and roles are checked when the query is created ("upon query creation"). "Data Kiosk only allows one request per query / selling partner / application combination." [official] (Data Kiosk API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/data-kiosk-api, read 2026-10-14)
- **Amazon's pages disagree on the role, dataset by dataset (reconciled 2026-10-14).** Role Mappings for SP-API Operations lists Brand Analytics as the only role for `createQuery` ("Required roles (need at least one)"), as the Data Kiosk API page does. The changelog note "Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements" says: "The Vendor Analytics and Sales and Traffic datasets require the Brand Analytics role. The Economics dataset requires the Selling Partner Insights role." Neither page names the seller analytics dataset. The Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide says access "is governed by the role assigned to your application" and names no role, and its schema names PAAM rights, not roles (below). So for `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24` Amazon's pages agree on Brand Analytics. For `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31` no Amazon page read names a role, and the binding answer is the authorization error (or its absence) on the first `createQuery` with the seller's credential. [official] (Role Mappings for SP-API Operations, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/role-mappings; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/changelog/correction-data-kiosk-role-requirements; Data Kiosk API; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; all read 2026-10-14)
- Every field in both analytics schemas carries `@resultRetention(duration : "P30D")` (as read on 2026-10-14; the directive in the schema in force is the binding value), and the schema says "The retention of a query's resulting documents always matches the retention of the query": a query and its document are kept 30 days, so a baseline cx wants to compare later must be saved by cx, not re-fetched from Data Kiosk's history of queries. [official] (analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql); [seat] the consequence.
- Amazon's own pages disagree on Data Kiosk against Reports. One says "Data Kiosk has all the functionality that the Reports API currently has and more" (Data Kiosk API). The other says "Currently, the available dataset is limited compared to the Reports API" (Reports API FAQ, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/reports-faq, read 2026-10-14). [official]
- **The lead dataset name `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2023_11_15` is gone.** The release note of August 27, 2025 says "The following dataset will be removed on February 26, 2026: `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2023_11_15`". The models repository's commit "Remove DK Analytics Sales and Traffic 2023" is dated 2026-07-31. The current sales-and-traffic dataset is **`analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24`**. [official] (SP-API Release Notes, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes; GitHub commit history of `schemas/data-kiosk`, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/commits/main/schemas/data-kiosk; both read 2026-10-14)
- **`analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24`** has three queries:
  - `salesAndTrafficByAsin` (aggregated by `PARENT`/`CHILD`/`SKU` over the range).
  - `salesAndTrafficByDate` (whole account, `DAY`/`WEEK`/`MONTH`).
  - Since the release note of August 27, 2025, **`salesAndTrafficTrends`**: "sales and traffic trend data for a specified list of ASINs within a date range", with `dateAggregation` `DAY`/`WEEK`/`MONTH`. Its `asinAggregation` is "currently only CHILD supported". Each filter is one marketplace with "up to 30 child ASINs", and an ASIN with no data in the period is simply absent.

  This is Amazon's own ASIN-by-day series. Its by-ASIN sales block carries `unitsRefunded`, `refundRate`, `unitsShipped` and `ordersShipped`, which the report's by-ASIN block does not. [official] (analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; SP-API Release Notes; read 2026-10-14)
- **Data Kiosk snaps weeks inward; the report expands them outward.** In Data Kiosk, a mid-week start moves to "the following Sunday" and a mid-week end to "the preceding Saturday" (likewise for months). The report expands to the enclosing week. The same dates asked of both can therefore cover different days. [official] (analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- **The two sources word the sessions definition differently.** Data Kiosk (both datasets): sessions are "calculated as the sum of browserSessions and mobileAppSessions". The report schema says activity by browser *or* mobile app within 24 hours "is counted as a single session". Whether a shopper who visits on both devices counts once or twice is not settled by the text (see Open questions). [official] (the two schemas)
- **The seller analytics dataset exists:** `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31`, one view `sellerAnalyticsView`. It was announced in the release of September 30, 2026: "Use the Seller Analytics dataset to access information including sales, traffic, promotions, …". The view is "available to seller accounts only" and its schema marks it `@supportedAccountTypes(types: [MERCHANT])`. [official] (SP-API Release Notes; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; all read 2026-10-14)
  - Access: "Access to the seller analytics dataset is governed by the role assigned to your application". There are two levels, "Sales and traffic access" (sales, traffic, promotion, account health, search, reviews, defects, returns) and "Sales and lending access". The guide names no SP-API role. "Requesting a field your application is not authorized for returns an authorization error rather than a null value." [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide). The schema marks fields with `@paamRights(require: [...])` naming the rights `analytics_sales_and_traffic_view_pa` and `analytics_sales_and_lending_view` ("Requesters must have at least one right from the list"); the `search`, `reviews`, `defects` and `returns` groups require `analytics_sales_and_traffic_view_pa`. How these rights map to an SP-API role is not stated on any page read. [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
  - Shape: "Every query must select at least one attribute in `groupByKey`" (guide). Monetary amounts default to USD when `currencyCode` is not given (schema). [official]
  - Latency: "All metrics in the seller analytics dataset are ready after 34 hours". "Data for a given day is generally available at 10 AM local time two days later." Dates not yet available return null. [official] (same)
  - Range: `startDate` "no more than two years ago". `aggregateBy` is `DAY`/`WEEK`/`MONTH`. The group-by attributes include `asin`, `parentAsin`, `msku`, `brand`, `brandCode`, `productTitle`, `productGroup`, `fulfilmentChannel` (the schema's spelling) and `marketplaceId`. **The guide and the schema spell one attribute differently:** the guide's access list says `fulfillmentChannel`, the schema's group-by key is `fulfilmentChannel` (both re-read 2026-10-14). A query is validated against the schema, so the name cx writes is read from the schema in force at run time. Week-over-week, month-over-month and year-over-year benchmarks exist only at `WEEK` and `MONTH`; the guide says "no benchmarking is available at `DAY` granularity". [official] (same; schema)
  - **Traffic group:** `sessions`, `browserSessions`, `mobileAppSessions`, `pageViews`, `browserPageViews`, `mobileAppPageViews`, `glanceViews` ("the number of times customers actively engage with your product detail page"), `featureOfferGlanceViews` (described as "the count of glance views where your product appeared as the Featured Offer on the search results page" — the "search results page" wording sits oddly beside a detail-page metric and is recorded as written), `featureOfferPct` and `conversionRatePct`. The guide still lists `conversationRatePct`, which the schema marks `@deprecated(reason: "No longer supported. Use conversionRatePct instead.")`. [official] (schema; guide)
  - **Different denominators.** In this dataset, `featureOfferPct` is "dividing the number of times your offer is the Featured Offer by the number of times customers viewed it (that is, Glance Views)". The report's `buyBoxPercentage` divides by page views, and so does the `buyBoxPercentage` of the Data Kiosk sales-and-traffic dataset ("The percentage of page views where the buy box (the add to shopping cart link) appeared", analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql, re-read 2026-10-14). There are therefore two Featured Offer shares on Amazon's APIs, one over page views (`buyBoxPercentage`, report and sales-and-traffic dataset) and one over glance views (`featureOfferPct`, seller analytics only). They are not one series. `conversionRatePct` is "the percentage of purchases to clicks for ASINs originated from the search results page". That is **a search conversion, not unit session percentage**. The deprecated `conversationRatePct` carries the same description word for word, so neither spelling is a sessions-based conversion. [official] (schema, re-read 2026-10-14)
  - **Search group:** `search.catalog` (impressions, clicks, cart adds, purchases with price, `clickRate` = "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a click", `conversionRate` = "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a purchase") and `search.query` (`queryKeyword` plus the same counts). Note that `conversionRate` here is purchases per **impression**. [official] (schema)
  - **Returns** lag: "Return data is computed with a delay of about 45 days". `returnedRate` "may appear low for recent dates". [official] (schema; guide)
  - **Reviews:** `totalReviewCount`, `lowRatingReviewCount`, `criticalReviewRate`, `displayRating`, `rawRating` and `totalRatingCount`. The schema has six fields. Rating fields "reflect the overall rating state as of the requested date". [official] (schema)
  - **Promotion:** `promotionGlanceViewCnt`, `promotionConversionRate` (units net of cancellations divided by detail page views for the promotion), `promotionType` (examples "Best Deal, Deal of the Day, Lightning Deal, Sales Discount"), and start and end dates. These are the confounder flags of a window. [official] (schema)

### C. Faster signals (hourly), and orders without traffic

- `DETAIL_PAGE_TRAFFIC_EVENT` "is sent five minutes after the beginning of each hour" and "shares traffic data at an ASIN level and includes data for the hour before the previous hour, as well as any delayed data from up to 24 hours earlier". "ASINs that have glance views are included." `startTime` and `endTime` are in UTC. `ITEM_SALES_EVENT_CHANGE` is also sent five minutes after each hour and "shares sales data at an ASIN level and includes data for the previous hour, as well as any delayed data from up to 24 hours earlier"; its schema carries `orderedUnits` ("can be negative if there are more cancellations than orders") and `orderedRevenue` ("Adjustments are made for cancellations"). The page lists the Amazon SQS workflow for both and does not list EventBridge. Notifications need a subscription and a destination. [official] (Notification Type Values, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, read in two passes, characters 0–200,000 of 214,068; DetailPageTrafficEventNotification.json and ItemSalesEventChangeNotification.json in the models repo, read 2026-10-14)
- Sales API `getOrderMetrics` gives units, order items, orders, average unit price and total sales per interval, for one `asin` or `sku`. It has no traffic. `granularityTimeZone` is "An IANA-compatible time zone for determining the day boundary" and must match the offset of `interval`. `granularityTimeZone` is "Required when specifying a granularity value greater than Hour". `Hour` granularity succeeds "only if the interval specified is less than or equal to 30 days from now"; other granularities work up to 2 years. If the interval does not align with the granularity, "the head and tail end of the response interval will contain partial data". [official] (sales.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/sales-api-model/sales.json, read 2026-10-14)

### D. The stage before the detail page: impressions and clicks (Brand Registry)

- Impressions are **not** in the sales-and-traffic report or its Data Kiosk twin. Amazon keeps them in Brand Analytics: Search Catalog Performance (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_CATALOG_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`) and Search Query Performance (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_QUERY_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`). Both were added to the Reports API in the February 26, 2025 release. Since June 25, 2025, SQP takes "a space-separated list of ASINs". The option is named `asin` in the SQP schema and `asins` in the SCP schema, and both schemas state "a 200-character limit". Both need the Brand Analytics role **and** registration in Brand Registry. Both are JSON, with `reportPeriod` `WEEK`/`MONTH`/`QUARTER`. "Requests cannot span multiple periods", and dates must fall on the first and last day of the period "or else a fatal error returns". The Sunday-to-Saturday week is in **both schemas**, not only on the page: "dataStartTime must be a Sunday for the WEEK reportPeriod" and "dataEndTime must be a Saturday for the WEEK reportPeriod" (re-read 2026-10-14). The Analytics Reports page says of SQP "This report can only be requested". [official] (Analytics Reports; SP-API Release Notes; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json in the models repo, read 2026-10-14)
- **History depth is stated per product, and the figures differ because the products differ** (reconciled 2026-10-14). Sales and Traffic: the report is cancelled for a start "more than two years before the date of the request" (Analytics Reports; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json), seller analytics takes a `startDate` "no more than two years ago" (guide), and Seller Central Business Reports data "is generally available for up to two years" (Business reports, G200633570 v8, capture capture `G200633570-v8`). Brand Analytics: "Data in Brand Analytics usually goes back three years" (Amazon's Brand Analytics blog), and the Repeat Purchase and Market Basket help pages each say "The report retains three years of data" (G4, G5). These do not contradict each other: two years binds the sales-and-traffic family, three years is Amazon's statement for Brand Analytics. The Analytics Reports page also lists under SQP "**Maximum reporting periods:** … **MONTH**: 17 months", introduced as "the maximum reporting periods that you can specify using the `dataStartTime` and `dataEndTime` parameters", while the same section says "Requests cannot span multiple periods". What the 17 months bounds is search-query-performance.md's question; this file does not rely on it. Every one of these spans is a figure Amazon keeps on a page or in a schema; the binding value for a request is the page or schema read at run time, and the report's own cancellation or fatal status is the evidence. [official] (sources named)
- Search Catalog Performance `impressionCount`: "Amazon counts an impression every time the ASIN displays in the search results page. Impressions include sponsored search results." `clickRate` is "The number of clicks divided by the number of impressions". `conversionRate` is "The number of purchases divided by the number of clicks". If `asins` is omitted, the report covers "all ASINs for which you are a brand owner". [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json). The SCP schema is not the only Amazon text on sponsored inclusion: both dashboard help pages say "organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page", widgets excluded (G8, with the SQP page's capture at capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`).
- **Search Query Performance's "click rate" is not a CTR.** `totalClickRate` is "the number of clicks divided by the search query volume". `asinImpressionShare`, `asinClickShare`, `asinCartAddShare` and `asinPurchaseShare` are the ASIN's fractions of the query's totals. A per-impression CTR for one ASIN on one query is `asinClickCount / asinImpressionCount`, which cx computes. [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json); [seat] the division.
- Amazon's framing of the funnel. "The Search Catalog Performance dashboard provides a comprehensive product view of key shopping metrics such as impressions, clicks, cart adds, and purchases… The end-to-end shopping behavior data helps you diagnose sales funnel issues." [official] (Seller Forums News_Amazon, "New Brand Analytics search dashboards are now available", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1afe683eb0acb90bb31abdc34950ef74, shown as "5 years ago", read 2026-10-14). ### E2. The Business Reports glossary and FAQ, read through the content call (gap fill 2026-10-14)

All items below are [official] (Business reports glossary, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G27691, version 7 / revision 1; Business Reports FAQ, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G28761, version 7 / revision 0; both read 2026-10-14 through the content call; captured as capture `G27691-v7` and capture `G28761-v7`).

- **Featured Offer percentage (the UI name for `buyBoxPercentage`).** It is "The percentage of page views where the Featured Offer (the "Add to shopping cart" link) appeared on the page for customers to add your product to their cart." Amazon lists three cases that hold it below 100%: "The product was out of stock", "The Featured Offer would result in the customer purchasing from another seller", and "Your product was not available for purchase using the Featured Offer". It also says: "This is not necessarily an indication of the availability of your product for purchase." The denominator is **page views**, as in the API schema.
- **Sessions - Total** are "Visits to your Amazon product detail pages by a customer within a 24-hour period". Also: "A storefront or browse node visit by a user is not counted as a session in Business Reports." This is Amazon's own text. It agrees with the Openbridge copy and settles the disagreement with MerchantSpring recorded under *What practitioners add*.
- **Page Views - Total**: "When a child ASIN is visited, it does not also count as a visit to the parent ASIN. Additionally, a storefront or browse node visit does not count as a page view."
- **A trap between two Business Reports views.** The text is printed under both Page Views - Total and Sessions - Total: "When viewing the "Detail Page Sales and Traffic" report, page views are summed only for days when there was sales activity on the ASIN. When viewing the "Detail Page Sales and Traffic by Child Item" report, page views are summed for the entire date range, regardless of sales activity." So the same ASIN and range can show fewer page views in one view than in the other.
- **Unit Session Percentage**: "Calculated by dividing the number of units by the number of sessions for a selected time period, and then expressed as a percentage."
- **Order Item Session Percentage**: "dividing the total order items by the number of distinct session counts for the selected time period."
- **Refund Rate**: "calculated by dividing the number of units refunded by the number of units sold". This is the right way round, so the glossary confirms that the report schema's inverted wording is a slip.
- **Week**: "Seven days, from Sunday 00:00:00 to the following Saturday 23:59:59 PT." **Same Week Last Year / Same Day Last Year** use ISO week numbers: "Week 1 starts with the first week of the year containing at least four days. Defined according to ISO standards."
- **Wording slip in the glossary.** "Sessions – Browser" is defined as visits "by a Amazon Business customer". That is the B2B wording on the non-B2B row. [official] text; [seat] that it is a slip.
- **Update cadence (FAQ).** "The "by date" reports are updated every hour with the data from the past few days". "All other reports are updated daily and consist of data from the previous day. For instance, Wednesday's data is shared on Thursday." "The data from the past week is shared by the end of each Sunday." "A week runs from Sunday through Saturday." Cancellations reach the "by date" reports hourly and all other reports "once a day".
- **Mobile traffic (FAQ).** "Since March 29, 2022, Page views and Sessions metrics include mobile app and browser traffic data". The list covers sessions, unit session percentage and Featured Offer (Buy Box) percentage. This is the Amazon date behind the ClearAds observation under *What practitioners add*. A series that crosses 2022-03-29 is not comparable.

## What practitioners add

- **Sales = sessions × conversion × price, and split before editing.** [opinion] Denis D, Seller Labs, "Sales Dropped? Split It Before You Raise Ad Spend", 2026-10-01 (https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/amazon-sales-drop-traffic-conversion-price/):
  - Decompose a period-over-period change into traffic, conversion and price effects.
  - Its diagnosis table row "Conversion, negative, Buy Box % down" reads "lost Featured Offer, stockout, or a hijacker"; check first "The Featured Offer (Buy Box) percentage column, inventory days of supply, the offer listing"; the lever is "Inventory, price, account health", not the listing.
  - Broad or auto ad traffic can lower conversion with no listing change: check the Search Term Report first; "Do not touch the listing until you have ruled it out."
  - "Always sum units and sum sessions, then divide." (its prompt text adds "Do not average the daily percentages.")
  - "Skip anything where the absolute conversion change is under one percentage point and sessions are in the low hundreds"; otherwise "Use a 60- or 90-day window, or skip the ASIN."
  - "Traffic data can run several days behind."
- **Sessions vs page views as a signal.** [opinion] Seller Labs knowledge base, "Business Reports Explained: What the Numbers Actually Mean", updated 2026-07-29: a page-view/session ratio near 1.0 means shoppers decide in one visit, and 1.5–2.0+ means repeat visits (price hesitation, missing information). It points to the By Child Item view because "one color or size could be dragging the whole listing down". "Unit Session Percentage measures units sold per session, not orders per session." It lists "Below 5%" as "Your listing likely needs work", while saying benchmarks vary widely by category. Amazon publishes no threshold, and the practitioners disagree (next items).
- **Benchmarks disagree and none is sourced to data.** [opinion] Brian Connolly, Jungle Scout, "How to Increase Your Amazon Conversion Rate in 2024", 2024-01-15: about 10% average, "based on our experience selling and speaking with other sellers". The article suggests comparing sessions and Unit Session Percentage before a change and "a couple of weeks after". [opinion] Emplicit (no author named), "The Hidden Amazon Report That Tells You Exactly Why Customers Aren't Buying", 2025-12-30, about the Detail Page Sales and Traffic report: with high traffic and low conversion "it could mean your images, pricing, descriptions, or reviews need attention"; "if traffic is low but conversions are strong, your product content is doing its job." Seller Labs says below 5% needs work. Amazon gives no benchmark on any page read. A skill must not carry a benchmark.
- **The parent level de-duplicates and totals do not add up.** [opinion] MerchantSpring help, "Why don't the Traffic and Conversion Summary match the by Child or by Parent reports?" (undated, © 2026): "Unlike sales and units ordered, Page Views and Sessions are not additive across products". A shopper who views several children in one visit is counted "only once at the parent level". The expected ordering is Summary ≥ by Child ≥ by Parent, and the Summary "includes visits to pages that are not associated with a single ASIN" (its examples: search results, browse pages, Brand Storefronts). **Disagreement:** a seller in the Seller Forums reported a child report with 40% fewer sessions than the parent (thread at https://sellercentral.amazon.com/forums/t/business-report-discrepencies/388339, seen only as a search result, not opened). Openbridge's glossary, which it says was "supplied by Amazon" ("Amazon Sales & Traffic Reports Glossary", Openbridge Support, 2022-09-20), says "A storefront or browse node visit by a user is not counted as a session in Business Reports". That disagrees with MerchantSpring's account of what the Summary includes.
- **Sessions include ad traffic; clicks and sessions do not tie out.** [opinion] Two sellers in the Seller Forums thread "Does "Sessions - Total" include sessions generated from PPC campaigns?" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a83adc25-b2a3-4a4b-bf9c-52275d2a29bb, "2 years ago") say yes. Neither is marked Amazon staff. [opinion] Seller Labs, "Sessions vs Impressions vs Clicks Explained", updated 2026-05-13: do not compare "PPC Clicks to Business Report Sessions 1:1". [opinion] Daniks, "Amazon Business Reports: Read Them Right (2026 Guide)", 2026-09-23:
  - Business Reports "describe all traffic to your detail pages, regardless of how the shopper arrived".
  - They "use the marketplace's local time zone; your ad console may be set to a different one".
  - "Do not react to yesterday's numbers, they are incomplete."
  - Data settles "within about 48 hours".
  - Session Percentage is "Almost never useful".

## Published research

- [research] Pavel Dmitriev, Somit Gupta, Dong Woo Kim, Garnet Vaz, "A Dirty Dozen: Twelve Common Metric Interpretation Pitfalls in Online Controlled Experiments", KDD 2017 (Microsoft). Narrow findings:
  - (§5.2, "Misinterpretation of Ratio Metrics") "ratio metrics can be misleading or potentially invalid if the denominator of the metric changed", so "we recommend to always define count metrics for the numerator and the denominator, and provide those in the result alongside the ratio metric". The same section **prefers the average of per-user ratios over the ratio of totals** for experiments, because the user is the randomization unit; it says nothing about averaging daily percentages, so it does not back "sum then divide" across days.
  - (§5.11, "Incomplete Funnel Metrics") "For funnel based scenarios it is crucial to measure all parts of the process", comparing success rates at each step, conditional and unconditional. In their Xbox case, image and messaging changes raised clicks to a sale with no corresponding revenue increase.
  - (§5.10, "Novelty and Primacy Effects") a treatment effect can change over time and flatten in the long run (their example ran four weeks).
  - (§5.8, "Segment Interpretation") recursively segmenting until something is significant invites Simpson's paradox and is a multiple-testing problem with inflated Type-I error.

  Transfer: direct for unit session percentage and Featured Offer %, which are ratios whose denominators (sessions, page views) a listing change can move. Partial for the rest, because these are randomized experiments and a listing change is a before/after observation.
- [research] Raluca M. Ursu, "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions", Marketing Science 37(4):530–552, 2018, DOI 10.1287/mksc.2017.1072 (volume and pages per IDEAS/RePEc and the Crossref record; the publisher's abstract page answered 403). **Design (reconciled 2026-10-14):** the publisher's abstract, read through the Crossref record, says the paper uses "the first data set with experimental variation in the ranking from a field experiment at Expedia". So the rankings were experimentally varied; the abstract does not use the word "randomized", and the INFORMS release says only "a field experiment at Expedia". Files that say the experiment randomised rankings describe the design correctly in substance, but neither text read uses that word. Narrow finding, per the abstract: rankings "affect what consumers search, but conditional on search, do not affect purchases"; the INFORMS release of 2018-07-11 words it as a "causal effect on what consumers search" that does "not necessarily affect purchase decisions directly". Transfer: a better search position should show up in impressions, clicks and sessions first, and not by itself in unit session percentage. This is a hotel marketplace, not Amazon, so it is suggestive only.
- [research] Kay H. Brodersen, Fabian Gallusser, Jim Koehler, Nicolas Remy, Steven L. Scott, "Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models", Annals of Applied Statistics 9(1):247–274, 2015. It predicts "the counterfactual market response in a synthetic control that would have occurred had no intervention taken place" from control series. Transfer: the formal version of "subtract the competitor set's same-period movement". It needs control series the change did not touch. Competitors' traffic is not available from SP-API (see "What cx must read"), so only proxies such as rank and search shares can serve.
- [research] Alex Deng, Ulf Knoblich, Jiannan Lu, "Applying the Delta method in metric analytics: A practical guide with novel ideas", arXiv 1803.06336 (preprint, v4 2018-09-12; no venue on the arXiv page). In big-data settings many online metrics "approximately follow normal distributions", which may allow closed-form Delta-method inference, and properties such as data-generating processes and aggregation levels "pose numerous challenges for constructing trustworthy estimation and inference procedures". Transfer: a daily unit session percentage is a ratio whose noise depends on the session count. The seat did not verify a seller-level formula for it.
- [research] Judith Chevalier and Austan Goolsbee (the NBER page lists them as Austan Goolsbee and Judith Chevalier), "Measuring Prices and Price Competition Online: Amazon and Barnes and Noble", NBER WP 9085, July 2002; published as "Measuring prices and price competition online: Amazon vs. Barnes and Noble", Quantitative Marketing and Economics 1(2), June 2003. It uses "sales ranks of about 20,000 books" to "derive quantity proxies". Transfer: the basis for reading Keepa's sales rank as a demand proxy for competitors whose sessions are invisible. The rank-to-units mapping is category- and period-specific and was not read in the full text.
- [research] Daria Sorokina and Erick Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The joy of ranking products", SIGIR 2016 (talk abstract on amazon.science). "Amazon Search powers the majority of Amazon's sales." Transfer: this is why the impression and click stage (Brand Analytics) matters as much as the detail page. The abstract read says nothing about which behavioural signals feed ranking, so no claim about conversion feeding rank rests on it.
- [research] Peter S. Fader, Bruce G. S. Hardie, Ka Lok Lee, "RFM and CLV: Using Iso-Value Curves for Customer Base Analysis", Journal of Marketing Research 42(4):415–430, November 2005 (DOI 10.1509/jmkr.2005.42.4.415; read as the Crossref record and its abstract, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1509/jmkr.2005.42.4.415, 2026-10-14; the authors' site answered 404).
  - The narrow finding, per the abstract: the paper "links the well-known RFM (recency, frequency, and monetary value) paradigm with customer lifetime value (CLV)". It uses "iso-value" curves that group "individual customers who have different purchasing histories but similar future valuations". The model is "based on the Pareto/NBD framework" with "a gamma-gamma submodel for spend per transaction". It is estimated on a cohort of the online music site CDNOW.
  - Transfer: the academic grounding for the RFM segmentation that Customer Loyalty Analytics uses (G3). It concerns valuing customers. It says nothing about listing content, so it supports reading loyalty segments as context for a listing review, not as a measure of a listing change.
- No published research was found, in this session, that tests whether matching a listing's images or copy to buyer demographics raises conversion on Amazon. The web search budget was exhausted before that search could run; see Open questions.

## Stable enough for a skill

- In Amazon's sales-and-traffic data, a session de-duplicates a user's repeat visits within the period the field's definition states, and a page view counts every visit, so page views are at least as many as sessions. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; the period is read from the definition in force)
- Unit session percentage is units ordered divided by sessions. Its numerator is units, not orders, and multi-unit orders raise it. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- Buy Box percentage in the report is the share of page views on which the seller's offer had the add-to-cart button. A drop in it lowers the seller's units without any change to the listing. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; Business reports glossary, G27691; Becoming the Featured Offer, G201687550; Maximize your sales potential with the Featured Offer)
- Amazon's APIs carry two Featured Offer shares: `buyBoxPercentage` over page views (the report and the Data Kiosk sales-and-traffic dataset) and seller analytics `featureOfferPct` over glance views. They are not one series and are not substituted for each other. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- Amazon is removing the seller-level Featured Offer eligibility requirement store by store, and says selection itself is unchanged. Whether a store has changed is read from that store's help page, not from a remembered date. (Updates to Featured Offer eligibility requirements; Featured Offer eligibility, G200418100; Becoming the Featured Offer, G201687550)
- Sessions belong to the ASIN, not to the seller holding the Featured Offer. (Seller Forums moderator reply, "Business Reports - Sessions column explanation")
- `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT` has a whole-catalogue by-date section and a by-ASIN section over the whole range. It has no ASIN-by-day table. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- Data Kiosk's `salesAndTrafficTrends` query gives child-ASIN series by day, week or month, in one marketplace per filter. An ASIN with no data in the period is absent rather than zero. (analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql)
- The sales-and-traffic report expands week and month ranges outward. Data Kiosk moves them inward. (both schemas)
- Search impressions are not in the sales-and-traffic data. They are in Brand Analytics' Search Catalog Performance and Search Query Performance, which need Brand Registry, and in the seller analytics dataset's search group. (Analytics Reports; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- Search Catalog Performance's click rate is clicks over impressions. Search Query Performance's click rate is clicks over the query's search volume. (the two report schemas)
- The seller analytics dataset's `conversionRatePct`, and its deprecated spelling `conversationRatePct`, are search purchases over search clicks, not unit session percentage. Its `featureOfferPct` divides by glance views, not page views. (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- Search impressions in Brand Analytics include Sponsored Products results, so ad spend moves the impression stage. (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard, GSWQU4LQSP2JH75Z)- Amazon states the role for the Data Kiosk sales-and-traffic dataset (Brand Analytics) but names no role for the seller analytics dataset; for that dataset the authorization error on query creation is the answer. (Role Mappings for SP-API Operations; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- Amazon marks recent data as not final in several sources: seller analytics returns null until a day is ready and its returns data lags weeks; the report's last week or month can be partial when the expanded end lies in the future; the hourly traffic and sales notifications carry late data from up to a day earlier. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Analytics Reports; Notification Type Values)
- Amazon states different freshness for different sources. Where each source's lag is written: door.md for reports, the dataset guide for seller analytics, Amazon's Brand Analytics page for search data. (sources in §F)
- In Business Reports, storefront and browse-node visits are not sessions or page views. A visit to a child ASIN does not count as a visit to its parent. (Business reports glossary, G27691)
- The "Detail Page Sales and Traffic" view sums page views only on days with sales activity. The "by Child Item" view sums them over the whole range. Page views from the two views are not compared. (Business reports glossary, G27691)
- Business Reports sessions and page views include mobile app traffic only from a date the Business Reports FAQ states; a series that crosses it is not comparable. Where the date lives: the FAQ. (Business Reports FAQ, G28761)
- How far back each source goes is stated per source, and the sales-and-traffic family and Brand Analytics differ. Where each limit lives: the report's page and schema, the dataset guide, and the Brand Analytics pages; the request's own cancellation or fatal status is the evidence. (Analytics Reports; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Business reports, G200633570; Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business)

(Demographics dashboard, GPHCEMH5FFH32KG8)

## Open questions

1. **Cross-device sessions.** Does a shopper who visits on browser and app in one day count as one session or two? The report schema says one ("counted as a single session"). Both Data Kiosk schemas say sessions are "the sum of browserSessions and mobileAppSessions". Settled by: one day, one ASIN, compared on the first real run across `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT`, `salesAndTrafficTrends` and Seller Central's By Child Item view.
2. **Day boundary.** Which time zone defines a "day" in the S&T report and in Data Kiosk? "The time component is ignored", and Seller analytics says "10 AM local time". Practitioners say marketplace local time. Settled by: the Business reports glossary (read; see the gap fill below) or a one-day comparison with `getOrderMetrics` using `granularityTimeZone=America/Los_Angeles` for US. **Gap fill:** the glossary (G27691 v7) defines a Business Reports week as "Sunday 00:00:00 to the following Saturday 23:59:59 PT". That points to Pacific time for the US UI. It defines no "day", and no time zone for other marketplaces or for the API report. The question stays open for the API and Data Kiosk.

## Positions

- **P-own-traffic-funnel-1** — When cx states a conversion figure from Business Reports data, it computes units ordered divided by sessions and names it unit session percentage, never orders over sessions or sessions over orders. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-2** — Whenever cx states a ratio (conversion, Featured Offer %, CTR), it gives the numerator and denominator counts beside it and the file they came from. A denominator that moved is checked before the ratio is read. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; A Dirty Dozen, KDD 2017; Seller Labs, "Sales Dropped? Split It…")
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-3** — cx treats "conversion rate" as ambiguous until it names the file and field. Unit session percentage (units/sessions), Search Catalog Performance `conversionRate` (purchases/clicks), seller analytics `conversionRatePct` and its deprecated spelling `conversationRatePct` (both search purchases/search clicks) and seller analytics `search.catalog.conversionRate` (purchases/impressions) are four different ratios. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-4** — For a per-ASIN daily series, cx uses Data Kiosk (`salesAndTrafficTrends` at CHILD/DAY, or the seller analytics view grouped by ASIN and day), because the sales-and-traffic report has no ASIN-by-day table. One report per day is the fallback. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-5** — cx reads the organic-search impression and click stage only from Brand Analytics' search reports (which need Brand Registry) or the seller analytics search group; when neither answers for the seller's credential, it says that stage is not measurable from these sources, rather than inferring it from sessions. (Analytics Reports; Amazon Brand Analytics | Sell on Amazon; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-6** — cx computes a per-impression CTR as clicks over impressions and does not use Search Query Performance's `totalClickRate`, which divides by search volume. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-7** — Before attributing a conversion drop to the listing, cx checks Featured Offer % for the same days, since a lost offer lowers units on the same sessions. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; Maximize your sales potential with the Featured Offer; Seller Labs, "Sales Dropped? Split It…")
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-8** — cx reads variation families at child level and does not add child sessions or page views to make a parent figure, because traffic is not additive across products. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; Get actionable B2B insights with Amazon business reports; MerchantSpring help; GitHub issue #2609 — the non-additivity itself rests on MerchantSpring [opinion]; no Amazon page read states it)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-9** — cx does not judge the most recent days of a traffic series as final. It notes which days a source marks partial or null, and re-reads the baseline window when it writes a verdict. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Analytics Reports; Notification Type Values; Daniks, "Read Them Right")
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-10** — For a window over several days, cx sums units and sessions and then divides; it does not average daily percentages. (Seller Labs, "Sales Dropped? Split It…"; consistent with Amazon's own definition of unit session percentage as unitsOrdered divided by sessions over the selected period, sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json. A Dirty Dozen §5.2 was removed as a source: it prefers per-user average of ratios in experiments and does not speak to days.)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-11** — When comparing the same window across the S&T report and Data Kiosk, cx checks the dates each actually covered, because the report widens weeks and months and Data Kiosk narrows them. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-12** — cx takes field names and definitions from the schema in force at run time (the models repository's report JSON and Data Kiosk GraphQL), because Amazon deprecates fields and removes datasets. (SP-API Release Notes; commit history of schemas/data-kiosk; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-13** — cx states no conversion benchmark as Amazon's, since Amazon publishes none, and practitioner figures disagree and rest on experience. (Jungle Scout, Brian Connolly 2024; Seller Labs; Emplicit)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-14** — A traffic rise from a better search position is read first in impressions, clicks and sessions, not in unit session percentage. (Ursu, Marketing Science 2018; Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-15** — For competitors, cx uses Keepa sales rank, `monthlySold` and Featured Offer holder history as proxies. It does not describe them as sessions or conversion, because no Amazon source gives another seller's traffic. (Keepa skill SKILL.md; Chevalier & Goolsbee 2002/2003)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-16** — cx does not compare page views between the "Detail Page Sales and Traffic" view and the "by Child Item" view, because Amazon sums them only over days with sales in the first and over the whole range in the second. (Business reports glossary)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-17** — cx does not build a Business Reports sessions or conversion series that crosses the date the Business Reports FAQ gives for adding mobile app traffic without saying so; it reads that date from the FAQ. (Business Reports FAQ)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-18** — When a listing review uses Demographics, cx states that it describes the Amazon account holders who bought, for products and brands above Amazon's customer minimum, and that it does not reconcile with Business Reports. cx offers it as context for whom the creative addresses, not as a measured effect of the creative. (Demographics dashboard; Amazon Brand Analytics | Sell on Amazon; SellerApp, Arishekar N)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-19** — When cx quotes a repeat-purchase figure, it names the range, because Amazon counts repeats only inside the selected range and excludes the first order. It does not compare repeat rates computed over ranges of different length. (Repeat Purchase Behavior report; sellingPartnerRepeatPurchaseReport.json)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-20** — For repeat-purchase and bought-together data, cx uses the SP-API reports `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_REPEAT_PURCHASE_REPORT` and `GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_MARKET_BASKET_REPORT` when the seller is a Brand Registry seller with the Brand Analytics role. For Demographics, Customer Loyalty Analytics, Category insights and ASIN Benchmarking, it asks the seller for the screen or export and names it. (Analytics Reports; the two report schemas; the help pages in G; Nova Analytics' "No export API" claim is contradicted for the two reports)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-21** — To set a listing's sessions and unit session percentage against peers, cx prefers Amazon's ASIN Benchmarking percentiles where the seller can show them. It does not use Category insights' search-to-purchase ratio as a conversion benchmark, because that ratio counts ASINs purchased against ASINs shown in search across a browse node. (ASIN Benchmarking; Marketplace Product Guidance; Megaficus and Blue Wheel as the practitioner readings it departs from)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-22** — cx describes Brand Analytics search impressions, clicks and purchases as organic plus Sponsored Products from the search results page, widgets excluded, never as organic-only. (Search Query Performance dashboard; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-23** — cx keeps Brand Analytics figures inside the seller's own files and reports, and does not copy them into anything shared beyond the seller. (Amazon Brand Analytics Terms and Conditions)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-24** — cx names which Featured Offer share it reads: `buyBoxPercentage` (Business Reports, the sales-and-traffic report or the Data Kiosk sales-and-traffic dataset; over page views) or seller analytics `featureOfferPct` (over glance views). It keeps a baseline and an observation window on the same one and never joins the two into one series. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Business reports glossary; Becoming the Featured Offer)
- **P-own-traffic-funnel-25** — cx does not tell a seller that Featured Offer seller eligibility still applies, or has ended, in their store without reading that store's own help page; a window that spans the rollout in the seller's store notes it as a confounder on Featured Offer %. (Updates to Featured Offer eligibility requirements; Featured Offer eligibility; Becoming the Featured Offer)

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- Amazon's `refundRate` and `browserPageViewsPercentage` descriptions in the report schema read as wording slips (an inverted division; "mobile" for "browser").
