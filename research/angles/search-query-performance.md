# Keywords, the official view: Brand Analytics (Search Query Performance, Search Catalog Performance, Top Search Terms)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Funnel stages:** all three. Exposure (impressions), click-through (clicks), conversion (cart adds, purchases). For each customer search query these reports set the seller's ASIN against the whole query market. Among the Amazon sources read for this file, no other one gives the seller's own ASIN against the query's totals at all four stages. The Search Terms report gives market-level shares only for each term's top three clicked ASINs, and the ads impression share covers ad impressions only.

**Skills fed:**
- **listing-review.** Use it to find the stage that dropped, query by query. It also covers the "keyword gap" trigger: a query that brings clicks but that the listing's words do not cover. It also names the report that carries each figure.
- **effect-review.** Here it measures the targeted stage on the queries the change bet on. Among the sources read, it is the only Amazon source that gives the whole query market's movement at each stage, for the same query and period, in the same row as the seller's ASIN. That figure is the "competitor set's same-period movement" the skill must subtract.
- **listing-change-log.** The "words bet on" field should name SQP queries, so the effect review can find them later.
- **Report script.** Three of its report types are here, along with their period rules.

**Why a senior operator cares.** Sessions and conversion rate from the Business Report cover every channel mixed together. Nothing in them says whether a listing lost visibility, lost the click, or lost the sale on the terms that matter. Brand Analytics search data is the one first-party view that splits these apart and normalises against the market: share of query impressions, share of clicks, share of cart adds, share of purchases.

The traps are what an operator expects a tool to know:
- shares that include ads;
- a click rate that is not CTR;
- a top-queries cap;
- a Sunday–Saturday calendar;
- a short attribution window;
- definitions that differ between Amazon's own surfaces.

### Search Query Performance (SQP): what it is (GUIDANCE and definitions)

- The dashboard "shows brand representatives how customers are searching for their brand, which focuses on the search results stage." It surfaces "query volume, impressions, clicks, add-to-cart, and buys." [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP)
- **Brand view.** "each row of the report shows the performance of a single query associated with your brand's catalog … with a maximum of 1000 query results." **ASIN view:** "each row shows the performance of a single query associated with a single brand's product". [official] (same page)
- **Scope (decisive for any reading).** Queries are "selected based on their overall performance in search results, and includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers. It excludes traffic from widgets on the search page such as "Top Rated" or "New Arrivals."" [official] (same page; the sentence is in the 2026-10-14 capture capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`). The SCP schema agrees for SCP: `impressionCount` "Impressions include sponsored search results". The page is readable, so a file that calls Amazon silent on this or the page unreachable is out of date.
- **Query Detail view.** It shows "the top 10 ASINs for the selected search query … in comparison to your selected ASIN", chosen by a "Query ASIN score, calculated using a combination of search funnel impressions, clicks, cart adds, and buys." [official] (same page)
- "An impression is counted every time the ASIN is displayed in the search results page." Precise definitions are deferred to a Metric Glossary inside Seller Central, which sits behind sign-in (see Pages read). [official] (same page)
- **"-" values.** "-" means "the data is unavailable. This occurs when data has not been captured for the selected time period, or if the metric is new." [official] (same page)
- **Cross-surface warning.** "Since dashboards may attribute and define customer actions in different ways, we do not recommend comparing the metric names of data across different dashboards." The metrics "do not replace metrics found in other Amazon reports, including advertising dashboards". [official] (same page; the same words are on the Search Catalog Performance Dashboard page)
- **Amazon's own suggested use.** "enhance your listings (including A+ content) by adding keywords that align with customer interests". [official] (same page). The Sell blog says SQP can "give you ideas for keywords to include in content like product descriptions". [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business, Daisy Quaker, 2025-10-08, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/grow-your-business/search-performance-dashboards?lang=en-US, read 2026-10-14)
- **Launch.** Amazon announced the dashboard as answering "what queries led customers to my product?". The forum page shows "5 years ago" on screen; the post's own timestamp in the page (its `datetime` attribute, shown as a hover title) reads 2022-04-14, which agrees with the dashboard link's `ref=SNA_SN_ABA_04112022`. [official] (New Brand Analytics search dashboards are now available, News_Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1afe683eb0acb90bb31abdc34950ef74, read 2026-10-14)
- **Downloads.** Amazon announced downloads in four forms:
  - "Simple view";
  - a "Comprehensive view" ("the report for your selected brand and date range for all of your queries");
  - an Amazon's Choice badge file;

  The last two are "only available at the monthly reporting range". Files land in the Download Manager. ### SQP field definitions in the SP-API report schema (RULES of the data shape)

Each row of `dataByAsin` is one ASIN × one query × one period. It carries `searchQueryData`, `impressionData`, `clickData`, `cartAddData` and `purchaseData`. [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)

- `searchQuery`: "A buying customer's search query that is relevant to your branded catalog."
- `searchQueryScore`: "The rank for a search query in comparison to other queries for the ASIN. The ranking mechanism measures overall performance (the highest rank is 1)." So 1 is best. It is a rank, not a volume.
- `searchQueryVolume`: "The number of times customers search for a query at a select time period."
- `totalQueryImpressionCount` / `asinImpressionCount` / `asinImpressionShare`. The share is "The fraction of impressions for the ASIN in comparison to the total impression count for the query."
- `totalClickRate`: "the number of clicks divided by the search query volume". It is clicks per search, **not** clicks per impression.
- `asinClickShare`: "The fraction of clicks for the current ASIN in comparison to the total click count for the query."
- Cart adds and purchases follow the same pattern: `totalCartAddRate` and `totalPurchaseRate` are divided by search query volume; `asinCartAddShare` and `asinPurchaseShare` are shares of the query totals.
- Median prices at each stage: `totalMedianClickPrice` vs `asinMedianClickPrice`, and the same for cart add and purchase. These show the ASIN's price against what the query's clickers and buyers paid.
- Delivery-speed counts at each stage: `totalSameDay…`, `totalOneDay…` and `totalTwoDay…` for clicks, cart adds and purchases. These are query-level totals for any ASIN.
- **Page and schema agree on the shape.** The Analytics Reports page's expandable attribute list for SQP carries `searchQueryData`, `impressionData`, `clickData`, `cartAddData` and `purchaseData`, median prices and delivery-speed counts included, the same fields as the schema. The definitions themselves are only in the schema. [official] (Analytics Reports; SQP schema)

### Search Catalog Performance (SCP)

- "Each row of the report represents the performance of an ASIN in your catalog, aggregated across all queries". It has the same organic-plus-Sponsored-Products scope and the same widget exclusion as SQP. [official] (Search Catalog Performance Dashboard, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GSWQU4LQSP2JH75Z, read 2026-10-14)
- **Schema definitions:**
  - `impressionCount` "Impressions include sponsored search results".
  - `clickRate` is "The number of clicks divided by the number of impressions for the ASIN". This is a true search CTR.
  - `conversionRate` is "The number of purchases divided by the number of clicks for the ASIN".
  - `searchTrafficSales` is a currency amount.
  - Median prices and delivery-speed counts appear at every stage.

  [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
- **No shares.** SCP has no market totals, so it cannot show the market's movement.
- Amazon's own pitch: "Identify drop-off points and address problems with product discoverability and sales conversion." [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics | Sell on Amazon). The Sell blog's funnel framing, verbatim: top of the funnel, "Adjust keywords to help products rank in search results and earn more impressions"; middle, "Analyze clicks to tweak product descriptions for higher click-through rates"; bottom, "Set competitive prices to improve conversion rates and boost purchases". It also asks: "Are customers finding products, but dropping off before they hit the buy button?" [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business, 2025-10-08)

### Amazon Search Terms report, "Top Search Terms" on the Sell page

- It "shows the most popular search terms in the Amazon store during a given time period, along with each term's search frequency rank and the top three products that aggregate customers clicked on after searching that term", with click share and conversion share for each of the three. [official] (Amazon Search Terms report, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G5NXWNY8HUD3VDCW, read 2026-10-14)
- **Search frequency rank** is "the numeric rank of a specific search term's popularity when compared to all other search terms during a given time period". It is a rank, not a count.
- **Click share** is that product's clicks after the term divided by all clicks after the term. Conversion share is defined the same way for purchases. "Click share and conversion share are independent of one another, and conversion share can be higher than click share." [official] (same page)
- The dashboard also lists the brand's products "that have dropped from the top three clicked ASINs for a given search term along with the click rank from the prior week. The table refreshes weekly." [official] (same page)
- The Sell page calls the dashboard "Top Search Terms": it "gives a broad view of customer search activity across Amazon stores." [official] (Amazon Brand Analytics | Sell on Amazon)
- **API size.** "This report is limited to approximately 12 million items … This limit may cause this report to differ from other reporting, such as Amazon Retail Analytics." [official] (Analytics Reports)
- **Schema fields:** `departmentName`, `searchTerm`, `searchFrequencyRank`, `clickedAsin`, `clickShareRank`, `clickShare`, `conversionShare`. Click share is a fraction: "Multiply the value by 100 to represent as a percentage." [official] (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json, read 2026-10-14)

### Data Kiosk: the seller analytics dataset's search group

- `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31` was added 2026-09-30. It has a `search` metric group:
  - `search.query` returns metrics "per search keyword", with `queryKeyword` and `priceAndCountMetrics`;
  - `search.catalog` aggregates across keywords and adds `clickRate`, `conversionRate` and `trafficSales`.

  `aggregateBy` is `DAY`, `WEEK` or `MONTH`. Search comes under "Sales and traffic access". [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide, read 2026-10-14; SP-API release notes, 2026-09-30)
- **Which SP-API role opens it: Amazon's pages do not line up.** The guide names an access level ("Sales and traffic access"), not a role, and says "Requesting a field your application is not authorized for returns an authorization error rather than a null value". The schema gates the group as `search: Search @paamRights(require: ["analytics_sales_and_traffic_view_pa"])`. Role Mappings for SP-API Operations (`updatedAt` 2026-10-13) lists Brand Analytics as the only required role for every Data Kiosk operation (`createQuery`, `getQuery`, `getQueries`, `getDocument`, `cancelQuery`). The changelog note "Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements" says "The Vendor Analytics and Sales and Traffic datasets require the Brand Analytics role. The Economics dataset requires the Selling Partner Insights role"; it predates this dataset and does not name it. None of these says whether Brand Registry is needed for `search`. The binding answer is the `createQuery` reply (an authorization error names what is missing), read at run time. [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Role Mappings for SP-API Operations, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/role-mappings, read 2026-10-14; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/changelog/correction-data-kiosk-role-requirements, read 2026-10-14)
- `priceAndCountMetrics` holds the seller's **own** counts: `impressionsWithPrice`, `clicksWithPrice`, `cartAddsWithPrice`, `purchasesWithPrice`, plus same-, one- and two-day delivery counts. **The schema has no query-total and no share field.** Data Kiosk therefore gives the ASIN's own funnel per keyword, but not the market's. [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, read 2026-10-14)
- **Definitions differ between Amazon's two surfaces.**
  - Data Kiosk's `SearchCatalogMetrics.conversionRate` is "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a purchase".
  - The SCP report's `conversionRate` is "purchases divided by the number of clicks".

  [official] (the graphql schema vs sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json)
- **A third definition inside the same dataset.** The dataset's top-level `conversionRatePct` (on `SellerAnalyticsView`) reads, verbatim: "The conversion rate describes the successful transition of a customer from clicks to purchase. This metric includes the percentage of purchases to clicks for ASINs originated from the search results page." That matches the SCP report and not `search.catalog.conversionRate`. The older spelling `conversationRatePct` carries the same text and `@deprecated(reason: "No longer supported. Use conversionRatePct instead.")`. Neither is a detail-page conversion over sessions (unit session percentage). Which definition binds is read from the schema's own description at run time, not carried from one name to another. [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, re-read 2026-10-14)
- **Date handling differs from Reports.** In Data Kiosk, for `WEEK` the start date is rolled back to the preceding Sunday and the end date to the preceding Saturday, not refused; a range under seven days after adjustment returns an error. `startDate` must be "no more than two years ago". The end date "is additionally capped to the most recent date for which all requested metrics have data". "All metrics in the seller analytics dataset are ready after 34 hours. Data for a given day is generally available at 10 AM local time two days later." [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **Incompatible metrics.** A metric that cannot be broken down at the requested grouping returns null: read null as "this metric is not available at this granularity" rather than as "this metric was zero". [official] (same page)

## What practitioners add

- **Perpetua (Martin Saunders), "Amazon Search Query Performance Report: tapping into the superpowers of search query data", 2024-10-16.** [opinion]
  - Brand view covers the top 1,000 queries and ASIN view the top 100 queries per ASIN.
  - Purchases are attributed within 24 hours, and "Amazon does not exclude purchases that are canceled and returned."
  - SQP totals "may not match the total count from your sales reports".
  - Low impression share means weak ranking, which he ties to title, bullet and backend-term work.

- **Intentwise (Michael Waters), "Troubleshoot your sales funnel with Search Query Performance", 2025-06-23.** [opinion] He gives the gap rules:

  - purchase share below click share: "something going wrong with your on-page content";

## Published research

- **Ursu, R. M., "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions", *Marketing Science* 37(4):530–552, 2018.** [research]
  - Finding: the paper uses "the first data set with experimental variation in the ranking from a field experiment at Expedia". Rankings "affect what consumers search, but conditional on search, do not affect purchases." (Gap fill correction: the first pass wrote "randomised its rankings". The abstract read says "experimental variation in the ranking", not how the variation was assigned; angles/own-traffic-funnel.md is right that randomisation is not stated in what was read.)
  - Transfer: hotels on Expedia, not Amazon products, so the transfer is partial. It is consistent with reading the exposure/click stage and the purchase-given-click stage as separately caused: in that setting, position changed which hotels were looked at, not whether a looked-at hotel was bought. What does decide the purchase is not tested by the paper. (Abstract read via the Crossref record of DOI 10.1287/mksc.2017.1072.)
- **Blake, T., Nosko, C., Tadelis, S., "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment", *Econometrica* 83(1):155–174, 2015 (NBER w20171, 2014).** [research]
  - Finding: at eBay, brand-keyword search ads showed "no measurable short-term benefits". For non-brand keywords, new and infrequent users were positively influenced, but frequent users accounted for most of the spend, and average returns were negative.
  - Transfer: Google/Bing search ads for eBay, not Amazon Sponsored Products. The transfer is weak but directional. A rise in purchase share on a branded query bought with Sponsored Products may not be incremental sales. SQP mixes organic and sponsored.
- **Reddy, C. K. et al. (Amazon), "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search", arXiv:2206.06588, 2022 (KDD Cup 2022 dataset).** [research]
  - Finding: Amazon's own relevance labelling of query–product pairs uses four classes: Exact, Substitute, Complement, Irrelevant. The labelled pairs carry title, description, bullet points, brand and colour.
  - Transfer: this documents how Amazon frames relevance for research. It does not describe the production ranker. It supports the idea that whether listing text matches the query matters to the exposure stage, and nothing stronger.
- **Gap fill. Barnett, A. G., van der Pols, J. C., Dobson, A. J., "Regression to the mean: what it is and how to deal with it", *International Journal of Epidemiology* 34(1):215–220, 2005 (online 2004-08-27).** [research]
  - Finding (abstract): regression to the mean "can make natural variation in repeated data look like real change". It "happens when unusually large or small measurements tend to be followed by measurements that are closer to the mean". Its effect "becomes more noticeable with increasing measurement error and when follow-up measurements are only examined on a sub-sample selected using a baseline value". It "should always be considered as a possible cause of an observed change".
  - Transfer: general statistics, not about Amazon. SQP's ASIN list is a sub-sample selected on performance in the same week (Amazon: queries "selected based on their overall performance"). Queries picked because they ranked high in a baseline week are, by this paper's mechanism, likely to look worse in the observation weeks with no change at all. Low-volume queries have the most measurement noise, so they show it most. (Abstract read via Europe PMC, PMID 15333621; the OUP page served the same abstract.)
- **Gap fill. Helsel, D. R., "Fabricating data: how substituting values for nondetects can ruin results, and what can be done about it", *Chemosphere* 65(11):2434–2439, 2006.** [research]
  - Finding (abstract): substituting a value for each observation "below detection limits" is a "fabrication of values" that "produces poor estimates of statistics, and commonly obscures patterns and trends in the data". Studies using it "may conclude that significant differences … do not exist, when in fact they do. The reverse may also be true." "Substituting values for nondetects should be used rarely".
  - Transfer: by analogy only. A query that drops out of an ASIN's SQP list was not observed above the list's cut-off. It is a censored value, like a lab reading "below the detection limit", not a measured zero. Writing it as zero is the substitution Helsel warns against. The paper is environmental chemistry; the analogy is the seat's. (Abstract read via Europe PMC, DOI 10.1016/j.chemosphere.2006.04.051.)
- Not used for a finding: Heckman, "Sample Selection Bias as a Specification Error", *Econometrica* 47(1), 1979. Only the Crossref metadata was read (no abstract), so it is not cited for a finding.
- Not used for a finding: Sorokina & Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products", SIGIR 2016, pp. 459–460. Only the metadata was reachable, not the text.

## Stable enough for a skill

- SQP gives, per ASIN, per query and per period, the ASIN's count and the query's total count at four stages: impressions, clicks, cart adds, purchases. The share at each stage is the ASIN's count divided by the query total. (SQP schema)
- SQP and SCP count actions that start on the search results page, organic and Sponsored Products together, and exclude search-page widgets. (SQP and SCP help pages; SCP schema)
- SQP's `totalClickRate`, `totalCartAddRate` and `totalPurchaseRate` are divided by search query volume, not by impressions. SCP's `clickRate` is clicks over impressions and its `conversionRate` is purchases over clicks. (SQP and SCP schemas)
- Amazon defines the same-named rate differently on different surfaces. It advises against comparing metric names across dashboards. (SQP help page; SCP schema vs Data Kiosk schema)
- `searchQueryScore` is a rank where 1 is best, and search frequency rank is a popularity rank. Neither is a volume. (SQP schema; Amazon Search Terms report help)
- SQP holds queries "selected based on their overall performance in search results", and the help page states a maximum for the Brand view (the figure lives on that page). Queries not selected, and sales that did not start on the search results page, are not in SQP. The ASIN view's cap is not stated by Amazon. (SQP help page)
- A Brand Analytics search report covers exactly one whole period. A week runs Sunday to Saturday, and dates outside that end FATAL after a 202. (Analytics Reports; SQP schema; Amazon's error texts)
- SQP needs the `asin` option and serves ASIN rows, not the Seller Central Brand view. SCP without `asins` covers every ASIN the brand owns. (Analytics Reports; SCP schema)
- Data Kiosk's seller analytics `search.query` carries the seller's own per-keyword counts and no query totals. Shares come only from the SQP report or dashboard. (Data Kiosk graphql schema)
- Amazon's Search Terms dashboard and report show only a few top clicked ASINs per term, with click and conversion share; how many is stated on the help page and in the schema's description and is read there. (Amazon Search Terms report help; Search Terms schema)
- POE's search volume and niche data have no SP-API counterpart among the pages read. The Customer Feedback API carries POE's review and return insights. (Customer Feedback API page; SP-API release notes; POE announcement)
- SQP's query list per period is selected on performance and capped, so the set of queries changes from period to period without any change to the listing. (SQP help page; Amazon staff on Helium 10 #485 [opinion] for the churn)
- SCP gives each ASIN's search funnel across all queries, so it is not cut by SQP's query list. (SCP help page)
- The API's SQP report returns ASIN rows only. The Brand view, the Comprehensive view, the Query Detail view and the monthly Amazon's Choice and outliers files exist only as Seller Central dashboards and downloads. (Analytics Reports; SQP schema; Download your Brand Analytics Search dashboard data)
- In an SQP report, each query's totals are the query's whole market and repeat unchanged in each requested ASIN's row for that query. (SQP schema example)
- The API's Search Terms report carries search frequency rank and the top clicked ASINs with click and conversion share. The dashboard's top-clicked brands and categories are not in it. (Search Terms schema; Analytics Reports; Sell blog 2025-10-08)
- Amazon states on its Brand Analytics help node how long after a period's close the data is generally available; the figure lives there. (Brand Analytics help node)
- Amazon reserves the right to change Brand Analytics' content, format and update frequency at any time, so field lists are read from the schema at run time, not from memory. (Amazon Brand Analytics Terms and Conditions)

## What cx must read at run time

- **The report types and their options**: `createReport`, then `getReport` until it is processed, then `getReportDocument`. The report names, `reportOptions`, the period rules, the wait between status reads, and what to tell the seller when a report stays `IN_QUEUE` are door.md's. The playbook names the three report types and points there.
- **The period boundaries.** Before each create, compute the period's first and last day: Sunday to Saturday, first to last of the month or quarter. Ask for one report per period. The playbook should say "one period per report". The calendar arithmetic is cx's to do with a shell, and the rule's wording lives in door.md and the schema.
- **The report schema** is the field truth for SQP, SCP and Search Terms (`sellingPartner…Report.json` in selling-partner-api-models). If a field is missing from the document, read it there, not from memory.
- **Data Kiosk.** Run `createQuery` → `getQuery` → `getDocument` on `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31` (`search.query`, `search.catalog`) for daily or weekly own-funnel counts per keyword. Its availability cap and null semantics are stated in the Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide. The operation pacing is door.md's.
- **What Brand Analytics does not have.** The listing's own text that a query should match is read from Listings Items `getListingsItem` (`attributes`, including `generic_keyword`). What Amazon shows is read from Catalog Items `getCatalogItem`. Brand Analytics has neither.
- **Keepa.** Keepa has no search-query data. It describes content and price history only (the Keepa skill's product-file reference has no search field). The competitor ASINs that SQP's Query Detail view or Search Terms names can be read through Keepa (`cx get amazon/us/<ASIN>/product --source keepa`). Those ASINs give the price, rank and content history that explain a share movement.
- **The balanced panel (gap fill).** For an effect review on SQP:
  - Request the same ASIN list for every week of the baseline and observation windows (`createReport` → `getReport` → `getReportDocument`, one report per week; the waits are door.md's).
  - From the documents, keep for judgement only the (ASIN, query) pairs present in every week of both windows. List the pairs present in only one window apart, as "entered" or "left", without values.
  - Request SCP for the same ASINs and weeks (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_CATALOG_PERFORMANCE_REPORT` with `asins`) for the all-query total that the list cut cannot affect.
  - Data Kiosk `search.catalog` grouped by `asin` is the daily or weekly own-count alternative for the all-query total (no query totals, no shares).
- **The child or parent being asked about.** Before requesting SQP for a variation family, read which ASIN is the child and which the parent. Use Catalog Items `getCatalogItem` with `includedData=relationships`: a `VARIATION` relationship carries `childAsins`, `parentAsins` and `variationTheme` (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, read 2026-10-14). Request the child ASINs. What a parent ASIN returns in SQP is unknown (Open question 12).
- **The seller's own downloads.** The Brand view, the Comprehensive view and the monthly Amazon's Choice badge and outliers files are Seller Central downloads (Download Manager), with no API counterpart. cx reads them only as files the seller places in the tree, and names which download and which reporting range it needs.
- **The ads side.** Search-term-level ad spend and its own impression share are in the Ads console and Ads API (`spSearchTerm` report). There is no Ads API source today. Without it, per-product ad spend by ad type and date range is in the Data Kiosk Economics dataset (`ads: [AdSummary]`, "The advertising spend data for the product within the specified date range"; `adTypeName`, for example "Sponsored Products charge"; analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql, read 2026-10-14). Its role is Selling Partner Insights by the 2024 correction note, and the binding answer is the `createQuery` reply. That source has no search-term grain, so it shows whether ad spend on the ASIN moved in the window but cannot split an SQP share movement on one query into organic and paid. (angles/paid-keywords.md owns the Economics route.)

## Open questions

1. **When does a finished week appear in SQP?** The publication lag after Saturday is unstated. Amazon says only "generally available within three days" for Brand Analytics as a whole. To settle it, ask for the latest completed week on a Tuesday and a Wednesday in a real run and record `DONE` vs `FATAL`/empty, or read the Metric Glossary signed in.
2. **The attribution window and definitions.** The 24-hour window, the rendered-page impression rule and the cancellation treatment are stated by Amazon staff and practitioners, not by a page read. To settle them, read the Metric Glossary (sign-in) in a seller's session.
3. **What the 17-month line bounds in practice.** By its heading it bounds the `dataStartTime`–`dataEndTime` span, which the same section's "Requests cannot span multiple periods" contradicts; it may in practice be how far back a `MONTH` report can start. To settle it, ask for a single month 18 months back in a real run.
4. **ASIN view cap.** Practitioners report 100 queries per ASIN. An internal run's document for one ASIN-week was consistent with that. No Amazon page read states it, and whether a multi-ASIN request is capped per ASIN or in total is not known. To settle it, run a two-ASIN request and count rows per ASIN.
5. **Brand view through the API.** `asin` is required for SQP, so the API seems to serve only ASIN rows. Whether a list of all of a brand's ASINs reproduces the Brand view's totals (brand share counts each query once) is unknown. To settle it, compare against a Seller Central Comprehensive-view download.
6. **Data Kiosk `search.query`.** Does it work at `aggregateBy: DAY`, or come back null? Does it need Brand Registry? Is it capped to top keywords? To settle it, run the guide's "Seller search report" query at `DAY` for one ASIN.
7. **Data Kiosk conversion rate.** Is `search.catalog.conversionRate` really purchases over impressions, as its schema says, or a wording slip against the report's purchases over clicks (and against the same dataset's top-level `conversionRatePct`, described as purchases to clicks from the search results page)? To settle it, compute both from one week's counts.
8. **The day-2 request.** It ran from a Sunday to the next Sunday 00:00. Since "Amazon ignores the time component", it should end FATAL ("dataEndTime must be a Saturday"). Reading that report's final status settles it, and confirms that half-open date ranges are a trap.
9. **The "low-volume queries suppressed" claim (ZonGuru).** No Amazon page read states it. The Metric Glossary may settle it.
10. **POE search-volume API.** None was found. An SP-API model search for "opportunity" or "niche" settles whether one exists.
11. **For another seat (lead contradicted).** The roadmap's "SP-API has no review text" is narrowed by the Customer Feedback API: review topics with "customer snippets", Brand Analytics role.

## Positions

1. **P-search-query-performance-1.** To locate the stage that dropped, compare the ASIN's share at each SQP stage on the same query and period. Impression share, click share, cart-add share and purchase share each come from the ASIN's count over the query total. A share that falls between two adjacent stages names the stage. (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard help; Helium 10 podcast #485 [opinion]; Intentwise [opinion])
2. **P-search-query-performance-2.** Before calling a fall in impression or click share a listing problem, check whether Sponsored Products spend or placement changed on that query in the same period. SQP and SCP count organic and sponsored together. (Search Query Performance dashboard help; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard help; SCP schema; Perpetua [opinion])
3. **P-search-query-performance-3.** Read `totalClickRate`, `totalCartAddRate` and `totalPurchaseRate` as per-search rates (divided by search query volume), not as CTR or conversion. Compute any CTR or conversion from the counts and say which counts were divided. (SQP schema; SCP schema; Data Kiosk graphql schema)
4. **P-search-query-performance-4.** Never set a Brand Analytics rate against a same-named rate from another Amazon report, dashboard or dataset. Amazon defines them differently and advises against the comparison. (Search Query Performance dashboard help; SCP schema vs analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
5. **P-search-query-performance-5.** In an effect review, use whole Sunday–Saturday weeks (or whole months) for the baseline and observation windows, because Amazon's search reports cover exactly one whole period. (Analytics Reports; SQP schema)
6. **P-search-query-performance-6.** Subtract the market's own movement first, using the query totals in the same SQP row (`searchQueryVolume`, `totalQueryImpressionCount`, `totalClickCount`, `totalPurchaseCount`). Judge the ASIN by its share, not by its raw counts. (SQP schema; Intentwise [opinion]; ZonGuru [opinion])
7. **P-search-query-performance-7.** Ask for one Brand Analytics search report per period. A range covering several periods, or dates not on period boundaries, is accepted and then ends FATAL. (Analytics Reports; SQP schema; Amazon's error texts in the 2026-10-13/14 runs)
8. **P-search-query-performance-8.** Use SQP for query-level shares, and SCP (without `asins`) for each brand ASIN's search funnel across all queries. Do not expect SQP rows to add up to SCP or to the Business Report: SQP holds only top queries and only search-results-page actions. (SQP and SCP help pages; SCP schema; Perpetua [opinion])
9. **P-search-query-performance-9.** In the change log, record the "words bet on" as SQP queries, verbatim as Amazon spells them, with the ASIN's share at each stage for the baseline weeks. The effect review then reads the same rows. (SQP schema)
10. **P-search-query-performance-10.** Do not read `searchQueryScore` or search frequency rank as search volume. Both are ranks where 1 is highest. Volume is `searchQueryVolume`. (SQP schema; Amazon Search Terms report help)
11. **P-search-query-performance-11.** When a listing's stage drops on a query, use the Search Terms report's top clicked ASINs (reachable through the API) and, where the seller reads it in Seller Central, SQP's Query Detail view (a dashboard view; the SQP report schema carries no other ASIN's rows) to name the competitors that took the clicks. Then read their content and price history through Keepa. (Amazon Search Terms report help; Search Terms schema; Search Query Performance dashboard help; SQP schema)
12. **P-search-query-performance-12.** When reading a click- or purchase-stage gap, read the ASIN's median price against the query's at the same stage (`asinMedianClickPrice` vs `totalMedianClickPrice`, and the same for cart adds and purchases) and the query's delivery-speed counts alongside the shares, before attributing the gap to listing text. (SQP schema)
13. **P-search-query-performance-13.** For daily or weekly own-funnel counts per keyword, use Data Kiosk's seller analytics `search.query`. Use the SQP report for shares, because Data Kiosk carries no query totals. (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
14. **P-search-query-performance-14.** Tell the seller that the search figures cover the search results page only. Discovery through widgets is outside them, and so are purchases that did not start from a search query. (Search Query Performance dashboard help; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard help)

15. **P-search-query-performance-15.** In an effect review, judge SQP only on the (ASIN, query) pairs present in every week of both the baseline and observation windows, and read the same ASINs' all-query Search Catalog Performance figures for the same weeks beside them. The SQP list is chosen by performance and capped, so its membership changes week to week without any change to the listing. (Search Query Performance dashboard help; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard help; Helium 10 podcast #485, Amazon staff [opinion]; Barnett, van der Pols & Dobson 2005 [research])
16. **P-search-query-performance-16.** Treat a query absent from a week's SQP rows as not observed (below the list's cut-off), never as zero. Do not report "queries gained" or "queries lost" as an effect of a change; list them apart, without values. (Search Query Performance dashboard help; Helium 10 podcast #485, Amazon staff [opinion]; Helsel 2006 [research])
17. **P-search-query-performance-17.** Choose the "words bet on" from the queries that held their place across several baseline weeks, not from the single strongest week. A query picked because it was unusually strong in one week tends to come back toward its usual level, which looks like a loss. (Barnett, van der Pols & Dobson 2005 [research]; SQP schema `searchQueryScore`)
18. **P-search-query-performance-18.** For a variation family, request SQP for the child ASINs and judge a change to one child on that child's rows. By Amazon's staff account, a search purchase counts only for the child that was shown and clicked, and a swatch click credits the clicked child while the impression stays with the child shown. A sibling's movement is not the changed child's effect. (Helium 10 podcast #485, Amazon staff [opinion]; seller reply on the launch post [opinion]; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json for reading the family)
19. **P-search-query-performance-19.** Do not rebuild the brand's per-query figures by summing API rows: the query totals repeat in each ASIN's row, and each ASIN's own list is cut. For the Brand view (its query cap is stated on the SQP help page) or the Comprehensive view, ask the seller for the Seller Central download. (SQP schema example; Analytics Reports; Download your Brand Analytics Search dashboard data; Helium 10 podcast #485, Amazon staff [opinion])
20. **P-search-query-performance-20.** Describe SQP purchases as search-attributed orders, not units: per Amazon's staff, a multi-unit purchase counts once, and only purchases within a short attribution window after the search through the results page count (the window's length is stated by Amazon staff on a podcast, not on a page read; see What practitioners add). Set them against orders, not against Business Report units. (Helium 10 podcast #485, Amazon staff [opinion]; Perpetua [opinion])
21. **P-search-query-performance-21.** When naming who won a term, use the API's Search Terms report for the top clicked ASINs with click and conversion share. Top-clicked brands and categories come only from the Seller Central dashboard, as a file the seller provides. (sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json; Analytics Reports; Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business)
22. **P-search-query-performance-22.** Do not request a week's Brand Analytics search report before the availability Amazon states on its Brand Analytics help node has passed after the week's close. Read the figure there, not from memory. (Brand Analytics help node; Amazon Brand Analytics: Get data to grow your business)

**The seat's own (not for a skill)**

- (Gap fill) Amalytix's "33 columns" matches the count of the SQP schema's row fields: 3 query fields, 3 impression fields and 9 each for clicks, cart adds and purchases. This suggests that the dashboard's ASIN view and the API row carry the same columns. It is a count, not a statement by Amazon.
- (Gap fill) A change that lifts an ASIN on a query can pull that query into the ASIN's list. A query's entry can therefore be a real effect, but from the list alone it cannot be told apart from churn near the cut-off. SCP's all-query totals, and the query's own share in weeks where it is present, are the only clean readings.
- (Gap fill) Because Amazon staff say a sponsored and an organic appearance on one page count as two impressions, a change in ad placement can move impression share with no change in organic visibility. This strengthens P-2.

- A Sunday 00:00 end date is the half-open-interval habit, and Amazon reads it as Sunday. The end should be written as the Saturday. A date-only Saturday or `T23:59:59Z` both name it. This rests on the schema's "ignores the time component" plus one internal observation. It is not a page statement.
- **Gap rules.**
  - Impression share above click share means a click-through problem: main image, title start, price, badges, review count.
  - Click share above cart-add share means a detail-page or offer problem.
  - Cart-add share above purchase share means a checkout-stage problem: price, delivery promise.

  These are practitioner heuristics (Intentwise; Amazon staff on Helium 10 #485). Amazon's pages never state them. They should enter a skill only as practitioner signals, not as Amazon's rule.
- With the 24-hour attribution window (if confirmed), a change that lengthens consideration could lower SQP purchase share while total orders hold. Read orders alongside the share.
- Low-volume queries swing week to week. A share change on a query with few searches should not carry a verdict. The threshold is a judgement, not a sourced figure.
- An internal run saw this store's SQP reports processed one at a time per seller and slowly. The wait belongs in door.md, not here.
- In an effect review, treat the week in which the change went live as neither baseline nor observation (moved here from P-5 at verification: no page read states it; it is the seat's method).
- Price and delivery promise are confounders of a share gap, not listing text (the interpretive half of P-12, moved here at verification; the schema only supplies the fields).
