# AI shopping surfaces: what Amazon's assistant and AI features read from a listing, how sellers see that traffic, and who else rewrites the listing

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Funnel stages.** All three, plus one that is not a funnel stage.

- **Exposure.** Amazon's assistant (Rufus until 2026-05-13, now "Alexa for Shopping") answers questions typed into the main search bar. It puts "AI-generated overviews at the top of search results" and recommends products in chat, in AI Shopping Guides and in Help Me Decide. Branded searches can show brand-site products under "Shop brand sites directly", some with Buy for Me. Ads ("prompts", Sponsored Products in Alexa+ conversations) also reach these surfaces.
- **Click-through.** Sponsored prompts "can appear in shopping results and product detail pages". Practitioners report assistant modules inside search results.
- **Conversion.** The detail page has AI overviews, "Customers say" review highlights, Hear the Highlights audio summaries, personalized size recommendations, a price history the assistant shows the shopper, and assistant answers to product questions.
- **None (a change actor).** Amazon's own generative tools write or rewrite listing content. Some do it at the seller's request (listing creation, Enhance My Listing / View enhancements, Seller Assistant, Seller Assistant workflows). Others do it on Amazon's initiative (Review listing changes, the 2026 title AI recommendations, the 2024 bullet rewrites, personalized descriptions).

**Skills fed.**

- **listing-review.** It covers what these surfaces read, which listing elements Amazon names as their inputs (and which it does not), and the trigger "Amazon changed my content".
- **listing-change-log.** Each change file needs an *actor* field (seller, cx, another contributor, Amazon AI, the seller's own Seller Assistant workflow) and a pointer to any Review listing changes item.
- **effect-review.** Assistant-origin traffic cannot be separated in any seller report read here. Amazon-initiated rewrites inside a window are confounders. The assistant shows shoppers a price history.

**Why a senior operator cares.** A growing share of discovery bypasses classic keyword search. The research files disagree on two points. Do structured attributes or prose matter more to the assistant? Are AI rewrites a lever or a confounder? This file states what Amazon has said and what it has not, and separates the lever (seller-invoked AI drafts) from the confounder (Amazon-initiated changes). It also states what a seller can and cannot measure.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. The assistant: name, reach, and what Amazon says it reads

A1. **Name and date (fact).** The 2024 Rufus launch post, the 2025-11-18 Rufus post and the 2025-11-18 GenAI/AgenticAI roundup each carry the line "On May 13, 2026, Rufus was renamed Alexa for Shopping." The Alexa for Shopping launch post (page metadata `datePublished` 2026-05-13) says "Amazon brings together Rufus and Alexa+ to create 'Alexa for Shopping' on the Amazon Shopping app and website." The Seller Central help page says "Amazon brought Rufus and Alexa together to create one AI assistant". It also says "the Alexa for Shopping experience is available to US customers in the Amazon Shopping app and on Amazon.com". [official] ('Amazon Rufus' AI experience comes to the Amazon Shopping app, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus; Meet Alexa for Shopping, your personalized, agentic AI assistant on Amazon, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant; Alexa for shopping, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYYH9SLHSTHKT3CZ, v6 r2; all read 2026-10-14)

A2. **What Amazon says the assistant reads: the only seller-facing statement (GUIDANCE).** The Seller Central help page says: "For specific questions about your product such as "What are the dimensions?" or "Is this machine washable?", Alexa for Shopping uses information from the product's detail page." When answers rest on wrong attributes, Amazon gives sellers a two-step remedy:
1. "Verify that the product detail page contains the most current information".
2. "Submit a ticket via the help center" with the ASIN, "A screenshot of the question and Alexa's response", "The nature of the issue", "Additional context, including your expected response", and supporting materials ("Screenshot of the relevant product listing section", "Product or user manuals", "Details from the manufacturer's website").

[official] (Alexa for shopping, GYYH9SLHSTHKT3CZ v6 r2, read 2026-10-14)

A3. **Structured attributes are named (GUIDANCE).** Amazon's Attributes guide lists three benefits of "Complete and accurate attributes". The third is: "Enable Rufus (Amazon's AI shopping assistant) to provide more accurate product information to customers." It is the only Amazon page found that names a listing element as an assistant input. The page also says attributes "Improve your product's visibility in search results and enable customers to filter results". [official] (Attributes guide, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GBWHYLJ7NNQMXBAQ, v3 r2, read 2026-10-14)

[official] (Amazon Rufus: Amazon's AI shopping assistant gets smarter and more personal, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus-ai-assistant-personalized-shopping-features, page metadata 2025-11-18, read 2026-10-14)

### B. The other AI shopping features and what each reads

B1. **AI review highlights ("Customers say").**
- 2023 launch: "a short paragraph right on the product detail page that highlights the product features and customer sentiment frequently mentioned across written reviews". They "use only our trusted review corpus from verified purchases". [official] (How Amazon continues to improve the customer reviews experience with generative AI, Vaughn Schermerhorn, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/amazon-ai/amazon-improves-customer-reviews-with-generative-ai, page metadata 2023-08-14, read 2026-10-14)
- 2024 how-to: "we use text-based reviews from Amazon Verified Purchases and require that multiple customers share the same opinion before we generate a review highlight." If highlights are missing, "there may not have been enough reviews with shared mentions". Markets: "the U.S., the UK, Australia, India, Singapore, and the United Arab Emirates". [official] (Here's how Amazon's AI-generated review highlights help you make better shopping decisions, Ivy Levine, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-ai-generated-review-highlights, 2024-07-30, read 2026-10-14)
- 2025: "based solely on the content from customer reviews, automatically updating as new feedback comes in". [official] (How Amazon is using generative and agentic AI…, 2025-11-18)

B2. **Hear the Highlights.** "conversational audio summaries of product information, reviews, and information from across the web", "On select product detail pages … directly under the product image". [official] (same roundup)

B3. **AI Shopping Guides.** Guides use "large language models (LLMs) to uncover the most helpful attributes, use cases, features, brands, and terminology for each product type from our catalog data". They appear "in search autocomplete suggestions", in "Keep Shopping For", and at Amazon.com/AIShoppingGuides. At launch they covered "over 100 product types"; the Help Me Decide post (2025-10-23) says "hundreds of product types". [official] (Amazon's new AI Shopping Guides make it easier to research product types and buy smarter, Daniel Lloyd, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-ai-shopping-guides-product-research-recommendations, 2024-10-09; Help Me Decide post; both read 2026-10-14)

B4. **Help Me Decide.**
- The button appears "on product detail pages after viewing several similar items". It "analyzes your browsing activity, searches, shopping history, and preferences". It "matches this information with product details and customer reviews to recommend the perfect products for you". It returns one pick with "an upgrade pick and a budget option".
- It runs on "Amazon Bedrock, Amazon OpenSearch, and Amazon SageMaker". It is available on the US app and mobile browser.

[official] (What to buy on Amazon? Tap the 'Help Me Decide' button for personalized recommendations, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-things-to-buy-help-me-decide-gen-ai, 2025-10-23, read 2026-10-14)

B5. **Buy for Me and Shop Direct (off-Amazon selection inside Amazon search).**
- Branded searches can show "additional relevant products from other stores in a separate section of search results labeled 'Shop brand sites directly.'"
- Buy for Me opens an in-app detail page for the brand-site item and lets "Amazon make the purchase from the brand retailer's website on their behalf". Delivery, returns and service stay with the brand store. Amazon says "brands have the choice if they want to participate".

[official] (Amazon's new 'Buy for Me' feature helps customers find and buy products from other brands' sites, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-shopping-app-buy-for-me-brands, 2025-04-03; Amazon's new shopping feature lets you search for products even if they're not sold on Amazon, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/search-products-amazon-shopping-feature, 2025-02-11; both read 2026-10-14)

Figure, for human readers: Buy for Me selection grew "from 65K at launch to over half a million". [official] (roundup, 2025-11-18)

B6. **Alexa+ (devices).** Alexa+ helps customers shop on Echo devices. Shopping on Amazon.com and in the app goes through Alexa for Shopping. Alexa+ is "$19.99 per month" and free for Prime members. [official] (Introducing Alexa+: skills, cost, and availability, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/new-alexa-generative-artificial-intelligence, page metadata 2025-02-26, read 2026-10-14)

B7. **Lens Live.** "Lens Live integrates Amazon's AI shopping assistant, Alexa for Shopping, to offer product insights, summaries, and answer questions as you browse". [official] (You can now shop what you see using AI powered Amazon's Lens Live, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/search-image-amazon-lens-live-shopping-rufus, 2025-09-02, read 2026-10-14)

B8. **Personalized size recommendations** draw on "your fit preferences, product details, such as style, size chart, and customer reviews". [official] (roundup, 2025-11-18)

B9. **Personalized product descriptions (Amazon changes what one shopper sees).** "Amazon is innovating with generative AI to personalize … product descriptions on our website and in our shopping app … provide personalized product descriptions that are more relevant to individual customers … if a customer regularly searches for gluten-free products, the term "gluten-free" may be added to a relevant product description". [official] (Amazon sellers can now automatically improve product listings with our new Gen AI tool, Mary Beth Westmoreland, https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/amazon-generative-ai-seller-growth-shopping-experience, page metadata 2024-09-19, read 2026-10-14) The post gives no date for when this went live, its scope or its current status.

### C. How Amazon reports this traffic to sellers

C1. **Seller-side reports carry no field for assistant-origin traffic (FACT by absence).** No field, enum or description names Rufus, Alexa, an assistant, a conversational surface or AI overviews in any of these:
- the five report schemas: Search Query Performance, Search Catalog Performance, Sales and Traffic, Search Terms, `marketplaceAsinPageViewMetrics`;
- the two Data Kiosk schemas: `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24`, `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31`;
- the Report Type Values (analytics) page;
- the SP-API Release Notes (page `updatedAt` 2026-10-01).

[official, by absence] (selling-partner-api-models `schemas/reports/*.json` and `schemas/data-kiosk/*.graphql`, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models; Report Type Values – Analytics, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics.md; SP-API Release Notes, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes.md; all read 2026-10-14)

C2. **Brand Analytics impressions are search-results-page impressions (RULE by definition).**
- The SQP schema counts impressions "that originate from the search results page".
- The SCP schema: "Amazon counts an impression every time the ASIN displays in the search results page. Impressions include sponsored search results."
- The SQP dashboard help page: the data "includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers. It excludes traffic from widgets on the search page such as "Top Rated " or "New Arrivals."" It also says "An impression is counted every time the ASIN is displayed in the search results page."

None of the three says whether ASINs named in an AI overview at the top of search results, or in an assistant answer to a question typed into the search bar, count as impressions, or whether such a question becomes an SQP query row. [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, v5 r0, read 2026-10-14 and saved as capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`)

So Amazon does say that SQP and SCP impressions include Sponsored Products results on the search results page: the SQP help page in words, the SCP schema in its field description. Files that call this unstated (angles/paid-keywords.md Open question 1) or call the SQP page unreachable (angles/search-rank-and-volume.md Open question 2) are out of date on this point; the capture above holds the page's text.

C3. **Business Reports split traffic by device, not by origin.** "Sessions are visits to your Amazon.com mobile app pages by a user. All activity within a 24-hour period is considered a session". The glossary defines no traffic-source dimension. [official] (Business reports glossary, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G27691, v7 r1, read 2026-10-14) Amazon's own seller-analytics agent skill lists traffic metrics with "device splits" only (`DESKTOP_`, `MOBILE_APPLICATIONS_`, `MOBILE_BROWSER_`). [official] (selling-partner-agentic-toolkit, skills/seller-analytics/references/metrics-catalog.md, read 2026-10-14)

C4. **Ads side: Alexa+ placements are blended into existing reporting (RULE as announced).** Release note of July 15, 2025, "Alexa+ tests product discovery through conversational AI":
- "Sponsored products campaigns will now be automatically integrated into shopping conversations when customers chat with Alexa+ about products … receiving a mix of organic and sponsored options."
- "Performance metrics will be consolidated within existing reporting dashboards."
- "At this time, Sponsored Products on Alexa+ is only available on the Echo Show."

[official] (Amazon Ads API release notes, markdown source https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/release-notes/index.md of https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/index, read 2026-10-14)

C5. **Ads side: prompts have their own report (RULE).**
- What they are: "Prompts can appear in shopping results and product detail pages on Amazon. When clicked, prompts may open a dialog in Rufus or respond to the customer directly on the page". "Prompts leverage Amazon's first-party signals from your detail pages, Brand Store, campaign data, and more".
- Enrolment and dates: campaigns "will be automatically enrolled in prompts". Open beta from November 2025; general availability "in the U.S. on March 25, 2026", when Amazon begins "to charge for these ads". A prompt can be paused in the Ad Console. [official] (Sponsored Products prompts and Sponsored Brands prompts, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/unboxed-2025-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands-prompts, page metadata 2026-03-10, read 2026-10-14)
- The API report: report types `spPromptAdExtension` and `sbPromptAdExtension`, grouped by `promptAdExtension`. Columns include `promptText`, `advertisedAsin`, `impressions`, `clicks`, `cost` and purchases at 1, 7, 14 and 30 days. Maximum date range 90 days, data retention 95 days, marketplace filter "US". "Prompts with clicks will show in your existing Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands reporting". [official] (Prompt Ad Extension reports, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/report-types/prompt-ad-extension.md, read 2026-10-14)
- The release note of August 24, 2026: "The `spPromptAdExtension` and `spVideoAdExtension` report types in version 3 of the reporting API now support four additional columns for Sponsored Products" (`portfolioName`, `campaignBudgetCurrencyCode`, `purchaseClickRate7d`, `purchaseClickRate14d`); "Availability: United States." The note does not name `sbPromptAdExtension`. [official] (Amazon Ads API release notes, read 2026-10-14)

### E. Amazon-initiated changes (the confounder) and the Review listing changes window

E1. **Review listing changes: what it shows (RULE).**
- Who: "only available to selling partners who have a Brand Representative role". It tracks "upcoming or recently published Amazon-initiated content changes across their brand's catalog". It covers "products you list, and products listed by other selling partners".
- Two views: "Changes for review" (upcoming changes, "including changes published after the review window ended") and "Amazon-applied changes: other content changes Amazon applied directly. No action is needed from you". Both show "material product data changes … over the last 60 days".
- Exclusions: "This view does not currently include updates to product images or browse nodes, and does not surface normalization updates such as capitalization of an existing value."
- Warning: "Despite careful review, Amazon-recommended content changes may contain inaccuracies, biases, or errors."

[official] (Review listing changes, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G7T47V48CGE6FS32, v5 r0, read 2026-10-14)

E2. **The acceptance window (RULE).**
- "You have 14 days to review an upcoming change before it publishes automatically." "You remain in control … but you must respond before the Publish date."
- "Disagreeing with an upcoming change pauses its publish date while Amazon reviews your feedback." Responses can be in bulk, "up to 100 ASINs at a time".
- In Edit mode, "Entering a different value marks your response for that attribute as Disagree."
- "By using the proposed content, you agree that you have reviewed the content and that it is accurate, complete, and compliant".

[official] (Review listing changes, v5 r0) The staff guide says the same: "If no feedback is provided within this window, the proposed changes will automatically be applied to your product detail pages", and "you can still request changes after the review period closes by using "Contact Us"". [official, staff forum post] (🚨 Review Listing Changes Tool: Your Guide to Managing AI Product Updates 🚨, Michelle_Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/e1c8d51d-c27b-4d8a-bea1-26413e283699, `datePublished` 2026-04-14, read 2026-10-14)

E3. **What the tool changes and from what (GUIDANCE).**

## Published research

- **Vijai Mohan, Yiwei Song, Priyanka Nigam, Choon Hui Teo, Weitian Ding, Vihan Lakshman, Ankit Shingavi, Hao Gu, Bing Yin, "Semantic product search", KDD 2019 (Amazon).** [research]
  - Finding: a deep model trained on behaviour data ("random negative examples, impressed but not purchased examples, and positive examples (purchased items)") retrieves semantically related products beyond lexical matching. It reports "at least 4.7% improvement in Recall@100 and 14.5% improvement in mean average precision (MAP)" offline, plus online A/B tests.
  - Transfer: Amazon has matched queries to products semantically since at least 2019, learning from purchases. Exact keyword presence is not the only path into a result set. It says nothing about which listing field the model reads most. (https://www.amazon.science/publications/semantic-product-search)
- **Changlong Yu, Xin Liu, Jefferson Maia, Tianyu Cao, Laurence (Yang) Li, Yifan Gao, Yangqiu Song, Rahul Goutam, Haiyang Zhang, Bing Yin, Zheng Li, "COSMO: A large-scale e-commerce common sense knowledge generation and serving system at Amazon", SIGMOD/PODS 2024 (Companion).** [research]
  - Finding: LLM-generated, human-filtered commonsense relations are mined from query-purchase and co-purchase behaviour.
  - Online, "approximately 10% of Amazon's U.S. traffic" in search navigation saw "a notable 0.7% relative increase in product sales" and "an 8% increase in navigation engagement rate". The paper calls this "a single, relatively minor feature on the search page".
  - In its relevance experiments, product side information ("product title, descriptions and attributes") is concatenated "into one single text span".
  - Transfer: Amazon models shopper intent (use, audience, occasion). In this research setting, structured attributes and prose are one text input, not ranked against each other. The results are platform-level, not evidence about one listing's wording. (publication page and PDF https://cdn.amazon.science/19/5d/bde30d0d4019be6421e79e50cda9/cosmo-paper.pdf; blog "Building commonsense knowledge graphs to aid product recommendation", Changlong Yu and Zheng Li, 2024-05-10)
- **Chandan K. Reddy et al., "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search", arXiv 2206.06588, 2022 (Amazon; KDD Cup 2022).** [research]
  - Finding: "around 130 thousand unique queries and 2.6 million manually labeled (query,product) relevance judgements". COSMO's evaluation used this dataset.
  - Transfer: it shows Amazon grades relevance in four classes (exact, substitute, complement, irrelevant). It does not show live ranking.
- **Maarten Buyl, Paul Missault, Pierre-Antoine Sondag, "RankFormer: Listwise learning-to-rank using listwide labels", KDD 2023 (Amazon; blog by Paul Missault, 2023-08-03).** [research]
  - Finding: labels are "0 if it isn't clicked on … 1 if it is clicked on, and … 2 if it's purchased". The model beat baselines "On a large set of internal Amazon search data".
  - Transfer: Amazon's rankers learn from clicks and purchases. Behaviour, not text alone, decides rank. This research is not the production ranker.
- **Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan, Ameet Deshpande, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", KDD 2024 (arXiv 2311.09735).** [research]
  - Finding: on a web benchmark (GEO-bench), content strategies "can boost visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses", and efficacy "varies across domains".
  - Transfer: weak. These are web generative search engines, not Amazon's assistant, which also uses reviews, behaviour and personal history (A4).
- **Aounon Kumar, Himabindu Lakkaraju, "Manipulating Large Language Models to Increase Product Visibility", arXiv 2404.07981, 2024.** [research]
  - Finding: adding a "strategic text sequence" to a product page made fictitious coffee machines more likely to be the LLM's top recommendation.
  - Transfer: it shows that LLM recommenders can be steered by page text. That is a manipulation risk and a reason never to write such text into a listing. It is not an Amazon test.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Amazon's shopping assistant is called Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus); Amazon renamed it on 2026-05-13. (aboutamazon Rufus posts; Meet Alexa for Shopping…; Alexa for shopping help page)
- Amazon tells sellers that the assistant answers questions about a specific product from the product's detail page, and that a wrong answer is fixed by making the detail page current and then opening a help-center ticket. (Alexa for shopping)
- Amazon names complete and accurate attributes as one way to help the assistant give accurate product information. (Attributes guide)
- Amazon names no other listing field as an assistant input and gives no field weights. (by absence across the pages read)
- Amazon says the assistant also draws on customer reviews, community Q&A, information from across the web, and each customer's own shopping history. (Rufus posts; Amazon Science Rufus post; Meet Alexa for Shopping…)
- "Customers say" is generated from verified-purchase text reviews, only when multiple customers share an opinion. The seller cannot write it. (review highlights posts)
- Amazon's seller reports and Data Kiosk datasets have no field that separates assistant-origin traffic. Brand Analytics impressions are defined on the search results page and include Sponsored Products results there. (report schemas; Data Kiosk schemas; SQP dashboard; Business reports glossary)
- Sponsored Products served in Alexa+ conversations is reported inside existing ad reporting. Sponsored prompts have their own prompt-level report in the Ads API. (Ads release notes; Prompt Ad Extension reports)
- Amazon-initiated content changes for a brand appear in Review listing changes. An upcoming change publishes automatically unless the brand representative responds within the review window. Disagreeing pauses it. Image, browse-node and normalization updates are not shown there. (Review listing changes; Michelle_Amazon guide)
- Amazon rewrites non-compliant titles with AI recommendations without any submission by the seller. (Updates to improve your product titles…; Answers to your product title update questions)
- Amazon's generative listing tools produce suggestions the seller is responsible for. Amazon warns they can add features and claims that were not in the original content. (Suggest changes to your product detail page; Review listing changes)
- The seller's own Seller Assistant can update titles, descriptions and bullets with permission. Its workflows can be set to act on a schedule. (Seller Assistant help; Seller Assistant Workflows; plugin post)
- What one shopper sees can differ from the catalog text, because Amazon personalizes product descriptions and generates overviews and summaries. (Enhance My Listing post; Meet Alexa for Shopping…)
- The window lengths, character limits, latencies and usage figures above change by announcement, and Amazon's pages give different latencies for different scopes. The live limits are in the product-type schema (`getDefinitionsProductType`); the review window is on the Review listing changes page; a change is confirmed by reading it back, not by a fixed wait.

## What cx must read at run time

- **What the listing says (the assistant's detail-page source).** `getListingsItem` with `includedData` attributes and issues for the seller's submission. `getCatalogItem` / `searchCatalogItems` with `includedData` `attributes,summaries,images` for Amazon's reconciled view, which is what shoppers and the assistant see. A Catalog value that differs from the submission is evidence of an Amazon rewrite, a split or another contributor (operations from catalogItems_2022-04-01.json and listingsItems_2021-08-01.json).
- **Which attributes exist and are recommended for the product type.** `getDefinitionsProductType`. This is the attribute set Amazon's Attributes guide refers to (Required / Recommended / All).
- **Amazon-initiated content changes.**
  - `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` and `ITEM_PRODUCT_TYPE_CHANGE` notifications (subscription via `createSubscription`; destinations and waits are door.md's).
  - Periodic Catalog Items re-reads for images and browse nodes, which Review listing changes does not show.
  - Review listing changes has no SP-API operation in the models checked. Its proposals reach cx only as a file or screenshot the seller puts in the tree.
- **Review themes (not the "Customers say" text).** Customer Feedback API `getItemReviewTopics` ("ten most positive and ten most negative review topics") and `getItemReviewTrends` ("past six months") (customerFeedback_2024-06-01.json). cx must not present these as Amazon's "Customers say" paragraph.
- **Search-side metrics.** Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance through `createReport` (report type names, options and waits are door.md's). Data Kiosk sales-and-traffic / seller-analytics through `createQuery` (door.md's). None isolates the assistant.
- **Paid assistant surfaces.** Amazon Ads `spPromptAdExtension` / `sbPromptAdExtension` reports. Not reachable today: the package has no Ads API source (see angles/paid-keywords.md). Without the Ads API, per-product ad spend by day can be read from the Data Kiosk Economics dataset (`ads`, which needs the Selling Partner Insights role; angles/paid-keywords.md and the README's ER-15 own it). - **Keepa.** No Keepa field read in this session describes assistant answers, overviews or review highlights [seat]. Keepa fields for listing content are owned by angles/listing-content-and-health.md and angles/competitor-set.md.
