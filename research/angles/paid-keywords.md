# Paid keywords: Amazon Ads search terms (outside this package, the reconciliation layer)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage.** Exposure, paid side: which shopper queries showed the seller's ads, which got clicks, which turned into orders. It also touches organic exposure, because ad-driven sales and ad placements share the same results page as the organic ranking. Click-through and conversion come in only as ad-side evidence: a search term that converts when paid is a sign that the listing fits that query.

**Which skills it feeds.**
- `listing-review`. One trigger family: "a paid search term converts, but the listing's text does not cover it" (the keyword gap). A second signal: a query where the brand gets a high share of impressions but a low share of clicks.
- `listing-change-log`. The "words bet on" field. Each word a change adds can later be checked against that word's paid search-term rows. This is the "reconciliation layer" named in the roadmap (§1.1 angle table row "付费关键词"; §3: 「真要加的只有 §6 Ads API（付费侧的验证，旧框架 §7 的对账层）」).
- `effect-review`. Manual changes to ad spend, bids, budgets, placements and campaign state are confounders of any organic before/after reading. Amazon's automatic bid responses under dynamic or rule-based bidding are not confounders. They are part of the change's effect (gap fill, §N, P-paid-keywords-17). A lost Featured Offer switches the ads off (§O). The package must also say what a verdict is worth when ad data is absent.
- `listing-review` (gap fill). Auto-targeting's matched terms and Amazon's keyword and product recommendations for the ASIN are Amazon's own reading of what the product is (§K). Competitors' ads on the seller's detail page are a conversion leak that copy does not close (§L).

**Not in the package's sources today.** The Amazon Ads API is a separate product. It has its own Login with Amazon client, its own approval and its own host (below). The 70 model files in `amzn/selling-partner-api-models` at commit `bad4c9b6` include no advertising API; the seat checked file names only. So `cx raw amazon-sp-api` cannot reach a campaign, a keyword or a search-term report. The roadmap's angle table marks this row "无；BOARD 计划行".

**Why a senior operator cares.** On most accounts the ad search-term report is the only place a seller sees, term by term, what shoppers typed before buying. The Brand Analytics Search Query Performance (SQP) report gives the total funnel per query, organic and Sponsored Products together (§I), but it does not say how much of it was paid. If ads are left out, a listing change can look like it worked when a bid raise did the work, or look like it failed when a campaign ran out of budget.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

Below, RULE marks something Amazon enforces: an API shape, a limit or a deprecation. GUIDANCE marks a recommendation.

### A. Access: a separate API, a separate grant

- RULE. "To use the Amazon Ads API, you must apply for access and be approved." Requests come from "a client application administered by Login with Amazon". "Application approval may take up to 1 business day." [official] (Amazon Ads API onboarding overview, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/onboarding/overview, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. Direct advertisers apply through their own form, signed in "using the email address you used to create the Amazon Developer account". Partners apply through the Amazon Ads Partner Network. [official] (Apply for Amazon Ads API access, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/onboarding/apply-for-access, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. Each reporting request carries a client ID, a bearer access token and `Amazon-Advertising-API-Scope`, "The profile ID associated with an advertising account in a specific marketplace". The tutorial's endpoint is `https://advertising-api.amazon.com`, the North American one. [official] (Getting started with reports (version 3), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/get-started, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. Amazon also runs an Amazon Ads MCP server. NA endpoint: `https://advertising-ai.amazon.com/mcp`; EU and FE have their own. It uses the same LwA client and tokens (or, alternatively, an OAuth 2.1 browser login the page offers). A reporting skill guides agents through the `reporting-create_report` and `reporting-retrieve_report` tools. [official] (Connecting to the Amazon Ads MCP Server, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/mcp/get-started; Overview [MCP], https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/mcp/mcp-overview; Ads Reporting Skill, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/mcp/skills/skills-library/reporting; all read 2026-10-14)

### B. Reporting surfaces, and one that is ending

- RULE. "Reporting API v3 will be deprecated on June 30, 2027." On that date v3 reporting, Sponsored Ads v2 reporting and Marketing Stream v1 "will stop delivering data". The deprecation notice is dated September 2026. [official] (Search term reports [v3], https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/report-types/search-term; Deprecations, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/deprecations; both read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. The replacement is the Reporting API v1. A report is created with `POST /adsApi/v1/create/reports` and polled with `POST /adsApi/v1/retrieve/reports`. Statuses are `PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `COMPLETED` and `FAILED`. The recommended poll rate is "once per minute". A finished report hands back pre-signed S3 URLs. v1 has no report types: a report is a list of fields, such as `searchTerm.value`, `metric.impressions` and `metric.purchases`. [official] (Getting started with the Reporting API v1, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/quickstart, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. Marketing Stream v2 and the Reporting API v1 went generally available on September 30, 2026. [official] (Amazon Ads API v1 release notes, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/ads-api-v1, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. "The reporting API v1 supports one concurrent report per advertiser, which allows approximately 100 reports per day based on average run times." [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/overview; Amazon Ads reporting overview, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/overview; both read 2026-10-14)
- RULE and GUIDANCE for v3:
  - "report generation can take up to three hours".
  - A duplicate request returns `425`.
  - Polling too often returns `429`.
  - GUIDANCE: "1-2 report requests, per advertiser, per day, for each report type".

  [official] (Getting started with reports (version 3), above; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/faq, read 2026-10-14)
- Amazon's migration pages map each v3 report type to v1 fields: `spSearchTerm`, `spTargeting` and `spCampaigns`. For example, v3 `searchTerm` becomes v1 `searchTerm.value`, and v3 `keyword` with `matchType` becomes `target.value` with `target.matchType`. [official] (Migrating from: spSearchTerm, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/report-types/sp-search-term; Migrating from: spTargeting, …/sp-targeting; Migrating from: spCampaigns, …/sp-campaigns; all read 2026-10-14)

### C. The search term report

- RULE (v3 shape):

  | | Sponsored Products | Sponsored Brands |
  |---|---|---|
  | `reportTypeId` | `spSearchTerm` | `sbSearchTerm` |
  | Maximum date range | 31 days | 31 days |
  | Data retention | 65 days | 60 days |
  | `groupBy` | `searchTerm` | `searchTerm` |

  The `keywordType` filter takes `BROAD`, `PHRASE`, `EXACT`, `TARGETING_EXPRESSION` and `TARGETING_EXPRESSION_PREDEFINED`. The Sponsored Brands report is "currently available in preview". [official] (Search term reports [v3], above)
- RULE, the decisive one for keyword-gap work: "search term reports only include impressions that resulted in at least one ad click." The FAQ states the gap with the console: "The search term report in the advertising console contains all search terms that had an impression, regardless of if that term resulted in a click. The API search term report only contains impressions that also had at least one click." [official] (Search term reports [v3]; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); both above)
- RULE. "If a placement does not have a search keyword associated with it on a product detail page, the search term in the report will be an asterisk `*`." [official] (Search term reports [v3], above)
- RULE. Entries that look like ASINs appear as customer search terms. They "correspond to `ASIN`s and the related product detail page on which your ad displayed", and appear only for automatic and product-targeted ad groups. [official] (Search term reports in bulksheets, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/no-code-tools/bulksheets/bulksheets-search-term-report, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE. The bulksheets search-term tab always uses a 7-day lookback: "The lookback window in bulksheets search term reports is always 7 days for both vendors and sellers". The bulk file's date range "does not affect the search term report metrics". [official] (same page)
- Two definitions on that bulksheets page are inverted. Amazon writes Click-through Rate as "calculated as impressions divided by clicks" and Conversion Rate as "The total number of orders divided by sales". The v3 column reference has it right: `clickThroughRate` is "Clicks divided by impressions". cx should compute rates from the counts and not trust a precomputed rate column. [official] (Search term reports in bulksheets; Reporting columns (version 3), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/columns; both read 2026-10-14)
- GUIDANCE, Amazon's own use case for the report: "identify high-performing searches from customers and to create negative keyword or product targets for search terms that do not meet your goals." Amazon points the report at targeting decisions. ~~No Amazon page read here points it at listing copy.~~ Corrected by the gap fill: Amazon Ads' listing guide does point it at listing copy ("add these to your product title, bullet points, and/or product description"; §M). [official] (Search term reports in bulksheets, above; How to improve your product detail page for advertising, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/improve-your-products-for-advertising, read 2026-10-14)

### D. Metrics and attribution windows

- RULE (v3 Sponsored Products columns), each with Amazon's wording:
  - `purchases7d`: "Number of attributed conversion events occurring within 7 days of an ad click".
  - `sales7d`: "Total value of sales occurring within 7 days of an ad click".
  - `purchasesSameSku7d` and `attributedSalesSameSku7d`: the same counts where "the purchased `SKU` was the same as the `SKU` advertised".
  - `salesOtherSku7d`: where the purchased SKU "was different".
  - `acosClicks7d`/`acosClicks14d`: "Advertising cost of sales based on purchases made within 7/14 days of an ad click".
  - `roasClicks7d`/`roasClicks14d`.
  - `costPerClick`: "Total cost divided by total number of clicks".

  The SP search-term report carries 1d, 7d, 14d and 30d variants of purchases, sales and units. [official] (Reporting columns (version 3); Search term reports [v3]; both above)
- RULE (v3 Sponsored Brands). `purchases`, `sales` and `unitsSold` are "within 14 days of an ad click or view". `purchasesClicks` and `salesClicks` are within 14 days of a click. [official] (Reporting columns (version 3), above)
- RULE (v1). Conversion metrics drop the window suffix:
  - "Sponsored Products: Base metrics automatically align with campaign defaults—7-day look-back for sellers and 14-day look-back for vendors".
  - "Other campaign types: The standard look-back is 14 days".
  - "All conversion metrics are now reported on 'traffic date'", meaning the day of the ad interaction.
  - Every conversion metric has a Promoted and a Halo version. Halo is "highly relevant ('Halo') products defined by the expansion rules of each campaign".
  - Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands CPC "will continue to report 0 for conversions from views".

  [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, above). The v1 metric page maps `metric.purchases` to v3 "`purchases, orders14d, purchases7d, purchases14d [SP: 7d sellers / 14d vendors]`". [official] (Purchases (Amazon retail conversions), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/amazon-retail-conversions/purchases, read 2026-10-14)
- The v1 purchases page defines ROAS as "sales / total cost". No ACOS field was found in the v1 metric pages read here. [official] (same page; Metrics library, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/library, read 2026-10-14). ACOS is the inverse of ROAS. The bulksheets page defines it as "Total spend divided by your total sales" [official] (Search term reports in bulksheets, above).
- GUIDANCE from the seat's reading: the window is not a choice cx makes. It follows the account type and the ad product. An effect-review that compares ad sales across products must say which window each figure carries. [seat]

### E. Freshness, restatement, retention

- RULE.
  - "Initial impression and click data is available using the API within 12 hours."
  - Clicks can change for up to three days through traffic validation.
  - "Initial conversion data is available within 24 hours".
  - "Restatements of conversion data occur 1, 7, and 28 days after the conversion event". With a 14-day window, "restatement can update attribution up to 42 days (14 plus 28) after the report date."

  [official] (Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3), above)
- GUIDANCE.
  - "The invalid traffic detection system monitors traffic data for up to 30 days after the fact".
  - "We recommend waiting at least 5 days for the most accurate click data to be available before evaluating campaign performance."
  - "Conversion data … is subject to change for up to 60 days back from the current date. We recommend re-retrieving reports monthly".

  [official] (Amazon Ads reporting overview, above)
- RULE (retention):
  - v3: search-term data 65 days for SP and 60 for SB; targeting and campaign data 95 days for SP.
  - v1, `date.value`: retention 24 months, maximum report range 120 days.
  - v1, `week.value`: retention 24 months, maximum range 15 months.
  - The extension from 15 to 24 months was announced August 26, 2026.

  [official] (Search term reports [v3]; Targeting reports, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/report-types/targeting; Campaign reports, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/report-types/campaign; Dates and historical data retention, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/dates; Amazon Ads API v1 release notes; all read 2026-10-14)
- v1 aggregates each day "based on each campaign timezone". [official] (Dates and historical data retention, above)

### G. Search term impression share and rank: the lead, corrected

The lead held that "search term impression share/rank" are metrics in the SP search term report. **The sources contradict this.**

- The v3 `spSearchTerm` column list has no impression-share or rank column (full list read). [official] (Search term reports [v3], above)
- The console has a separate report:
  - Search term impression share for Sponsored Products, March 22, 2021: "Download it from the Report Center". The page says nothing about API access.
  - The Sponsored Brands rank report, October 30, 2020, lists "Amazon Ads API and the Advertising console" as channels.

  [official] (Search term impression share report for Sponsored Products | Amazon Advertising, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/search-term-impression-report-sponsored-products; Introducing the search term impression rank report | Amazon Ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/search-term-rank-report; both read 2026-10-14)
- In v1 the figures are fields:
  - `metric.impressionShare`: "The percentage share of all your impressions compared to all other advertisers".
  - `metric.impressionShareRank`: "The numeric rank of your account-wide impression share".

  Both are for SP and SB, both require `searchTerm.value`, and both are *incompatible* with the ad, ad group, target, campaign and advertised-product dimensions. They are account-wide per search term and cannot be tied to one ASIN. [official] (Search (Impression share), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/impression-share/search, read 2026-10-14)
- Sponsored Brands keyword recommendations also return search-term impression share and rank over "last 7 days", "only available if your account has generated impressions for search terms matching the recommended keywords" (May 25, 2023 release note). [official] (All releases, above)

### K. What a Sponsored Products ad can target, and what auto-targeting reads

- RULE. A manual SP campaign needs "at least one targeting clause": keyword targeting or product targeting. "Targeting is assigned at the ad group level. All products in an ad group will share targeting clauses." "Product targeting can be applied at either the product, category, or brand level." [official] (Creating product and category targeting expressions, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/product-targeting/overview, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, product (ASIN) targeting. An ASIN target uses `matchType` `PRODUCT_EXACT` ("target a specific `ASIN` exactly") or `PRODUCT_SIMILAR` ("a specific `ASIN` and other similar `ASIN`s, including substitutes, complements, and other related products"). "You can only add one `ASIN` per targeting clause." [official] (same page). In the SP 3.0 model, the manual predicates include `ASIN_SAME_AS`, `ASIN_CATEGORY_SAME_AS`, `ASIN_BRAND_SAME_AS`, price and review-rating ranges (`ASIN_PRICE_BETWEEN`, `ASIN_REVIEW_RATING_BETWEEN`, and so on), `ASIN_IS_PRIME_SHIPPING_ELIGIBLE`, and `ASIN_EXPANDED_FROM`: "Target products similar in performance to the ASIN expressed." [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsCreateTargetingExpressionPredicateType`, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, category targeting. A category target takes a `productCategoryId` and optional refinements (brand, rating range and others). "When using refinements, you must first define the category." The tree of targetable categories comes from `GET /sp/targets/categories`, which "returns a large JSON string containing a tree of category nodes". `POST /sp/targets/categories/recommendations` "Returns a list of category recommendations for the input list of ASINs". [official] (Creating product and category targeting expressions; Sponsored Products product category targeting suggestions and refinements, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/product-targeting/category-suggestions-refinements; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; all read 2026-10-14)
- GUIDANCE, where a product target shows. "When targeting a product, your ad will be eligible for impressions on its product detail page, as well as search result pages where the targeted product appears among the top search results." Category targets can be refined "by product attributes, such as brand, price range, ratings, Prime shipping eligibility, and more." [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products | Amazon Ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, auto targeting. Each auto ad group gets "four auto targeting expressions" automatically. They cannot be created by hand, and each takes its own bid. The SP 3.0 model maps them to the console's names:
  - `QUERY_HIGH_REL_MATCHES` is "Close match": "search terms closely related to your products".
  - `QUERY_BROAD_REL_MATCHES` is "Loose match": "search terms loosely related to your products".
  - `ASIN_SUBSTITUTE_RELATED` is "Substitutes": shoppers on "detail pages of products similar to yours".
  - `ASIN_ACCESSORY_RELATED` is "Complements": shoppers who "view the detail pages of products that complement your product".

  The v1 Targets API returns them as `THEME` targets (for example `KEYWORDS_CLOSE_MATCH`). [official] (Sponsored Products auto targeting, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/auto-targeting; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsTargetingExpressionPredicateType`; both read 2026-10-14)
- GUIDANCE, what auto-targeting reads. Amazon gives one sentence on the inputs: "With automatic targeting your ad is automatically matched with keywords and products that are similar to the product you're advertising. It's based on previous shopping queries and your product information." Ads are "eligible to appear on Amazon shopping results pages, as well as product detail pages, based on multiple default strategies that we use on your behalf". Amazon names no listing field (title, bullets, search terms, attributes). It names shopping behaviour next to "product information". [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products, above)
- RULE, Amazon's other reads of "what this product is". Three recommendation endpoints take the advertised ASINs as input:
  - `POST /sp/targets/keywords/recommendations` "returns recommended keyword targets given either A) a list of ad ASINs or B) a campaign ID and ad group ID". Version 4 added search-term impression share and rank over "the last 30 days", "only available for keywords the advertiser targeted with ad impressions". Version 5 added theme-based bids.
  - `POST /sp/targets/products/recommendations` suggests ASINs to target using "historical performance of your ad, items that shoppers they frequently view and purchase together, etc." Its themes include "Top converting targets": ASINs that "generated conversions for the input ASIN in the past 30 days (e.g. your product appeared as an ad on the detail page of these items…)".
  - `POST /sp/targets/categories/recommendations` suggests categories.

  [official] (Sponsored Products keyword recommendations, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/keywords/suggestions; Sponsored Products product targeting recommendations, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/product-targeting/suggestions; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; all read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, keyword match types. Positive keywords take `BROAD`, `PHRASE` or `EXACT`. Amazon's guide defines them:
  - Broad: "A shopping query can contain the keyword terms in any order".
  - Phrase: "contain all components of the targeted keyword in the same order".
  - Exact: "matched word for word (same words and in the same order)".

  [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsKeywordMatchType`; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products)
- In the reports, an auto or product target's detail-page hits appear as ASIN-shaped "search terms" (§C). Amazon's guide tells advertisers to reuse them: "Targeted ASINs that generate clicks or conversions show up in the "search term" column … These can be added to product targeting campaigns as targets". The v1 Reporting API adds a dimension for the page itself: `matchedTarget.value`, "The product page where the ad appeared". It is compatible with `searchTerm.value`, the advertised-product dimensions and `placementClassification.value`. In v3 the matching column `matchedTargetAsin` exists only for Sponsored Display reports. [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; Matched target (Targeting), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/dimensions/targeting/matched-target; Search term (Targeting), …/dimensions/targeting/search-term; Reporting columns (version 3); all read 2026-10-14)

### L. The detail page as an ad slot: competitors' ads, and targeting your own

- GUIDANCE. Detail pages are an ad placement for every SP campaign type. Auto targeting's Substitutes and Complements groups place ads on other products' detail pages (§K). A product target makes an ad "eligible for impressions on its product detail page" (§K). `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE` is one of the SP placements a bid can be adjusted for (§N). So any seller's ad can appear on the seller's own listing if it targets that ASIN, its category or its brand. [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI)
- GUIDANCE, Amazon's own word on self-targeting. "Target your own product pages to showcase your product range: Targeting your own products and bidding higher on them can help you increase visibility for your range on your product pages." [official] (Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-products-best-practices, read 2026-10-14). The targeting guide lists campaign strategies: "Protect: focus on branded keywords and targeting" and "Conquest: target competitors' products". It also says "Consider bidding on your branded keyword as a brand defense strategy since your competitors may be bidding on your branded keywords, too." [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products)
- RULE, keeping your own ads off pages. Negative product targeting "allows you to exclude certain products and brands from your targeting" (predicates `ASIN_SAME_AS`, `ASIN_BRAND_SAME_AS`). Amazon's guide: "add a negative list of products to prevent ads from displaying on an ASIN's product page". A seller cannot stop a competitor's ad from showing on the seller's own page. The negative only steers the seller's own ads. [official] (Sponsored Products negative product and brand targeting overview, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/negative-targeting/product-brand; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; all read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, Sponsored Display on detail pages. Contextual targeting "helps promote your product among audiences who are actively browsing your product or similar products and categories with ads that appear on relevant product detail pages", now also "outside the Amazon store". It uses tactic `T00020` (contextual) or `T00030` (audiences). Amazon recommends `similarProduct` targeting, "dynamically generated using machine learning models that are built on retail data". [official] (Amazon Ads Sponsored Display contextual targeting API, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-display/contextual-targeting; Amazon Ads API for Sponsored Display overview, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-display/overview; both read 2026-10-14)
- **Not found on any Amazon page read:** the on-page names of the detail-page ad carousels ("Products related to this item", "Sponsored products related to this item", "Compare with similar items"), which ad product fills each, and a measure of how often a competitor's ad shows on a seller's page. Practitioners disagree on all three (below). No Amazon Ads report read here gives "share of my own detail page held by my ads". The v1 `matchedTarget.value` dimension tells the seller only where the seller's own ads appeared.

### M. Negative keywords and harvesting

- RULE. "Negative keywords help prevent ads from appearing on shopping results pages that do not meet your campaign objectives. Negative keyword targeting can be used at either the campaign level or ad group level." In SP 3.0 a negative keyword's match type is `NEGATIVE_EXACT`, `NEGATIVE_PHRASE` or `NEGATIVE_BROAD`. The endpoints are `/sp/negativeKeywords` and `/sp/campaignNegativeKeywords` (each with `/list`), and `/sp/negativeTargets` and `/sp/campaignNegativeTargets` for products and brands. In v1, all negatives are created through `POST /adsApi/v1/create/targets` with `negative` set to `true`. [official] (Negative keyword targeting, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/negative-targeting/keywords; Get started with auto campaigns, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/sponsored-products/get-started/auto-campaigns; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; all read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, with a conflict between Amazon pages. The negative-targeting guide warns: "Campaign-level negative product targeting for manual campaigns is only available for vendors. Sellers can add campaign-level negative targeting to auto campaigns, but not manual." The release note of May 22, 2025 says negatives "can now be saved on campaigns and ad group regardless of targeting type in US marketplace". Which page holds for a seller is open. [official] (Sponsored Products negative product and brand targeting overview; All releases, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/index; both read 2026-10-14)
- GUIDANCE, Amazon's harvesting thresholds:
  - "We recommend using phrase or exact match with negative keywords".
  - "Before deciding to add a keyword as a negative target, we recommend evaluating its performance after it has received at least 20 clicks."
  - "let your negative keywords run for two weeks or more, before making any changes to your strategy".
  - Candidates are "Low-performing keywords", "Shopping terms you don't want your ads to appear against", and, for product targets, "Non-complementary ASINs in an ad group".

  [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products, above)
- GUIDANCE, the harvest workflow is Amazon's own. The auto campaign exists partly so that "You want to identify new keywords that drive clicks and sales that you can to add to your manual campaigns" [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products). The Amazon Ads MCP Server's open-beta announcement lists a workflow that will "automate keyword harvesting from automatic to manual campaigns" [official] (All releases, entry of February 18, 2026). That workflow tool was not found among the 32 tool paths of the published MCP campaign-management specification read the same day (`https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/mcp/campaign_management.json`).
- GUIDANCE, listing-side use of the same report. In its listing guide Amazon Ads does point the search-term report at listing text, which corrects the claim in §C that no Amazon page does: "Leverage the search term report … to find queries shoppers use to find your products and add these to your product title, bullet points, and/or product description." [official] (How to improve your product detail page for advertising | Amazon Ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/improve-your-products-for-advertising, read 2026-10-14). No Amazon page read treats the reverse, irrelevant terms that need negatives, as evidence about the listing's text.
- Conflict on report scope. Amazon's best-practice guide says the console's search term report shows terms "that resulted in at least one click on your ad". The API FAQ (§C) says the console report holds all terms with an impression. Amazon's two pages disagree, so cx reads the header and contents of whatever file it is given. [official] (Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3))

### N. Bidding strategies and placement modifiers

- RULE. A SP campaign's `dynamicBidding.strategy` takes one of these values, defined in the model:
  - `LEGACY_FOR_SALES`, "Dynamic bids - down only": "Lowers your bids in real time when your ad may be less likely to convert to a sale."
  - `AUTO_FOR_SALES`, "Dynamic bids - up and down": "Increases or decreases your bids in real time by a maximum of 100%. With this setting bids increase when your ad is more likely to convert to a sale, and bids decrease when less likely to convert to a sale."
  - `MANUAL`, "Fixed bid": "Uses your exact bid and any placement adjustments you set, and is not subject to dynamic bidding."
  - `RULE_BASED`, "Rule based bidding".

  [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsBiddingStrategy`, read 2026-10-14)
- GUIDANCE, Amazon on up-and-down. "we will increase your bids in real time to help win impressions that may be more likely to convert to a sale, such as when your ad appears for a highly relevant shopping query." Amazon says "Ensure you are prepared to potentially spend double the amount of the bid you input", and "We recommend that you limit your changes while testing strategies, so that you can attribute the difference in performance to a specific change." [official] (Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down with Sponsored Products | Amazon Ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/dynamic-bidding-sponsored-products, read 2026-10-14). The targeting guide recommends "dynamic bidding up and down to help maximize performance" for auto campaigns, or "dynamic down only" when optimizing for ROAS. [official] (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products)
- **Amazon's pages disagree on the ceiling.** The up-and-down guide and the model say up to 100% "for all placements". Amazon's author guide says "Bids will be increased by up to 100% for placements at the top of the first page of shopping results and by up to 50% for all other placements." [official] (Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; The author's guide to Sponsored Products, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/authors-guide-to-sponsored-products, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, rule-based bidding. "For any existing campaign running for at least 10 days, you can apply a rule with a guardrail of ROAS and Amazon Ads may then adjust your base bids up and down to increase conversions while maintaining your guardrails." The campaign needs "a minimum of 10 conversions in the last 30 days" and a minimum daily budget per marketplace. The guardrail "is based on the previous 21 days". Amazon disables the rule if the campaign's last-20-day ROAS "drops to less than 30% of your ROAS over a 20-to-40-day period". [official] (Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/overview, read 2026-10-14)
- RULE, placement modifiers. `dynamicBidding.placementBidding` holds a `percentage` (0–900) per `placement`: `PLACEMENT_TOP` ("Top of search (first page)"), `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE` ("Product pages"), `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH` ("Rest of the search") or `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`. The model's example: "a 50% Top of Search adjustment on a $1.00 bid would increase the bid to $1.50". A separate `shopperCohortBidding` adjustment multiplies on top: a further "100% adjustment would further increase the bid to $3.00". [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsPlacement`, `SponsoredProductsDynamicBidding`). Amazon's best-practice guide: "Your final cost-per-click will be based on your adjusted bid plus additional factors, but will never exceed your maximum adjusted bid." [official] (Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads)
- For Sponsored Brands, top-of-search and rest-of-search adjustments up to 900% arrived on June 19, 2025. Negative SB placement adjustments were deprecated, with the shutoff moved to June 15, 2026. [official] (All releases)

## What practitioners add

- **SQP is paid plus organic.**
  - Perpetua (Martin Saunders, "Amazon Search Query Performance Report: tapping into the superpowers of search query data", 2024-10-16): in its definition of SQP impressions, "It includes both organic and Sponsored Products ads but excludes ASINs displayed in widgets". The same article lists the widget exclusion, which the Amazon schema read here does not state either. [opinion]
  - A seller in the Seller Forums thread "Organic sales by keyword": SQP "will include both organic and ppc sales (once you start advertising)". [opinion]

  Amazon's SQP help page says the same (§I, gap fill): organic and Sponsored Products from the search results page, widgets excluded. Perpetua's widget exclusion matches Amazon's wording.
- **Cannibalization test.** Perpetua (same article): if your sales share holds after you cut spend on a keyword, "your Sponsored Products ad was likely cannibalizing your organic sales". Test one keyword at a time. A flat trend "suggests that you may not need to spend on Sponsored Products ads for that specific keyword". [opinion]
- **Keyword mapping.** Perpetua (same article) suggests "mapping the relevant Search Query Performance terms with the targeted keywords in your existing PPC campaigns" to find terms not yet targeted. [opinion]
- **Harvesting, PPC side.** Helium 10 (Lauren Stair, "Reclaim 20+ Hours Per Week: How to Automate Amazon PPC Management", 2026-04-02) gives an example rule: promote "any search term generating two orders" "with ACoS below 30%" to exact match, after reviewing "the past 30-60 days". The article says nothing about the listing. [opinion]
- **Harvesting, listing side.** Scale Insights ("How to Use Amazon Search Term Report for PPC", undated): "Update titles, bullet points, and descriptions to match high-converting search terms", and "Insert pertinent keywords in the backend of your product listing to improve organic searchability". It gives no thresholds and no waiting period. [opinion]
- **TACoS.** Jungle Scout (Brian Connolly, "What Is Amazon TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)?", 2023-05-24): TACoS = ad spend ÷ total revenue × 100. A falling TACoS means "your organic sales are increasing". As ad sales rise, "the higher your Amazon Best Sellers Rank and organic keyword ranking will be". The article offers that as illustration, not data. [opinion]
- **Ads help organic, said with a hedge.** SellerApp (Ankitha Nagaraj, "How Can Amazon Advertising Catapult Your Organic Ranking on SERP?", 2026-10-06): A9 "takes multiple signals like the number of clicks, CVR, sales velocity through targeted ads". It concedes that "Amazon doesn't explicitly say" external traffic raises organic rank. It is a promotional piece, and its case-study timelines are anecdotes. [opinion]
- **The results page is mostly paid.**
  - Marketplace Pulse (Juozas Kaziukėnas, "Amazon Is Burying Organic Search Results", 2022-03-09): "Of the first twenty products a shopper sees when searching on Amazon, only four are organic results."
  - The same author ("Amazon Demotes Organic Results in Search", 2019-10-30): organic results start "in the sixth row or later" for many searches, and ranking "heavily weights product reviews and sales velocity". That last part is his reading, not Amazon's word.

  [opinion]
- **Where they disagree.**
  - Jungle Scout and SellerApp present ad sales as lifting organic rank.
  - Perpetua treats an ad on a term where the ASIN already ranks as possibly cannibalizing organic sales.
  - Both can be true on different terms, and neither cites a measurement.
  - Harvest thresholds (two orders, 30% ACoS, 30–60 days) are one vendor's example. No two vendors give the same thresholds.
  - Practitioners commonly want harvested terms in the listing. Amazon's own stated use of the report is mainly targeting and negatives (§C), but its listing guide also says to add found queries to the title, bullets or description (§M).

## Published research

- **Yang & Ghose, "Analyzing the Relationship Between Organic and Sponsored Search Advertising: Positive, Negative, or Zero Interdependence?"**, *Marketing Science*, Articles in Advance 2010 (doi 10.1287/mksc.1100.0552).
  - Data: Google search, one nationwide retailer, several hundred keywords, three months.
  - Finding: "click-throughs on organic listings have a positive interdependence with click-throughs on paid listings, and vice versa", and the effect is asymmetric. A controlled field experiment found total CTR, conversion and revenue higher with both listings present.
  - Transfer: weak. This is web search, not a marketplace. It does suggest that an organic and a paid listing for the same query are not simply additive. [research]
- **Blake, Nosko & Tadelis, "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment"**, *Econometrica* 83 (2015) 155–174 (NBER WP 20171, 2014).
  - eBay experiments on Google and other search engines.
  - Brand-keyword ads showed no measurable short-term benefit. Non-experimental estimates overstate returns "Because search clicks and purchase behavior are correlated". For non-brand terms, frequent users, whose purchases ads did not change, carried most of the spend.
  - Transfer: attributed sales are not incremental sales. This applies directly to how cx reads "ad sales" on brand-name terms. The setting is not Amazon. [research]
- **Moshary, "Does Sponsored Search Advertising Augment Organic Search? Evidence from an E-Commerce Platform"**, *Management Science* 71(11) (2025) 9687–9709.
  - A holdout blocked all sponsored search ads for 3% of visitors on an unnamed e-commerce platform.
  - Sponsored ads "steals business from organic listings" and lowered total transactions.
  - Transfer: the closest marketplace setting found. It speaks to the platform's total, not one seller's organic rank. [research]
- **Joo, Shi & Abhishek, "Do Sellers Benefit from Sponsored Product Listings? Evidence from an Online Marketplace"**, *Marketing Science* 43(4) (2024) 817–839.
  - Field study on a mobile-app marketplace.
  - "consumers prefer organic listings in the top-ranked positions to sponsored listings of the same product/position", and consumers are indifferent lower down. A simulation found a seller can still gain by replacing a low organic slot with a top sponsored one.
  - Transfer: a top-of-search ad and a top organic slot are not equivalent exposures. Not Amazon. [research]
- **Dash, Ghosh, Mukherjee, Chakraborty & Gummadi, "Sponsored is the New Organic: Implications of Sponsored Results on Quality of Search Results in the Amazon Marketplace"**, AIES 2024 (arXiv 2407.19099).
  - 4,800 simulated Amazon searches in four countries.
  - Products ranked beyond 100th organically appeared as sponsored results above the top organic ones.
  - Transfer: on Amazon a product's paid exposure for a query can be unrelated to its organic rank for that query. Audit data; no causal claim about rank. [research]
- **Ju, Zhao & Aral, "Advertising Spillovers in Mobile Apps: Evidence from Ad Shutoffs and Store Rankings"**, arXiv 2504.16151, 2025, revised 2026. A preprint, not peer-reviewed.
  - A global install-ad shutoff cut organic installs by about 20–30%.
  - The spillover runs through store rankings: "paid installs boost app store category rankings".
  - Transfer: it shows the mechanism practitioners claim for Amazon (paid sales → rank → organic sales) in another store with sales-driven rankings. It is not Amazon evidence. [research]
- **Sorokina & Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The joy of ranking products"**, SIGIR 2016 (Amazon Science).
  - The abstract describes a machine-learning ranking framework and names no inputs.
  - Nothing Amazon has published, as far as this seat read, confirms that ad-attributed sales feed organic rank. [research]

- **Angrist & Pischke, *Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion***, Princeton University Press, 2009, §3.2.3 "Bad Control" (section title from the book's contents page; the text of the section was not opened here). The passage as quoted by Cinelli, Forney & Pearl (below), citing Angrist and Pischke (2009, p.64): "Bad controls are variables that are themselves outcome variables in the notional experiment at hand. That is, bad controls might just as well be dependent variables too. Good controls are variables that we can think of having been fixed at the time the regressor of interest was determined." [research] (gap fill)
- **Cinelli, Forney & Pearl, "A Crash Course in Good and Bad Controls"**, UCLA Technical Report R-493, revised March 2022, marked "Forthcoming, Journal Sociological Methods and Research".
  - When Z is a mediator (X → Z → Y), "Controlling for Z will block the very effect we want to estimate (the total effect of X on Y ), thus biasing our estimates (this is usually known as "overcontrol bias")."
  - If the target is instead the controlled direct effect, "the effect of X while holding Z constant by intervention", then adjusting for Z can be appropriate. An unobserved common cause of Z and Y, however, biases that estimate as well.
  - Transfer to this package: when a listing change (X) moves Amazon's predicted conversion, which moves dynamic bids and so ad impressions (Z), which move sessions and orders (Y), the ad response is a mediator. Subtracting it measures something other than the change's total effect. Holding bids fixed is the "by intervention" route. The mapping to Amazon's bidding is the seat's. [research] (gap fill)

Taken together [seat]: paid exposure can substitute for organic sales (Blake; Moshary), complement it (Yang & Ghose), or feed rank (Ju et al., another platform). The sign depends on the term and the setting. The research does not let effect-review assume either direction. It supports treating ad changes as a confounder to remove, not as a known adjustment.

## Stable enough for a skill

1. The Amazon Ads API is a separate API from SP-API, with its own Login with Amazon client, approval and profile scope. An SP-API connection does not reach campaigns, keywords or search-term reports. (Amazon Ads API onboarding overview; Getting started with reports (version 3); SP-API models repository)
2. The API search-term report lists only search terms that got at least one ad click. The console's report also lists terms that had impressions without a click. (Search term reports [v3]; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3))
3. Ad-attributed purchases are counted within a look-back after the click and dated on the click's day. The window depends on the ad product and account type and lives in Amazon's metric definitions. (Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Reporting columns (version 3))
4. Ad-attributed sales can include products other than the advertised one (other SKU / halo). Same-SKU or "promoted" figures are reported separately. (Reporting columns (version 3); Overview of the Reporting v1 API)
5. Recent ad figures are provisional: clicks are validated after the fact and conversions are restated afterward. A window's ad figures settle only some time after it closes; the lag lives in Amazon's reporting pages. (Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); Amazon Ads reporting overview)
6. Search-term impression share and rank are account-wide per search term. They cannot be tied to one ASIN or ad group. (Search (Impression share))
7. Top-of-search impression share is a campaign-level or keyword/target-level figure, not a search-term figure. (Reporting columns (version 3); Targeting reports; All releases; Search (Impression share))
8. Sponsored Products ads appear in on-Amazon placements (today named top of search, rest of search and product pages; the list is the model's placement enum, read at run time), and also off Amazon. (All releases; Sponsored Products - Help increase product sales; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI)
9. SP-API's Data Kiosk Economics dataset reports ad spend per product, at the aggregation its schema allows, without an Ads connection. It does not report terms, clicks or ad-attributed sales. Which role it needs is read from the query's result, because Amazon's pages disagree. (analytics_economics_2024_03_15 schema; Data Kiosk reporting suite launches to general availability; Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements; Data Kiosk API)
10. Brand Analytics' search-query counts come from search-results-page activity, include Sponsored Products results, exclude search-page widgets, and are not split into paid and organic. Amazon advises against comparing them with figures of the same name in advertising dashboards. (Search Query Performance dashboard help page; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport schema; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport schema)
11. An Amazon community manager, answering in the Seller Forums, said Amazon has no tool that gives organic sales by keyword. (Organic sales by keyword, Amazon staff replies)
12. v3 reporting stops delivering data on a date Amazon has announced; the replacements are the Reporting API v1 (ad-hoc reports) and Amazon Marketing Stream v2 (which Amazon calls its primary programmatic reporting surface). Which surface is live is read from Amazon's deprecation page, not remembered. (Deprecations; Amazon Ads reporting overview)
13. A Sponsored Products ad can target keywords, single ASINs, categories (with refinements such as brand, price and rating) and brands. An auto campaign targets the groups the model defines for it (today close match, loose match, substitutes and complements). Substitutes and complements place ads on other products' detail pages. (Creating product and category targeting expressions; Sponsored Products auto targeting; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI)
14. Amazon says auto-targeting matches ads using "previous shopping queries and your product information". It does not say which listing fields it reads. (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products)
15. Any advertiser can target a seller's ASIN, category or brand, so competitors' ads can appear on the seller's own detail page. Negative targets keep only the seller's own ads off pages. Amazon suggests targeting one's own product pages to showcase the range. (A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads; Sponsored Products negative product and brand targeting overview)
16. Negative keywords exist at campaign and ad-group level in exact, phrase and broad forms. Amazon recommends phrase or exact negatives and judging a term only after a minimum number of clicks stated in its guide. (Negative keyword targeting; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products)
17. A Sponsored Products campaign's bidding strategy is one of the values of the model's strategy enum, read at run time (today down only, up and down, rule based and fixed bid). Down only and up and down change the bid in real time with Amazon's estimate of how likely a click is to convert, rule-based bidding adjusts bids against a ROAS guardrail, and only the fixed-bid strategy uses the advertiser's exact bid. (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview; Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down)
18. Placement adjustments raise the bid by a percentage for top of search, rest of search, product pages or Amazon Business. Their range and the dynamic-bid ceiling live in the model and Amazon's guides, and the guides disagree on the dynamic ceiling. (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down; The author's guide to Sponsored Products)
19. A product must be in stock and able to be the Featured Offer to be advertised. An ad whose product is not the Featured Offer has its own serving and eligibility status (`NOT_IN_BUYBOX`) in the Ads API. (How to improve your product detail page for advertising; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; Amazon Ads API v1 OpenAPI; Eligibility 3.0 OpenAPI; Sponsored Products and Buy Box, staff reply)
20. A Sponsored Products ad shows the listing's title, image, price (and list price), and the Prime badge when the offer has Prime shipping. Changing those listing elements changes the ad as well. (How to improve your product detail page for advertising)
21. The Search Query Performance dashboard includes organic and Sponsored Products activity from the search results page, so an ad change can move its impressions, clicks and purchases. (Search Query Performance dashboard help page)
22. Traffic from outside Amazon to a listing is measured, when tagged, by Amazon Attribution, which is a separate product available to brand-registered sellers in listed markets. (Amazon Attribution API overview)

## What cx must read at run time

The package states none of the following figures: windows, retention, ranges, look-backs, deprecation dates, rate limits. cx reads them where they live.

**When the seller's Ads data reaches the tree** (a future Ads source, the Amazon Ads MCP server, or a file the seller downloads from the console and places in the tree):
- Search terms: v1 `POST /adsApi/v1/create/reports` with fields that include `searchTerm.value`, `target.value`, `target.matchType`, the advertised-product dimension (its field id is on Amazon's dimension page; not read here) and the conversion metrics, then `POST /adsApi/v1/retrieve/reports`. Before the v3 shutoff: `POST /reporting/reports` with `reportTypeId` `spSearchTerm` (and `sbSearchTerm`), then `GET /reporting/reports/{reportId}`. Report shapes, waits and paces belong to whatever door carries Ads. Today none exists, and `door.md` is the SP-API door's.
- Placements: v3 `spCampaigns` with `groupBy` `campaignPlacement`, or v1 `placementClassification.value`.
- Share: v1 `metric.impressionShare`, `metric.impressionShareRank` and `metric.topOfSearchImpressionShare`, with the dimensions their pages allow.
- Freshness and window: Amazon's reporting overview and FAQ pages for how long to wait before calling a window's ad figures final. The v1 metric page for the look-back that applies to this account.
- A console download: the file's own header row and date range. The bulk-sheet search-term tab has its own fixed look-back, stated on Amazon's bulksheets page.

**From the SP-API door today, no Ads connection** (operation names here; report and query names, options and waits are door.md's):
- Per-product daily ad spend: Data Kiosk `createQuery` on the Economics dataset's `ads` field, then `getQuery`/`getDocument`. The role is read from the `createQuery` result against the seller's granted roles: the correction changelog says Selling Partner Insights, the Data Kiosk API page lists Brand Analytics, and Data Kiosk checks roles per field at query creation (§I).
- Per-query total funnel: the Search Query Performance report through `createReport`/`getReport`/`getReportDocument` (door.md's type and options), or the Data Kiosk seller-analytics `search.query` group (fields gated by PAAM rights; access read from the query result, §I). The SQP request's period bounds (WEEK, MONTH, QUARTER and their first and last days) are read from the SQP report schema.
- Sponsored Products invoice charges: Finances v0 `listFinancialEvents` (and the by-group and by-order variants), whose `FinancialEvents` include `ProductAdsPaymentEventList`. These are account-level only. The prior SP-API report says these v0 reads are deprecated, with a removal date. That claim is a lead this seat did not re-read.

**Added by the gap fill, when an Ads source exists.** These are operation names only. Ceilings, ranges and thresholds are read from the model at run time.
- Bidding state of every campaign advertising the changed SKU, read at the baseline's start and again at the window's end: SP 3.0 `POST /sp/campaigns/list` gives `dynamicBidding.strategy`, `dynamicBidding.placementBidding[]` and `shopperCohortBidding[]`. The v1 equivalent is a query on the Campaigns object. Rules come from `POST /sp/rules/optimization/search` (optimization rules) and `GET /sp/campaigns/{campaignId}/budgetRules` (budget rules).

The v1 equivalent is `status.deliveryStatus` and `status.deliveryReasons`. Then `POST /eligibility/product/list` (MCP: `check_product_eligibility`) for `NOT_IN_BUYBOX`, `LISTING_SUPRESSED`, `MISSING_TITLE`, `MISSING_IMAGE` and `OUT_OF_STOCK`.
- What targets and negatives the ads ran under: `POST /sp/targets/list` (auto groups and product/category expressions), `POST /sp/keywords/list`, `POST /sp/negativeKeywords/list`, `POST /sp/campaignNegativeKeywords/list`, `POST /sp/negativeTargets/list` and `POST /sp/campaignNegativeTargets/list`.
- Amazon's own read of what the product is, used as a listing-review input: `POST /sp/targets/keywords/recommendations` with `recommendationType` for a list of the seller's ASINs, `POST /sp/targets/products/recommendations` and `POST /sp/targets/categories/recommendations`.
- Search terms per advertised product and per page: a v1 report with `searchTerm.value`, the advertised-product (or SKU) dimension, `targetingMatchType.value`, `matchedTarget.value` and `placementClassification.value`. Each of these dimension pages lists the others as compatible.

**From the SP-API door, for the Featured Offer gate** (door.md's operation and report names): the seller's Featured Offer share and current Featured Offer state for the window, read before any ad or content explanation is offered.

**From Keepa:** no ad field. Keepa's coupon, deal and price histories are the other promotional confounders (`couponHistory` and the lightning-deal price type in Keepa's product-file reference).

**From the seller:** whether bids, budgets, campaigns, targets or negatives changed in the baseline or observation window. That answer is recorded in the change-log file as the seller's own words.

## Open questions

1. ~~**Does SQP include sponsored impressions, clicks and purchases?**~~ **Settled by the gap fill:** yes. Amazon's SQP help page (G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, version 5) says the dashboard "includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page" and excludes widgets (§I). Still open: whether SQP *purchases* count a Sponsored Products click that converted later through another path. A first real run can check this: compare one query's SQP purchases with the same week's paid search-term orders.
2. **Is a paid–organic split per term defensible?** "SQP minus paid" mixes populations. The ad figures are dated on the click with a look-back window, include other-SKU purchases, and include product-page placements whose term is `*`. SQP counts results-page activity per reporting period. The schema gives `WEEK`, `MONTH` or `QUARTER`, and (corrected by the gap fill) a WEEK must start on a Sunday and end on a Saturday, "or else a fatal error returns". So an ad report for the same week must use the same Sunday–Saturday dates in the campaign's time zone. Amazon's SQP help page itself advises against the comparison: dashboards "may attribute and define customer actions in different ways, we do not recommend comparing the metric names of data across different dashboards" (§I). To settle: one real week of both reports for one ASIN, compared term by term. Until then, any "organic estimate" is labelled an estimate: a practitioner method, not Amazon's.
3. **Does the v1 Reporting API carry an ACOS field?** No ACOS field was found in the v1 metric pages read (Purchases; Costs, which lists only cost fields such as `metric.totalCost`). To settle: the remaining v1 metrics-library family pages.

## Positions

1. **P-paid-keywords-1.** listing-review treats "a paid search term that converts but whose words the listing does not contain" as a keyword-gap trigger. It reads that from the seller's search-term data, never from memory of what usually converts, and ties a term to a listing through the ad group's advertised products, because the search-term report itself carries no advertised-ASIN column. (Search term reports [v3]; Search term reports in bulksheets; Perpetua, Amazon Search Query Performance Report …; Scale Insights, How to Use Amazon Search Term Report for PPC)
2. **P-paid-keywords-2.** When cx reads an API search-term report, it says the report lists only terms with at least one ad click. Absence from that report is not evidence that a term showed no impressions. (Search term reports [v3]; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3))
3. **P-paid-keywords-3.** cx ignores `*` rows and ASIN-shaped search terms when it looks for words for the listing's text. These are product-page placements and product targets, not shopper queries. (Search term reports [v3]; Search term reports in bulksheets)
4. **P-paid-keywords-4.** cx computes CTR, conversion rate, CPC and ACOS from the report's counts and spend. It does not trust a precomputed rate column, because Amazon's own bulksheets page defines two of them inverted. (Search term reports in bulksheets; Reporting columns (version 3))
5. **P-paid-keywords-5.** Every ad-attributed figure cx quotes names its look-back window and whether it counts only the advertised SKU. Windows differ by ad product and account type, and include other-SKU and halo sales. (Reporting columns (version 3); Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Purchases (Amazon retail conversions))
6. **P-paid-keywords-6.** effect-review does not treat the most recent days' ad-attributed figures as final. Clicks are validated after the fact, and conversions are restated for weeks on Amazon's stated schedule, so cx reads that schedule from Amazon's reporting pages. (Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); Amazon Ads reporting overview)
7. **P-paid-keywords-7.** listing-change-log records, for each word a change bets on, whether that word had paid search-term rows in the baseline window. effect-review can then reconcile the bet against the same term's paid rows in the observation window. (Search term reports [v3]; roadmap §3, internal)
8. **P-paid-keywords-8.** effect-review lists, among the confounders it checks before a verdict, any change in ad spend, bids, budgets, campaign state, targets or negatives during the baseline or observation window. Paid exposure can substitute for organic sales, complement them, or feed rank. (Moshary 2025; Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015; Yang & Ghose 2010; Ju, Zhao & Aral 2025; Perpetua, Amazon Search Query Performance Report …)

**The seat's own (not for a skill)**

- S1. A term with high paid conversion but a low purchase share in SQP (which includes Sponsored Products, P-paid-keywords-11) is the strongest gap candidate. A term where the ASIN already holds a high SQP purchase share and also buys top-of-search may be cannibalizing. This combines Perpetua's test with SQP shares; no source states the combination.
- S2. Brand-name terms in the paid report should not count as listing-gap evidence. Blake et al. suggest their ad sales are largely not incremental, and their words are usually in the listing already.
- S3. Without an Ads source, an effect smaller than the swing a plausible bid change could cause should lean to "cannot tell". No source gives a size for such a swing, so this is not a rule.
- S4. Because ad figures are dated on the click day and restate for weeks, an effect-review verdict that uses them is best written after the restatement period closes on the observation window, not on the window's last day.
- S5. The Ads source, when built, might be Amazon's own MCP server rather than a new door. That trades a gated `cx raw` record for Amazon-maintained tools, which is a product question.
- S6 (gap fill). Down-only bidding is a mediator too. It lowers bids less often once clicks are judged likelier to convert, so a conversion gain can still raise effective bids and impressions. Only `MANUAL` is defined as "not subject to dynamic bidding".
- S7 (gap fill). Even fixed bids do not hold ad *exposure* constant. Competitors' bids, the auction and Amazon's relevance judgement can all respond to the listing change or to the season. Fixed bids remove Amazon's automatic bid response, not every ad-side path. No Amazon page read describes the auction's use of listing quality.
- S8 (gap fill). The earlier positions P-paid-keywords-8, 10 and 14 (and other files' instructions to treat ad changes as confounders) should be read with P-17: a *manual* ad change is a confounder, while an *automatic* bid response is part of the effect. A practical split: confounders are entries in the seller's or the tool's change history, and mediators are movements with no human edit behind them.
- S9 (gap fill). The surest sign that a fall was ads and not copy is the product ad's serving status or eligibility reason (`NOT_IN_BUYBOX`, `OUT_OF_STOCK`) during the window. Without an Ads source, the nearest proxy is the SP-API's Featured Offer share for the SKU.
- S10 (gap fill). Auto close-match terms reflect shopper behaviour as well as text. A title change can shift them only slowly while behavioural associations persist, so a before/after comparison of matched terms needs weeks, not days. No source gives the lag.
