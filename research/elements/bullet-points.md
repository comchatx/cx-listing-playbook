## What this covers

**Stage: conversion first, exposure second.** On the detail page, bullets (Seller Central's "Bullet points", the schema's `bullet_point`, shown under the heading "About this item") are the shopper's quickest way to check whether the product fits. Amazon's own page says "Well-crafted bullet points can increase product discoverability in search results and can improve conversion rates", so bullets also count toward exposure. Amazon also reserves the right not to use all of that text for search retrieval (both below). For click-through, bullets are at most marginal. Amazon says Item Highlights, not bullets, sit "below titles in search results". No Amazon page read places bullets in the result tile. One practitioner (Flywheel, 2018) says results "will sometimes show" a matching bullet; that is unverified.

**Skills fed.**
- **listing-review**: which stage bullets serve; what "good" looks like in Amazon's own answers (`issues` empty for `bullet_point`; the Catalog's `bullet_point` equal to what was submitted); the content rules a draft is checked against; where the live limits live (the product-type schema); where current bullets are read for our SKU (Listings Items, Catalog Items) and for competitors (Catalog Items, Keepa `features`). `references/writing.md` gets the bullet-specific consequences of its three facts. A bullet edit is a whole-attribute replace. Count, length and unit live in the schema. Amazon may remove or AI-rewrite non-compliant bullets.
- **listing-change-log**: a bullet change records the whole before and after arrays, in order, and the words bet on in each bullet.
- **effect-review**: a bullet change is judged first on conversion (unit session percentage) and orders. Exposure for the words bet on comes second, via Search Query Performance. Manage Your Experiments is Amazon's own A/B test for bullets. Where the seller is eligible, it beats any before/after read.

**Why a senior operator cares.** Bullets are the cheapest content lever on the page: text only, editable at any time, no image hosting. That also makes them where most self-inflicted damage enters. The common sources are keyword stuffing, claims that trigger policy (e.g. "anti-microbial", "eco-friendly", "made from bamboo"), guarantee wording, emojis, and ALL CAPS headers. Since 2024-08-15 Amazon has said it will remove such content and may replace it with its own AI-written bullets. The two beta runs show the gap. On day 2, cx proposed reordering all five bullets and adding expectation-setting copy for a refund problem. It had never read the `bullet_point` attribute or the product-type schema, and it never wrote the bullet patch it promised (beta-browser run report, 2026-10-14, item D and the "what cx needed to be told" list, points 7 and 8).

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### Rules: enforced (schema, policy, removal, suppression)

**R1. The content Amazon removes from bullets.** The current page lists the following under "Prohibited content", with the note "Bullet points that contain the following information may be removed or updated":
- Special characters: "™, ®, €, …, †, ‡, o, ¢, £, ¥, ©, ±, ~, â".
- Emojis: "☺, ☹, ✅, ❌".
- Placeholder text: "not applicable," "NA," "n/a," "N/A," "not eligible," "yet to decide," "to be decided," "TBD," "copy pending".
- ASINs.
- Prohibited claims: "eco-friendly," "environmentally friendly," "ecologically friendly," "anti-microbial," "anti-bacterial," "made from bamboo," "contains bamboo," "made from soy," "contains soy," with a pointer to General Listing Restrictions.
- Guarantee information: "Full refund," "If not satisfied, send it back," "Unconditional guarantee with no limit".
- External information: "Company information, website links, external hyperlinks, contact information".
- Repetitive content: "Each bullet point must mention unique product information".
- All caps: "ELEGANT DESIGN," "MACHINE WASHABLE".
- Abbreviations: "qty," "pkg," "w/," "approx.".
- Content for dedicated fields: "Fabric content, care instructions, and country of origin. These are populated in separate fields, so don't repeat them in bullet points."

[official] (Product bullet point requirements, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GX5L8BF8GLMML6CX, read 2026-10-14; the hub reports revision 8, status Live, parent "Listing quality guidelines")

Note for an operator: the all-caps row bans the common "ALL-CAPS HEADER: text" bullet style that several practitioner guides still recommend (see the practitioners section).

**R2. Detail-page rules that bind bullets by name.** "None of the following are allowed in product detail page titles, descriptions, bullet points, or images". The list includes "Phone numbers, addresses, email addresses, or website URLs", "Details of availability, price, or condition", "Links to other websites for placing orders, or alternative shipping offers, such as free shipping", "Reviews, quotes, or testimonials", "Requests for positive customer reviews", "Adverts, promotional material…", "Time-sensitive information", "Brand-specific information… on a 'generic' product listing" and "Restricted phrases such as 'FSA/HSA eligible'". On capitals: "Do not use all capital letters throughout the attribute. This applies to product titles, bullet points, and product descriptions. Use of emojis, emoticons, or symbols at the beginning of each word is prohibited." The page also sets: "Product titles, product descriptions, and bullet points must be clearly written and help customers understand the product", "Ensure that the product detail page outlines any additional items that are essential to the function of the product", and "You must not use product detail pages to cross-merchandise or cross-promote a product". No HTML or code may be used ("As a special case, you can use line breaks </br> in the description", in the description only). Editing is bounded: "You may only update detail pages to better or more accurately describe the product as originally listed". Repeatedly adding and removing prohibited claims "to circumvent this policy" may lead to "the immediate termination of selling privileges". [official] (Product detail page rules, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640, read 2026-10-14)

**R3. Claims need proof.** Claims "about a product's performance, composition, ingredients, or origin must be backed by verifiable evidence and documentation". Superlatives and comparisons such as "best", "100% pure" and "guaranteed" "should only be used when supported by verifiable and objective proof". Disease claims (treat, cure, mitigate, prevent) are prohibited without FDA approval. The policy "applies to claims on all product detail pages, including images, descriptions, packaging and marketing materials". [official] (Misleading and prohibited claims, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202024200, read 2026-10-14) General Listing Restrictions adds that environmental claims must follow the FTC Green Guides. It gives "Is anti-microbial", "Is anti-bacterial" and "Is made of a particular material, such as cashmere, bamboo or leather" as claims that "need supporting proof". [official] (General Listing Restrictions, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201707070, read 2026-10-14) The bullet page's own guidance row says the same thing in short form: "Avoid subjective, performance, or comparative claims unless they're verifiable on packaging. Avoid unsubstantiated award claims or consumer survey results." It also says "Do not compare your product to competitor brands." [official] (Product bullet point requirements)

**R4. The 2024 announcement, which is where the AI rewrite comes from.** On 2024-07-31 (forum timestamp; posted by News_Amazon), Amazon announced: "Effective August, 15, 2024, we're updating our existing product bullet point requirements". Key changes were "Restriction of special characters, emojis, and some phrases, such as refund-related guarantees" and guidance for "clear and concise" bullets. On enforcement: "In addition to our existing review processes, we'll use generative artificial intelligence (AI) to help us optimize listing quality. We'll remove non-compliant content and use AI to generate compliant, high-quality bullet points. We'll share any AI-generated quality-related improvements with you for review before we publish them to your listings." The announcement states no character limit. [official] (Review updated bullet point requirements to optimize your listings, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/65f8e647-977d-49ac-9036-2049b96720b2, read 2026-10-14) Amazon does not say where the AI suggestions are shown for review, or for how long. See Open questions.

**R5. Count and length are product-type data, enforced by the schema.** The current bullet page says, under "Character length": "Bullet point character limits vary by product type." Under "Bullet point count" it says "Include at least three bullet points." It gives no maximum count and no maximum length. [official] (Product bullet point requirements) The schema is "an extension of the JSON Schema Draft 2019-09", with custom keywords `maxUniqueItems`, `minUniqueItems`, `maxUtf8ByteLength`, `minUtf8ByteLength` and `selectors`. `selectors` "define the combination of properties that make an object unique". `maxUtf8ByteLength` measures a string "in UTF-8 bytes", so a limit may be in characters (`maxLength`) or in bytes, depending on which keyword the schema uses. If a client ignores the custom vocabulary, "you may get validation errors". [official] (Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1), https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/product-type-definition-meta-schema.md, read 2026-10-14) "Unless you specify a previous `productTypeVersion`, the Amazon Product Type Definitions always describe the latest up-to-date Amazon catalog requirements." [official] (Product Type Definitions API, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/product-type-definitions-api.md, read 2026-10-14) The schema links returned "are valid for seven days". [official] (Retrieve a Product Type Definition, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/retrieve-a-product-type-definition.md, read 2026-10-14) In Amazon's own example of property groups, `bullet_point` sits in `product_details`: "Information and characteristics to describe the product to support search, browse and detail page content (e.g., bullets, product features, model, style name)". [official] (Manage Product Listings with the Selling Partner API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/manage-product-listings-guide.md, read 2026-10-14)

[copy] Two product-type schemas downloaded by a third party show what the live object looks like: `AUTO_PART` (English) and `PRODUCT` (Spanish), in the GitHub repository ztao0916/demo-js, `schemajson/json/au.json` and `es.json`, file committed 2025-08-05. In both, `bullet_point` is an array of objects `{value, language_tag, marketplace_id}` with `minItems` 1, `minUniqueItems` 1, `maxUniqueItems` 10 and `selectors` `["marketplace_id","language_tag"]`, and each `value` has `maxLength` 700. In the English copy, the attribute's title is "Key Product Features". Its human-readable description says "Max. 100 characters per line" and that each line "will be displayed as a separate bullet point above the product description". The `value` description says the bullets "display directly under or next to your product photo" and "Do NOT use all caps or abbreviations". Two lessons, neither of them a number: (a) the description text and the validating keyword can disagree (100 in prose, 700 in `maxLength`), so cx must read the keyword, not the prose; (b) `maxUniqueItems` here counts items unique by `marketplace_id` + `language_tag`, so it may not bound the number of bullets at all. What, if anything, enforces "five" is not visible in the copy (Open questions).

**R6. Where a bullet change can be written, and how.** "JSON Patch documents can add, replace, or delete entire attributes. Patching content within attributes is not supported." [official] (Partially update a listing, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/partially-update-a-listing.md, read 2026-10-14) Changing one bullet therefore means sending the whole `bullet_point` array. `putListingsItem` is the wrong tool: "if the listing previously contained bullet points and a `putListingsItem` submission is made with the `LISTING_OFFER_ONLY` requirements without including the bullet points, the `putListingsItem` submission will remove the bullet points content". [official] (Listings Items API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-items-api.md, read 2026-10-14) Amazon's own agent skills hold the same line: "Always patch", and "(1) preview with `mode: VALIDATION_PREVIEW`, (2) seller approval…, (3) verify by re-reading the item after the live write". [official] (amzn/selling-partner-agentic-toolkit, `skills/listing-issues/SKILL.md` and `references/fix-playbook.md`, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-agentic-toolkit, repository created 2026-09-21, read 2026-10-14) `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` "synchronously performs the same checks that are done on production submissions" [official] (Listings APIs FAQ, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-apis-faq.md, read 2026-10-14), "has lower rate limits and is intended for testing a small number of listings. It is not designed for production workflows." [official] (Manage Listings Issues, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/manage-listings-issues.md, read 2026-10-14)

The Listings Items API v2021-08-01 provides information about the specific listing submitted by a selling partner." [official] (Listings APIs FAQ) Catalog `attributes` "conform to the related Amazon product type definitions"; Amazon's model example shows `bullet_point` as an array of `{language_tag, value, marketplace_id}`. [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/catalog-items-api-model/catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, read 2026-10-14) A change shows in `getListingsItem` "within 1-5 minutes". **R13. A claim in a bullet can move the product into a restricted category.** Amazon's restricted-product pages classify a product by what its listing says, not only by what the product is:
- **Pesticides.** Pesticides and pesticide devices include "Any product that makes antimicrobial, antifungal, antibacterial, anti-mold, or pesticide claims". The claim "could appear anywhere in the detail page text, on an image, or both". Examples given: "Socks or shoes that claim antimicrobial properties", a "mattress that claims to be antibacterial", a "\"mildew-resistant\" shower curtain". The treated-article exemption requires that the product "Doesn't make any public health claims or pesticide claims beyond preservation of the article itself". [official] (Pest control products and pesticides, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202115120, version 16, read 2026-10-14) This is why "anti-microbial" and "anti-bacterial" sit in the bullet page's prohibited-claims row.
- **Dietary supplements.** "Amazon may classify a product as a dietary supplement based on one or more of the following on the listing, label, or marketing materials". The list includes "Health benefit claims such as \"Helps maintain healthy blood sugar levels\"" and "Structure or function claims such as \"Supports immune health\" or \"Promotes joint flexibility\"". "Product listing attributes and product images must not" carry the FDA logo, claim "FDA approved" without approval, or make disease claims. [official] (Dietary supplements, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201829010, version 19, read 2026-10-14)
- **Cosmetics.** "Products making cosmetic claims such as moisturizes, deodorizes, or exfoliates are also classified as cosmetics and subject to this policy". The page's required listing attributes include one named "Product features", and its claims rules apply to "Product listing attributes and product images". [official] (Cosmetics, skin, and hair care, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200164470, version 11, read 2026-10-14)
- **Medical devices.** The same "Product listing attributes … must not" wording covers "FDA approved"/"FDA cleared" and disease claims. [official] (Medical devices and accessories, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200164650, version 15, read 2026-10-14)
- **Warnings go in their own attributes, not in bullets.** For California Proposition 65 the seller must "Provide Amazon with the correct Prop 65 warning using the instructions on this page (and not in any other way, such as by email)", and must "Not provide us with any warning materials such as labels, labeling, shelf signs, or tags to display with your product unless we otherwise instruct you to do so in writing". The route is the Compliance tab or the template's Compliance group (Warning Type and Chemical Names). "If you provide a Prop 65 warning on a parent SKU, you will receive an error message and the submission will fail." [official] (California Proposition 65 (Prop 65), https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202141960, version 7, read 2026-10-14) This page is about Prop 65 only. No page read states a general rule for safety warnings in bullets. That a warning written into a bullet does not count as the Prop 65 warning is the seat's inference from "not in any other way".

**R14. Removal, suppression and how each shows in Amazon's answers.**
- **Bullet-level outcome.** The bullet page's own outcome is "may be removed or updated" (R1). That acts on the bullet, not the listing.
- **Listing-level outcomes.** "Any products that don't comply with our guidelines may be suppressed from search until the quality issues are resolved". The troubleshooting table separates "search suppressed" from "detail page removed" status ("ASINs with search suppressed or detail page removed status are not searchable due to listing quality issues"). [official] (Search optimization, GNYWHX2TP7C8GXHK, version 3, read 2026-10-14)
- **What the Suppressed listings page names.** Its list of suppression triggers (images, item type, title length by category group, price per unit, missing child attributes in Shoes, Jewelry, Watches, Luggage, Consumables) does not mention bullets. [official] (Suppressed listings, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200898440, version 13, read 2026-10-14)
- **The API vocabulary.** Each issue carries `attributeNames` ("Names of the attributes that are associated with the issue, if applicable") and `enforcements.actions[].action`, with four values:
  - `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`: "An attribute's value on the listing item is invalid, which causes it to be rejected by Amazon".
  - `LISTING_SUPPRESSED`: "This enforcement takes down the current listing item's buyability".
  - `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`: "This value indicates that the catalog item is hidden from search results".
  - `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED`: "This catalog item is inactive on Amazon, and all offers against it in the applicable Amazon store are non-buyable".

  `enforcements.exemption.status` is `EXEMPT`, `EXEMPT_UNTIL_EXPIRY_DATE` (with `expiryDate`) or `NOT_EXEMPT`. `NOT_EXEMPT` means "Amazon has already taken the specified enforcement actions". [official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, definitions `Issue`, `IssueEnforcements`, `IssueEnforcementAction`, `IssueExemption`, read 2026-10-14)
- **What is still unknown.** No page read lists an issue code that is specific to bullets. Which action a given bullet violation draws is therefore read from the `issues` answer, never assumed (Open questions).

**R15. Bullets feed Amazon's product-type classification.** Warning code 18367 "occurs when Amazon categorizes your product under a different product type based on the attributes that you had provided, such as, title, product description, and bullet points in your listing instead of the product type that you had specified". Amazon's prevention advice is to "Ensure that the title, product description, and bullet points are matching the product that you are trying to sell". [official] (Warning code 18367: Product type modified by Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GXMA2QU3JZTG8JNL, version 1, read 2026-10-14) A bullet rewrite can therefore change more than the bullets. It can move the product type, and with it the schema (R5) and the browse placement.

**R16. Patch `merge` exists, but not for bullets.** The Listings Items model lists `add`, `replace`, `merge` and `delete` as `PatchOperation.op` values ("The `merge` operation merges the target property"). [official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json) The API page bounds it: "**The merge operation is not supported for other attributes outside of `fulfillment_availability` and `purchasable_offer`**." [official] (Listings Items API, read 2026-10-14) R6 therefore stands for `bullet_point`: a bullet change is a whole-attribute `replace`. Files that call `merge` "unknown" (angles/listing-content-and-health.md) can settle on this sentence.

Amazon's own pages disagree on the operation set. *Partially update a listing* names only three operations: "JSON Patch documents can add, replace, or delete entire attributes". The Listings Items model's `PatchOperation.op` enum and the *Listings Items API* page add `merge`, limited to `fulfillment_availability` and `purchasable_offer`. The binding set is the model JSON's enum together with the API page's limit. The two disagree only on attributes other than bullets, so for `bullet_point` the result is the same: `replace` of the whole array. (Partially update a listing; Listings Items API; listingsItems_2021-08-01.json; all re-read 2026-10-14 during reconciliation)

Limits that follow for judging bullets (the seat's reading of these rules):
- The smallest unit is a Sunday-to-Saturday week. A bullet change made mid-week contaminates that whole week.
- Only queries that rank in the brand's top set appear. A long-tail word bet on in a bullet may never show.
- Sponsored and organic impressions are mixed, so an ad change in the same weeks confounds the read.
- The funnel starts at the search result. The ASIN's click, cart-add and purchase shares for a query do reflect detail-page persuasion of search-originated shoppers. They do not reflect shoppers who arrived from elsewhere, and they do not isolate the bullets from the price, the images or the review count.

So SQP is a secondary read for bullets (P-bullet-points-1), and the ASIN-level conversion read comes from Sales and Traffic (see *What cx must read at run time*).

All [opinion].

**Where this contradicts Amazon.** The current Amazon page (revision 8) says "Bullet point character limits vary by product type" and gives no 10 or 255. The 2024 announcement states no limit. The [copy] schemas show `maxLength` 700. Whether 10–255 ever stood on an earlier revision could not be checked. The Wayback Machine holds captures of the page from 2024-04-21 on, but the raw copies checked are the JavaScript shell with no article text. Either way, it is not Amazon's word today. Fecoms itself separates "Policy (Aug 2024) 10–255" from "Field capacity Up to 500 characters in many categories".

**Per-bullet capacity and count: practitioners disagree among themselves.**
- Seller Labs ("How to Write Bullet Points That Convert", 2026-05-05, modified 2026-07-29): "typically limited to 255 characters per bullet for standard third-party sellers, or up to 500 characters for Brand Registry sellers".
- Flywheel Digital (Carina McLeod, "How to Write the Perfect Product Feature Bullet Points on Amazon", 2018-10-09): sellers "up to 500 characters per bullet point, while vendors can only have up to 255"; vendors may have up to 10 bullets.
- Alex Wong Copywriting (2016, modified 2025-10-17): "anywhere from 100 to 200 characters" by category.

All [opinion]. None cites a schema.

**"Only the first 1,000 bytes across all five bullets are indexed."** Keywords.am (Ash Metry, "Amazon Bullet Points 2026…", 2026-01-21, updated 2026-04-09, and "Amazon Character Limits 2026…", updated 2026-08-04) states this as a rule [opinion]. No Amazon page read says it. The closest Amazon text is the experiments page's guidance to keep all five bullets "under 1,000" characters, which is about length, not indexing, and the Search optimization page's reservation not to use all bullet content. The figure is unsourced and stays out of every skill.

**Indexing in general.** Seller Labs calls bullets "a secondary (but meaningful) keyword placement zone after your title and backend search terms". Flywheel says "Bullet points are indexed for search" and that search results "will sometimes show that bullet point with the relevant keywords italicized". Hector AI: "Amazon indexes bullet points for SEO, but with the A10 algorithm, keyword relevance matters more than volume". All [opinion]. "A10" is a practitioner name; Amazon does not use it on any page read.

**Mobile.** Practitioners contradict each other:
- Flywheel (2018): on mobile, bullets "appear further down the page, after the A+ or Enhanced Brand Content and the product description".
- Hector AI (Meher Patel, "Amazon Bullet Points 2025: Guidelines, Examples & Best Practices", 2025-09-16): "only the first three are shown"; keep bullets "≤200 characters" to avoid truncation.
- Seller Labs (2026): bullets "may be collapsed and require a tap to expand — which is why the first two bullets are the most critical".
- Keywords.am (2026): "each bullet truncates at ~70-80 characters".
- eComEngine (2025): "the same feature bullets are shown in different locations on mobile, desktop, and the Amazon app".

All [opinion]. The layout has changed over the years and differs by app and browser. None of these is a stable fact.

**Writing frameworks** [opinion]:
- **Benefit-first.** Flywheel: "it's more accurate to call these bullet points 'key benefits' instead of 'key features'". Keywords.am: "Benefit-First, Not Feature-First". Hector AI: "80% Benefits → 20% Features".
- **Feature then benefit.** Seller Labs: "[FEATURE in ALL CAPS or bold phrase]: [Benefit explained in plain language.]". SellerSprite: "Summarized phrase or selling point + Detailed explanation of how this feature benefits the buyer".
- **Objection handling.** Hector AI: "5. Address Customer Objections". Keywords.am orders bullets as "Main benefit first, then second benefit. After that, trust-building, then technical stuff. Finally, any objections."
- **Voice of the customer.** Seller Labs: "Check your Voice of the Customer data in Seller Central and read your product reviews. Buyers will tell you exactly what matters to them".

Two disagreements with Amazon:
- **ALL CAPS headers.** Seller Labs ("FEATURE in ALL CAPS"), Hector AI ("Start each bullet with a short ALL-CAPS header") and Keywords.am's own example ("WITHSTANDS HEAVY USE - …") recommend exactly what Amazon's page lists as prohibited ("All caps… 'ELEGANT DESIGN'"). Amazon's own header form is "Cotton fabric: Made from…", in sentence case with a colon.
- **Benefit-first framing vs Amazon's caution on claims.** Amazon asks for "features, benefits, and how the product meets customer needs" but bars "subjective, performance, or comparative claims unless they're verifiable on packaging". A benefit is safe as a use ("folds flat for carry-on bags"). It is not safe as an unsupported performance claim ("lasts twice as long").

## Published research

- **Reddy, Màrquez, Valero, Rao, Zaragoza, Bandyopadhyay, Biswas, Xing, Subbian, "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search", Amazon, arXiv 2206.06588 (2022; used in KDD Cup 2022).** Amazon's public relevance benchmark (about 130 thousand queries and 2.6 million human-labelled query–product judgements) ships, for each product, `product_title`, `product_description` and `product_bullet_point`, "the bullet point descriptions of the product as it appears in the amazon.com site". The baseline rankers in the dataset's README used "the query and title of the product". Narrow finding: Amazon's search researchers treat bullets as part of a product's text for relevance work. It does not say production search ranks on bullets, or with what weight. [research] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06588; https://github.com/amazon-science/esci-data README)
- **Nigam, Song, Mohan, Lakshman, Ding, Shingavi, Teo, Gu, Yin, "Semantic Product Search", KDD 2019 (Amazon).** A semantic matching model trained on Amazon behaviour data, run in online A/B tests. Product representation: attributes "like title, brand, and color", concatenated as "an ordered bag of words", because embedding attributes separately gave "up to 5% lower recall". It also gives a lexical-match failure example: "latex free examination gloves" retrieved for "latex examination gloves" "simply because all the words in the query are also present in the product title and description". Transfer: Amazon retrieval is not purely lexical, and a word in the text is not by itself a match for the query's intent. Bullets are not named. [research] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00937)
- **Zheng, Mukherjee, Dong, Li, "OpenTag: Open Attribute Value Extraction from Product Profiles", KDD 2018 (Amazon).** Extracts attribute values (e.g. flavor) from "titles, descriptions, and bullets" of Amazon product profiles, with F-score 83% from 150 labelled samples. It notes "over 60% bullets in descriptions contain fewer than 10 words". Transfer: Amazon has long mined bullet text for structured attributes. A fact stated plainly in a bullet can become an attribute value (and so a filter or a Rufus fact), while a fact buried in a keyword string may not. That last step is inference. [research] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01264)
- **Gong, Luo, Zhu, Ou, Li, Duan, "Automatic Generation of Chinese Short Product Titles for Mobile Display", IAAI 2019 (Innovative Applications of AI, held with AAAI-19; Alibaba/Taobao).** Online A/B test, 3% of users, 2017-11-02 to 2017-11-06: shortened titles for mobile gave "2.31% and 1.22% improvements of sales volume and turnover conversion rate". Transfer: on a phone, how much text is visible changes conversion. This is titles on Taobao, not Amazon bullets. [research] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11359)
- **Chen, Lin, Zhang, Yang, Zhou, Tang, "Towards Knowledge-Based Personalized Product Description Generation in E-commerce" (KOBE), KDD 2019 (Alibaba).** Generated descriptions, deployed on Taobao. The paper reports text-quality metrics (BLEU +9.7%), no conversion result. Transfer: none on conversion. Listed so no one cites it for one. [research] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12457)
- **Morkes and Nielsen, "Concise, SCANNABLE, and Objective: How to Write for the Web", Nielsen Norman Group, 1997** (lab usability studies, not peer-reviewed: 81 users across three studies; the measured comparison of five page versions, Study 3, used 51 users). Measured usability was "58% higher" when concise, "47% higher when the text was scannable", "27% higher when it was written in an objective style instead of the promotional style", and "124% higher" combined. Elements that aid scanning include "bulleted lists". Transfer: directional support for Amazon's own "concise", "easy-to-scan", "avoid brand marketing stories" guidance. These are usability scores on an information site, not purchase rates on Amazon. [research] (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)
- **Pernice, "F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web: Misunderstood, But Still Relevant (Even on Mobile)", NN/g, 2017-11-12** (eye-tracking; original study 2006). The F-pattern appears when text has "no bolding, bullets, or subheadings". Advice: "Use bullets and numbers to call out items in a list". Transfer: the front of each bullet gets more attention than the tail. This is web reading in general, not Amazon specifically. [research] (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)

No peer-reviewed study was found that measures the effect of Amazon bullet content on conversion. The search budget ran out before journals (Marketing Science, JMR, Management Science) could be searched; see Open questions.

*Added by the gap-fill pass, 2026-10-14: research on lists of product claims and on feature text.* Each entry was read at its abstract (via Crossref, the publisher's page or arXiv). None of them studies Amazon bullets.

- **Shu and Carlson, "When Three Charms but Four Alarms: Identifying the Optimal Number of Claims in Persuasion Settings", *Journal of Marketing* 78(1), 127–139, 2014 (doi 10.1509/jm.11.0504).**
  - Finding, narrowly: "when consumers know that the message source has a persuasion motive, the optimal number of positive claims is three". Impressions improve "until the fourth claim, at which point consumers' persuasion knowledge causes them to view all the claims with skepticism". The first experiment found the peak at three "for sources with a persuasion motive but not for sources without a persuasion motive".
  - Transfer: a seller's bullets are an obvious persuasion source. This supports Amazon's floor of "at least three" and the "Repetitive content" ban. It argues against filling every slot with weaker claims.
  - Limits: lab experiments on short claim sets, not purchase data on a marketplace. Whether a "claim" maps to a bullet (one fact) or to a phrase within a bullet is not settled by the paper. [research] (Crossref record and abstract, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1509/jm.11.0504)
- **Weaver, Garcia and Schwarz, "The Presenter's Paradox", *Journal of Consumer Research* 39(3), 445–460, 2012 (doi 10.1086/664497).**
  - Finding, narrowly: evaluators judge by weighted averaging, so adding mildly favourable information to highly favourable information can lower the overall evaluation. Across seven studies, presenters did not anticipate this and included everything.
  - Transfer: a weak fifth bullet can dilute four strong ones.
  - Limits: lab studies of bundles and arguments, not listings. [research] (The Presenter's Paradox, https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/39/3/445/1798375, abstract read 2026-10-14)
- **Meyvis and Janiszewski, "Consumers' Beliefs about Product Benefits: The Effect of Obviously Irrelevant Product Information", *Journal of Consumer Research* 28(4), 618–635, 2002 (doi 10.1086/338205).**
  - Finding, narrowly: information that does not bear on a product's key benefit lowered consumers' belief that the product delivers that benefit (a dilution effect). The effect was smaller when the information was processed with the desired benefit in mind.
  - Transfer: bullets that wander from the benefit the shopper is checking (brand stories, which Amazon also discourages) may weaken the bullets that address it.
  - Limits: lab studies. [research] (https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-lookup/doi/10.1086/338205, abstract read 2026-10-14)
- **Thompson, Hamilton and Rust, "Feature Fatigue: When Product Capabilities Become Too Much of a Good Thing", *Journal of Marketing Research* 42(4), 431–442, 2005 (doi 10.1509/jmkr.2005.42.4.431).**
  - Finding, narrowly: consumers "give more weight to capability and less weight to usability before use than after use". They therefore choose overly complex products that do not maximise satisfaction in use.
  - Transfer: the paper is about the product's features, not the listing text. It suggests that a feature list can win the purchase while the product later disappoints, which would show in returns and reviews rather than conversion.
  - Limits: weak transfer to bullet wording; it is listed so no one cites it as evidence that long bullet lists convert worse. [research] (Crossref record and abstract, https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Feature+Fatigue)
- **Fang, Yuan, Zhang, Donati and Sarvary, "Generative AI and Sales Productivity: Field Experiments in Online Retail", arXiv 2510.12049, 2025 (preprint, not peer-reviewed).**
  - Design: randomized field experiments on "a leading cross-border online retail platform". One workflow added GenAI-written text descriptions, which "summarizes key features and selling points", to about 45,000 randomly selected self-sold products whose vendors "typically provide image-based product presentations". The treatment group saw them "on top of the original, human-created" descriptions. The test ran for one week in December 2023.
  - Finding, narrowly: "Product Description generation raises sales by 2.1% (p < 0.05)". Across workflows, the gains came "primarily through higher conversion rates rather than larger cart values", with return rates and ratings not worse.
  - Transfer: adding a structured text summary of features where little or none existed raised sales modestly. It says nothing about rewriting existing bullets that already state the features.
  - Limits: one week, one non-Amazon platform. [research] (arXiv abstract and PDF, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12049, read 2026-10-14)

Still not found: a peer-reviewed study that measures, on Amazon or a comparable marketplace, the conversion effect of rewriting existing feature bullets. The web-search budget ran out during this pass as well (Open questions).

## Stable enough for a skill

- Bullets serve the conversion stage first. Amazon says well-written bullets can also raise discoverability and that keyword search matches against bullet points, while it reserves the right not to use all bullet content for retrieval. (Product bullet point requirements; Classify your products using Browse Tree Guides - BTG; Search optimization; Optimize your product discoverability)
- The text Amazon places under the title in search results is Item Highlights, a separate field from bullets. (Product title requirements and guidelines)
- The number, length and unit (characters or UTF-8 bytes) allowed for `bullet_point` are set per product type and store, in the product-type schema. Amazon's help page says limits "vary by product type". (Product bullet point requirements; Meta-Schema (v1); Product Type Definitions API)
- Amazon's bullet page sets a minimum bullet count and no maximum. Other Amazon pages give an upper figure as guidance. The binding count is the one in the product-type schema; the help pages' figures are quoted from the page, never from memory. (Product bullet point requirements; Create a listing experiment; Optimize your product discoverability; Amazon Ads guide)
- A bullet edit replaces the whole `bullet_point` attribute: send every bullet, in display order, each with `language_tag` and `marketplace_id`. Never use `putListingsItem` for a bullet change. (Partially update a listing; Listings Items API; Catalog model example)
- `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` runs the same checks as a real submission and changes nothing. Amazon offers it for a small number of listings, not production volume. (Listings APIs FAQ; Manage Listings Issues)
- Listings Items returns the bullets the seller submitted. Catalog Items returns the ASIN's reconciled bullets, which may differ (another contributor, a brand owner, or Amazon's AI rewrite of non-compliant bullets). (Listings Items API; Listings APIs FAQ; Review updated bullet point requirements…)
- Amazon removes or rewrites bullets that carry prohibited content. The categories on its page are special characters, emojis, placeholder text, ASINs, prohibited claims, guarantee wording, company or contact or link information, repetition, all caps, abbreviations, and content that belongs in dedicated fields. (Product bullet point requirements)
- Detail-page rules ban price, availability, condition, shipping offers, contact details, URLs, reviews and testimonials, time-sensitive information and cross-promotion from bullets. The page must state additional items essential to the product's function. (Product detail page rules)
- A claim about performance, composition, origin, environment, health or a certification needs verifiable proof. Adding such a word is a compliance change, not a search change. (Misleading and prohibited claims; General Listing Restrictions; agentic toolkit listing-searchability)
- Rufus (renamed "Alexa for Shopping" on 2026-05-13, per Amazon's note on its posts) answers detail-page questions from listing details, reviews and Q&A; Amazon gives no weight for bullets. (aboutamazon Rufus posts; Amazon Science Rufus post)
- Brand owners can test bullets with Manage Your Experiments, subject to traffic eligibility, and Amazon sets the duration options. (Create a listing experiment)
- Inventory reports do not carry bullets. Bullets are read per SKU or per ASIN through the APIs. (Inventory Reports)

(Pest control products and pesticides; Dietary supplements; Cosmetics, skin, and hair care)

## Positions

- **P-bullet-points-1.** A bullet change is aimed at the conversion stage and judged first on the ASIN's unit session percentage and orders; exposure for the words it adds is a secondary read, because Amazon reserves the right not to use all bullet content for search. (Product bullet point requirements; Search optimization)
- **P-bullet-points-2.** Before drafting bullets, cx reads `bullet_point` in the SKU's product-type schema from `getDefinitionsProductType` (count keywords, length keyword and whether it counts characters or UTF-8 bytes) and never takes a bullet limit from memory or a guide, because Amazon says limits vary by product type. (Product bullet point requirements; Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); Product Type Definitions API)
- **P-bullet-points-3.** cx reads the validating keyword (`maxLength`, `maxUtf8ByteLength`, `maxItems`/`maxUniqueItems` with `selectors`), not the attribute's description text, because the schema's prose and its keywords can disagree. (Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); the [copy] schemas illustrate it but are not the source)
- **P-bullet-points-4.** A bullet edit is one `patchListingsItem` `replace` on `/attributes/bullet_point` carrying every bullet in display order, each with `language_tag` and `marketplace_id`; cx never uses `putListingsItem` for it. (Partially update a listing; Listings Items API; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; agentic toolkit fix-playbook)
- **P-bullet-points-5.** Every bullet draft goes through `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` first, and the `issues` it returns are Amazon's final word on the draft. cx uses preview for a few SKUs, not bulk work. (Listings APIs FAQ; Manage Listings Issues; agentic toolkit listing-issues)
- **P-bullet-points-6.** cx checks a draft against the prohibited categories on Amazon's current bullet and detail-page rules pages and names the page when it flags something. The categories are special characters, emojis, placeholders, ASINs, prohibited claims, guarantee wording, company, contact or link content, price, availability or shipping, reviews or testimonials, time-sensitive content, repetition, all caps, abbreviations and dedicated-field content. (Product bullet point requirements; Product detail page rules)
- **P-bullet-points-7.** A bullet that adds or changes a claim about performance, composition, origin, environment, health, certification, an award or a survey is flagged to the seller as a compliance change needing proof, never as a search improvement. (Misleading and prohibited claims; General Listing Restrictions; Product bullet point requirements; agentic toolkit listing-searchability)
- **P-bullet-points-8.** cx writes header-style bullets in Amazon's own form ("Header: description", sentence case) and does not use ALL-CAPS headers, even though practitioner guides recommend them. (Product bullet point requirements; Product detail page rules)
- **P-bullet-points-9.** A listing review compares `getListingsItem` `attributes.bullet_point` (submitted) with `getCatalogItem` `attributes.bullet_point` (shown). A difference is reported as Amazon's or another contributor's version, not as cx's error. (Listings Items API; Listings APIs FAQ; Review updated bullet point requirements to optimize your listings; Product detail page rules, on how one brand contributor's attribute is chosen for display)
- **P-bullet-points-10.** "Good" for bullets in Amazon's own answers is: no issue naming `bullet_point`, the Catalog bullets equal to the submitted ones, at least the minimum bullet count that Amazon's current bullet page and the schema set, and no repetition of title, description, Item Highlights or dedicated-field content. (Product bullet point requirements; Listings APIs FAQ; Product detail page rules)
- **P-bullet-points-11.** When a return reason or review theme shows a mismatch between expectation and product, a bullet that states the fact plainly (a requirement, a limit, an item needed to operate it) is a legitimate conversion-stage fix. Guarantee or refund wording is not. (Product bullet point requirements; Product detail page rules)
- **P-bullet-points-12.** cx does not state where bullets sit on mobile or how many are visible, and it does not promise any Rufus (since 2026-05-13 "Alexa for Shopping", per Amazon's note on those posts) effect from a bullet change. Amazon says only that Rufus draws on listing details. (aboutamazon Rufus posts; Amazon Science Rufus post; absence noted in Open questions)
- **P-bullet-points-13.** The change file for a bullet change holds the full before and after arrays, the words bet on per bullet, and the `productTypeVersion` of the schema read. (Product Type Definitions API; Partially update a listing)
- **P-bullet-points-14.** For a brand-registered seller with an eligible ASIN, cx proposes a Manage Your Experiments bullet test as the stronger evidence before a plain before/after read, and leaves its duration and threshold to Amazon's page. (Create a listing experiment; Optimize product content and increase sales with A/B testing)
- **P-bullet-points-15.** Competitors' bullets are read from Catalog Items `attributes` or Keepa `features`. A competitor "changed its bullets" only when two saved observations differ, because Keepa keeps no bullet history. (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Keepa Product.java)
- **P-bullet-points-16.** cx keeps bullets consistent across a variation family unless the bullet describes the variant's own attribute. (Product bullet point requirements)

- **P-bullet-points-17.** For a bullet change, the baseline window ends at submission. The observation window starts only after `getCatalogItem` shows the new array and the general latency stated in Amazon's search glossary has passed. cx states that latency from the glossary page (its 2026-10-14 text is the capture GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV-v2 under the office's capture folder (outside this repository)), not from memory. (analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)

(Search Query Performance dashboard; Analytics reports)

(Pest control products and pesticides; Dietary supplements; Cosmetics, skin, and hair care; Medical devices and accessories; Misleading and prohibited claims)
- **P-bullet-points-21.** cx does not put a Prop 65 or other regulatory warning in a bullet as a substitute for the compliance attribute. It points the seller to the Compliance attributes, which Amazon names as the only route for Prop 65. (California Proposition 65 (Prop 65); Product bullet point requirements, "Content for dedicated fields")
- **P-bullet-points-22.** When an issue names `bullet_point`, cx reports its `enforcements.actions` and exemption status exactly as Amazon gives them. It distinguishes "this bullet was rejected" (`ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`) from "the listing is down or hidden" (`LISTING_SUPPRESSED`, `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`, `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED`), and does not infer either from the bullet page's "may be removed or updated". (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json; Product bullet point requirements; Search optimization)
- **P-bullet-points-23.** After a bullet write, cx re-reads the listing's product type. If Amazon changed it, cx reports that as part of the change's result and re-reads the new type's schema. (Warning code 18367: Product type modified by Amazon)
- **P-bullet-points-24.** cx treats Item highlights and bullets as separate levers: Item highlights for what shoppers see under the title in search results, bullets for the detail page. A change file names which one was changed. (Product title requirements and guidelines; Product bullet point requirements)
- **P-bullet-points-25.** For a store other than the US, cx reads that store's own bullet requirements page and that store's schema, and does not carry over US figures or US examples. (Product bullet point requirements, UK/CA/IN/AU/DE/JP layers; Product Type Definitions API)
- **P-bullet-points-26.** Where a dated Amazon category style guide disagrees with the current bullet requirements page or the schema, cx follows the current page and the schema and says the guide is older. (Product bullet point requirements; Bulk listing templates; CATEGORY STYLE GUIDE: CLOTHING & ACCESSORIES 2019)
- **P-bullet-points-27.** cx does not repeat in a bullet a fact that an attribute already displays in the bullet area (Amazon's example is fabric type in apparel). It fills the attribute instead. (Product bullet point requirements, "Content for dedicated fields"; CATEGORY STYLE GUIDE: CLOTHING & ACCESSORIES 2019; Category Style Guide Summary 2022)
- **P-bullet-points-28.** cx does not fill every bullet slot the schema allows with weak or repeated claims. Strong, distinct bullets that meet Amazon's stated minimum are enough, and research on claim lists finds that added weaker claims can lower the overall impression. (Product bullet point requirements; Shu and Carlson 2014; Weaver, Garcia and Schwarz 2012)
- **P-bullet-points-29.** cx calls the assistant "Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus)". It does not claim that bullets feed the assistant or the detail-page AI overviews, because Amazon's 2026-05-13 post names neither listing details nor bullets. (Alexa for Shopping: Amazon's AI assistant for personalized shopping; the older Rufus posts' editor's note)
- **P-bullet-points-30.** For a vendor account, cx states that it holds no Amazon-sourced vendor bullet rule and reads count and length from the schema returned for that account. It does not use the practitioners' vendor figures. (Listings Items API; Product Type Definitions API)

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- Benefit-first ordering and putting the decisive fact in the first words of each bullet are practitioner advice with some support from reading research (NN/g). No Amazon-specific measurement backs them; they belong in writing.md as a craft note at most, and are untested.
- The "first 1,000 bytes indexed" rule, the "10–255 characters" rule, "first three bullets on mobile" and "70–80 characters visible" all circulate as fact and have no Amazon source. If a seller cites one, cx should say where Amazon's own words stand.
- A bullet change and a title or Item Highlights change made in the same window cannot be told apart in Search Query Performance. One change at a time (effect-review) matters more for bullets than usual, because bullet changes are the cheapest to bundle.
- Because Amazon may AI-rewrite non-compliant bullets, a seller's "my bullets changed by themselves" is more often Amazon than a hijacker. The Catalog-vs-submitted comparison plus the `issues` list answers it in one read.
- Amazon's own agent toolkit (2026-09-21) covers the same ground for search content: patch only, preview → approve → verify, a claims gate. The playbook should read as consistent with it, not as a rival method.
- *Gap fill, 2026-10-14:*
- Whether an overrun of the glossary's combined bullet figure draws an issue, a silent truncation or nothing is unknown. The seat expects an issue rather than a preview `INVALID`, because the figure is not a schema keyword. That expectation is untested.
- Moving a fact from bullets to Item highlights moves it from the detail page to the search result tile. That is a click-through bet, not a conversion bet, and it should be logged as one. Amazon states the placement but not the effect.
- The time from a bullet write to a search-visible change is probably shorter than the glossary's maximum for most ASINs ("slower or faster"). Until runs measure it, waiting out the stated latency costs a few days of observation, and starting early risks crediting the old bullets with the new bullets' effect.
- Warning 18367 makes a bullet rewrite riskier on listings whose product type was chosen narrowly. A rewrite that leans on another category's vocabulary (e.g. "supplement", "disinfect") could reclassify the item.
