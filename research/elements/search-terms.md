# Element: backend search terms (generic_keyword)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Funnel stage: exposure only.** Backend search terms are hidden from shoppers. They can only affect whether, and for which queries, an ASIN is eligible to be retrieved. They do nothing for click-through or conversion. Amazon: "These keywords are not visible to customers, but they can still help a product rank for specific searches" (Amazon SEO blog, 2025). The archived Seller Central page says: "Being indexed means that your ASIN is eligible (but not guaranteed) to appear in search results for matching Search Terms."

**Skills it feeds.**

- **listing-review.** Which element serves exposure. What a healthy search-terms value looks like in Amazon's own answers. Where the limit lives (the product-type schema). The difference between "accepted by the API" and "indexed by Search".
- **listing-change-log.** A search-terms change records the whole before and after value array, its byte count, the words bet on, and the observation window.
- **effect-review.** A search-terms change is judged by exposure metrics (impressions for the targeted queries) plus orders, not by CTR or CVR.
- **references/writing.md.** `generic_keyword` is the clearest case of all three writing facts:
  - the schema carries the limit, and it differs by marketplace and product type;
  - `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` tests a body without changing anything;
  - what the API accepts and what Search indexes are two different limits.

**Why a senior operator cares.**

- The field is cheap to change and invisible to shoppers. That makes it the easiest place to make silent mistakes: going over the indexing limit, putting in a competitor's brand, or wasting bytes on words already in the title.
- Amazon's own pages disagree with each other on the number and on what counts. Practitioners repeat the disagreement.
- On an ASIN that several sellers offer, Amazon has not said whose search terms Search uses (§F5). A reseller's edit may do nothing, and a brand owner cannot see a reseller's terms. A senior operator asks "whose listing is this?" before touching the field.
- In the zero-playbook run of 2026-10-14 (internal run report, day 2), cx proposed a search-terms line annotated "not repeating the title, within 250 bytes". It read no schema, counted no bytes and never wrote the patch. The proposed line also carried a brand name, which every Amazon page below lists as prohibited in search terms. Among the ten things cx had to be told was "Read the schema before writing a body".

### A. RULES (enforced: schema validation, policy, suppression)

1. **RULE: prohibited content, with a stated consequence.** "Violating these rules may result in your ASIN being suppressed and action against your account." The page lists:
   - "Your brand or other brand names"
   - "ASINs"
   - "Profanity"
   - "Temporary statements such as 'new,' or 'on sale now.'"
   - "Subjective claims, such as 'best,' 'cheapest,' 'amazing,'"
   - "Abusive or offensive terms"

   Example lists on the same page:
   - brand names: "Apple, Nike, Amazon";
   - temporary words: "available now, brand new, current, discounted, just launched, last chance, last minute, latest, limited time, new, on sale, this week … today";
   - subjective words: "amazing, best, cheap, cheapest, effective, fastest, good deal, least, most, popular, trending".

   [official] (Using Search Terms effectively, US, archived 2022-07-05, https://web.archive.org/web/20220705140739/https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/23501, read 2026-10-14; same text in the 2021-06-15 snapshot)
   - Note that the seller's **own** brand is on the prohibited list too, not only competitors' brands.
2. **The schema's own description for the attribute (informational text inside the schema, not a validation keyword; the enforcement is the policy in item 1).** `generic_keyword.value`: "Provide any terms that may be relevant to customer searches. No repetition, no competitor brand names or ASINs." The AU AUTO_PART copy carries the same English text; the ES copy carries it in Spanish. Note the schema text names only "competitor" brands, while the help page in item 1 prohibits "Your brand or other brand names". [official, via a third-party copy of Amazon's schema document] (Product type schema PRODUCT for Amazon.de, `$id` https://schemas.amazon.com/selling-partners/definitions/product-types/schema/v1/PRODUCT, copy in github.com/germanbisurgi/jedison `src-docs/json/examples/amazon.json`, committed 2024-11-14, read 2026-10-14)
3. **RULE: what counts as catalog spam.** "it is not allowed to include other brand names or ASINs in an ASIN's catalog data … including 'phone cover' in the catalog data of shoes is catalog spam." [official] (Amazon search glossary, archived 2022-08-09, https://web.archive.org/web/20220809020641/https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/help.html?itemID=F6B8ZPNY2AP72VV&language=en_US&ref=efph_F6B8ZPNY2AP72VV_relt_YZNTFDKYWU75VZ8, read 2026-10-14)
4. **RULE: over the indexing limit, nothing is indexed.**
   - "If Search Terms are over the limit, none of the Search Terms for that ASIN will be indexed by Amazon Search." [official] (Using Search Terms effectively, US 2022-07-05; UK 2022-06-29; IN 2022-05-16; JP 2022-07-06 in Japanese, all archived, read 2026-10-14; the UK, IN and JP snapshot URLs carry a `&ref=` suffix, see Pages read)
   - The keyword-attributes page says: "Search Terms (generic_keywords) should be less than 250 bytes long … If it exceeds the limit, the attribute is completely ignored by Amazon Search. For other length limits in the table above, if limit exceeds, Amazon Search indexes up until the length limit and ignores any excess." [official] (Keyword attributes explained, archived 2021-03-23, https://web.archive.org/web/20210323073334/https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/F2C2L6RCFZGWBXC, read 2026-10-14)
   - So for search terms Amazon describes **all-or-nothing**. For the other keyword attributes it describes **truncation**.
5. **RULE: the limit is measured in bytes, not characters.** "The Search Terms field in Seller Central has a built-in byte counter which stops accepting input at the length limit that is quoted in bytes and not characters". "Bytes correspond to 1:1 for alphanumeric characters … umlauts in German (for example ä), which can be 2 bytes per character. Other complex characters, such as Japanese and Chinese characters can be 3 or 4 bytes." [official] (Using Search Terms effectively, US archived 2022-07-05, read 2026-10-14)
   - An Amazon staff reply, quoting a newer wording of the help page, restates this: "the Generic keyword field in Seller Central stops accepting inputs when the byte limit is reached." [official, forum staff] (How many characters limit of search terms for the latest?, Blake_Amazon reply, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/0283121e-841c-49f8-a8c8-5f112a1a0715, shown "3 years ago", read 2026-10-14)
6. **RULE (API side): the product-type schema carries the write limits, in a custom keyword measured in UTF-8 bytes.**
   - `maxUtf8ByteLength`: "Defines the maximum length of a `string`, measured in UTF-8 bytes". Its purpose is "Validation".
   - `maxUniqueItems` / `selectors`: "Defines the maximum number of unique items in an array. Use in conjunction with `selectors`."
   - "If you choose to ignore this custom vocabulary and use only the standard JSON Schema Draft 2019-09 vocabularies, you may get validation errors."

   [official] (Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1), https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/product-type-definition-meta-schema, page `updatedAt` 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14)
   - Amazon's example validator counts `Encoding.UTF8.GetBytes(value.ToString()).Length`, the whole string spaces included. Its error reads "Value must be less than or equal {maxUtf8ByteLength} bytes in length." Its `maxUniqueItems` validator counts items per combination of `selectors` values and raises "Each combination of selector values may only occur {maxUniqueItems} times." So `maxUniqueItems` is a per-(marketplace, language) count of values, not a count of distinct values. [official] (C# Example of Meta-Schema v1, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/product-type-definition-meta-schema-v1-example-c, read 2026-10-14)
7. **RULE (API side): the error code for "too long".** Issue `90225` reads "Value for `attribute_name` is longer than the allowed maximum (limit unit)." The page lists "`generic_keyword`: 500 bytes" among "Common attributes with the maximum number of characters permitted". [official] (Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-items-api-issues-troubleshooting, `updatedAt` 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14)
   - Amazon's own documents disagree on the write limit: this troubleshooting page lists "`generic_keyword`: 500 bytes", while the schema copies in item 8 carry `maxUtf8ByteLength` 250 (DE PRODUCT) or 2000 with `maxLength` 500 (AU AUTO_PART, ES PRODUCT). The 500 is an example list on a docs page. The binding value is the one `getDefinitionsProductType` returns for the listing's product type and marketplace, read at run time; neither figure here is to be remembered.
8. **RULE (API side): the shape of `generic_keyword` in the schema.** This was read from third-party copies of Amazon schema documents, so the dates are uncertain.
   - It is an **array** of `{value, language_tag, marketplace_id}` objects, with `selectors: ["marketplace_id","language_tag"]`, `minItems 1` and `maxUniqueItems 1000`.
   - `value` has `"editable": true` and `"hidden": false`.

   The limits on `value` differ by copy:

   | Schema copy | Marketplace | `maxLength` | `maxUtf8ByteLength` | Committed |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | PRODUCT | Amazon.de | 500 | 250 | 2024-11-14 |
   | AUTO_PART | Amazon.com.au | 500 | 2000 | 2025-08-05 |
   | PRODUCT | Amazon.es | 500 | 2000 | 2025-08-05 |

   [official, via third-party copies] (jedison copy above; github.com/ztao0916/demo-js `schemajson/json/au.json` and `es.json`, committed 2025-08-05, read 2026-10-14)
   - Two consequences:
     - The **write** limit differs by marketplace and product type. Both keywords apply at once: `maxLength` counts characters, `maxUtf8ByteLength` counts bytes. Where `maxUtf8ByteLength` is 2000 and `maxLength` is 500, the binding ceiling for plain ASCII text is the 500 characters (= 500 bytes), which matches the troubleshooting page's "500 bytes"; in the DE copy the byte limit (250) binds first. Either way the accepted length sits above the indexing figures Amazon states on help pages.
     - The value is a list. With `maxUniqueItems` 1000 and `selectors` `["marketplace_id","language_tag"]`, and by the C# validator's reading of `maxUniqueItems` (count per selector combination), a listing may carry more than one `generic_keyword` value per marketplace and language. Compare `item_name` in the DE copy: `maxUniqueItems` 1, one title per marketplace and language.
9. **RULE (API side): what Catalog Items returns.** "The `generic_keyword` attribute is proprietary seller-specific data and will no longer be returned by the Catalog Items API. Selling Partners can use the Listings Items API to view submitted values." [official] (SP-API Release Notes, entry of August 16, 2023, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes, read 2026-10-14)
10. **RULE (API side): previewing and patching.**
    - `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW`: "Indicates the submitted data should be validated without persisting to the selling partner's catalog."
    - Status `VALID`: "The listings submission was valid. Only returned when the `mode` is `VALIDATION_PREVIEW`." `INVALID`: "not valid and was not accepted for processing."
    - "Only top-level listings item attributes can be patched. Patching nested attributes is not supported."

    [official] (patchListingsItem, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/patchlistingsitem, `updatedAt` 2026-09-10, read 2026-10-14)
    - Responses to put/patch "indicate whether or not the submission was accepted for processing"; `getListingsItem` "can include issues that occur after the submission has been processed". [official] (Listings Items API, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-items-api, read 2026-10-14)
11. **RULE: Amazon may decline to use supplied terms.** "Amazon reserves the right to not use all supplied keywords for retrieving products. Reasons … search computational efficiency, potential manipulation of Search results, irrelevant Search Terms, and offensive or illegal terms." [official] (Using Search Terms effectively, US archived 2022; Optimize your product discoverability, archived 2021-10-23, https://web.archive.org/web/20211023012109/https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/10471, read 2026-10-14)
    - FAQ 7: "Amazon determines the relevancy of search terms using a machine learning model. The terms we deem relevant may change over time … This is an automated process". [official] (Using Search Terms effectively, US archived 2022-07-05, read 2026-10-14)
12. **RULE: preconditions for indexing that no search term can fix.** "Parent ASINs are not indexed by Amazon Search". "Unassigned ASINs are not indexed by Amazon Search" (no browse node). "ASINs without offer are unsearchable". A future launch date makes an ASIN unsearchable. [official] (My ASIN is unsearchable, archived 2022-08-11, https://web.archive.org/web/20220811090920/https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/help.html?itemID=HGHDMU4X5K9QGCV&language=en_US&ref=efph_HGHDMU4X5K9QGCV_relt_YZNTFDKYWU75VZ8, read 2026-10-14)
    - The parent-ASIN rule matters for writes: search terms on a variation parent do not make the parent findable.

### C. Do spaces count? Amazon contradicts itself

- **US** (archived 2021 and 2022): "No. For the purpose of calculating the length of Search Terms, Amazon Search does not count spaces or punctuation." The **JP** and **IN** copies say the same.
- **UK** (archived 2022-06-29): "Yes, Amazon search counts spaces and punctuations."
- **The schema validator** (meta-schema and C# example) counts the UTF-8 bytes of the whole string, spaces included.

[official] (pages as cited above, read 2026-10-14)

- Gap fill 2026-10-14: the live UK page (G23501 v9 r3, Published/GB) now says "No. For the purpose of calculating the length of search terms, Amazon search does not count spaces or punctuation." So do the live pages of the fifteen other marketplaces read (§F2). The UK "Yes" survives only in the 2022 archive. The contradiction that remains is between Search's count (spaces excluded) and the schema validator's (whole string), which §F4 resolves.

**F4. One statement of how the limit is counted (resolving the five figures).** Amazon names two different limits and counts them differently:
1. **The indexing limit (Amazon Search).** Stated on help pages per marketplace (F2, F3): "less than 250 bytes" (US table: 249), JP 500 / fashion 250. Search counts bytes and "does not count spaces or punctuation" (every live page read). Over it, the whole attribute is ignored (F3; F2).
2. **The write limit (API validation).** In the product-type schema, `maxUtf8ByteLength` (UTF-8 bytes of the whole string as sent) and `maxLength` (characters) on `generic_keyword.value` (items 6 and 8). Amazon's validator counts every byte, spaces included (item 6, C# example). The troubleshooting page's "`generic_keyword`: 500 bytes" (item 7) and the schema copies' 250 / 2000 are write limits, not indexing limits.

A string whose UTF-8 bytes, **spaces and punctuation included**, are under the marketplace's indexing figure is under the indexing limit by Search's own count (which excludes them), and it is under every write limit seen in the schema copies. [seat, from the official pages cited] What no page read settles: whether a value accepted between the indexing figure and the schema's write limit is ignored (as F3 says) or partly used, and whether the indexing figure applies per array value or to all values for a marketplace together (Open questions 2 and 3). A `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` against the live schema answers only the write limit; it says nothing about indexing.

## What practitioners add

- **What happens over the limit: whole field dropped, or truncated?**
  - Whole field dropped:
    - My Amazon Guy (Ken Zhou, COO, "Mastering Amazon Backend Keywords", Feb 11, 2025): "If you exceed the 250-byte limit, Amazon may ignore all backend search terms for that listing."
    - keywords.am (Ash Metry, "Amazon Character Limits 2026: Title, Bullets, Description, Backend", Jan 21, 2026, updated Aug 4, 2026): "Exceeding it by one byte silently de-indexes every backend term you've added."
    - Epinium Editorial Team ("Amazon Backend Keywords: The 250-Byte Guide for 2026", May 20, 2026): "the algorithm drops the entire field from its index."
  - Truncated at the cutoff:
    - Ad Badger ("How Many Keywords Can You Use in Amazon Listings?", "The Badger", Jun 11, 2026): "Amazon silently stops indexing at the cutoff", "In practice, Amazon indexes a maximum of 249 bytes."
    - A seller reply (shown "6 years ago") in the thread "Please reduce your generic keyword length to less than generic_keyword bytes", describing a "fall of 2018" change, says "the complete string listed in that box will be suppressed from Amazon's A10 Search Algorithm". Despite the wording, this describes the whole string dropping, so it belongs with the whole-field-dropped reading above, not with truncation.
  - Amazon's archived words support the whole-field-dropped reading. [opinion]
- **Do spaces count?** Practitioners split the same way Amazon's own pages do.
  - Helium 10 ("Amazon Search Term Character Limits", published Apr 19, 2018, modified Dec 22, 2022): "249 bytes (excluding spaces and punctuation)"; "Amazon doesn't count spaces as characters".

## Published research

- **Nigam, Song, Mohan, Lakshman, Ding, Shingavi, Teo, Gu, Yin. "Semantic Product Search." KDD 2019, Applied Data Science Track (arXiv:1907.00937).** [research]
  - Finding (narrow): Amazon trained a deep-learning semantic matching model on customer behaviour data because "Pure lexical matching via an inverted index falls short". The abstract names lack of understanding of "hypernyms, synonyms, and antonyms", "fragility to morphological variants (e.g. 'woman' vs. 'women')" and "sensitivity to spelling errors". It reports offline gains and "online A/B tests which demonstrate the efficacy of our method". (Read: arXiv abstract and Amazon Science page; the Amazon Science page lists the same nine authors in a different order, Mohan first.)
  - Transfer [seat]: this is consistent with Amazon's guidance that plurals, case variants and common misspellings need not be typed in. It suggests the "ALL of those words" lexical rule in the 2022 glossary is not the whole of retrieval in 2019 and later, so a product may be retrieved for a query whose words are not in its catalog text, and the "ASIN + keyword" check is then not a clean test of lexical indexing. The paper does not say whether or how this model is used in production retrieval today.
  - The paper does not say how hidden search terms enter the model.
- **Sorokina and Cantú-Paz. "Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products." SIGIR 2016 (industry talk).** [research]
  - Finding: Amazon moved from "several handtuned ranking functions using a handful of input features" to "a general machine learning framework used for ranking within categories", with "NLP techniques used for matching queries and products".
  - Transfer [seat]: the talk separates matching queries to products from ranking. That search terms help the matching step and not ranking directly is the seat's reading combined with Amazon's FAQ ("eligible (but not guaranteed) to appear"), not a claim of the talk. Only the abstract page was read.
- **Reddy, Màrquez, Valero, Rao, Zaragoza, Bandyopadhyay, Biswas, Xing, Subbian. "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search." arXiv:2206.06588, 2022 (KDD Cup 2022).** [research]
  - Finding: about 130,000 difficult queries and 2.6 million human relevance judgements in English, Japanese and Spanish, labelled Exact/Substitute/Complement/Irrelevant.
  - Transfer [seat]: Amazon builds human relevance labels on that scale. That fits its warning that irrelevant search terms may not be used. The dataset says nothing about backend fields directly.
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures the effect of backend search terms on Amazon exposure. The web-search budget ran out before a wider search could be made.
- **Gap fill 2026-10-14: Nigam et al. (KDD 2019), full text.** On the product side the paper says "Products have multiple attributes, like title, brand, and color, that are material to the matching process"; embedding each attribute separately "led to 5% lower recall than simply concatenating the attributes. Hence, we decided to use an ordered bag of words of these attributes." Its lexical-matching failure example is products "retrieved simply because all the words in the query are also present in the product title and description." [research] (arXiv:1907.00937 PDF, read 2026-10-14)
  - Transfer [seat]: the paper names visible attributes; it never names hidden search terms as a model input. Whether `generic_keyword` feeds Amazon's semantic matcher is not stated; Amazon's live US page calls search terms an input to "lexical matching" (§F1).
- **Chang, Jiang, Yu, Teo, Zhang, Zhong, Kolluri, Hu, Shandilya, Ievgrafov, Singh, Dhillon. "Extreme Multi-label Learning for Semantic Matching in Product Search." KDD 2021, Applied Data Science Track (arXiv:2106.12657).** [research]
  - Finding (narrow): treating semantic product search as extreme multi-label classification, with "hierarchical linear models with n-gram features", gave "a low latency of 1.25 milliseconds per query" and "a 65% improvement of Recall@100 (60.9% v.s. 36.8%) over a competing embedding-based DSSM model" on a catalog "of size 100 million, or more"; the method "can retrieve products that are complementary to existing product search system and add diversity to the match set." Embedding models, it says, learn "from customer behavior data". (Read: arXiv abstract page.)
  - Transfer [seat]: Amazon researchers describe retrieval paths learned from shopper behaviour alongside lexical matching. A product can thus be matched to queries through behaviour as well as through its text, which weakens any one-to-one reading of "term added → impressions for that term". The abstract does not name product text fields.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Backend search terms are invisible to shoppers, so they serve exposure only. (Amazon SEO blog 2025: "not visible to customers"; Optimize your product discoverability, live v13: "These are hidden from customers")
- The attribute is `generic_keyword`. It is an array of values keyed by `marketplace_id` and `language_tag`; how many values one marketplace-and-language pair may carry is the schema's `maxUniqueItems`. (Schema copies; Meta-Schema `selectors`/`maxUniqueItems`; C# Example of Meta-Schema v1)
- Its write limits are in the product-type schema, as `maxLength` (characters) and `maxUtf8ByteLength` (UTF-8 bytes) on `value`. They differ by marketplace and product type. (Meta-Schema; schema copies; troubleshooting page)
- What the API accepts and what Search indexes are separate limits. Amazon has stated the indexing limit on help pages and in staff replies; no schema copy or API page read labels any figure as the indexing limit. (Keyword attributes explained; staff replies; troubleshooting page; schema copies)
- Amazon states that search terms over the indexing limit are not indexed at all, not truncated. (Keyword attributes explained, live; Use search terms effectively, live in every marketplace read except the US, whose page has no over-limit FAQ)
- The limit is in bytes. A byte count differs from a character count for any non-ASCII character. (Using Search Terms effectively; Meta-Schema)
- Prohibited: brand names (the seller's own included), other brands, ASINs, profanity, temporary statements, subjective claims, and abusive or offensive terms. Violations can suppress the ASIN and draw account action. (Using Search Terms effectively; Product type schema description; Amazon search glossary)
- Amazon's guidance:
  - Separate terms with single spaces.
  - Drop punctuation and stop words.
  - Do not repeat words, within the field or from the title and other fields.
  - Use one of singular or plural.
  - Include synonyms, abbreviations and alternate names.
  - Order words the way shoppers type them.

  (Using Search Terms effectively; Optimize your product discoverability; blogs 2024/2025)
- Amazon may decline to use any supplied term, by a machine-learned relevance judgement. A term being in the field is not evidence that it is used. (Using Search Terms effectively FAQ 7; Optimize your product discoverability)
- A parent ASIN, an ASIN with no browse node, no offer, or a future launch date is not searchable whatever its search terms say. (My ASIN is unsearchable)
- Catalog Items does not return `generic_keyword`. Only the seller's own Listings Items record shows it. (SP-API Release Notes, 2023-08-16)
- `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` validates a patch without persisting it and answers `VALID` or `INVALID` with issues. (patchListingsItem)
- Platinum keywords are redundant. (Keyword attributes explained; Amazon search glossary)
- Amazon states a go-live allowance for listing changes to reach search, and says latency "can be slower or faster"; the allowance is a help-page figure that can change, so it is read from the page's current text, never carried as a number. (Amazon search glossary, live; My ASIN is unsearchable, live)
- Gap fill 2026-10-14:
  - The live "Use search terms effectively" page is G23501, and each marketplace serves its own version with its own figure. (Use search terms effectively, sixteen marketplaces)
  - Amazon Search counts the indexing limit in bytes without spaces or punctuation; the schema validator counts the bytes of the whole string. A whole-string count under the marketplace's indexing figure satisfies both. (Use search terms effectively; Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); C# Example of Meta-Schema v1)
  - Over the indexing limit the whole attribute is ignored, not truncated; other keyword attributes are truncated. (Keyword attributes explained, live)
  - `generic_keyword` is seller-specific data held on each seller's listing; Catalog Items, which is reconciled at ASIN level, does not return it. (SP-API Release Notes, 2023-08-16; Listings APIs FAQ)
  - When several sellers contribute to one ASIN, Amazon chooses what the detail page shows, and on a brand-registered ASIN the Brand Representative's contribution is displayed. Amazon does not say how this applies to hidden search terms. (Suggest changes to your product detail page; About products and listings)
  - Brand Analytics (Search Query Performance, Amazon Search Terms) is available only to sellers with a Brand Representative role (in the API's words, the Brand Analytics Selling Partner API role and Brand Registry); its data arrives some time after a period closes, a delay stated on the Brand Analytics help page and read from it. (Search Query Performance dashboard; Brand Analytics; Analytics reports)

## What cx must read at run time

- **The write limit and the attribute's shape.** `getDefinitionsProductType` for the listing's product type and marketplace, with `requirements=LISTING`, the seller's `sellerId`, `locale`, and `parentageLevel` where it applies. - **The current value.** `getListingsItem` with `includedData=attributes,issues,summaries` (and `issueLocale`). - **Before a write.** `patchListingsItem` with `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW`, `op: replace`, path `/attributes/generic_keyword`, carrying the complete intended array. Expect `VALID` and empty `issues`. A `90225` issue means over the schema limit.
- **After a write.** `patchListingsItem` without the mode returns `ACCEPTED` and a `submissionId` (the receipt). - **The byte count.** `printf %s "$t" | wc -c` on each value and on the joined values (`printf %s`, not `echo`, so no trailing newline is counted). It counts the bytes of the string as encoded, UTF-8 in the box, with spaces included, which is the conservative count. A string in decomposed Unicode form (e + combining accent) counts more bytes than the composed form; the schema validator counts whatever string is sent. ## Positions

- **P-search-terms-1.** Backend search terms serve the exposure stage only. A search-terms change is never proposed to fix click-through or conversion. Sources: Amazon SEO: 7 ways to improve your product's search rankings; Optimize your product discoverability; Using Search Terms effectively.
- **P-search-terms-2.** Before writing `generic_keyword`, cx reads the product-type schema through `getDefinitionsProductType` for that marketplace and product type, and takes the limits (`maxUtf8ByteLength`, `maxLength`, `maxUniqueItems`) from it rather than from memory, because they differ by marketplace and product type. Sources: Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting; the schema copies (PRODUCT DE, AUTO_PART AU, PRODUCT ES); Using Search Terms effectively (JP).
- **P-search-terms-3.** cx treats the schema's write limit and Amazon Search's indexing limit as two different limits, and says that passing validation does not mean the terms are indexed. Sources: Keyword attributes explained; Generic Keyword Field Length (Manny_Amazon); amazon generic keywords 250 or 500 ??? (Cooper_Amazon); Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting.
- **P-search-terms-4.** cx keeps the search terms under the indexing limit Amazon states for the marketplace. Amazon says that over it none of the terms are indexed. Sources: Use search terms effectively (live, every marketplace read except the US; earlier US, UK, IN, JP archives); Keyword attributes explained (live).
- **P-search-terms-5.** cx counts UTF-8 bytes of the whole string with spaces included (`printf %s "$t" | wc -c`) and shows the count with every proposal. Amazon Search does not count spaces or punctuation (every live page read, the UK included; only the 2022 UK archive said it did), while the schema validator counts the whole string, so the whole-string count is the one that satisfies both. Sources: Use search terms effectively (live, US and UK FAQ); Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); C# Example of Meta-Schema v1.
- **P-search-terms-6.** cx never puts brand names (the seller's own or anyone else's), ASINs, profanity, temporary statements, subjective claims or offensive terms in search terms, and tells the seller that Amazon may suppress the ASIN and act on the account for them. Sources: Using Search Terms effectively; Amazon search glossary; Product type schema `generic_keyword` description.
- **P-search-terms-7.** cx spends bytes only on new words. That means no word already in the title or other fields, no repeats, no stop words, one of singular or plural, no punctuation, single spaces between words, and words in the order shoppers type them. Sources: Using Search Terms effectively; Optimize your product discoverability; Improve product visibility through effective Amazon keyword research; Amazon SEO: 7 ways.
- **P-search-terms-8.** cx proposes synonyms, abbreviations and alternate names. It does not add common misspellings or case and spacing variants by default, because Amazon says its systems cover them. Where a seller wants a misspelling, cx labels it a practitioner's bet. Sources: Using Search Terms effectively; Optimize your product discoverability; Semantic Product Search (Nigam et al., KDD 2019).
- **P-search-terms-9.** cx reads the current search terms only from `getListingsItem` `attributes.generic_keyword`, every value for the marketplace, and never from Catalog Items. Sources: SP-API Release Notes (2023-08-16); schema copies (array, `selectors`, `maxUniqueItems`); C# Example of Meta-Schema v1.
- **P-search-terms-10.** A search-terms write replaces the whole top-level `generic_keyword` attribute with the full intended array. It is first sent with `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW`, which must answer `VALID` with no issues. Sources: patchListingsItem.
- **P-search-terms-11.** After the write, cx records the `submissionId`. It then re-reads `getListingsItem` to confirm the stored value equals what was sent and that `issues` is empty; that read, not a remembered figure, is what confirms the write. Before reading any exposure effect it waits out the go-live allowance Amazon's search glossary states at the time of the change, read from the page and quoted with its version, and it tells the seller Amazon says latency "can be slower or faster". Sources: patchListingsItem; Listings Items API; Amazon search glossary (live); My ASIN is unsearchable (live).
- **P-search-terms-12.** cx never presents a term in the field as proof of indexing, because Amazon's machine-learned relevance model may decline to use any supplied keyword. Sources: Using Search Terms effectively (FAQ 7 and Note); Optimize your product discoverability.
- **P-search-terms-13.** cx rules out non-search-term causes first when exposure is missing: a parent ASIN, no browse node, no offer, a future launch date, or a search-suppressed status. Sources: My ASIN is unsearchable; Amazon search glossary; Why Isn't My Listing Showing in Search Results?
- **P-search-terms-14.** For a Brand Registry seller, the effect of a search-terms change is judged on Search Query Performance's per-ASIN impressions (`asinImpressionCount`, `asinImpressionShare`) for the targeted queries, plus orders, over matched baseline and observation periods. Sources: Analytics reports (report type values); sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json.
- **P-search-terms-15.** cx does not fill platinum keywords. It treats target-audience and "other attributes" keyword fields as browse inputs that Amazon said were not indexed by search, and fills any such field only when the current schema has it. Sources: Keyword attributes explained; Amazon search glossary.
- **P-search-terms-16.** cx does not claim to know a competitor's search terms. Keepa and Catalog Items expose only public keyword attributes (item type, target audience, uses), not `generic_keyword`. Sources: SP-API Release Notes (2023-08-16); Keepa Product.java.

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- The safest reading of all the figures is to keep each value, and the total, at or under the smallest indexing figure Amazon states for the marketplace. Today that would be 249 bytes in the US, counted with spaces. This is conservative and costs at most a few words. [seat]
- The "ASIN + keyword" search is a widely used practitioner check. Under semantic retrieval it can show an ASIN for a term that is not in its catalog text, so it is weaker evidence than practitioners claim. Running it from the platform would be scraping result pages, which the product's own rule forbids. If used, the seller runs it in their own browser. [seat]
- The ads search-term report (terms that converted in auto campaigns but are absent from the listing) is the roadmap's source of candidate words, described as a "keyword gap". It was not re-read at the Amazon Ads source in this session. [seat]
- The schema's `maxUniqueItems` of 1000 together with `maxLength` 500 suggests the UI's several boxes are separate array values. Whether Search reads them as one string is unknown (Open question 3). [seat]
- Gap fill 2026-10-14:
  - The over-limit FAQ's wording "none of the Search Terms for that ASIN will be indexed" reads as ASIN-level indexing, while the 2023 release note calls the attribute seller-specific.
