## What this covers

- **Stage.**
  - The **main image** serves **click-through**: it is the picture in the search result, and with title, price, rating and badges it decides whether the shopper clicks.
  - The **rest of the image block** serves **conversion**: secondary images PT01–PT08, the swatch, video in the image block, 360/3D, and A+ imagery further down.
  - An image rule violation can also hit **exposure**. Amazon's listing issues carry a `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` enforcement and the `MISSING_IMAGE` / `INVALID_IMAGE` categories, so a bad or missing image can take the item out of search or off sale.
- **Skills fed.**
  - `listing-review`: diagnosing images against the stage that dropped, what Amazon's answers show about images, which report carries each figure, and which Keepa fields describe a competitor's images and video.
  - `listing-change-log`: an image change has properties a text change lacks. It applies to the ASIN in every marketplace, it needs a fetchable URL, and Amazon processes it asynchronously.
  - `effect-review`: which metric a main-image change is judged on (search click rate, click share against impression share), and which one a gallery change is judged on (conversion).
- **The boundary.** The package's first round changes **text attributes only** (roadmap §2, Jack 10-09). Images and A+ need a publicly fetchable URL, and the seller's tree has none. The skill can **diagnose** images: count them, read pixel sizes, notice a competitor's image change, name a rule a main image breaks, and tie a click-through drop to the main image as one candidate cause. It **cannot produce** images or video, and it should not write image attributes. The day-2 run without a playbook is the warning case. cx proposed a "high-definition" main image carrying a text badge (a rule breach, below) and drafted a PATCH to `/attributes/main_image_url`, which is not an attribute in Amazon's image table (below). It did this without reading the schema.
- **Why a senior operator cares.** [seat] In most categories the main image is likely the largest single lever on search click-through (unverified; no Amazon page read here ranks the search-tile elements). Main-image compliance is enforced by suppression. The gallery is where a shopper who has clicked is converted or lost. An operator expects the AI to know the following: what the rules are, where they live, that images are global across marketplaces, that "ACCEPTED" is not "live", that Amazon's displayed image set can differ from the seller's submission, and that Amazon's own lift figures are marketing claims without method.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### RULES (enforced: policy, schema, suppression)

1. **The main image has a pure white background.** Amazon staff, in a Seller Forums post titled "Product Image Requirements and Best Practices", list "Pure white background (RGB 255,255,255)". They link the "product image guide" (G1881) and "image standards" (G200216080), both on sellercentral.amazon.co.uk [official] (Seller Forums thread "Product Image Requirements and Best Practices", post by TaylorR_Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/13af96ea-6b07-4bf9-8dbe-a13292c2e3b1, read 2026-10-14). Another Amazon staff answer gives the same rule: "Background: Pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255) for MAIN images" [official] (Seller Forums thread "Product Image Aspect Ratio - white side bars", reply by Lola_Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a40898ca-7fe7-43e2-bb35-7965bbb003d4, read 2026-10-14).
2. **Main images that break the background rule get suppressed.** In a thread titled "Suppressed items", sellers report suppression "because background not white enough". Amazon staff (Glenn_Amazon) answers that "Amazon requires a pure white background per the" Product image requirements page. Staff adds that after replacing the background with pure white "you shouldn't have any further suppression" [official] (Seller Forums thread "Suppressed items", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/1c6419f2-dcdc-4f73-af85-c0c1926a6969, read 2026-10-14).
3. **Fill.**
   - Amazon staff: "MAIN image requirement: Product must fill at least 85% of the image frame" [official] (thread "Product Image Aspect Ratio - white side bars", Lola_Amazon, as above).
   - Amazon staff, second thread: "Product should fill 85% or more of the frame" [official] (thread "Product Image Requirements and Best Practices", TaylorR_Amazon).
   - Amazon's public photo guide: "Have the product fill 85% or more of the frame" [official] ("6 tips for taking product photos in 2025", https://sell.amazon.com/blog/product-photos, published Dec 4, 2024, read 2026-10-14).
4. **No text, graphics or watermarks; the actual product; no props.** Amazon staff list:
   - "Exclude additional text, graphics, or watermarks"
   - "Display the actual product being sold (no illustrations)"
   - "No props or accessories not included in the purchase"
   - "Professional quality, well-lit, in focus"

   [official] (thread "Product Image Requirements and Best Practices", TaylorR_Amazon). A fuller list attributed to G1881 is quoted only by **sellers**, not by staff [official-page wording, seller-quoted] (Seller Forums thread "Unable to upload an image despite meeting image requirement.", amazon.ca, https://sellercentral.amazon.ca/seller-forums/discussions/t/759f82d5-d4d3-4eff-bda6-e536390ad295; and thread "Suppressed items", as above; read 2026-10-14). It includes:
   - "Not have text, logos, borders, color blocks, watermarks, or other graphics covering the product or in the background"
   - "Show the entire product within the frame"
   - "Only show the product once in the image"
   - "Not include customer reviews, five-star imagery, claims (for example, free shipping), or seller-specific information"
   - "Not include any Amazon logos or trademarks"
   - "Not include any badges used on Amazon, or … anything confusingly similar to such badges"
   - "Match the product's title"
   - "Products with multiple pieces must be relative in size, with no one piece enlarged."
   - Category-specific main-image items in the same seller-quoted list (amazon.ca thread only): "Not show any part of a mannequin, regardless of the mannequin's appearance."; "Show a single shoe, facing left, at a 45-degree angle (applies to all footwear)."; "Show clothing accessories or multipacks flat with no model."; "Show children's clothing flat with no model." (all images); adult clothing on a standing model (paraphrase); "Not include text or pricing details (Swatch only)."

   The "Suppressed items" thread's seller quote carries only four of these items (white background, 85%, no text/logos/borders, multi-piece size); the full list is the amazon.ca thread's.
5. **Pixel size and format.**
   - Amazon's public guide states images must be "500 to 10,000 pixels on their longest side", in "JPEG, TIFF, PNG, or non-animated GIF file formats", and that "We prefer images larger than 1,000 pixels on each side to allow for zoom, which can enhance sales" [official] ("6 tips for taking product photos in 2025").
   - Amazon staff: "Minimum dimensions: 500 pixels on the longest side" and "Recommended dimensions: 1,000 pixels or larger … (this enables the zoom function)". Also: "Amazon does not mandate a specific aspect ratio", and "Amazon's system automatically adds white padding" to non-square images [official] (thread "Product Image Aspect Ratio - white side bars", Lola_Amazon).
   - A second staff post words it as "Minimum 1000 pixels on the longest side for zoom capability". It also gives "Maximum 10GB file size" and "Colour mode: RGB or CMYK" [official] (TaylorR_Amazon). The same post requires "File names must contain the product identifier (ISBN, UPC, EAN, or JAN)" and links the .co.uk help pages, which reads like a UK media/books image standard; its figures may be category- or marketplace-specific [seat]. The "10GB" figure agrees with the uploader's own error text, which a seller quotes: "Please select a valid file (JPEG,jpeg,JPG,PNG,png,gif,tif,tiff,photostudio,webp , 1 KB - 10485760 KB) to upload." (amazon.ca thread): 10,485,760 KB is 10 × 1024 × 1024 KB, i.e. 10 GB. The uploader string also lists `webp`, which neither Amazon public page read here names. Practitioners' "10 MB" (SupplyKick, below) is their own figure.
   - **The 1,000 px figure is the zoom threshold, not the upload floor.**
6. **At least one image; image rules apply to API writes too.**
   - "Every product on Amazon must have at least one image, and we recommend having at least six" [official] ("6 tips for taking product photos in 2025").
   - The SP-API states as a prerequisite: "All images to comply with Amazon's product image requirements", linking G1881 [official] ("Submit media attributes", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/submit-media, page updated 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14).
7. **The API's image attributes (schema).** "Submit media attributes" has a "Supported image variants" table:

   | Variant | Attribute | Amazon's description |
   |---|---|---|
   | `MAIN` | `main_product_image_locator` | "Primary product image displayed on the detail page" |
   | `PT01`–`PT08` | `other_product_image_locator_{1-8}` | "Additional product images displayed in the image gallery" |
   | `SWCH` | `swatch_product_image_locator` | "Swatch image displayed on variation selectors" |

   The same page says: "Supported image variants and their requirements differ by product type and marketplace. To determine which image attributes are available for your product type, call the getDefinitionsProductType operation." It also says that response "includes all supported image attributes along with their constraints, such as required vs. optional, accepted formats, and resolution requirements." Each value is an array of objects carrying `media_location`, for example `"main_product_image_locator": [ {"media_location": "https://your-bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/main.jpg"} ]` [official] ("Submit media attributes"). The same names appear in the `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` notification schema [official] (`schemas/notifications/BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json`, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models, read 2026-10-14). **The lead is confirmed.** The attribute cx wrote on day 2, `main_image_url`, is not in Amazon's table.
8. **Amazon's own pages disagree on the count.**
   - "Building Listings Management Workflows Guide" says "A main product image and up to nine secondary images can be uploaded using the Listings Items API". It names `main_offer_image_locator` and `other_offer_image_locator_1` as examples of image attributes in the schema [official] (https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/building-listings-management-workflows-guide, read 2026-10-14).
   - "Submit media attributes", updated more recently, lists eight (PT01–PT08). The Catalog Items model's image `variant` enum stops at PT08 [official] (`models/catalog-items-api-model/catalogItems_2022-04-01.json`).
   - sell.amazon.com says "You can upload up to nine photos" [official] ("How to create Amazon product listings", https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-product-listings, Apr 29, 2026, read 2026-10-14). That count includes the main image (item 44), so it agrees with eight secondary slots; the one Amazon page that says nine *secondary* images is the Building guide.
   - **Amazon's pages disagree (the Building guide: main plus nine secondary; "Submit media attributes" and the Catalog Items model: main plus PT01–PT08). The binding count for a write is the product type's schema, read at run time from `getDefinitionsProductType`.** Third-party copies of four Amazon product-type schemas, read in the reconciliation (item 44), each carry `other_product_image_locator_1` to `_8` and no ninth.
   - *Offer* image locators (`main_offer_image_locator`, …) are a different thing from *product* image locators. No Amazon page this seat read explains them; three of the schema copies carry them (item 44, open question 4).
9. **Not every image kind can be written through the API.** "Seller Central's Image Manager supports additional image variants that are not available through the Listings Items API. Only the preceding variants can be submitted programmatically." [official] ("Submit media attributes"). 3D models are uploaded "using the Image manager in Seller Central" or scanned with the Amazon Seller app [official] ("3D models and augmented reality (AR) | Sell on Amazon", https://sell.amazon.com/tools/3d-ar, read 2026-10-14).
10. **Where an image write must point.** Amazon accepts images from:
    - "Publicly accessible Amazon S3 bucket content" or "Publicly accessible Amazon CloudFront distribution content" over HTTP(S)
    - "Private Amazon S3 bucket content downloaded through S3 URL", for which the bucket policy must let the role `arn:aws:iam:XXXXXXXXXXXX:role/Media-Download-Role` call `GetObject` and `ListBucket`
    - "Images hosted on third-party servers that are publicly available to Amazon"

    Also: "Private Amazon S3 content is treated as immutable … New media content requires a new Amazon S3 object key." [official] ("Submit media attributes"). **The lead "needs a publicly reachable URL" is roughly right but incomplete**: a private S3 bucket that grants Amazon's role also works.
11. **Images are global per ASIN.** The Listings APIs FAQ ("Did my image update apply globally?") says the image attributes "map images to ASINs at a global level where the ASIN exists", "even if you specify the marketplace_id selector", consistent with "Add a Product within Seller Central" [official] ("Listings APIs FAQ", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/listings-apis-faq, read 2026-10-14).
12. **What the shopper sees can differ from what the seller sent.** Under the FAQ question "Why are my edits not making it to the detail page even though they appear as successful?", Amazon says that where it already has a listing for a product, "the information submitted by a vendor might not have priority" (the sentence names vendors, not sellers), and "Brand owners have the highest precedence to update data on the product details page on Amazon" [official] ("Listings APIs FAQ"). The heading itself states the general point: a successful write need not reach the detail page. The Catalog Items API returns the displayed images per marketplace. Each image carries `variant` (MAIN, PT01–PT08, SWCH), `link`, `height` and `width` in pixels [official] (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, `ItemImage`). The Listings Items `summaries` carry only `mainImage` (`link`, `height`, `width`) [official] (`models/listings-items-api-model/listingsItems_2021-08-01.json`).
13. **"Accepted" is not finished; image problems arrive later as issues.**
    - "**Accepted** does not mean the process is complete … any of these processes can generate issues that return asynchronously" [official] ("Manage Listings Issues", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/manage-listings-issues, updated 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14).
    - The issue model has categories `MISSING_IMAGE` ("One or more required images are missing") and `INVALID_IMAGE` ("One or more images are invalid").
    - Its enforcement actions include `LISTING_SUPPRESSED`, `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`, `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED` and `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` ("the catalog item is hidden from search results") [official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, `Issue`, `IssueEnforcementAction`).
    - The FAQ puts `getListingsItem` results at "within 1-5 minutes" and the issue/status notifications at "15-20 minute delay" [official] ("Listings APIs FAQ").
14. **Image-change signal for brand owners.** `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` fires "whenever there is a change to the title, description, bullet points, or images, for any ASIN that the selling partner has a brand relationship with". However, "the attributes that contain the word 'image' all mean the same thing right now, which is that one or more images changed", and their number "does not necessarily match the number of images changed" [official] ("Notification Type Values", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, read 2026-10-14; schema description in BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json).
15. **Video in the image block is a Seller Central path, not a Listings Items attribute.**
    - The tools page, under the tool name "Shoppable videos": "Inform and empower customer purchases by adding video to the main image block on your product detail pages." and "Videos may help increase your sales and reduce customer returns." Its "Get started" link goes to Seller Central Creator Hub's upload page (`sellercentral.amazon.com/creatorhub/video/upload`). The page states no eligibility [official] (sell.amazon.com tools index, https://sell.amazon.com/tools, read 2026-10-14).
    - The Listings Items image table has no video variant ("Only the preceding variants can be submitted programmatically") [official] ("Submit media attributes").
    - The Seller Central help page "Upload and manage videos" named in the lead was **not read**; its help id was not found. The gap fill read the *Shoppable video guide* (GUZBYWTTVULY6C9Y v4) instead, which states eligibility, specifications and timelines (item 49).
16. **The API's only video path is A+.**
    - The A+ Content API gained `createMedia`, `getMedia` and `updateMedia` "to create image and video media assets … used by Premium video modules" [official] ("SP-API Release Notes", September 30, 2026 entry, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/sp-api-release-notes, read 2026-10-14).
    - Media is uploaded through the Uploads API (`createUploadDestinationForResource`) and then registered. "Videos must comply with video content requirements". Statuses run `PENDING_PROCESSING` → `PENDING_REVIEW` → `AVAILABLE`, or else `UNABLE_TO_PROCESS` / `NOT_APPROVED`. "The `mediaUrl` value is non-permanent" [official] ("Create, Edit, and Retrieve A+ Media Assets", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/create-edit-retrieve-aplus-media, updated 2026-09-30, read 2026-10-14).
    - "Processing and moderation can take up to 48 hours". A document whose video fails "is not published". A video-pairing title needs "at least 3 words" and must not be all uppercase [official] ("A+ Content Troubleshooting Guide", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/a-plus-troubleshooting, read 2026-10-14).
    - Premium video module minimum sizes are in "A+ Premium Modules" (for example `PREMIUM_HERO_VIDEO`) [official] (https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/a-plus-premium-modules, updated 2026-09-30, read 2026-10-14). They belong to the A+ element's file.
    - The FAQ still says "A+ premium content is not supported", which the September 30, 2026 release supersedes. **The FAQ is stale on this point.**
17. **The old image feed is gone.** `POST_PRODUCT_IMAGE_DATA`, with the other legacy XML and flat-file feeds, had its removal date moved "from June 30, 2025 to July 31, 2025". Amazon points to the JSON listings workflows instead [official] ("SP-API Release Notes", 2025 deprecation notice). A playbook must not route images through the XML image feed. `JSON_LISTINGS_FEED` can carry images for many SKUs [official] ("Submit media attributes").
18. **Schema metadata changes monthly.** Amazon publishes a product-type metadata change file per month (32 entries): September 28, 2026; August 31, 2026; July 27, 2026; and back to January 19, 2024 [official] ("SP-API Product Metadata Updates", https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/sp-api-metadata-updates, read 2026-10-14). Image attribute constraints sit in those schemas, so they can move month to month.

### GUIDANCE (recommendation, not enforced)

19. **Gallery content.** Amazon suggests individual, group, lifestyle, scale, detailed, packaging and 360 shots. It also suggests "standard dead-on front shot, back shots, side shots, overhead, closeups, and 45-degree shots". Lifestyle shots "tell the story behind your product". Amazon adds that "Many customers shop on their phones" [official] ("6 tips for taking product photos in 2025"). It says images should be "sharp and clear, ideally in high resolution", against "a simple backdrop" [official] ("How to create Amazon product listings").
20. **3D / AR.** View in 3D, View in Your Room, and Virtual Try-On for shoes and eyewear are free, shown "for enabled products" in the app. Amazon says Virtual Try-On "can increase customer engagement and reduce returns", giving no figure [official] ("3D models and augmented reality (AR) | Sell on Amazon"). The photo guide adds a figure without method: "customers who viewed a listing with a 3D model were 2x more likely to make a purchase" [official] ("6 tips for taking product photos in 2025").
21. **Testing images.** "Manage Your Experiments" can "test different versions of your product images, titles, bullet points, descriptions, and A+ Content". Its requirements and outputs:
    - It needs a Professional account, Brand Representative status in Brand Registry, and a product that "has received enough traffic in recent weeks".
    - Amazon recommends "8 to 10 weeks", or "as soon as four weeks" in the to-significance mode.
    - It reports "units sold, sales, conversion rate, units sold per unique visitor, and sample size" and "the projected one-year impact" [official] ("Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments | Sell on Amazon", https://sell.amazon.com/tools/manage-your-experiments, read 2026-10-14).
    - The SP-API documentation index contains no page or operation about experiments [official] (https://developer-docs.amazon/llms.txt, read 2026-10-14). **The lead "MYE supports main image tests" is confirmed** as "product images". The page does not single out "main".
22. **Amazon's lift figures and their basis.** None of these are usable as an expected range:
    - Optimized content can "increase sales by up to 20%" (MYE page). The Brand Registry page, under its "Manage Your Experiments" heading, says "implementing changes to your listings based on optimized content can increase sales by up to 25%" for the same thing. Both footnote "Amazon internal data", and the two Amazon pages disagree; both were re-opened in the reconciliation and both figures stand. The 25% is Amazon's own Brand Registry page, not only a practitioner's figure.
    - "Basic A+ Content can increase sales by up to 8%" and Premium "up to 20%" ("Amazon internal data").
    - Sponsored Products with video: "+67% higher click-through-rate (CTR)" and "+9% higher conversion rate (CVR)", footnoted "Amazon internal data, US, June 2026." [official] ("Sponsored Products - Help increase product sales | Amazon Ads", https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-products, read 2026-10-14).
    - AI lifestyle images in a mobile Sponsored Brands ad: CTR "can be 40% higher compared to ads with standard product images", with no method given [official] ("Amazon rolls out AI-powered image generation to help advertisers deliver a better ad experience for customers", aboutamazon.com, undated, read 2026-10-14).
23. **Amazon's 2025–2026 AI imagery is an ads tool, not a main-image rule change.** The image generator Amazon describes is for **advertisers** (lifestyle backgrounds for ads). Its article ties it to a Sponsored Brands ad, not the detail page [official] (same aboutamazon page). The seller-side generative tool creates listing text "from a few words, image, or existing listings" [official] ("How to create Amazon product listings"; "Amazon launches new generative AI feature for sellers to create product descriptions", aboutamazon.com, undated). **The lead about a 2025 "main image AI generation" or "lifestyle in main image" test was not found** in any page this seat read. It is not refuted, only unfound.

### Gap fill: Amazon's live help pages and the read-back route (read 2026-10-14)

Each help page below was read through the content call described under the title. "v" is the page's `topic.version` as served. RULE and GUIDANCE keep the meanings above.

**The image rules themselves (G1881 and its satellites)**

24. **Technical rules, G1881 v28.** These are RULES: a file that fails them "cannot be uploaded to our system".
    - Formats: "JPEG (.jpg or .jpeg), TIFF (.tif or .tiff), PNG (.png), non-animated GIF (.gif)", "JPEG recommended".
    - Size: "Minimum 500 pixels and maximum Maximum 10,000 pixels on the longest side" (Amazon's own doubled word); "Images below 500 pixels on the longest side cannot be uploaded. Do not artificially enlarge small images."
    - Resolution: "At least 72 dpi". Quality: "Clear, non-pixelated, no jagged edges". Color mode: "RGB", marked "Preferred".
    - GUIDANCE on the same page: "We recommend 1,000+ pixels for the best customer experience. Images with 1,000+ pixels on the longest side enable the zoom function."
    - File naming ("Product identifier + variant code + file extension", for example `B000123456.MAIN.jpg`) is marked "Optional" and applies to bulk upload.
    - `webp` (in the uploader's error string, item 5) is not on G1881's list.

    [official] (Product image guide, G1881 v28, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1881, read 2026-10-14; text saved at capture `G1881-v28`)
25. **MAIN image rules, G1881 v28.** These are RULES. Verbatim:
    - "Accurately represent the product as a realistic, professional-quality image."
    - "No placeholders", because "the first image uploaded defines the product identity."
    - "Show the product as 85% of the image."
    - "Show the entire product within the frame of the image."
    - "Only show the product once in the image."
    - "Show only one unit of the product and any accessories that are included with the product."
    - "Have a pure white background (RGB color values: 255, 255, 255)".
    - "Only a limited number of product types are allowed to have a lifestyle image as a main image (with no text or extra logos)."
    - "Don't include packaging, unless it's an important product feature".
    - "Don't show accessories or any props that aren't included with the product".
    - "Don't show any part of a mannequin".
    - "Show a single shoe, facing left, at a 45-degree angle (applies to all footwear)."
    - "Show clothing accessories or multipacks flat, without a model."
    - Adult clothing on a model, who "must be standing".

    The live v28 MAIN section does **not** carry the line "Not have text, logos, borders, color blocks, watermarks, or other graphics covering the product or in the background" that sellers quoted in item 4. That line survives on the *Text, logo, graphics, and watermarks* page as a quote of "our Product image requirements" (item 27). The seller-quoted list is therefore an older G1881 version, and its rule is still enforced through item 27. [official] (Product image guide, G1881 v28)
26. **Rules for all images, G1881 v28.** These are RULES. Every image must:
    - "Match the product's title."
    - Carry "No images of customer reviews, five-star imagery, claims (for example, free shipping), or selling partner-specific information."
    - Carry "No text or pricing details (swatch image only)."
    - Carry no Amazon logos or trademarks ("Amazon," "Prime," "Alexa," the Smile).
    - Carry no Amazon badges or look-alikes ("Amazon's Choice," "Premium Choice," … "Best seller," or "Top seller").

    Children's and baby form-fitting items are shot flat without models. Enforcement has three tiers:
    - "Images that fail to meet technical requirements cannot be uploaded to our system."
    - Images that fail the content requirements "may be removed".
    - "If there is no main image that meets the requirements, we may temporarily remove the product listing from search until a compliant main image is provided."

    [official] (Product image guide, G1881 v28)
27. **Text, logos and claims: the scope differs by page.** RULE.
    - The *Text, logo, graphics, and watermarks* page says this rule applies "to the main image and swatch". It allows graphics on device screens if they "look realistic". It says "Synthetic images that represent the product realistically and are indistinguishable from a good product photo are not text, logo, graphics, and watermarks". It bars "composites of multiple images of products shown at different scale" and "Additional views of the product … on the main image" [official] (Text, logo, graphics, and watermarks, GB6V3RU3M5XG24GC v4, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GB6V3RU3M5XG24GC, read 2026-10-14).
    - The *Prohibited badging* page reaches **every** image: "Claims about the product such as warranties, regulatory approval, or promotions are not allowed in any product image. This includes badging, such as stamps of approval, logos, or text that explains product features or quantity, shipping options, copyright notices, email addresses, or watermarks of any kind." It names words an image "must not contain": "APPROVED, CERTIFIED, FDA, DOT, USDA, EPA, UL, NSF, GLUTEN, LEAD FREE, ENERGY STAR, ISO, FC, FCC". It lists "prohibited badge text strings" (among them "best", "free", "sale", "deal", "guarantee", "warranty", "recommend", "review", "safe", "value", "save", "ship", "stock"). It warns that sellers who add such content "are subject to additional enforcement actions" [official] (Prohibited badging and abusive and banned words and graphics, GQ2VU6MXD8H6VGSU v1, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GQ2VU6MXD8H6VGSU, read 2026-10-14).
    - [seat] The two pages together settle the infographic question only partly. Plain text on PT images is not barred: the text rule is main-and-swatch. A claim, a certification word or a listed badge string is barred on every image. Whether "text that explains product features" reaches an ordinary feature callout or only badge-styled text is not settled by the wording; the sentence's subject is "badging".
28. **Background, fill and props, as Amazon's reason pages define them.** RULE.
    - *Non-white background*: the attribute is present if the background "is not pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255) with minimal shadows", or if "There is a visual frame, line, or border on any portion of the edges". "Shadows that run to the edge of the image frame … are not allowed." Leftover off-white or gray from background removal is not allowed either. "Alternate images may have a neutral or environment-based background." [official] (Non-white background, G75PWC4THA8J269P v3, read 2026-10-14)
    - *Product too small*: "The product must occupy at least 85% of the image area … without cropping the product or letting shadows run to the edge of the image." [official] (Product Too Small, GKPLUEFR7TJ9EDAN v1, read 2026-10-14)
    - *Propping*: "Additional elements that are not part of the product being sold must not be added to main images." [official] (Propping, G5SD576VXDFLCR32 v1, read 2026-10-14)
29. **Lifestyle main images are allowed for six product types only.** RULE: "We allow for a lifestyle image to be the main image in limited product types. These are: BED, BED_FRAME, MATTRESS, RUG, RUG_PAD, SOFA. We continuously review these matters." Category style guides are PDFs downloaded from the "Inventory file templates, style guides, and browse tree guides (BTG)" page [official] (Product page style guides, G200270100 v18, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200270100, read 2026-10-14).
    - The one style guide that page links directly, the 2018 *Quick Start Style Guide* (© Amazon Commercial Services Pty Ltd, Australia), still says "at least 80 percent of the image area" and "at least 1,000 dpi". Those figures disagree with G1881 v28 (85%; 72 dpi; 1,000 *pixels* for zoom) [official, dated] (Selling on Amazon Quick Start Style Guide 2018, https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/rainier/help/Selling_on_Amazon_Quick_Start_Style_Guide_2018.pdf, read 2026-10-14).
    - [seat] This old guide is the likely origin of practitioners' "80%" (Helium 10, below).
30. **Technical file requirements.** CMYK is "Not preferred. Convert to RGB before uploading." Grayscale is "Acceptable only for … gray or silver metal products." The page says "Do not rely on ICC color profiles". EPS, BMP, PDF and application files (PSD and others) are not accepted. Amazon "reserve[s] the right to reject images that do not meet our content standards, even if the images meet the technical formatting specifications." [official] (Technical image file requirements, G9FUUH87RBNXGKB7 v4, read 2026-10-14)
31. **Category rules read.**
    - Clothing (RULE): the main image is on a model, with exceptions for children's form-fitting items and accessories. The "Minimum aspect ratio is close to 3:4 (width/height) and 85% of the total image area occupied". "Every parent and child product must have a main image". "Children's wear images at a resolution less than 1,000 pixels" are prohibited. The prohibited list for clothing main images includes "Borders, logos, watermarks, text, color blocks, inset images, or other graphics" and "Folded products" [official] (Image guidelines for clothing, G200498950 v8, read 2026-10-14).
    - Multipacks (RULE): "The main image must show the total quantity that will be delivered", with the product in its packaging. The title must carry the total quantity. Amazon's own pack-quantity sticker "will not appear in search", and imitating it brings "image removal or search suppression" [official] (Multipack imaging standards, G94GSFMC79RSLDBY v5, read 2026-10-14).
32. **AI-generated people must be tagged.** RULE: "If your image includes photorealistic AI-generated people, you must tag that image with the appropriate metadata before uploading". The tag is the keyword `contains-synthetic-performer` in the XMP `dc:subject` field, after which "Amazon will add a disclosure". The page names three cases where the tag is not used: real people altered with AI, characters from expressive works, and no people [official] (Product image guide, G1881 v28). The same rule covers video [official] (Shoppable video guide, GUZBYWTTVULY6C9Y v4). The tagging page gives exiftool, macOS Preview and Windows steps [official] (How to tag media that contains an AI-generated person, GFXHCHYZRGJRBZA5 v1, read 2026-10-14). No other rule on AI-made product images was found. Read with item 27's "indistinguishable from a good product photo" sentence, a realistic synthetic image is not barred as such [seat].

**Suppression: where it shows and what Amazon calls it**

33. **Image suppression.** RULE.

    - The reasons table names the image each reason applies to. Text, logo, graphics or watermarks; non-white background; image missing; additional items; cropped product; packaging or tags; and propping apply to "Any main image". Blur or pixelation, product too small and prurience apply to "Any image". Multiple views apply to Apparel; human model to Softlines other than adult apparel; mannequin or hanger to Apparel or Accessories; non-standing model to Apparel.

**How a submitted image becomes (or does not become) the displayed image**

37. **Upload is checked twice and processed asynchronously.** "Immediately after upload, Amazon checks file format, permissions, and offer status". Then "Next, we perform additional checks for technical compliance and product image requirements. Processing can take up to 24 hours." [official] (Upload images, GUGPCYWV2P8KVFUT v12, read 2026-10-14). G1881 v28 adds that "If your image is selected for display, it can take up to 24 hours after you upload it to appear on the product detail page." The *Add product images and video* page gives the same 24-hour figure [official] (Add product images and video, G200216080 v15, read 2026-10-14).
38. **Display is Amazon's choice, not the seller's.**
    - "Uploading an image doesn't guarantee it will be displayed on the product detail page. We select and arrange images from multiple selling partners". Also: "We may also modify images that you submit" [official] (G1881 v28).
    - The display-issues page goes further: "We may select images different from those you provided, including images that are different from those provided by sellers with a selling role (Brand Representative or Reseller) assigned in the brand." It adds: "If your product has multiple sizes or colors (a variation family), we automatically select images from one option to display across all size variations of that color or style." [official] (Image upload errors and display issues, G17811 v20, read 2026-10-14)
    - This qualifies item 12. The FAQ's "Brand owners have the highest precedence" is about detail-page *data*, and for images Amazon reserves the choice even against brand-role sellers.
39. **Seller Central shows who won; the API does not.**
    - In Image Manager "Each image tile will display the image status on the product detail page (Live, Not Live or Status Unknown)". If the live image's uploader "is not under your partner account, we don't share any identity information and notify you that the live image is selected from another selling partner".
    - "Live images" previews the displayed set "in the same order they appear on the product page".
    - "The order of the images displayed for your products in Image Manager may not be the same as what is shown on the product detail page."
    - "You can't delete the main image because all ASINs require a main image", and "Images shared between child ASINs must be deleted individually from each ASIN."

    [official] (Manage Images, G7F9QAFM53E8C3X3 v2, read 2026-10-14). No SP-API operation or report among the pages read returns this Live/Not Live status or the winning contributor [official, by absence: Listings Items and Catalog Items models; Report Type Values – Inventory Reports].
40. **Contribution conflicts have their own errors.** RULE.
    - "Authoritative image already exists": "A preferred image already exists for this ASIN and variant … Your image will not be accepted for this variant."
    - "Image with identical ASIN and variant submitted": "The newer image takes precedence."
    - "Resubmission of previously accepted or rejected images": "Do not upload the same file multiple times." The page adds: "If you upload the same set of images as a previous submission, we may detect it as a duplicate and your contribution will remain unchanged."
    - Error 990003: "The MAIN image that you have submitted shows a product that is different than the product represented by the ASIN's existing MAIN image". Its reasons include "Our image recognition detected significant visual differences … such as differences in product category, shape, color, size, or brand" [official] (G17811 v20; Error 990003, GJBM8ZUVXP7J2DVP v3, read 2026-10-14).
    - Error 100239: "The title and the image that you provided on this SKU don't seem to represent the same product" [official] (Image and title do not match (error 100239), GCNDJ7XFEQWDJ73E v4, read 2026-10-14).
    - [seat] For a main-image change, a new main image that looks too different from the existing one can be refused as a different product (990003). A redesign of the main image is not a free move.
41. **Global and country-specific images are both possible in Seller Central.**
    - "Using the Upload Images tool, you can upload your images to the Global store or to a specific country store." "To upload images for the US store, use the Global option."
    - A country-specific set must include MAIN, or "none of the submitted images will be considered for display in that country".
    - "Subsequent global image uploads will not override your country-specific images."
    - "If your images contain text, it must be in the country's official language or English."

    [official] (Upload images, GUGPCYWV2P8KVFUT v12). The *Add product images and video* page says images added through Add products go "to Global/US regardless of the account" [official] (G200216080 v15). This sits beside item 11 (the API maps images globally "even if you specify the `marketplace_id` selector"). [seat] Through the API, an image write is global. A country-specific set made in Seller Central is not overridden by a later global write, so the API write may not change what a non-US store shows.
42. **The inventory-file URL route has its own rules, and one conflicts with the API page.** G17811 v20 says "You must use a direct URL. Pop-up or redirecting URLs will not be accepted". Error 15 says "You can use http, but not https, ftp, or "file" protocols" [official] (Invalid URL format (error 15), G30601 v5, read 2026-10-14). "Submit media attributes" (updated 2026-09-09) accepts S3 and CloudFront content "downloaded through HTTP or HTTPS URL". [seat] Error 15 is an inventory-file page and older; for a Listings Items write the API page governs. The US, AU and ES schema copies (item 44) give `media_location` as `"type": "string", "format": "uri", "pattern": "^(https?|s3)://"`, which admits https and s3; the DE copy gives only `"type": "string"`. None of the four carries a file-format list or a pixel or resolution constraint [official, third-party copy]. Error 18893 (item 34) adds that a URL behind hotlink protection fails later as "Copyright Image Misuse".

**Video, 3D/360, testing, Lens**

49. **Video in the image block: eligibility, specs and timing (GUZBYWTTVULY6C9Y v4).**

## What practitioners add

All [opinion]:

- **Visible slots.** Brian Connolly (Jungle Scout, "Amazon Product Images Requirements & Best Practices", April 5, 2024) says up to 9 images can be uploaded but "only the first 7 images (or 6 if your listing features a product video) will be shown on your actual live listing". UFO 3D (Ian Diev, "Amazon Image Requirements: Sizes, Formats and Slot Rules", undated; a 3D-rendering vendor whose post promotes its own rendering services) says "with six or seven visible before the shopper clicks through". Amazon's pages read here state no visible-slot count. *Practitioners agree with each other and are unconfirmed by Amazon.*
- **What the gallery should contain.** Practitioners converge on the same stack with different orders.
  - Jungle Scout (Connolly, 2024): angles, lifestyle, infographics/text overlays, comparison charts, zoomed features, cross-sections, before/after, instructional.
  - SupplyKick ("Amazon Product Image Requirements: Rules and Best Practices", published Jun 24, 2020, updated Sep 14, 2026): main, lifestyle, key features, what's included, dimensions/fit, materials/details, "objection handling".
  - Amazon's own guide does not mention infographics or text overlays on secondary images (see 19). Practitioners treat them as standard, and UFO 3D says text and callouts on secondary images are allowed, "provided the claims are accurate". The seller-quoted G1881 list (rule 4) bars "customer reviews, five-star imagery, claims (for example, free shipping)" and Amazon badges on **all** product images, not only the main one, which narrows what an infographic may carry. *No Amazon page read here confirms or forbids text on secondary images.*
- **Resolution.**
  - Helium 10 (Lailama Hasan, "Amazon Product Photography…", Aug 23, 2023): upload minimum 500 px, zoom 1,000 px, "Amazon suggests 1600px", author recommends 2,500 px.
  - SupplyKick: a "Minimum:" of 1,000 px on the longest side, 2,000 px or larger "Recommended:", and a file size of no more than 10 MB (paraphrase of its specification list).
  - UFO 3D: 1,000 px minimum and 1,600 px recommended.
  - **Disagreement:** SupplyKick and UFO 3D call 1,000 px the minimum, while Amazon's public guide says 500 px minimum and 1,000 px for zoom.
- **Fill.** Helium 10 gives both 85% and "at least 80% of the screen" without reconciling them. Feedvisor (Marissa Incitti, "Amazon Product Images: Requirements, Best Practices, and What Actually Moves Conversions", 2017, updated May 22, 2026) claims "Books, Music, and Video/DVD require 100% frame fill". Feedvisor also says products that look full "often fill only 60-70% of the frame when measured precisely". The 100% claim is uncited and was not confirmed at an Amazon source.
- **Suppression mechanics.** Feedvisor describes enforcement as automated with no seller notification. It lists triggers (off-white, text/logos, under 85% fill, under 1,000 px) and gives a Seller Central audit path ("Inventory > Manage All Inventory > … > Manage Images"), with recovery in "24-48 hours". SupplyKick: "Amazon will suppress listings with images that violate platform rules". It adds, unsourced, that a background value of 254 "may" be flagged. *The "under 1,000 px suppresses" claim conflicts with Amazon's 500 px floor.*
- **3D renders in the main slot.** UFO 3D calls photoreal 3D in the main image "a grey area". Feedvisor says renders and mockups are not allowed in the main image. Amazon staff: "Display the actual product being sold (no illustrations)". *The practitioners disagree with each other. Amazon's wording forbids illustrations and does not mention renders.*
- **Video.**
  - Connolly (Jungle Scout, "Amazon Product Videos: The Ultimate FBA Seller Guide", Feb 28, 2024): "anyone can upload a video to a product listing". Brand Registry sellers can attach one video to up to 300 ASINs, others to one. The path is "Upload and Manage Videos" under Catalog, and the video shows under the main images.
  - Feedvisor says listings allow up to seven videos and one gallery slot can be a video.
  - Both are unconfirmed at an Amazon page here.
  - Prohibited video content per Jungle Scout: promotional or time-sensitive language, discount codes, defamatory statements about competitors (it suggests saying "our competitors" instead), health claims against Amazon's terms, and links that send viewers off Amazon.
- **Effect figures, all unsourced.** Feedvisor: professional images "up to 30%" higher conversion, lifestyle images "15-30% more clicks", main images under 85% fill "CTR reduced by up to 23%", video "15-30% conversion lift". Helium 10: images "increase conversion rates by up to 70%". Jungle Scout: "96% of consumers stated they find product videos helpful when making purchases online" (survey, no method shown). None of these cite a study, and they should not become expected ranges.
- **Practitioners and Amazon agree on one thing:** Amazon's Seller Central version of the rules wins over any guide. UFO 3D says the Seller Central version "always wins". Amazon's API pages link G1881 as the standard.
- **Gap fill: where the live help pages settle a practitioner dispute.**
  - *1,000 px.* SupplyKick's and UFO 3D's "1,000 px minimum" and Feedvisor's "under 1,000 px" suppression trigger match Amazon's Error 18203 ("at least 1000 pixels on the longest side for most product types … rejected"). They do not match G1881 v28 (500 px upload floor; 1,000 px recommended). Each side has an Amazon page behind it (item 34).
  - *80% vs 85%.* Helium 10's "at least 80%" matches Amazon's 2018 Quick Start Style Guide, not the live G1881 (85%) (item 29).
  - *Seven videos / 300 ASINs.* Amazon's live video guide states neither figure. It states eligibility (account active more than three months), specs and timelines instead (item 49).
  - *Renders in the main image.* Amazon's TLGW page says synthetic images "indistinguishable from a good product photo" are not text, logo, graphics or watermarks (item 27). UFO 3D's "grey area" and Feedvisor's "not allowed" both overstate a clear answer. The test is realism, and G1881 still requires "a realistic, professional-quality image".
- **Amazon Lens.** Dilip Vamanan (SellerApp, "What Is Amazon Lens Live and Why Your Product Images Now Matter More Than Keywords", October 6, 2026) [opinion] says Lens converts the camera image into "a visual fingerprint" compared against catalog images. He advises a pure white hero image with the product as the main element, lifestyle images "only in the secondary slots", a 45-degree shot and close-ups. He cites no Amazon source for how matching uses listing images; Amazon's own page says only "a deep learning visual embedding model" (item 52). *The headline claim (images matter more than keywords) is the author's, unsupported by Amazon's text.*

## Published research

All [research]:

- **Zhang, Lee, Singh, Srinivasan, "What Makes a Good Image? Airbnb Demand Analytics Leveraging Interpretable Image Features", *Management Science* 68(8):5644–5666, 2022** (DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2021.4175; abstract read via Semantic Scholar's record).
  - Finding: with difference-in-differences on 7,423 properties over 16 months, "properties with verified images had 8.98% higher occupancy" than those with host-taken images. For twelve interpretable attributes of composition, color and figure-ground relationship, "most of the correlations" with demand were "significant and in the theorized direction" (correlations, not the causal DiD estimate).
  - Transfer: narrow. Airbnb sells experiential goods, the photos are of rooms, and the treatment is professional photography as a bundle. The result supports "image quality moves demand on a marketplace". It gives no size for an Amazon main-image change, and it does not separate click-through from conversion.
- **Di, Sundaresan, Piramuthu, Bhardwaj, "Is a picture really worth a thousand words? On the role of images in e-commerce", WSDM 2014, pp. 633–642** (DOI 10.1145/2556195.2556226; abstract read via OpenAlex).
  - Finding: on "a well-known online marketplace" (the abstract does not name it; all four authors are listed at eBay in OpenAlex), image aspects "such as quantity and quality" can be leveraged "to effectively raise sale". The study looks at image impact "across various dimensions: product categories, user segments, conversion rate". In its joint model of "watch" behavior, the image "still holds an important role … especially for products for which appearance is important".
  - Transfer: moderate on direction (count and quality matter; category-dependence). It is observational, and eBay listings are seller-shot and auction-era. No Amazon effect size.
- **Zakrewsky, Aryafar, Shokoufandeh, "Item Popularity Prediction in E-commerce Using Image Quality Feature Vectors", arXiv 1605.03663, 2016** (Etsy; abstract read via OpenAlex).
  - Finding: pixel-level image-quality features combined with text predict listing popularity (search clicks, favorites, purchases) better than text alone.
  - Transfer: predictive, not causal. It shows image quality carries signal about clicks on a marketplace.
- **Ge et al., "Image Matters: Visually modeling user behaviors using Advanced Model Server", arXiv 1711.06505, 2017** (Taobao; abstract read via OpenAlex).
  - Finding: adding images of the user's past behavior to CTR prediction improved online and offline evaluation in Taobao display advertising.
  - Transfer: only that large marketplaces' ranking and CTR models can use image content. It says nothing about Amazon's organic ranking.
- **Chaudhuri et al., "A Smart System for Selection of Optimal Product Images in E-Commerce", IEEE BigData 2018, pp. 1728–1736** (DOI 10.1109/bigdata.2018.8622259; abstract read via OpenAlex; authors listed at Walmart).
  - Finding: a retailer-side system that picks an image set "with optimal image count and quality" and orders it. The impact on engagement metrics was measured, but the abstract gives no figure.
  - Transfer: image count, quality and order are treated as levers by a large retailer. No effect size.
- **Kohavi, Tang, Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*, Cambridge University Press, 2020** (DOI 10.1017/9781108653985), and **Kohavi, Longbotham, Sommerfield, Henne, "Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide", *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 18(1):140–181, online 2008** (DOI 10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1); abstracts read via OpenAlex.
  - Finding (2008 abstract): controlled experiments "embody the best scientific design for establishing a causal relationship between changes and their influence on user-observable behavior". The survey names statistical power, sample size, variance reduction and randomization as the areas critical to running them. (The book's content was not read beyond its bibliographic record.)
  - Transfer: direct to the choice between Manage Your Experiments, a randomized split, and before/after reading. A main-image change judged before/after (effect-review) is weaker evidence than an MYE result, and the skill should say so.
- **De, Hu, Rahman, "Product-Oriented Web Technologies and Product Returns: An Exploratory Study", *Information Systems Research* 24(4):998–1010, 2013** (DOI 10.1287/isre.2013.0487; abstract read via OpenAlex, gap fill).
  - Finding: on one women's-clothing retailer's product-level usage data, "zoom usage has a negative coefficient, suggesting that a higher use of the zoom technology is associated with fewer returns". "A higher usage of alternative photos is associated with more returns and perhaps more importantly, with lower net sales". "Color swatch … does not seem to have any effect on returns." These are econometric associations, not experiments.
  - Transfer: low to moderate, and the only paper here that bears on returns rather than demand. It supports the zoom threshold mattering beyond clicks. It warns that a gallery change can move returns in either direction, so an effect review of a gallery change should look at returns as well as orders. One retailer, apparel only.
- **Gap fill on two records another file calls unreadable.** The publisher pages of Di et al. 2014 (ACM) and Zhang et al. 2022 (INFORMS) answer 403. Their abstracts are open through OpenAlex (`api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145/2556195.2556226`) and Semantic Scholar (`api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1287/mnsc.2021.4175`), and both were re-opened on 2026-10-14. The findings above are quoted from those abstracts. Semantic Scholar records Zhang et al.'s year as 2021 (online first); the issue is 68(8), 2022.
- **Not read:** Zhang, Mehta, Singh, Srinivasan, "Do Lower-Quality Images Lead to Higher Demand on Airbnb?", SSRN 4588974, 2023. Only the title and authors were seen (Crossref/Semantic Scholar record with no abstract), and its finding is not stated here. The gap fill re-checked OpenAlex (record W4388053496): it still has no abstract.

## Stable enough for a skill

Each sentence holds regardless of date and seller.

- The main image is judged on the click-through stage. Secondary images, video and 3D are judged on conversion. A main-image rule breach can also cost exposure through `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`. (Listings Items model `IssueEnforcementAction`; Search Catalog Performance report schema; roadmap §1.1.)
- Amazon's main-image rules are policy and are enforced by suppression: a pure white background, the product filling most of the frame, no added text, logos, badges or watermarks, the actual product, no props. The exact figures live on Seller Central help G1881. (Seller Forums staff posts by TaylorR_Amazon, Lola_Amazon and Glenn_Amazon; "6 tips for taking product photos in 2025".)
- A main image with a text badge or a claim on it breaks Amazon's rules, whatever it does for clicks. (Same sources.)
- Image attributes in a write are `main_product_image_locator`, `other_product_image_locator_N` and `swatch_product_image_locator`, each holding `media_location` values. Which of them a product type takes, and their constraints, come from `getDefinitionsProductType`. ("Submit media attributes".)
- An image write needs a URL Amazon can fetch: public S3/CloudFront/third-party, or private S3 that grants Amazon's download role. A file inside the seller's box has no such URL. ("Submit media attributes".)
- Images set through the Listings Items API apply to the ASIN in every marketplace where it exists, whatever marketplace is named. ("Listings APIs FAQ".)
- The images a shopper sees are the Catalog's images, which can differ from what the seller submitted. Brand owners' contributions take precedence. ("Listings APIs FAQ"; Catalog Items model.)
- A write's `ACCEPTED` is not the end. Image problems come back later as issues (`MISSING_IMAGE`, `INVALID_IMAGE`) and can carry suppression. ("Manage Listings Issues"; Listings Items model.)
- Video in the image block, 3D models and some image kinds are uploaded in Seller Central, not through the Listings Items API. The API's video path is the A+ media library. ("Submit media attributes"; sell.amazon.com tools and 3D pages; "Create, Edit, and Retrieve A+ Media Assets".)
- Amazon's published lift figures for content, A+ and video rest on "Amazon internal data" with no method, and two Amazon pages give different figures for the same claim. They are not an expected range for a change. (MYE page; Brand Registry page; A+ page; Sponsored Products page.)
- Manage Your Experiments can test product images for brand-registered sellers with enough traffic, and it has no SP-API operation. Its results reach cx only through files the seller exports. (MYE page; SP-API docs index.)

## What cx must read at run time

- **Which image attributes this product type takes, and their constraints.** `getDefinitionsProductType` (Product Type Definitions 2020-09-01) for the SKU's product type and marketplace, with `requirements=LISTING`. The schema is the authority on how many secondary slots exist, whether the main image is required, and any format or size constraints. Do not state counts from memory. The schema changes monthly ("SP-API Product Metadata Updates").
- **What the shopper sees.**
  - `getCatalogItem` (Catalog Items 2022-04-01) with `includedData=images` gives, per marketplace, each image's `variant` (MAIN, PT01…PT08, SWCH), `link`, `height` and `width`. That supports counting the gallery, reading the main image's pixel size against Amazon's zoom guidance, and comparing with competitors.
  - The same call works for any ASIN, competitors included.
- **What the seller submitted, and its health.**
  - `getListingsItem` (Listings Items 2021-08-01) with `includedData=summaries,issues,attributes`. `summaries.mainImage` gives the main image's link and size. `issues` gives categories `MISSING_IMAGE` / `INVALID_IMAGE` and enforcement actions (`SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`, `LISTING_SUPPRESSED`, `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`, `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED`).
  - Read it again a few minutes after any write, and again later: issues are asynchronous.
- **A dry run of an image write**, when one is ever sanctioned: `patchListingsItem` or `putListingsItem` with `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW`. This changes nothing and returns issues. Amazon fetches the image URL asynchronously, so a preview may not exercise the download (open question).
- **Did images change on the seller's own brand ASIN?**
  - Notification `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` (Notifications API) says that at least one image changed, not which.
  - Without a subscription, compare successive `getCatalogItem` image `link` values.
- **Did a competitor change images or video?**
  - Keepa product object `images[]`, where each entry has `l`, `lH`, `lW` for the large file and `m`, `mH`, `mW` for the medium. A changed filename means a changed image [seat]. Diff successive saved observations; Product.java documents no image-history field, only the current `images`. `imagesCSV` is marked "@deprecated use the field images instead".
  - `videos[]` carries `title`, `image`, `duration`, `creator` (one of `Main`, `Customer`, `Seller`, `Influencer`, `Vendor`, `ThirdParty`, `Amazon`, `Merchant`, `Brand`), `name` and `url`, and covers "all image carousel videos and up to 10 community videos".
  - `aPlus[]` holds modules with text and image.
  - `videos` and `aPlus` need the raw route `GET /product` with `videos=1` / `aplus=1` and, for live data, `offers`. The typed path `cx get amazon/us/<ASIN>/product --source keepa` does not request them: its fixed parameters are `stats`, `offers`, `buybox` and `rating` (Keepa `Product.java` / `Request.java`, github.com/keepacom/api_backend; Keepa skill `references/raw-routes.md`; platform `src/source-packages/keepa/gate/adapter.ts` on origin/main).
  - Keepa's Product Finder has `imageCount_*`, `hasMainVideo`, `videoCount_*` and `hasAPlus` filters for screening a category.
- **The click-through measure for a main-image change.**
  - Brand Analytics Search Catalog Performance report (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_CATALOG_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`): per ASIN, search `impressionCount`, `clickCount`, `clickRate` ("clicks divided by … impressions"), cart adds and purchases, by WEEK, MONTH or QUARTER, one period per request.
  - Search Query Performance (`GET_BRAND_ANALYTICS_SEARCH_QUERY_PERFORMANCE_REPORT`): per query, `asinImpressionShare` against `asinClickShare`.
  - Data Kiosk `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31`, search catalog metrics `clickRate`.
  - **Sponsored results are inside these counts.** The Search Catalog Performance schema says "Impressions include sponsored search results" [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, re-read 2026-10-14). The Search Query Performance dashboard page says the data "includes organic and Sponsored Products originating from the search results page for all customers" and "excludes traffic from widgets on the search page" [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP v5, read 2026-10-14; text at capture `G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP-v5`). A main-image click rate read from either therefore mixes paid and organic clicks, and an ad change in the same window confounds it.
  - Report options, waits and names: door.md's. The operations are `createReport`, `getReport` and `getReportDocument`, plus Data Kiosk `createQuery`.
- **The conversion measure for a gallery or video change.** Sales and Traffic (unit session percentage, by ASIN): door.md's (`createReport`). Data Kiosk seller analytics has no unit-session-percentage field: its traffic group carries `unitsOrdered` and `sessions`, from which a ratio can be computed [seat], and its `traffic.conversionRatePct` is "the percentage of purchases to clicks for ASINs originated from the search results page", which is not unit session percentage and does not cover detail-page traffic from outside search [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, re-read 2026-10-14).
- **Two Amazon schemas give `conversionRate` two meanings.** The Search Catalog Performance report schema defines `conversionRate` as "The number of purchases divided by the number of clicks for the ASIN"; the Data Kiosk schema defines `SearchCatalogMetrics.conversionRate` as "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a purchase" [official] (both schemas, re-read 2026-10-14). cx takes the definition from the schema of the source it actually read, at run time, names that source beside the figure, and never compares one source's `conversionRate` with the other's (open question 9).
- **Manage Your Experiments results.** There is no operation. Read them only from a file the seller puts in the tree.

Added by the gap fill:

- **The current image rules.** The pages are G1881 "Product image guide", GT8RSE9S9NK4LP6Z "Image suppression", GQ2VU6MXD8H6VGSU "Prohibited badging…", GB6V3RU3M5XG24GC "Text, logo, graphics, and watermarks", G200270100 "Product page style guides" (lifestyle-main product types), and the category page that applies (G200498950 clothing, G94GSFMC79RSLDBY multipacks). cx does not depend on the undocumented help hub content call at run time (P-help-page-access-7; its standing use is for the office to rule): it names the page and the version recorded in the tree (for G1881 and G200216080, the captures under the office's capture folder (outside this repository)), or the page is read in a signed-in browser seat. Record each page's `version` in the review. For a non-US store, the same id on that store's host and locale is the page; a US page is not the rule for another store.
- **Image suppression on the seller's catalog.** The Suppressed Listings Report (`GET_MERCHANTS_LISTINGS_FYP_REPORT`, columns `Reason` and `Issue Description`), requested through `createReport` → `getReport` → `getReportDocument`. Options and waits are door.md's. Then per SKU `getListingsItem` `includedData=issues` for `INVALID_IMAGE` / `MISSING_IMAGE` and the enforcement actions. The issue `message` may carry the 18000-series wording (unverified, open question 13).
- **What is displayed.** `getCatalogItem` `includedData=images`, read for the marketplace in question. This is the only per-slot read-back among the operations read. Compare it with the seller's submitted locators (`getListingsItem` `includedData=attributes`) to see which submissions Amazon did not display.
- **Who won a slot, and the Live/Not Live status.** No SP-API route was found. Ask the seller to read Image Manager ("Live images"; tile status Live / Not Live / Status Unknown) and save what it shows into the tree.
- **Image pixel sizes against the zoom threshold.** Read `height` and `width` from `getCatalogItem` `images` and `summaries.mainImage`. Read the product type's schema (`getDefinitionsProductType`) for any resolution constraint before calling an image too small. G1881 and Error 18203 disagree on whether 1,000 px is a recommendation or a rule (item 34). "Submit media attributes" says the schema carries "resolution requirements", but none of the four schema copies read (item 44) carries one, so the live schema may be silent and the two help pages are then the only statement.
- **Video.** No SP-API read of detail-page video was found. Keepa `videos[]` (raw `/product` with `videos=1`) shows carousel videos and their `creator`. The seller's Video Metrics Dashboard and video status in "Upload and Manage Videos" are Seller Central-only.

## Positions

- **P-images-and-video-1.** Diagnose the main image against the click-through stage (search impressions to clicks for the ASIN, and impression share against click share per query). Diagnose secondary images, video and 3D against conversion. Never credit or blame an image for a stage it does not serve. *Sources:* Schema sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Schema sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Jungle Scout "Amazon Product Images Requirements & Best Practices" [opinion].
- **P-images-and-video-2.** Treat main-image compliance as an exposure risk as well as a click-through matter: read `issues` for `MISSING_IMAGE` / `INVALID_IMAGE` and enforcement actions such as `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` before discussing image quality. *Sources:* Model listingsItems_2021-08-01.json; Manage Listings Issues; Seller Forums "Suppressed items".
- **P-images-and-video-3.** A proposal never puts text, a badge, a logo or a claim on the main image, and it states that Amazon's main-image rules live on Seller Central help G1881 rather than quoting figures as the skill's own. *Sources:* Seller Forums "Product Image Requirements and Best Practices"; Seller Forums "Product Image Aspect Ratio - white side bars"; 6 tips for taking product photos in 2025.
- **P-images-and-video-4.** Name image attributes only from the product type's schema: `getDefinitionsProductType`, where Amazon documents `main_product_image_locator`, `other_product_image_locator_N` and `swatch_product_image_locator` with `media_location`. Never from memory or legacy flat-file names. *Sources:* Submit media attributes; Schema BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json; SP-API Product Metadata Updates.
- **P-images-and-video-5.** In this package's first round cx diagnoses images and does not write them. An image write needs a URL Amazon can fetch, and nothing in the seller's tree has one. *Sources:* Submit media attributes.
- **P-images-and-video-6.** Any image change recorded in the change log says that it applies to the ASIN in every marketplace where the ASIN exists, and the seller hears this before deciding. *Sources:* Listings APIs FAQ.
- **P-images-and-video-7.** Judge what shoppers see from the Catalog's images (`getCatalogItem` `includedData=images`: variant, link, pixel size), not from the seller's submission, because Amazon may display other contributors' images and brand owners take precedence. *Sources:* Model catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Listings APIs FAQ.
- **P-images-and-video-8.** After any image-related write, the change log records `ACCEPTED` as "submitted", not "live". The image counts as live only once the Catalog shows the new image and `issues` carry no image problem. *Sources:* Manage Listings Issues; Listings APIs FAQ.
- **P-images-and-video-9.** Main-block video, 3D models and Image-Manager-only image kinds are Seller Central tasks the seller does; the skill can recommend them but not perform them. The API's video path is the A+ media library only. *Sources:* Submit media attributes; sell.amazon.com tools index; 3D models and augmented reality (AR) | Sell on Amazon; Create, Edit, and Retrieve A+ Media Assets; SP-API Release Notes.
- **P-images-and-video-10.** Detect a competitor's image or video change by diffing Keepa `images` filenames, and `videos` when requested through raw `/product` with `videos=1`, between successive saved observations. On the seller's own brand ASINs, `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` says only that some image changed. *Sources:* Keepa Product.java / Request.java; Notification Type Values; Schema BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json.
- **P-images-and-video-11.** Judge a main-image change on the ASIN's search click rate and per-query click share against impression share. Say that these search counts include Sponsored Products results, so an ad change in the same window is a confounder. Judge a gallery or video change on conversion and orders, with each conversion figure's definition taken from the schema of the source read. Report options and waits come from door.md. *Sources:* Schema sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Schema sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; Search Query Performance dashboard (G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP); Schema analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql.
- **P-images-and-video-12.** Do not use Amazon's or practitioners' published lift figures (the MYE, Brand Registry, A+ and Sponsored Products pages' claims; practitioners' conversion and click claims) as a change's expected range. Amazon's rest on undisclosed "Amazon internal data" and disagree between pages, and practitioners' are unsourced. *Sources:* Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments | Sell on Amazon; Amazon Brand Registry | Sell on Amazon; A+ Content | Sell on Amazon; Sponsored Products - Help increase product sales | Amazon Ads; Feedvisor "Amazon Product Images…" [opinion].
- **P-images-and-video-13.** Where a brand-registered seller's ASIN has enough traffic, a Manage Your Experiments test is stronger evidence for an image change than a before/after reading. It has no API, so cx reads its result only from a file the seller exports. *Sources:* Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments | Sell on Amazon; SP-API docs index (llms.txt); Kohavi, Tang, Xu 2020 [research].

- **P-images-and-video-24.** Recommend a detail-page video only with Amazon's eligibility and timing attached, as the current video guide states them (an account-age condition; review, then publication). Start a video's observation window at publication. In softlines, check the image count against the guide's desktop video-suppression rule. *Sources:* Shoppable video guide (GUZBYWTTVULY6C9Y); Add product images and video (G200216080).

- **P-images-and-video-28.** For a gallery change, effect-review reads returns alongside conversion and orders. Published evidence ties zoom use to fewer returns and heavier use of alternative photos to more returns. *Sources:* De, Hu, Rahman 2013 [research]; Product image guide (G1881) ("Reduce product returns").

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- [seat] On the main image, a high-impression-share, low-click-share pattern in Search Query Performance cannot isolate the image. Title, price, rating count, delivery badge and Prime badge share the same search tile. The skill should name the main image as one candidate among those, never as the cause.
- [seat] A main-image change and a title change in the same window cannot be told apart in click rate. One-change-at-a-time therefore matters more for the main image than for most elements.
- [seat] Catalog `height`/`width` below Amazon's zoom guidance is a cheap, factual diagnosis cx can make without seeing the image, provided open question 5 confirms the figures are the original's.
- [seat] Seasonal or event main images, such as holiday props, likely sit poorly with the "no props" rule. Unverified.
- [seat] Whether Amazon's ranking uses image content (as Taobao's ad CTR model does per Ge et al. 2017) is unknown for Amazon organic search. The skill should not claim images affect ranking directly, only through clicks and sales.
- [seat] Mobile thumbnail legibility (small search tiles) is probably the practical test for a main image. No Amazon page read here quantifies it.
- [seat] Amazon Lens Live matches by visual embedding against the catalog. A distinctive, accurate main image probably helps a product be matched, but Amazon does not say which images are indexed. A skill must not claim a Lens benefit.
- [seat] Customer review photos compete with the seller's gallery at conversion. No Amazon page on them was read, so they are outside what the skill can state.
- [seat] The 18000-series messages use a `${variant}` placeholder, so the issue `message` returned by `getListingsItem` probably names the slot (MAIN, PT0n). Unverified until a real `issues` read shows it.
