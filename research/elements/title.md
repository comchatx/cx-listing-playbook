# Element: the title (item_name, title_differentiation / Item Highlights)

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

The product title in its 2026 shape: two attributes, `item_name` (Seller Central calls it "Item name") and `title_differentiation` (shown to sellers and shoppers as "Item Highlights"/"Item highlights"). Both are searched; both are shown in search results and on the detail page.

- **Funnel stages.** Exposure first: the title and the Item Highlights are inputs to Amazon search matching. Click-through second: the title is "the first piece of information that customers see when they search", and since 2026-08-10 the Item Highlights show under it on desktop and mobile. Conversion only a little: the detail page shows the title, and a variation family shows the parent's title, with a child's title appearing only in the cart.
- **Skills it feeds.**
  - `listing-review`: what a compliant, well-built title is; where the rules live; the Listings-vs-Catalog check for "did Amazon change my title"; which Keepa fields carry competitor titles.
  - `references/writing.md`: the three facts (schema, validation preview, split or rewrite in the Catalog).
  - `listing-change-log`: a title change logs both attributes, before and after, the words bet on, and which Catalog title was live when the window opened.
  - `effect-review`: when the window opens (display latency), which metrics a title targets, and a confounder that is easy to miss: Amazon or another contributor changing the title during the window.
- **Why a senior operator cares.** Between January 2025 and July 2026 Amazon moved the title rule twice:
  1. January 2025: 200 characters, the special-character ban and the two-repetition limit.
  2. July 2026: a 75-character `item_name` plus 125 characters of Item Highlights.

  After the second change Amazon began rewriting non-compliant titles with AI, on its own clock. Two things follow. A model working from memory risks writing to a dead rule; the 2026-10-14 run report shows cx drafting a title patch with no `getDefinitionsProductType` read and the length limit guessed (its proposed `item_name` happened to fit). And a title can now change under the seller without any action of theirs, which breaks any effect review that does not check for it.

1. **RULE — length.** "Titles must not exceed 75 characters, including spaces." The scope: "all product types, except media product types, listed in all stores, except for Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Türkiye, and the United Arab Emirates. To list a product for the first time, your title must meet the requirements described below." [official] (Product title requirements and guidelines, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC, read 2026-10-14)
2. **RULE — special characters.** "Titles must not use the following special characters: !, $, ?, _, {, }, ^, ¬, ¦."
   - "Other special characters, such as ~, #, <, >, and *, are allowed only in specific contexts", meaning identifiers ("Style #4301") or measurements ("<10 lb").
   - "Decorative usage of special characters is not allowed."
   - For a brand name that contains a prohibited character, the page says to enter it "in the 'Brand name' field. Special characters in the brand field are exempt from title requirements". It does **not** say the title may carry them. This differs from the lead "unless part of brand", which is Amazon's own January 2025 wording ("are not allowed, unless they are part of the brand name", News_Amazon, item 10a below); the current page moves the exemption to the Brand field.

   [official] (Product title requirements and guidelines, as above)
3. **RULE — repetition.** "Titles must not contain the same word more than twice."
   - "Prepositions, articles, and conjunctions may be used more than twice in a title. However, brand names are also subject to the two-instance limit."
   - A brand fragment used in another sense ("Old Navy and Navy Blue") is not a duplicate.
   - The lead said "prepositions/articles". The page adds conjunctions and puts brand names under the limit.

   [official] (as above)
4. **RULE — content.**
   - Titles must not contain promotional phrases ("free shipping", "100% quality guaranteed").
   - They must not contain restricted phrases ("FSA/HSA eligible").
   - They "must contain the minimum amount of information that can be used to clearly describe the product".

   [official] (as above)
5. **RULE — enforcement on existing listings.** "If your product is already listed and the title doesn't comply with these requirements or contains inaccurate information, the title might be automatically corrected or it might not appear in our search results."
   - Policy-violating titles show in Manage All Inventory.
   - "Brand owners can also see these titles in Review listing changes" (`/myinventory/inventory/views/rlu`).
   - The troubleshooting table names "Title suppression" and "Title blocked due to policy violations". The fix is to edit the title; "We will automatically check for compliance and lift suppressions".

   [official] (as above)
6. **GUIDANCE — best practices table on the same page.**
   - Length: "75 or fewer characters is required because mobile screens truncate long titles."
   - Redundancy: avoid it.
   - Special characters: "Use only standard letters and numbers. Don't use non-language ASCII characters such as Æ, Š, Œ, Ÿ, or Ž"; hyphens, slashes, commas, ampersands and periods are allowed.
   - Information order: "Brand name, flavor or style, product type, key attribute, color, size or pack count, model number"; "Order the words to reflect the most important product information upfront."
   - Parent-child: "Include size and color in child ASINs only and not in the title of the parent ASIN"; "The detail page displays the title of the parent ASIN, and the title of the child ASIN will only appear once the ASIN is added to the customer's cart."
   - Numerals rather than words; no subjective commentary ("Hot Item", "Best seller"); no all caps; capitalise each word except prepositions, conjunctions and articles; measurement abbreviations allowed.
   - Model numbers are "recommended for inclusion in titles for certain categories", with a pointer to the category style guides.

   [official] (as above)
7. **GUIDANCE — FAQ on the same page.**
   - Item highlights "provide an additional 125 characters for product details like materials or recommended use cases. These should be written as comma-separated phrases, instead of full sentences."
   - They are "visible below titles in search results and on product detail pages".
   - Timing: "the change is accepted immediately but can take 24 to 48 hours to be processed and displayed on Amazon stores, including search results and the product detail page."
   - Unbranded products: enter "generic" in Brand name and "Do not enter a brand name in the title."
   - Variating attributes: "include the most important variating attributes in the Item name field. If the 75-character limit prevents you from including all attributes in Item name, you can retain the most important attributes there and move the others to Item highlights."

   [official] (as above)
8. **RULE — restated in the detail page rules.** "Product titles must not have more than 75 characters, including spaces, for all product types, excluding media product types. Use item highlights … which has a character limit of 125 characters (including spaces)."
   - No all caps; "Use of emojis, emoticons, or symbols at the beginning of each word is prohibited."
   - No phone numbers, URLs, availability, price or condition, reviews or testimonials, or promotional material in titles.

   [official] (Product detail page rules, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200390640, read 2026-10-14)
9. **RULE — media exemption.** The exempt product types are listed by name:
   - `ABIS_BOOK`, `ABIS_EBOOKS`, `ABIS_MUSIC`
   - `PHYSICAL_MOVIE`, `PHYSICAL_TV_SERIES`
   - `DOWNLOADABLE_*` video, music and software types
   - `MOBILE_APPLICATION`, `SKILL_APPLICATION`, `PODCAST_EPISODIC_AUDIO_BROADCAST` and others

   [official] (Media product types, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GMF8CVX6S6CF8MM2, read 2026-10-14)
10. **RULE — identity.** When updating "the item name or title, make sure your updates match the original product identity to avoid error 8541."
    - Contributions are weighed: "we consider sales volume, refund rate, buyer feedback, and A-to-z guarantee claims".
    - "ASINs registered for a brand will most likely show the brand owners contributions".
    - "If your contribution is verified by Amazon, they will show on the product detail page within 48 hours. If … your contributions do not show …, it means Amazon's system has selected another contributor's information".

    [official] (Suggest changes to your product detail page, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200335450, read 2026-10-14)

### C. Where the limit lives, and how it is counted

16. **RULE — the schema is an extended JSON Schema with two length units.** The Product Type Definition meta-schema extends JSON Schema Draft 2019-09 and adds `maxUtf8ByteLength`: "Defines the maximum length of a `string`, measured in UTF-8 bytes" (likewise `minUtf8ByteLength`). "If you choose to ignore this custom vocabulary … you may get validation errors." Standard `maxLength` counts characters. [official] (Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1), https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/product-type-definition-meta-schema, read 2026-10-14)
17. **RULE (the standard Amazon builds on).** `maxLength`: "A string instance is valid against this keyword if its length is less than, or equal to, the value of this keyword. The length of a string instance is defined as the number of its characters as defined by RFC 8259." [official standard, not Amazon] (JSON Schema Validation, draft 2019-09, https://json-schema.org/draft/2019-09/json-schema-validation, read 2026-10-14)
18. **RULE — retrieving the schema.**
    - `getDefinitionsProductType` returns the definition with "links to retrieve the meta schema and product type schema documents. The links provided are valid for seven days."
    - Optional `sellerId` returns "seller-specific attributes"; optional `parentageLevel` (`CHILD`, `PARENT`, `NONE`) narrows the schema to a listing type.
    - Example query: `requirements=LISTING`, `locale=en_US`.

    [official] (Retrieve a Product Type Definition, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/retrieve-a-product-type-definition, read 2026-10-14)
19. **Amazon's pages disagree on length figures. No page settles them; the store's schema and the listing's `issues` do.** All of the following are live on 2026-10-14 (figures kept for human readers):
    - **Title, 200 characters.** The troubleshooting page (`updatedAt 2026-09-09`) still lists, under issue `90225` ("Value for attribute_name is longer than the allowed maximum (limit unit)."), "`item_name`: 200 characters" and "`generic_keyword`: 500 bytes" (re-read at reconciliation). Manage Listings Issues' example preview message reads "exceeds the maximum length of 200 characters" (item 45), and Error 96009's fix says "does not exceeds 200 characters" (item 45a). [official] (Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/listings-items-api-issues-troubleshooting; Manage Listings Issues; Error 96009; read 2026-10-14)
    - **Title, 75 characters.** The title page (item 1), Product detail page rules (item 8), Error 90117 (item 45) and Create a listing experiment ("Product titles must not have more than 75 characters, including spaces.", GYZYA77YZTWTF7YZ v13).
    - **Title, 80 / 130 characters.** Suppressed listings (G200898440 v13): "complete product information, with images, category (item-type), titles (parent titles), and price per unit (PPU) along with less than 80 characters for apparel, shoes, and luggage categories and 130 characters for all other categories", followed by "we will hide (or suppress) from search and browse non-media listings that do not meet certain standards".
    - **Title, "under 100".** Tips for experiments (G2BNMJ8NFCGZQVFH v5) lists "Reducing product title length to under 100 characters" as an experiment idea, not a rule.
    - **Search terms.** Optimize your product discoverability (G10471 v13): "less than 250 bytes". Keyword attributes explained (GF2C2L6RCFZGWBXC v4): its table, headed "Length Limit (Bytes)", gives `generic_keywords` "249", and its text says "should be less than 250 bytes long … If it exceeds the limit, the attribute is completely ignored by Amazon Search." The live US Use search terms effectively (G23501 v14) states no byte figure. The troubleshooting page says 500 bytes. elements/search-terms.md records third-party schema copies carrying `maxUtf8ByteLength` 250 in one store and 2000 in others, with `maxLength` 500.
    - **Units.** The title page counts characters "including spaces"; the keyword table counts bytes. Use search terms effectively (G23501 v14) explains the difference: "With respect to alphanumeric characters used in the English language (for example a - z, A - Z, 0 - 9), one byte corresponds to one character. However, more complex characters, such as umlauts in German (for example, ä), can correspond to two or more bytes".

    The binding value for a write is not chosen among these: it is the `maxLength` / `maxUtf8ByteLength` on `item_name`, `title_differentiation` or `generic_keyword` in the product type's schema, read at run time with `getDefinitionsProductType` for the store, and the `issues` Amazon returns. [official] (Keyword attributes explained, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GF2C2L6RCFZGWBXC; Use search terms effectively, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G23501; Optimize your product discoverability, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G10471; Suppressed listings, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200898440; Create a listing experiment, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYZYA77YZTWTF7YZ; Tips for experiments, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G2BNMJ8NFCGZQVFH; all read 2026-10-14 from the captures under the office's capture folder (outside this repository))

### D. Validation preview and read-back

20. **RULE — validation preview.** `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW` "Indicates the submitted data should be validated without persisting to the selling partner's catalog." [official] (patchListingsItem reference, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/reference/patchlistingsitem, read 2026-10-14)
    - The guide adds: "This mode has lower rate limits and is intended for testing a small number of listings. It is not designed for production workflows."
    - Its error codes are being remapped: "update your code to handle both current and new error codes."

    [official] (Manage Listings Issues, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/manage-listings-issues, read 2026-10-14)
21. **RULE — two views of one listing.** "The Catalog Items API … provides information at the ASIN level, which is reconciled from various listings against that item. The Listings Items API … provides information about the specific listing submitted by a selling partner."
    - "Brand owners have the highest precedence to update data on the product details page".
    - Why edits may not show: "the information submitted by a vendor might not have priority."
    - The summary view "displays information about the ASIN you are creating a listing against".
    - `getListingsItem` reflects a change "within 1-5 minutes".

    [official] (Listings APIs FAQ, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/listings-apis-faq, read 2026-10-14)
22. **RULE — model wording.** In the Listings Items model, `summaries[].itemName` is "Name, or title, associated with an Amazon catalog item". In the Catalog Items model it is "The name that is associated with the Amazon catalog item"; Catalog `attributes` "conform to the related Amazon product type definitions". [official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models, read 2026-10-14)
23. **RULE — notification.** `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` is sent "whenever there is a change to the title, description, bullet points, or images, for any ASIN that the selling partner has a brand relationship with". The sample `AttributesChanged` includes `item_name`. It needs a Notifications API subscription with a destination. [official] (Notification Type Values, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, read 2026-10-14)

### E. Search, display and latency

24. **GUIDANCE — what search uses.**
    - Optimize your product discoverability (G10471 v13): "keywords, which are matched against information such as title and description that you provide for a product".
    - The same page: "We reserve the right to not use all supplied content from the product description, including bullet points, for retrieving products in customers' shopping search results." The reservation names the description and bullet points, not the title.
    - Amazon search glossary (GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV v2): "Title is a required catalog attribute, and every ASIN must have a title. A search result is made up of a title, description, image, price, and other features, such as badges."

## Published research

1. **Karmaker Santu, Sondhi, Zhai, "On Application of Learning to Rank for E-Commerce Search", SIGIR 2017 (ACM DOI 10.1145/3077136.3080838; arXiv 1903.04263).** [research]
   - Finding: in e-commerce learning-to-rank on industry data, "popularity-based features defined solely on product items are very useful". Order rate is the most robust training objective, then click rate.
   - Transfer: text relevance (which the title feeds) is one input among behavioural ones. A title change cannot be expected to move rank by itself when sales history dominates. The experiments use "industry data sets"; the abstract does not name the retailer, and nothing says it is Amazon.
2. **Sorokina, Cantú-Paz, "Amazon Search: The joy of ranking products", SIGIR 2016 (Amazon Science page).** [research] A talk abstract ("In this talk we are going to cover…"), not a full paper.
   - Finding (abstract only): Amazon search uses a machine-learning ranking framework within categories, blends rankings in All Product Search, and uses "NLP techniques used for matching queries and products".
   - Transfer: confirms matching on product text. No weight for the title is published.
3. **Reddy et al., "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search", arXiv 2206.06588 (2022; KDD Cup '22).** [research]
   - Finding: about 130 thousand Amazon queries and 2.6 million human relevance judgements of (query, product) pairs.
   - Transfer: shows Amazon evaluates relevance as Exact/Substitute/Complement/Irrelevant per query-product pair. It says nothing about title length or CTR.
4. **Sun et al., "Multi-Source Pointer Network for Product Title Summarization", CIKM 2018 (arXiv 1808.06885; Alibaba).** [research]
   - Finding: short titles generated for mobile display that keep "key information (e.g., brand name and commodity name)" and add nothing irrelevant gave "a significant business impact, as measured by the click-through rate" in online deployment.
   - Transfer: a different platform (Taobao), machine-shortened display titles, effect size not stated in the abstract. It supports the narrow claim that on mobile, a short title that keeps brand and product name need not cost clicks.
5. **Wang et al., "A Multi-task Learning Approach for Improving Product Title Compression with User Search Log Data", AAAI 2018 (arXiv 1801.01725; Alibaba).** [research]
   - Finding: merchants make titles "redundant and lengthy for Search Engine Optimization". Compression learned jointly with user queries improved "online business values" in deployment.
   - Transfer: same caution as 4. It is the closest published analogue of Amazon's 2026 move: shorter display titles, with query words preserved.
6. **Mane, Kedia, Mantha, Guo, Achan (Walmart Labs), "Product Title Generation for Conversational Systems using BERT", arXiv 2007.11768 (2020; preprint, no venue stated).** [research]
   - Finding: a web title such as "Lucky Charms Gluten Free Break-fast Cereal, 20.5 oz a box Lucky Charms Gluten Free" "is acceptable to display on a webpage", but a spoken short title is easier over voice; BERT summarisation "outperforms comparable baseline models" on a real-world industry dataset and human evaluation.
   - Transfer: Walmart, not Amazon. Evidence only that long, repetitive titles get rewritten for other surfaces. Not a CTR result.

## Stable enough for a skill

- A write is validated against the product type's schema from `getDefinitionsProductType`. The title's length limits, their unit, and whether `title_differentiation` exists for that product type live there. (Submit modular titles; Retrieve a Product Type Definition; SP-API Product Metadata Updates)
- In that schema `maxLength` counts characters and `maxUtf8ByteLength` counts UTF-8 bytes. The unit is whichever keyword the attribute carries. (Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); JSON Schema Validation 2019-09)
- `title_differentiation` (Item Highlights) is accepted only alongside an `item_name` within the length the schema's description and Amazon's issue name. Otherwise Amazon answers issue 100476 and suppresses the attribute. (Submit modular titles)
- `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW` validates without persisting and returns `issues`. Amazon intends it for a small number of listings, not production workflows. (patchListingsItem reference; Manage Listings Issues)
- An over-limit `item_name` is still accepted. Amazon may split it across `item_name` and `title_differentiation` in the Catalog, or replace it with its AI recommendation. `getListingsItem` returns what was submitted; Catalog Items shows Amazon's version. (Submit modular titles; Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27)
- Amazon's pages disagree on whether a title nobody resubmitted is changed: the developer guide says current listings are not affected unless an update is submitted, while the seller announcements say over-long titles are moved to the AI recommendation gradually. So whether Amazon changed a title is read from the Catalog against the submission, not assumed either way. (Submit modular titles; Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; Answers to your product title update questions)
- Amazon's own pages carry several title and search-term length figures at once. The figure that binds a write is the product type's schema for the store, read at run time, and the `issues` Amazon returns. (Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting; Product title requirements and guidelines; Suppressed listings; Keyword attributes explained; Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1))
- Brand owners get a review window in Seller Central's Review Listings Changes before an AI title recommendation is applied. If they do nothing, it is applied. (Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; Answers to your product title update questions)
- Title policy (prohibited characters, repetition, promotional and restricted phrases, element order, parent/child rules) lives on Seller Central's "Product title requirements and guidelines". Non-compliant titles may be corrected automatically or kept out of search. (Product title requirements and guidelines)
- The title and the Item Highlights are both search inputs and are both shown in search results and on the detail page. (Answers to your product title update questions; Product title requirements and guidelines)
- A parent ASIN's title is what the detail page shows; size and colour belong in child titles. (Product title requirements and guidelines)
- What the detail page shows is a reconciliation of contributions. Brand owners take precedence, and a reseller's title edit may never display. (Suggest changes to your product detail page; Listings APIs FAQ)
- Accepted is not displayed. Display and search lag the write, and Amazon gives a different lag for each path, so a title change's observation window opens when the new title is seen live, not when it was sent or after a fixed wait. (Product title requirements and guidelines; Amazon search glossary; Listings APIs FAQ; Answers to your product title update questions)
- A title can change without a new edit by the seller: an AI recommendation, another contributor's value, or a Manage Your Experiments winner published automatically. (Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; Suggest changes to your product detail page; Create a listing experiment)
- Manage Your Experiments, Review Listings Changes and View Change History are Seller Central tools with no operation in the SP-API references read. ## What cx must read at run time

- **The rule for this SKU.** `getDefinitionsProductType` (`GET /definitions/2020-09-01/productTypes/{productType}` with `marketplaceIds`, `requirements=LISTING`, `locale`, optionally `sellerId` and `parentageLevel`). Then fetch the schema document from the returned link, which lives seven days, and read for `item_name` and `title_differentiation`:
  - presence
  - `maxLength` and/or `maxUtf8ByteLength`
  - `editable`
  - `description` text
  - `selectors`

  The product type comes from `getListingsItem` `includedData=productTypes` or `summaries`.
- **What the seller submitted.** `getListingsItem` (`/listings/2021-08-01/items/{sellerId}/{sku}`) with `includedData=summaries,attributes,issues`:
  - `attributes.item_name` and `attributes.title_differentiation` are the submission.
  - `summaries[].itemName` describes the ASIN's catalog item and is not proof of the submission.
  - `issues[]` gives `code`, `message`, `severity`, `categories`, `attributeNames` and `enforcements`.

  For many SKUs use `searchListingsItems`.
- **What Amazon shows.** `getCatalogItem` (`/catalog/2022-04-01/items/{asin}`) with `includedData=summaries,attributes`: the reconciled `summaries.itemName` and the `attributes.item_name` / `title_differentiation` values. A difference from the submission is a finding ("split", "rewritten", "another contributor"), not an error.
- **The check before showing a write.** `patchListingsItem` (or `putListingsItem`) with `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW` returns `status` and `issues`. On a real store, the brief's rule decides whether even a preview may be sent.
- **Length, by machine.** `printf %s "$t" | LC_ALL=C.UTF-8 wc -m` counts characters; `printf %s "$t" | wc -c` counts bytes. Use `printf`, not `echo`, which adds a newline. The schema's keyword says which count matters; Amazon's `issues` are the final word.
- **The policy text.** Seller Central "Product title requirements and guidelines" (GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC), read live when a title is in question; the 2026-10-14 text is captured at capture `GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC-v21`, historical once read. The help page is JavaScript-rendered, so a plain fetch returns an empty shell; the route that reads it is the one `research/method/help-page-access.md` describes, whose standing use is for the office to rule.
- **The seller's listing in bulk.** door.md's entry for the all-listings report (`GET_MERCHANT_LISTINGS_ALL_DATA`, its `item-name` column), via `createReport` / `getReport` / `getReportDocument`. door.md on origin/main does not yet carry report-type entries (read 2026-10-14); the entry is still to be written there. Whether that column carries the submitted or the catalog name is not stated in what was read.
- **The title's effect.**
  - Query-level impressions and clicks (the click-through stage): door.md's Brand Analytics Search Query Performance report entry, or the Data Kiosk dataset door.md names.
  - Sessions and orders: door.md's `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT` entry.
- **Change alerts (optional).** `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` via the Notifications API (`createSubscription`). It needs a destination the box does not have today.
- **Competitors' titles.** Keepa product object fields:
  - `title` (Keepa's own description: "Amazon limits titles to 75 characters … in all categories except media"; Keepa's wording, not the rule)
  - `itemHighlights` (Keepa: up to 125 characters, "undefined if not available")
  - `parentTitle`, `parentHighlights` (snapshots, "there is no history for this field")

  The product object documents no title-history field (Keepa states "no history" explicitly only for `parentTitle`), so a competitor's title change is a diff between kept snapshots. A fresh `--source keepa` read overwrites the landed `product.json`: the beta run's day-2 report observed it (`office-mailbox/from-engineering/20261014-0921-beta-browser-listing-run-day-2.md`, lesson 6, "A `--source keepa` read overwrites `product.json`"), so cx keeps its own dated copy of each read. The Keepa skill on origin/main does not state the overwrite; it says a kept reply can be re-read with `cx raw keepa --record <id>`.
- **Things with no API, as files in the tree:** Review Listings Changes proposals, View Change History, Manage Your Experiments results.

Added by the gap fill, 2026-10-14:

- **A parent's title versus a child's.** `getDefinitionsProductType` with `parentageLevel=PARENT` for the parent SKU and `parentageLevel=CHILD` for a child. The family comes from Catalog Items `includedData=relationships` (or Keepa `parentAsin` / `parentTitle`).
- **The key shape before any write.** In the same schema document, read the attribute's `items.required`, `additionalProperties` and `selectors`, and the allowed values of `language_tag` and `marketplace_id`. The write is copied from that shape, never from memory. Then run a `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` (`patchListingsItem` or `putListingsItem`).
- **The enforcement on a title issue.** `getListingsItem` `includedData=issues` (or `searchListingsItems`), reading each issue's `enforcements.actions[].action` (`SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`, `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`, `LISTING_SUPPRESSED`, `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED`) and `enforcements.exemption` (`status`, `expiryDate`). Optional push: Amazon's guide recommends subscribing to `LISTINGS_ITEM_ISSUES_CHANGE` (and `LISTINGS_ITEM_STATUS_CHANGE`) "instead of repeatedly calling direct API operations" (Manage Listings Issues); the Notifications API setup was not read in this pass.
- **Another store.** The same operations with that store's `marketplaceIds` and `locale` (for example `en_GB`, `de_DE`, `ja_JP`). The policy text comes from the store's own Seller Central host.
- **Ads effect of a title change.** The ASIN's ad impressions and click-through would come from an Amazon Ads report. door.md carries no report-type entries yet (see above), and no Amazon Ads API page was read in this pass, so that entry and its operation are still to be written. Until then the source is the seller's own Ads export, as a file in the tree. The title page itself names no ads metric.
- **The category formula.** No API. A style-guide PDF or a category template, if the seller has one, put in the tree as a file. Otherwise the current title page's "Information order" row.

## Positions

1. **P-title-1.** Before drafting or changing a title, cx reads the SKU's product-type schema with `getDefinitionsProductType` for the seller's store and takes the limits, their unit (`maxLength` for characters, `maxUtf8ByteLength` for bytes) and whether `title_differentiation` exists from it, never from memory. Sources: Submit modular titles; Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); Retrieve a Product Type Definition; SP-API Product Metadata Updates.
2. **P-title-2.** cx reads the live Seller Central "Product title requirements and guidelines" when it judges a title's policy compliance (prohibited characters, repetition, promotional or restricted phrases), because policy can be stricter than the schema and Amazon's other pages carry stale figures. Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines; Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting; Amazon SEO: 7 ways to improve your product's search rankings.
3. **P-title-3.** A title proposal is a pair, `item_name` and `title_differentiation`:
   - `item_name` leads with the brand, then product type and the key attribute, and holds the most important variating attributes.
   - `title_differentiation` holds the rest as comma-separated phrases that do not repeat `item_name`.

   Sources: Submit modular titles; Product title requirements and guidelines; Answers to your product title update questions.
4. **P-title-4.** cx sends `title_differentiation` only with an `item_name` inside the length Amazon ties to it, and reads issue 100476 as "item name too long for Item Highlights". Sources: Submit modular titles.
5. **P-title-5.** cx counts a title's length with a shell command in the unit the schema names, and checks a write with `mode=VALIDATION_PREVIEW` before showing it. Amazon's returned `issues`, not cx's count, are the verdict. Sources: Amazon Product Type Definitions Meta-Schema (v1); JSON Schema Validation (draft 2019-09); Submit modular titles; patchListingsItem reference.
6. **P-title-6.** cx uses validation preview for a few SKUs at a time, never as a bulk pipeline. Sources: Manage Listings Issues.
7. **P-title-7.** After any title write, and at every review, cx reads both the submitted attributes (`getListingsItem` `attributes`) and Amazon's view (`getCatalogItem`). It reports a difference as Amazon's split, rewrite or another contributor's value, not as a failure. Sources: Submit modular titles; Listings APIs FAQ; Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; Suggest changes to your product detail page.
8. **P-title-8.** cx takes the seller's submitted title from `attributes.item_name`, not from `summaries[].itemName`, which describes the ASIN. Sources: Listings APIs FAQ; listingsItems_2021-08-01 model.
9. **P-title-9.** A title change's observation window opens when the new title is seen live in the Catalog or in a Keepa snapshot, not when the write was accepted. Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines; Amazon search glossary; Listings APIs FAQ.
10. **P-title-10.** Before judging any listing change, cx checks whether the live title changed during the window without the seller's action (an AI recommendation, another contributor, Amazon's data, an experiment's auto-published winner). Such a change is a confounder. Sources: Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; Answers to your product title update questions; Suggest changes to your product detail page; Create a listing experiment; Notification Type Values.
11. **P-title-11.** A title change targets exposure and click-through, so cx judges it on query-level impressions and clicks plus sessions and orders, not on conversion rate alone. Sources: Answers to your product title update questions; Product title requirements and guidelines; Amazon search glossary.
12. **P-title-12.** cx tells a brand owner that Amazon's AI title proposals wait in Review Listings Changes for a limited window and publish if left alone. cx cannot read that queue, so it asks the seller to put the proposals in the tree. Sources: Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27; 💡 Engage With Amazon thread; Answers to your product title update questions.
13. **P-title-13.** cx keeps size and colour out of a parent ASIN's title and in child titles, knowing the detail page shows the parent's title. Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines.
14. **P-title-14.** cx never puts a brand's special characters into the title (they belong in the Brand field), and for an unbranded product writes "generic" as brand and no brand in the title. Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines.
15. **P-title-15.** cx keeps a title edit within the product's original identity, since Amazon rejects identity changes (error 8541). Sources: Suggest changes to your product detail page.
16. **P-title-16.** For a seller who is not the brand owner, cx says a title edit is a contribution that may never display. Sources: Suggest changes to your product detail page; Listings APIs FAQ.
17. **P-title-17.** cx tracks competitors' titles through Keepa's `title`, `itemHighlights`, `parentTitle` and `parentHighlights` by diffing its own dated copies, since Keepa's product object documents no title-history field and a fresh read overwrites the landed file. Sources: Keepa product object; beta run day-2 report (lesson 6).
18. **P-title-18.** cx treats Amazon's AI title suggestions as drafts to check against the product and the seller's earning phrases, because Amazon says they may add claims not in the original. Sources: Enhance Listings.
19. **P-title-19.** For media product types cx expects no `title_differentiation` in the schema and does not apply the non-media title-length policy; it confirms both from that product type's schema. Sources: Media product types; SP-API Product Metadata Updates (September 2026 workbook).
20. **P-title-20.** Manage Your Experiments is the brand owner's tool for a controlled title test. Its result reaches cx only as a seller's export, and Amazon's page names conversion-side metrics, not click-through. Sources: Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments.

Sources: Product detail page rules; Listing quality; Product title requirements and guidelines; Error 90117; Bulk listing templates.

Sources: How to improve your product detail page for advertising; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products.

29. **P-title-29.** cx does not reuse a product title as a Sponsored Brands or display headline. Headlines are sentence case, Amazon rejects Title Case, and an ad's claims must be the ones on the detail page. Sources: A complete guide to Sponsored Brands and display ads moderation; Product title requirements and guidelines.
30. **P-title-30.** cx writes `item_name` and `title_differentiation` as arrays of objects with `value`, `language_tag` in the schema's form (for example `en_US`) and `marketplace_id`, never `languageTag` or `en-US`. It copies the shape from the schema it read and checks it with `VALIDATION_PREVIEW`. Sources: Submit modular titles; Submit Listings data; Manage Product Listings with the Selling Partner API.
31. **P-title-31.** cx reads a title issue by its enforcement action and exemption. `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` is reported as hidden from search, a visibility loss that looks like a ranking loss in the metrics, and is never read as a rank drop. `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED` means the value was rejected. `EXEMPT_UNTIL_EXPIRY_DATE` is reported with its date. Sources: listingsItems_2021-08-01 model.
32. **P-title-32.** cx matches title issues by message, `attributeNames` and enforcement as well as code. It accepts both the old and the new code for the same rule (for example `90004145` and `90225` for a too-long `item_name`), and it knows the title-policy codes Seller Central documents: `100470` (a word more than twice), `100471` (readability characters), `100473` / `990103` / `18540` (prohibited phrases), `990100` / `990190` (email, phone), `100339` / `8080` (markup), `90225` / `90197` (too long), `90230` (a value that differs across a variation family), `96009` (title too long) and `18559` (a child suppressed by its parent). Sources: Manage Listings Issues; Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting; Error 100470; Error 100471; Error 100473; Error 990103; Error 990100; Error 990190; Error 18540; Error 100339; Error 8080; Error 90225; Error 90197; Error 90230; Error 96009; Error 18559.

    - the page's prohibited characters and any decorative symbols;
    - any word used more than twice, counting singular and plural as the same word, per Amazon staff in 2025;
    - promotional, subjective and restricted phrases;
    - the content the detail-page rules bar: contact details, price or availability, reviews, time-sensitive information.

    Brand names and model numbers are "generally exempt" from the repetition count (Error 100470).

    Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines; Product detail page rules; Misleading and prohibited claims; About "New product title requirements effective January 21, 2025" (Glenn_Amazon reply); Error 100470; Error 100471; Error 100473.
34. **P-title-34.** cx states no fixed number of visible characters for mobile or desktop. It puts the brand, the product type and the phrase the change bets on at the front of `item_name`, because Amazon says visibility varies with the screen. Sources: Product title requirements and guidelines; Answers to your product title update questions; How to improve your product detail page for advertising.
35. **P-title-35.** A title audit in listing-review runs these checks, each answered from a source cx reads, not from memory:
    1. the schema's limit, unit and presence of `title_differentiation`;
    2. the policy screen (P-title-33);
    3. the information order and the category formula;
    4. parent/child consistency;
    5. submitted versus Catalog value (split, rewrite, other contributor);
    6. `issues` with their enforcements;
    7. pending AI proposals the seller exported from Review Listings Changes;
    8. whether the phrases the seller's traffic depends on (Search Query Performance) survive as contiguous phrases in `item_name` or Item highlights.

    Sources: Submit modular titles; Product title requirements and guidelines; Product variations overview; Listings APIs FAQ; listingsItems_2021-08-01 model; Updates to improve your product titles begin on July 27. Practitioner lists overlap with parts of it: AMALYTIX with checks 1–3, Canopy Management with check 3 (a template per category), Goat Consulting with check 4. Check 8 rests on Helium 10's phrase-breakage finding, not on an Amazon statement.

It does not claim that Item highlights or the Amazon title reach Google or other surfaces. Sources: Title [title] and structured title [structured_title] - Google Merchant Center Help; Product title requirements and guidelines (silent on off-Amazon surfaces).
