# Judging a change: baselines, windows, controls, confounders and verdicts

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage:** none of its own. This file is about method and applies to all three stages. It says how to decide whether one listing change moved the stage it targeted: exposure (impressions, sessions), click-through (clicks over impressions) or conversion (units or orders over sessions).

**Skills it feeds:**
- `effect-review`: the whole method. That means one change at a time, a baseline window and an observation window, the targeted stage's metric plus orders, the control's movement taken out first, a confounder check, no verdict during a major sale, and a verdict of *held*, *not held* or *cannot tell*.
- `listing-change-log`: what has to be written **before** the change so that the later verdict is a test and not a story. That is the expected range, the smallest move that would count, the metric and the file it comes from, the windows, the control set, and the expected lag.
- `listing-review`: when a listing was picked because it dropped, the review has to expect some recovery with no change at all (regression to the mean).

**Why a senior operator cares.** Most "this change worked" claims in Amazon selling are before/after readings of one noisy series, taken across a price move, an ad-budget change, a coupon, a stockout or a sale. The day-2 beta run shows what an assistant with no method does. Asked for a plan with "expected effects", cx proposed thirteen changes at once: images, title, bullets, A+, search terms, a coupon and ad changes. It gave each a percentage lift with no source. It then added content, coupon and image lifts together into one 30-day conversion forecast (`office-mailbox/from-engineering/20261014-0921-beta-browser-listing-run-day-2.md`, item D; figures not copied here). No later reading could say which change did what, and nothing written beforehand could be checked. Amazon's own A/B tool (Manage Your Experiments, MYE) avoids this by randomizing shoppers. For a seller or an ASIN that cannot use MYE, the rest of this file is the best method available. It is weaker, and the skill should say so plainly.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. Manage Your Experiments: Amazon's randomized test (RULES on eligibility and mechanics, GUIDANCE on duration)

RULES (what the tool is and does):
- **Eligibility.** "To access the Manage Your Experiments, you need a Professional selling account." "You also need to be a Brand Representative for a brand enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry." A product is eligible "if it belongs to your enrolled brand". For A+ or Brand Story experiments, "You must also have published a Brand Story or other A+ Content." [official] (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments | Sell on Amazon, https://sell.amazon.com/tools/manage-your-experiments, read 2026-10-14)
- **Traffic.** "An ASIN is eligible for testing if it belongs to your brand and has received enough traffic in recent weeks to produce valid experiment results." **No traffic threshold is published** on either Amazon page read. [official] (Optimize product content and increase sales with A/B testing, Avi Ravi, Amazon Selling Partner Blog, Mar 12, 2024, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/manage-your-experiments, read 2026-10-14; the tools page above says the same) Amazon's only hint is in a News announcement: "Depending on the category, high-traffic products might get several dozen orders per week, or more." It is an illustration, not a rule. [official, forum News] (Save time by running simultaneous experiments on brand listing content, News_Amazon, Seller Forums, shown "3 years ago", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/c9cc1344-6c4f-4f97-a079-1111cd0eeac9, read 2026-10-14)
- **Several attributes in one experiment.** "With the new Simultaneous Experiments feature in Manage Your Experiments, brands can A/B test various experiment types (title, images, bullet points, and A+ Content) for a single product ASIN within one experiment". [official, forum News] (Save time by running simultaneous experiments…) A seller in another thread reports that such a result shows only "version A and version B", with no breakdown by element [opinion, a seller, Manage Your Experiments (Multi-Attribute feature), https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/00a4626d-23ed-4c9d-842f-2b378312acac]. Even inside MYE, a multi-attribute test attributes its effect to the bundle.
- **Defaults.** A News announcement gives the defaults as "Duration: Experiment to Significance" and "Publication: Auto-publish. Once a test concludes, we automatically update your product listing." [official, forum News] (Brands can start A/B tests faster with the new Manage Your Experiments experience, News_Amazon, Seller Forums, shown "updated 3 years ago", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/c240077b552e0171dae4d8dda25032e2, read 2026-10-14)
- **What can be tested.** "test different versions of your product images, titles, bullet points, descriptions, and A+ Content", with Brand Story included under A+. [official] (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments) Price, coupons and search terms are not on the list.
- **Randomization.** "customers that view your product listing are randomly split into two groups". [official] (Optimize product content… with A/B testing)
- **Stopping and reporting.** The default setting is "to significance", "which means it will automatically end when there is enough data to declare a winner." "Manage Your Experiments updates experiment results once a week until the experiment ends." [official] (same)
- **The result's form.** "You'll see the percentage of the probability that one version of your content is better than the other." The page also lists units sold per unique visitor, conversion, sales, sample size and a projected one-year impact. **Neither page names the statistical method.** [official] (same; Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments)
- **Auto-publish.** "Winning content is also published automatically." [official] (Test listing content…) The blog states the condition: "we'll publish the alternate content automatically if it performed significantly better". [official] (Optimize product content…)

GUIDANCE:
- **Duration.** "we recommend running experiments for 8 to 10 weeks to allow enough time to generate statistically significant results." With "to significance", "you can sometimes get results as soon as four weeks." [official] (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments)
- **Design.** "Try to make the two versions as different as possible." [official] (Optimize product content…) This conflicts with the narrow "change one word" reading of one-change-at-a-time. See the research section: Kohavi et al. also reject the narrow reading for randomized tests.
- **Uplift claim.** "Optimized content can help increase sales by up to 20%", footnoted "Amazon internal data". [official] (Test listing content…) This is a marketing maximum. It is not a prior for any one change.
  - *Reconciled 2026-10-14:* Amazon's own pages disagree on this figure. The Brand Registry page, in its Manage Your Experiments paragraph, says the same kind of optimized content "can increase sales by up to 25%", also footnoted. [official] (Amazon Brand Registry | Sell on Amazon, https://sell.amazon.com/brand-registry, read 2026-10-14; `elements/images-and-video.md` records the same pair) Neither figure is used by a skill (P-2); a human reader is told both, with the page each is on.

- The lead's "minimum traffic" has no published threshold; Amazon's News post gives only the category-dependent illustration "several dozen orders per week, or more".
- **No SP-API operation for MYE was found** in the Listings, Catalog, Reports or Data Kiosk documents read. The result reaches cx only if the seller puts it into the tree.

The Seller Central help page on creating an experiment is Create a listing experiment, `GYZYA77YZTWTF7YZ` (the first pass tried the id without its leading "G" and reached only a page shell). ### B. When a change shows: Amazon's own words on latency (RULES about processing; figures for human readers)

- **Accepted is not done.** "However, **Accepted** does not mean the process is complete. Multiple downstream processes create the item in the Amazon catalog, and any of these processes can generate issues that return asynchronously." [official] (Manage Listings Issues, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/manage-listings-issues.md, updatedAt 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14) The model gives the receipt's states: `ACCEPTED`, "accepted for processing"; `INVALID`; and `VALID`, which is "Only returned when the `mode` is `VALIDATION_PREVIEW`". [official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/listings-items-api-model/listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, read 2026-10-14)
- **Minutes to the listing record.** "the Amazon backend updates every 15 minutes. As a result, every update might be delayed by 0-15 minutes". Notifications: "a 15-20 minute delay … is normal". `getListingsItem` "directly queries the current state, which is why the results are available faster (within 1-5 minutes)." [official] (Listings APIs FAQ, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-apis-faq.md, updatedAt 2026-09-09, read 2026-10-14)
- **The detail page may not follow at all.** "Why are my edits not making it to the detail page even though they appear as successful? … Downstream process and rules governing catalog data are the same regardless of submission method. In some cases, especially where we already have a listing for a product, the information submitted by a vendor might not have priority." The FAQ also separates the two reads: Catalog Items is "reconciled from various listings", while Listings Items is "the specific listing submitted by a selling partner". [official] (Listings APIs FAQ)
- **Up to 72 hours to search.** "Allow up to 72 hours for changes to reflect in search results." The same post says to allow 30 minutes for a bulk upload to process. [official, forum staff] (Why Isn't My Listing Showing in Search Results? A Complete Guide, Manny_Amazon, Seller Forums, shown "11 months ago", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/fb1124b0-adf9-4933-b470-f75160082cdf, read 2026-10-14)
- **What the lead said versus the sources.** The lead asked whether the figure is "up to 15 minutes" or "up to 48 hours". On the pages read, 15 minutes is the refresh cycle of the listing record and its notifications, not search. Amazon staff say up to 72 hours for search. Two Amazon help pages, read on 2026-10-14 from their captures, give the other figures: "24 to 48 hours" for a title or Item Highlights change to display, on Product title requirements and guidelines (`GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC`, version 21, capture `GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC-v21`), and "up to 72 hours" for any change to go live, on the Amazon search glossary (`GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV`, version 2, capture `GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV-v2`). See section K.
- **No Amazon page read says how long to wait before *judging* a change**, apart from MYE's 8–10-week recommendation for a randomized test. Any non-MYE observation window is a method choice. Amazon's words cover only latency.

### C. When the figures are ready, and in what grain (RULES of the data sources)

- **Data Kiosk seller analytics.** "All metrics in the seller analytics dataset are ready after 34 hours. Data for a given day is generally available at 10 AM local time two days later." When data is delayed, "the response is `null`". When data "is requested within one to two business days", the periods that are not available "contain null values". [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide.md, read 2026-10-14)
- **The same dataset carries confounder fields beside the outcome.**
  - `traffic`: `sessions`, `pageViews`, `featureOfferPct`, `conversionRatePct`. Amazon's guide and schema disagree here: the guide (updatedAt 2026-09-30, re-read 2026-10-14) still lists `conversationRatePct` under `traffic` and uses it in its example query, while the schema marks that spelling `@deprecated(reason: "No longer supported. Use conversionRatePct instead.")`. The binding field is the schema's, read at run time from `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql`. Either way it is search purchases over search clicks, not units over sessions (section F).
  - `sales`: `unitsOrdered`, `averageSellingPrice`.
  - `promotion`: `promotionType`, `promotionStartDate`, `promotionEndDate`, `promotionDiscountPct`, `dealId`, `dealTitle`, and more.
  - `reviews`: `totalReviewCount`, `lowRatingReviewCount`.
  - Promotion attributes "are not additive across periods".
  [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **Amazon's own year-over-year comparison.** `timeRangeBenchmarking` gives `weekOverWeek` and `yearOverYear` at `WEEK`. The week is "The preceding Sunday-to-Saturday week; the same ISO week number in the prior year". `monthOverMonth` and `yearOverYear` are given at `MONTH`. "no benchmarking is available at `DAY` granularity." [official] (same)
- **Search Query Performance periods.** "Requests cannot span multiple periods" (`WEEK`, `MONTH`, `QUARTER`). [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics.md, read 2026-10-14) An exposure or click-through verdict is therefore read in whole Amazon weeks.
- **Daily orders for one ASIN.** `getOrderMetrics` takes an `asin` filter, a `granularity`, and a `granularityTimeZone`, "An IANA-compatible time zone for determining the day boundary". `granularity=Hour` works only for intervals "less than or equal to 30 days from now". Other granularities allow up to "2 years". [official] (sales.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/sales-api-model/sales.json, read 2026-10-14)

### E. Ad spend without the Ads API: the Data Kiosk Economics dataset (RULES of the data source)

- **The field.** The Economics dataset's per-product record carries `ads: [AdSummary]`, described as "The advertising spend data for the product within the specified date range." `AdSummary` is "The advertisements spend data summary for the seller's account aggregated by transaction event type: charge". Its `adTypeName` reads, for example, "Sponsored Products charge", and its `charge` is an `AggregatedDetail`, whose fields include `amount`, `promotionAmount`, `taxAmount`, `totalAmount` and `quantity`. [official] (analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql, read 2026-10-14)
- **Grain.** "By default it's aggregated by MSKU and DAY." The product grain can be `CHILD_ASIN`, `MSKU` or `PARENT_ASIN`. `DAY` is "aggregated by day against marketplace local time", and `WEEK` is aggregated "against marketplace local time, Sunday is the first day of the week". "The start date must be no more than two years ago." A mid-week start or end date is snapped "in UTC+0 timezone": the start moves to "the following Sunday" and the end to "the preceding Saturday". [official] (same schema)
- **What it does not carry.** The `AdSummary` type holds only a type name and a charge. It has no clicks, impressions, search terms, bids or ad-attributed sales. The schema points to a Seller Central help page (GZ8Y22NL2FSRY8M5) for "advertisement charge types"; this seat did not read that page. [official] (same schema)
- **Role.** "The Economics dataset requires the Selling Partner Insights role." "The Vendor Analytics and Sales and Traffic datasets require the Brand Analytics role." The note says the original general-availability announcement was wrong when it said all datasets needed Brand Analytics. [official] (Correction: Data Kiosk role requirements, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/changelog/correction-data-kiosk-role-requirements, read 2026-10-14, re-read the same day)
  - *Reconciled 2026-10-14:* Amazon's pages do not agree on Data Kiosk roles. `angles/own-traffic-funnel.md` and `method/sp-api-access.md` record that Role Mappings for SP-API Operations and the Data Kiosk API page list Brand Analytics as the only role for `createQuery`, against the correction note's Selling Partner Insights for Economics. Neither page names a role for the seller analytics dataset (`analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31`): its guide speaks only of "Sales and traffic access" and "Sales and lending access", and its schema of PAAM rights (`analytics_sales_and_traffic_view_pa`, `analytics_sales_and_lending_view`). [official] (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; both re-read 2026-10-14) The binding answer for each dataset is the authorization result of the first `createQuery` under the seller's credential, which "returns an authorization error rather than a null value" for a field the application may not read (same guide).
- **Consequence for this file.** The earlier claim in this file (run-time list, Open question 6, P-14) that there is "no source today" for ad spend was wrong. Per-product daily ad *spend* is in Data Kiosk without an Ads API connection, given the Selling Partner Insights role. Ad *impressions, clicks and terms* still need the Ads API. Amazon names CPC ads as an input to search placement (section D), so spend is the minimum confounder read for an exposure verdict. [seat, from the two official sources above]

### F. Which "conversion"? Amazon's own definitions differ by file (RULES: field definitions)

Amazon itself warns against mixing them: "Since dashboards may attribute and define customer actions in different ways, we do not recommend comparing the metric names of data across different dashboards." [official] (Search Query Performance dashboard, G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, version 5, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G8J4CB5ZBF3NX7TP, capture read 2026-10-14)

| Name as Amazon gives it | Where | Numerator / denominator, in Amazon's words | Origin of the traffic |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unitSessionPercentage` | Sales and Traffic report (`TrafficByAsin`); Data Kiosk `analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24` | "calculated by dividing unitsOrdered by sessions" | all detail-page sessions |
| `unitSessionPercentageB2B` | same | "calculated by dividing unitsOrderedB2B by sessions" (Amazon's wording; the denominator is written as `sessions`, not `sessionsB2B`) | B2B units |
| `orderItemSessionPercentage` | Data Kiosk Sales and Traffic | "how many order items were generated relative to the number of people who viewed the products" | all sessions |
| `traffic.conversionRatePct` | Data Kiosk `analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31` | "the percentage of purchases to clicks for ASINs originated from the search results page" | search only |
| `traffic.conversationRatePct` | same | same text; `@deprecated(reason: "No longer supported. Use conversionRatePct instead.")` | search only |
| `search.catalog.conversionRate` | same dataset | "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a purchase." | search only |
| `search.catalog.clickRate` | same dataset | "Percentage of impressions that resulted in a click." | search only |
| `conversionRate` (Search Catalog Performance) | `sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json` | "The number of purchases divided by the number of clicks for the ASIN." | search only |
| `clickRate` (Search Catalog Performance) | same | "The number of clicks divided by the number of impressions for the ASIN." | search only |
| `asinClickShare`, `asinPurchaseShare` (SQP) | `sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json` | "The fraction of clicks for the current ASIN in comparison to the total click count for the query"; purchases likewise | search, per query |
| Featured Offer: `featureOfferPct` | Data Kiosk seller analytics | "dividing the number of times your offer is the Featured Offer by the number of times customers viewed it (that is, Glance Views)" | all |
| Featured Offer: `buyBoxPercentage` | Sales and Traffic report and dataset | "The percentage of page views where the buy box … appeared on the page for customers to add your product to their cart" | all |
| MYE "conversion rate" | Experiment results, GY6DCFA53HYDH79H | "the percentage of unique visitors in the experiment who saw the A+ Content and made a purchase" (Amazon's wording names A+ Content); "Units per unique visitor is units divided by sample size" | identified customers in the experiment |

[official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql and analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/tree/main/schemas/data-kiosk; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json and sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/tree/main/schemas/reports; Experiment results, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GY6DCFA53HYDH79H, version 4, capture; all read 2026-10-14)

- **Inside one Data Kiosk dataset, two search conversions differ.** `traffic.conversionRatePct` divides by clicks. `search.catalog.conversionRate` divides by impressions. The Search Catalog Performance report's `conversionRate` divides by clicks. A "lift" read across any two of these, or between any of them and `unitSessionPercentage`, can be produced by the change of denominator alone. [seat, from the definitions above]
- **Featured Offer share: Amazon's pages give two denominators.** The seller analytics schema divides `featureOfferPct` by "the number of times customers viewed it (that is, Glance Views)". The Business Reports figure is defined over page views on two help pages: "the total number of page views in which your offer is featured divided by the total number of page views for your products" (Becoming the Featured Offer, `G201687550`, version 20, capture capture `G201687550-v20`), and "The percentage of page views where the Featured Offer … appeared on the page" (Business reports glossary, `G27691`, version 7, capture capture `G27691-v7`). No page read says whether glance views and page views are the same count. The same help page also says "As of September 2026, Amazon no longer maintains a seller eligibility requirement, and offers from all sellers are now eligible to be evaluated for the Featured Offer" (also the whole text of the removed Featured Offer eligibility page, `G200418100`, version 23, capture). No page read says whether the share's meaning changed with that, which `angles/price-position.md` leaves open. [official] (sources named; all read 2026-10-14) The binding definition of a field is the one in the file it comes from, read at run time; a share is never carried across the eligibility change as one series without saying so. [seat]
- **Sessions are de-duplicated by day.** "All browser and mobile app activity within a 24-hour period is considered a session", and `sessions` "is calculated as the sum of browserSessions and mobileAppSessions". [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- **Latency differs by figure.** Seller analytics figures are ready "after 34 hours" (section C). Returns are slower: `returnedUnits` is "mapped to the day each unit was shipped", and "Return data is computed with a delay of about 45 days, so the most recent ~45 days may be empty or incomplete." `returnedRate` is "returned units / shipped units" and is "Not supported under totals". [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; the Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide repeats "the most recent ~45 days may be null or incomplete") The verifier's "30-day return lag" below is therefore not Amazon's figure for this data.

### G. The calendar each source uses (RULES of the data sources)

| Source | Week | Day boundary | What happens to a misaligned request |
|---|---|---|---|
| SQP, Search Catalog Performance, Market Basket (reports) | Sunday to Saturday | not stated | "dataStartTime must be a Sunday for the WEEK reportPeriod", "dataEndTime must be a Saturday", "or else a fatal error returns" (Market Basket: "a fatal error is returned") |
| Sales and Traffic report | Sunday to Saturday | not stated | expanded **outward** to the first day ("Sunday") and last day ("Saturday") of the period |
| Data Kiosk Sales and Traffic 2024-04-24 | Sunday to Saturday | not stated | snapped **inward**: start to "the following Sunday", end to "the preceding Saturday" |
| Data Kiosk seller analytics 2025-03-31 | Sunday to Saturday | "10 AM local time"; zone not defined | start "rolled back to the preceding Sunday" (outward), end "rolled back to the preceding Saturday" (inward); "After adjustment the range must span at least seven days" |
| Data Kiosk Economics 2024-03-15 | "Sunday is the first day of the week" | "marketplace local time" | inward, judged "in UTC+0 timezone" |
| `getOrderMetrics` | `firstDayOfWeek` "either Monday or Sunday. Default: Monday." | `granularityTimeZone`, "An IANA-compatible time zone for determining the day boundary"; it "must align with the offset of the specified interval" | "the head and tail end of the response interval will contain partial data" |

[official] (the three report schemas and three Data Kiosk schemas named above; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/seller-analytics-dataset-guide.md; sales.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/sales-api-model/sales.json; all read 2026-10-14)

- The Sunday-to-Saturday SQP week **is** in the schema, with a fatal error for a misaligned request. (This settles the contradiction with `angles/paid-keywords.md` Open question 2.)
- `getOrderMetrics` weeks start on **Monday** unless `firstDayOfWeek=Sunday` is sent. An order series built with the default is one day out of step with every Amazon search report around the change date. [seat, from sales.json]
- The same nominal dates asked of the Sales and Traffic report, the Sales and Traffic dataset and the seller analytics dataset can return three different date spans when they are not aligned to Sunday and Saturday. [seat, from the schemas]

### H. Seeing an Amazon-side content change inside a window (RULES on signals; GUIDANCE on seller-side views)

- **The push signal, brand owners only.** "Amazon sends a `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` notification whenever there is a change to the title, description, bullet points, or images, for any ASIN that the selling partner has a brand relationship with", a brand relationship meaning "a verified brand owner". The payload is `MarketplaceId`, `BrandName`, `Asin` and `AttributesChanged`, whose listed values are `bullet_point`, `item_name`, `product_description` "and all product image attributes". An image value means "*at least one* image changed". The payload has **no field naming who made the change or what the new value is**. The page does not list `title_differentiation` (Item Highlights) among the values. [official] (Notification Type Values, https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values.md, updated 2026-09-30, read 2026-10-14; BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/notifications/BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json, read 2026-10-14) The page does not say whether the notification fires for an Amazon AI rewrite, an MYE auto-publish or another contributor's edit. The wording "whenever there is a change" covers any change to those attributes, but no Amazon page read confirms it for each source. This stays open (Open question 11).
- **Amazon's title rewrites.** "After July 27, any titles still over 75 characters will be updated to the AI recommendation gradually." The post also says: "Review and approve AI-generated recommendations in Review Listings Changes up to 14 days before changes go live." "View any AI-applied changes in Manage All Inventory → click View Change History." The post also offers "Enhance Listings" to "Review, modify, and apply AI-recommended titles and Item Highlights". [official, forum staff] (Engage With Amazon: Get your Product Title Update Questions Answered!, title led by a light-bulb symbol, Dougal_Amazon, shown "4 months ago", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/ac660707-60c7-43e3-a3fd-420d7321cc4e, read 2026-10-14) View Change History is described only for AI-applied changes; the post does not say it lists other contributors' edits.
- **The general rule.** A non-compliant listed title "might be automatically corrected or it might not appear in our search results". ### I. MYE mechanics that bear on interference and on the edit freeze (RULES of the tool)

- **Both arms share the search rank.** "Customers in the experimental group will see your content everywhere it is available. For example, an experimental product title will show in search results, on the product detail page, and in cart/checkout. (Note: Experiments do not impact search rankings)." [official] (Manage Your Experiments, GVP453K5XRBJS7Y9, version 7, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GVP453K5XRBJS7Y9, capture read 2026-10-14)
- **Unit and method.** "Experiments are based on individual customer accounts." Customers see one version "persistently for the duration of the experiment regardless of device type". "Visits to your page where a customer cannot be identified are not included in the sample size." "We use a Bayesian approach to analyze experiment results", reporting "the mean effect size (in terms of change in units) and the 95% confidence interval (also known as a credible interval)". The projected one-year impact "doesn't take into account seasonality, price changes, or other factors". [official] (Experiment results, GY6DCFA53HYDH79H, version 4, capture)
- **One experiment per ASIN; content frozen in flight.** "Only one experiment can be run on any particular ASIN at a time" (Manage Your Experiments, capture). "While an experiment is in-progress, the content can no longer be revised. However, you can still revise the hypothesis and duration". To change content, "you must cancel the existing experiment and create a new one" (Create a listing experiment, capture). Neither page says whether a manual listing edit outside MYE is blocked while an experiment runs (Open question 12).
- **Variation families.** "you must also submit content for the parent ASIN since that is used more broadly throughout the customer experience". "Secondary image experiments are only available for ASINs with four or fewer child ASINs." [official] (Create a listing experiment, capture)
- **Duration and stop rule.** "Eight weeks is the recommended length for a set duration experiment"; "to significance" ends "whenever there are statistically relevant results—in as little as four weeks", available "for title, image, and bullet point experiments". [official] (same) "Don't check your results too frequently before an experiment ends: Interim results can be misleading." [official] (Tips for experiments, G2BNMJ8NFCGZQVFH, version 5, capture)
- None of the four MYE help pages states an uplift percentage. The uplift claims are on sell.amazon.com only, and they differ: "up to 20%" on the MYE tools page, "up to 25%" on the Brand Registry page (section A).

## What practitioners add

- **Jungle Scout, Brian Connolly, "Amazon A/B Testing: Step-by-Step Guide", 2024-01-19** [opinion]:
  - MYE: "You can choose from 4 to 10 weeks"; "both versions will be running at the exact same time". An example result reads "There is a 77% probability Version B is better".
  - Without MYE: "test only one component at a time". "If you test multiple components of your listing at once, you won't know which component is causing the change."
  - Duration: "let each version run for at least two weeks", and longer "If your listing has low sales".
  - It says to compare "sessions, conversion rates, and units ordered before and after each test".
  - It does **not** treat price, ads, season, stock or promotions as confounders.
  (https://www.junglescout.com/resources/articles/amazon-ab-testing/)
- **eComEngine, guest article by Jake Merrill (Launchbox Creative), "Boost Buyability with Amazon's Manage Your Experiments Tool", 2024-10-17** [opinion]:
  - MYE shows "an evidence strength level (eg. "Weak")". "For low-confidence winners, you will notice that the "worst case" impact may be negative."
  - "Running tests for at least 8-10 weeks can provide the sample size needed for reliable insights."
  - "Ensure that all test elements (aside from the one you're experimenting with) remain the same for a fair comparison."
  - The evidence-strength label and the worst-case figure are not on either Amazon page read. They are a practitioner's report of the dashboard.
  (https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/amazon-manage-your-experiments)
- **PickFu, Matt Ellis, "A complete guide to Amazon Manage Your Experiments", published 2021-06-21, updated 2025-11-18** [opinion; PickFu sells an alternative]:
  - "Amazon recommends that sellers run an experiment for 10 weeks". This is narrower than Amazon's own "8 to 10 weeks".
  - "MYE only lets you test high-traffic products", and "What constitutes high traffic depends on the category".
  - "Half of your users — *real* customers — will receive the inferior content."
  (https://www.pickfu.com/blog/amazon-manage-your-experiments)
- **Helium 10 Serious Sellers Podcast #119, Bradley Sutton with Justin Chen (PickFu), 2020-03-03** [opinion]:
  - Sutton describes the usual sequential test in his own voice: "I'm going to test this main image for like five days and check my impressions, check my sales". Chen warns of "a lot of confounding factors" in live testing.
  - It is old, and it is useful only as a record that sequential tests were the norm before MYE.
  (https://www.helium10.com/blog/amazon-split-testing/)
- **Seller Labs, Denis D, "Sales Dropped? Split It Before You Raise Ad Spend", 2026-10-01** [opinion]:
  - It decomposes "Sales = sessions × conversion rate × average selling price" into three effects that "add up exactly to the total change".

  - Confounders: the traffic mix ("The traffic mix changed, not the page"); "coupon, Lightning Deal, Subscribe & Save mix, or a repricer"; "lost Featured Offer, stockout, or a hijacker".

- **Helium 10, Bradley Sutton, "Amazon Title Requirements: How to Split Your Title…", 2026-08-03** [opinion]:
  - It reports Amazon's position that "relevancy, indexing, and advertising performance do not change when an existing title is simply split across the two fields".
  - It names the real risk as "keyword phrases getting broken apart during an AI rewrite". For a change log, this means Amazon can itself change the treatment during the window.
  - The weight of words at the end of Item Highlights is unproven: "nobody has proven it either way yet."
  (https://www.helium10.com/blog/amazon-title-requirements-how-to-split-your-title-into-item-name-and-item-highlights-without-losing-rankings/)

**Where practitioners disagree, with each other or with Amazon:**
1. MYE duration. Amazon says 8–10 weeks, or as soon as four weeks with "to significance". PickFu says 10. Jungle Scout says a choice of 4–10.
2. Variant design. Amazon says make versions "as different as possible". Jungle Scout and eComEngine say to change one element. These are compatible: Amazon's advice is about a randomized test of two versions, and the practitioners' advice is about attribution.
3. Manual test length. Jungle Scout says at least two weeks. Seller Labs says 60–90 days for low-traffic ASINs. Neither gives a basis in counts; the research section does.
4. No practitioner source read gives a control-series method or a regression-to-the-mean warning. Only Seller Labs lists confounders. This is the gap the research fills.

## Published research

- **Kohavi, Tang, Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing*, Cambridge University Press, 2020** [research]. Read: the publisher's free Chapter 1 and the table of contents (experimentguide.com).
  - Chapter 1 places "Randomized Controlled Experiments" above "Other controlled experiments (e.g., natural, non-randomized)" and "Observational studies" in a hierarchy of evidence (Figure 1.3, after Greenhalgh 2014). It says "Randomized controlled experiments are the gold standard for establishing causality."
  - Twyman's law: "Any figure that looks interesting or different is usually wrong".
  - Base rates: "Only one third of the ideas tested at Microsoft improved the metric(s) they were designed to improve", and "about 10–20%" in well-optimized domains.
  - The table of contents shows chapters on threats to internal and external validity, the A/A test, variance estimation, and "Observational Causal Studies" (with "Designs for Observational Causal Studies", "Pitfalls" and "SIDEBAR: Refuted Observational Causal Studies"). This seat did not read those chapters' text, so no finding from them is stated.
  - **Transfer:** direct for the ranking of evidence. An MYE result outranks any before/after a skill can produce. Base rates warn that most listing changes should be expected to move little.
- **Kohavi, Longbotham, Sommerfield, Henne, "Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide", *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 18:140–181, 2009** [research]:
  - Randomization: "Controlled experiments neutralize confounding variables by distributing them equally over all values through random assignment".
  - Sample size: formula (2) gives n = 16σ²/Δ² per variant for 95% confidence and 80% power. "Replace the 16 by 21 … to increase the power to 90%."
  - Timing (§6.2.5): "we strongly recommend running experiments for at least a week or two, then continuing by multiples of a week so that day-of-week effects can be analyzed". "This lesson can be generalized to other time-related events, such as holidays and seasons".
  - Novelty: "Primacy and newness effects… imply that some experiments need to be run for multiple weeks."
  - Pre-commitment (§3.2.4): "The choice of OEC must be made in advance … otherwise, there is an increased risk of finding what appear to be significant results by chance". §6.3.1: "Agree on the OEC upfront".
  - One factor at a time (§6.1.3): the advice "interpreted narrowly, is too restrictive". Their recommendation is "Conduct single-factor experiments for gaining insights and when you make incremental changes that could be decoupled", plus "some bold bets".
  - **Transfer:**
    - Whole weeks and multiples of a week transfer directly.
    - Choosing the metric in advance transfers directly to the change log.
    - The sample-size logic transfers with σ² taken from daily order counts.
    - The "neutralize confounding" sentence is exactly what a before/after lacks.
    - One change at a time is a *requirement* in a before/after, because no factorial design is possible on one listing in one period. In a randomized test it is only *advice*.
- **Kohavi, Deng, Frasca, Longbotham, Walker, Xu, "Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: Five Puzzling Outcomes Explained", KDD 2012** [research]:
  - Novelty and primacy "can be assessed by generating the delta graph … over time".
  - "Another option is to exclude the first week, as the delta usually stabilizes after a week."
  - "most cases of suspected Primacy and Novelty effects are not real, but just a statistical artifact."
  - It also documents carryover effects that lasted weeks after an experiment ended.
  - **Transfer:** a downward or upward drift in a listing change's first days is weak evidence of novelty. It is more likely noise.
- **Kohavi, Deng, Longbotham, Xu, "Seven Rules of Thumb for Web Site Experimenters", KDD 2014** [research]:
  - Rule #2: "Changes Rarely have a Big Positive Impact to Key Metrics". For sites like Bing, most experiments fail, and those that succeed "improve key metrics by 0.1% to 1.0%, once diluted to overall impact". With a prior of 1/3, a significant result is a true positive 89% of the time. At 1 in 500 the figure "drops to 3.1%".
  - "Results with borderline statistically significant results should be viewed as tentative and rerun".
  - "we recommend running experiments for two weeks". Novelty and primacy effects "are uncommon".
  - Rule #6: "simple designs are best".
  - **Transfer:** directional only. Bing's effect sizes do not carry over to a single Amazon ASIN, but the Bayes argument does: a large claimed lift from one wording change should be treated as more likely wrong.
- **Fabijan, Gupchup, Gupta, Omhover, Qin, Vermeer, Dmitriev, "Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch in Online Controlled Experiments: A Taxonomy and Rules of Thumb for Practitioners", KDD 2019, pp. 2156–2164** (author list from Crossref, DOI 10.1145/3292500.3330722; the Microsoft Research page lists only Fabijan) [research]:
  - An SRM is "a symptom for a variety of data quality issues". Ignoring it can let "a bad product modification appearing to be good and getting shipped".
  - **Transfer:** it applies to reading an MYE result. If the per-version sample sizes MYE shows are far apart, the seller should doubt the result and ask Amazon. A before/after has no randomized split, so SRM does not arise there.
- **Johari, Pekelis, Walsh, "Always Valid Inference: Bringing Sequential Analysis to A/B Testing", arXiv:1512.04922, 2015** [research]:
  - Classical p-values and intervals "are wholly unreliable if users endogenously choose samples sizes by *continuously monitoring* their tests".
  - **Transfer:** direct to a cx that wakes on a heartbeat. Reading the series every day and stopping when it looks good is peeking. The observation window's end has to be fixed in the change log, or a sequential rule used. MYE's "to significance" is a sequential stop on Amazon's side, and Amazon does not say which rule it uses.
- **Brodersen, Gallusser, Koehler, Remy, Scott, "Inferring causal impact using Bayesian structural time-series models", *Annals of Applied Statistics* 9(1):247–274, 2015 (arXiv:1506.00356)** [research]:
  - Control series "can be based, for example, on the same product in a different region that did not receive the intervention or on a metric that reflects activity in the industry as a whole".
  - "As long as the control series received no intervention themselves", the relationship can be assumed to continue. The approach "assumes that covariates are unaffected by the effects of treatment". Spill-over into controls "would lead to pessimistic inferences".
  - On DD: static models on serially correlated data "yield overoptimistic inferences with too narrow uncertainty intervals". "most DD analyses only consider two time points".
  - The impact intervals widen with time after the intervention ("predictions should become increasingly uncertain").
  - The same model can be used for power analysis on a hypothetical past intervention.
  - **Transfer:** this is the closest published method to "subtract the competitor set's movement". Two warnings carry over directly:
    - (i) A competitor set competes for the same shoppers, so it *is* affected by our change. If we win share they lose it, and subtracting their movement can overstate our effect. [seat: the sign of the bias here is the seat's reasoning from the paper's spill-over sentence. The paper's own example is a same-sign spill-over that understates.]
    - (ii) Long observation windows lose precision.
    - Query-level demand, such as search volume for the core queries, is a control our listing barely causes.
- **Abadie, Diamond, Hainmueller, "Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies…", NBER WP 12831 (2007), *JASA* 105 (2010)** and **Abadie, "Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects", *Journal of Economic Literature* 59(2):391–425, 2021** [research]:
  - Read only from the abstract pages. The 2007 abstract measures the treated unit "relative to a comparable synthetic control region", built as a weighted combination of untreated units. The 2021 article "aims to provide practical guidance" and describes "the settings where synthetic controls provide reliable estimates and those where they may fail".
  - The seat did not read the 2021 text, so its specific data requirements are not stated here.
  - **Transfer:** the idea of a weighted control fitted on the baseline period. The competitor-set sum is a crude, equal-weight version of it.
- **Bertrand, Duflo, Mullainathan, "How Much Should We Trust Differences-in-Differences Estimates?", NBER WP 8841 (2002); *QJE* 2004** [research, from the abstract page]:
  - With serially correlated data, conventional DD standard errors are badly biased. In their placebo tests, laws that did nothing appear significant at 5% in up to 45% of cases.
  - Collapsing the data to a before period and an after period is one remedy.
  - **Transfer:** comparing daily series day by day and counting "significant" days overstates certainty. Comparing window totals (or weekly totals) is the safer reading.
- **Lopez Bernal, Cummins, Gasparrini, "Interrupted time series regression for the evaluation of public health interventions: a tutorial", *Int J Epidemiol* 46(1):348–355, 2017** [research]:
  - "There are no fixed limits regarding the number of data points", and "Power increases with the number of time points".
  - "ITS can be affected by time-varying confounders that change more rapidly", especially "other events that occur around the same time as the intervention".
  - A controlled ITS adds "a control group or control outcome which would not have been affected by the intervention".
  - On the impact model (level change, slope change, lag): "It is important that this decision is made a priori".
  - **Transfer:** direct. A listing change is an interruption in one series. The expected shape and lag of the effect belong in the change log before the change.
- **Masoero, Vijaykumar, Richardson, McQueen, Rosen, Burdick, Bajari, Imbens, "Multiple Randomization Designs: Estimation and Inference with Interference", arXiv:2112.13495, 2021**, and **Bajari, Burdick, Imbens, Masoero, McQueen, Richardson, Rosen, "Experimental design in marketplaces", *Statistical Science*, 2023** (Amazon Science page) [research]:
  - Completely randomized designs "are of limited value … in marketplaces, where multiple populations of agents interact strategically, leading to complex patterns of spillover effects".
  - **Transfer:** these are Amazon economists confirming that marketplace units interfere. One seller's listing and its competitors are not independent, which supports warning (i) under Brodersen above.
- **Johari, Li, Liskovich, Weintraub, "Experimental Design in Two-Sided Platforms: An Analysis of Bias", *Management Science* 68(10), October 2022 (arXiv:2002.05670, v1 Feb 2020, v5 Sep 2021)** [research; journal citation from the Stanford GSB publication page]:
  - "This interference leads to biased estimates of the treatment effect of the intervention." "good experimental design depends on market balance": customer-side randomization is unbiased in highly demand-constrained markets and listing-side randomization in highly supply-constrained ones; each is biased in the other regime.
  - **Transfer:** the same point at the platform level. Effects measured on one listing against its rivals mix gain with transfer between them.
- **Barnett, van der Pols, Dobson, "Regression to the mean: what it is and how to deal with it", *Int J Epidemiol* 34(1):215–220, 2005** [research, from the abstract]:

## Stable enough for a skill

- MYE is Amazon's randomized test of listing content for brand representatives of a Brand-Registry brand with enough recent traffic. It tests images, titles, bullets, descriptions and A+. - A randomized split outranks any before/after reading as evidence of cause. (Kohavi et al. 2009; Kohavi, Tang, Xu 2020, ch. 1)
- `ACCEPTED` means accepted for processing, not live. Issues can arrive later, and the detail page may show reconciled data that is not what the seller submitted. (Manage Listings Issues; Listings APIs FAQ; listingsItems_2021-08-01.json)
- Search and the detail page can lag a listing change by days, on Amazon's own pages. The figures are Amazon's to restate and are quoted from the page, not built into a rule. - In a before/after, two changes made together cannot be told apart. One change per window is the condition for attribution. (Kohavi et al. 2009 §6.1.3 for when single-factor tests are right; Jungle Scout, Connolly 2024 [opinion])
- A control series is valid only if the change did not affect it. Competitors share the shoppers, so their movement is partly ours. (Brodersen et al. 2015; Masoero et al. 2021; Johari et al. 2020)
- A listing picked because it dropped will tend to recover on its own. (Barnett et al. 2005)
- Failing to find an effect is not finding none. *Not held* needs a window large enough to have seen the expected effect. (Lakens 2017; Greenland et al. 2016)
- Checking daily and stopping when it looks good inflates false wins. The window's end is set in advance. (Johari, Pekelis, Walsh 2015)
- Price, ads and account health are Amazon's own named inputs to search placement, so they are confounders for an exposure verdict. (Amazon SEO: 7 ways…)

- Returns and refunds in the seller analytics dataset are mapped to the ship day and computed with a delay the schema states; days inside that delay may be empty or incomplete. (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- `BRANDED_ITEM_CONTENT_CHANGE` tells a verified brand owner that an ASIN's title, bullets, description or images changed, without saying by whom or to what. (Notification Type Values; BrandedItemContentChangeNotification.json)
- Treating correlated events (impressions, searches) as independent understates variance. A pre-period of the same metric is the strongest covariate, provided the change cannot affect it. (Deng, Knoblich, Lu 2018; Deng, Xu, Kohavi, Walker 2013)
- Marketplace interference has been measured large in published field experiments (about two-fold; at least a fifth). (Blake, Coey 2014; Holtz et al. 2024)
- Short-term effects do not always predict long-term ones, and effects can persist after a change is reverted. (Hohnhold, O'Brien, Tang 2015)
- Most measured e-commerce page changes move revenue by about one percent or less, and cosmetic ones least. (Browne, Swarbrick Jones 2017 [vendor white paper]; Kohavi et al. 2014)
- Running a verdict on dates when nothing changed (an in-time placebo) is a published falsification check. Controls hit by their own shocks are left out. (Abadie, Diamond, Hainmueller 2015)
- Pre-trend tests have low power, and staggered rollouts read with one fixed-effects regression are biased when effects change over time. (Roth 2022; Goodman-Bacon 2021; Sun, Abraham 2021)
- Scanning many segments or queries finds chance "wins" (one in twenty at 5%). The primary metric is fixed in advance, and a scan needs a correction or pooling. (Dirty Dozen 2017; Kohavi et al. 2013; Benjamini, Hochberg 1995; Gelman, Hill, Yajima 2012)

## Positions

- **P-effect-measurement-1.** When the seller is a brand representative and the ASIN is eligible, cx proposes an MYE test for a change to images, title, bullets, description or A+ before any before/after reading, because randomization is the stronger evidence. (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments; Optimize product content… with A/B testing; Controlled experiments on the web)
- **P-effect-measurement-2.** cx quotes MYE durations, thresholds and result wording only from Amazon's page or the seller's dashboard. It treats Amazon's published "up to" uplift claims for optimized content as marketing maxima, never as an expected effect; they rest on undisclosed "Amazon internal data" and differ between Amazon's own pages. (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments; Amazon Brand Registry | Sell on Amazon; Seven Rules of Thumb)
- **P-effect-measurement-3.** An MYE result reaches cx only through a file or words from the seller. cx records it with its probability and per-version sample sizes, and treats a large imbalance in sample sizes as a reason to doubt it. (Optimize product content…; Diagnosing Sample Ratio Mismatch)
- **P-effect-measurement-4.** A running MYE experiment on the ASIN is a confounder: no before/after verdict is given for another change on that ASIN while it runs or auto-publishes. (Test listing content with Manage Your Experiments; Optimize product content…)
- **P-effect-measurement-5.** One change per ASIN per observation window. When several are made together, the change log records them as one combined change and the verdict is about the combination. (Controlled experiments on the web §6.1.3; Save time by running simultaneous experiments on brand listing content, for Amazon's own bundled test; Amazon A/B Testing, Jungle Scout [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-6.** Before the write, the change log fixes:
  - the targeted stage and its metric, plus orders;
  - the file or operation each comes from;
  - the expected direction and range;
  - the smallest move that would count;
  - the baseline and observation windows by date;
  - the dated control set;
  - the expected lag and shape of the effect.
  
  Any later change to these is recorded with its reason. (Controlled experiments on the web §3.2.4, §6.3.1; Interrupted time series regression…; The preregistration revolution; Equivalence Tests)
- **P-effect-measurement-7.** The observation window starts when Amazon shows the change, not when cx wrote it:
  - first `getListingsItem` holding the new value with no `ERROR` issue;
  - then `getCatalogItem` showing it;
  - and, for an exposure or click-through reading, the first whole Sunday-to-Saturday week that starts after Amazon's stated latency for search has run. The latency is quoted from Amazon's page when the change log is written, not fixed in the skill. (Reconciled 2026-10-14: this line used to build in a fixed number of hours.)
  
  (Manage Listings Issues; Listings APIs FAQ; Amazon search glossary; Product title requirements and guidelines; Why Isn't My Listing Showing in Search Results?)
- **P-effect-measurement-8.** If the Catalog value differs from what was submitted, or Amazon changes the listing during the window, the verdict is *cannot tell* for the submitted change. The report says what Amazon showed instead. (Listings APIs FAQ; Amazon Title Requirements…, Helium 10 [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-9.** Baseline and observation windows are whole weeks with matching weekdays. Exposure and click-through verdicts use Amazon's own weekly Search Query Performance periods. (Controlled experiments on the web §6.2.5; Analytics Reports; Seven Rules of Thumb)
- **P-effect-measurement-10.** cx reads no day whose figures Amazon has not yet finalized, and treats a null as missing, not zero. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)
- **P-effect-measurement-11.** The observation window's end is set in the change log. A heartbeat before that date reports progress but gives no verdict. (Always Valid Inference; Controlled experiments on the web §3.2.4)
- **P-effect-measurement-12.** The control's same-period movement is taken out before the listing's own movement is read. The report names the control and says it is imperfect. Competitors share shoppers with the changed listing, so their movement is partly caused by it. Query search volume for the core queries is the cleaner control for demand. (Inferring causal impact… (Brodersen et al.); Multiple Randomization Designs; Experimental Design in Two-Sided Platforms)
- **P-effect-measurement-13.** Where a window touches a seasonal swing, the same weeks last year are read alongside, using Amazon's `yearOverYear` benchmark where available. (Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Controlled experiments on the web §6.2.5; Sales Dropped?…, Seller Labs [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-14.** For both windows, cx reads and names in the verdict:
  - own price;
  - featured-offer share;
  - promotions and deals;
  - stock;
  - review count and rating;
  - ads: spend from the Data Kiosk Economics dataset (P-23); impressions, clicks, bids and targeting by the seller's word until the Ads API is connected;
  - the competitor set's price, deal and stock-out events;
  - major sales.
  
  A material move in any of them makes the verdict *cannot tell* unless the targeted metric's move clearly exceeds it. (Amazon SEO: 7 ways…; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide; Interrupted time series regression…; Sales Dropped?…, Seller Labs [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-15.** No *held* or *not held* verdict is given when either window overlaps a major sale or its run-up and ramp-down. The verdict is *cannot tell*, with the event named. (Controlled experiments on the web §6.2.5; Interrupted time series regression…)
- **P-effect-measurement-16.** When a listing was picked for review because it dropped, the baseline is not the drop weeks alone, and the report says that some recovery was expected with no change. (Regression to the mean…)
- **P-effect-measurement-17.** Every verdict states the counts behind it (orders, sessions, and impressions where used) in each window, not only percentages. (Controlled experiments on the web, formula (2); Seven Rules of Thumb, Rule #7; NIST Poisson Distribution; Sales Dropped?…, Seller Labs [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-18.** The three verdicts:
  - **Held:** after the control is taken out, the targeted metric moved in the expected direction by at least the logged smallest move, orders did not fall, and no confounder or event explains it.
  - **Not held:** the window had enough data to have seen the logged smallest move and did not, or the metric moved the wrong way.
  - **Cannot tell:** everything else, including too few counts, a confounder, an event, an Amazon-side change, or a change that never showed.
  
  (Equivalence Tests; Statistical tests, P values…; Controlled experiments on the web)
- **P-effect-measurement-19.** A large win from a small wording change is checked for a data or confounder explanation before it is reported as *held*. (Seven Rules of Thumb, Twyman's law; Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments, ch. 1)
- **P-effect-measurement-20.** Comparisons use window or weekly totals, not a count of "significant days". (How Much Should We Trust Differences-in-Differences Estimates?; Inferring causal impact…)
- **P-effect-measurement-21.** A drift in the first days after a change is not reported as a novelty effect or a trend. The full window is read. (Five Puzzling Outcomes Explained)
- **P-effect-measurement-22.** cx never adds the expected lifts of several changes into one forecast. Each change has its own expected range, its own window and its own verdict. (Controlled experiments on the web §6.1.3; Seven Rules of Thumb, Rule #2; day-2 run report [internal, as the failure observed])

  - Any content difference cx did not write makes the verdict *cannot tell* for the logged change (as P-8). The report names the attribute and the date it was first seen.
  - A title change submitted with Amazon's "Create A/B experiment on product name" box ticked is an MYE experiment, not a before/after. It is read as MYE's result, and its auto-publish or its end is a second change.

- **P-effect-measurement-27.** cx reports an MYE result as the effect of the content on shoppers who reached it, at a search rank both versions share. It does not present MYE's projected impact as a forecast of exposure or rank after publishing, and it does not add seasonality or price to it. (Manage Your Experiments, "Experiments do not impact search rankings"; Experiment results; Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products; Kohavi, Tang, Xu 2020, ch. 1)
- **P-effect-measurement-28.** An exposure verdict from SQP or Search Catalog Performance says that the impressions include sponsored placements. It is not given as an organic result when ad spend moved between the windows. (Search Query Performance dashboard; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Amazon Search Query Performance Report, Perpetua [opinion])
- **P-effect-measurement-29.** Noise is not computed as if each impression, search or session were an independent shopper.
  - A share's binomial interval from SQP or Search Catalog counts is stated as a lower bound on noise.
  - The Poisson count of orders is likewise a floor.
  - Where the ASIN has enough history, an overdispersed count model (negative binomial or quasi-Poisson) fitted to that history sets the noise.

  (Applying the Delta Method in Metric Analytics, eq. 5; Trustworthy Analysis of Online A/B Tests; Quasi-Poisson vs. Negative Binomial Regression; Amazon Search Query Performance Report, Perpetua [opinion]; NIST Poisson Distribution)
- **P-effect-measurement-30.** Every change has a guardrail on what it did not target: the ASIN's whole-catalog search funnel and its previous top queries.
  - The funnel is impressions, clicks and purchases across all queries, from `search.catalog` or Search Catalog Performance.
  - The previous top queries are those the change did not bet on.
  - A material fall in either blocks *held*, whatever the targeted metric did.

  (Kohavi, Tang, Xu 2020, ch. 1, guardrail metrics; Use of Interrupted Time Series Analysis… (Penfold, Zhang), on unintended outcomes; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; Amazon Title Requirements…, Helium 10 [opinion], on broken phrases)
- **P-effect-measurement-31.** One primary metric is fixed in the change log.
  - When a verdict also scans many queries or segments, any single "significant" one is reported as a chance finding. The exception is one that survives a false-discovery-rate control across the scan, or a pooled estimate across the queries.
  - Segments are only those declared in advance, defined by something the change could not move.

  (Online Controlled Experiments at Large Scale; Controlling the False Discovery Rate; Why We (Usually) Don't Have to Worry About Multiple Comparisons; A Dirty Dozen §5.8; Controlled experiments on the web §3.2.4)
- **P-effect-measurement-32.** Before cx gives its first *held* or *not held* on an ASIN, it runs a placebo check.
  - It applies the same verdict rule, with the same window lengths and controls, to past windows of that ASIN in which nothing was changed.
  - If the rule often returns *held* or *not held* on those placebo windows, verdicts on that ASIN are *cannot tell*, and MYE is proposed instead.
  - The report states the placebo rate.

  (Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method, in-time placebos; How Much Should We Trust Differences-in-Differences Estimates?; Kohavi, Tang, Xu 2020, ch. 19 "The A/A Test" [title only])
- **P-effect-measurement-33.** The baseline is long enough to show the series' trend: about eight weekly points where history allows. A trend visible in the baseline is carried into the expected "no change" path and widens the uncertainty. It is not tested once and then assumed away. A flat-looking short baseline is not taken as proof of parallel movement. (Use of Interrupted Time Series Analysis… (Penfold, Zhang); Interrupted time series regression… (Lopez Bernal et al.); Pretest with Caution; A More Credible Approach to Parallel Trends)
- **P-effect-measurement-34.** When the same kind of change is planned for several comparable ASINs of the seller, cx proposes a staggered, randomized rollout.
  - Which ASINs get the change first, and when, is randomized.
  - Each ASIN's own baseline of the same metric is the covariate.
  - Each changed ASIN is compared with those not yet changed, not through one pooled regression with ASIN and week effects.
  - ASINs in the same variation family as a changed ASIN are not controls.

  (Design and analysis of stepped wedge cluster randomized trials; Improving the Sensitivity… (CUPED); Difference-in-Differences with multiple time periods; Estimating dynamic treatment effects in event studies…; Difference-in-differences with variation in treatment timing; Create a listing experiment, on shared parent content)
- **P-effect-measurement-35.** Every *held* or *not held* is provisional. The change log fixes a later durability window for the same metric, and the verdict is re-read then. A changed reading is recorded with a dated note; the first verdict is not overwritten. (Focusing on the Long-term; Use of Interrupted Time Series Analysis… (Penfold, Zhang); Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method, on gradual effects; The preregistration revolution)
- **P-effect-measurement-36.** Reverting a change is a new change, with its own baseline, window and verdict. The report does not assume the listing returns to its old figures, because effects can carry over after a change ends. (Focusing on the Long-term; Design and Analysis of Switchback Experiments; Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: Five Puzzling Outcomes Explained, on carryover)
- **P-effect-measurement-37.** If the treated ASIN, the seller's untouched ASINs and the competitor set break in the same week, the verdict is *cannot tell*. The report names a market or Amazon-side shock, with the entry from `method/amazon-change-calendar.md` if one matches. A competitor that had its own listing change, deal or stock-out in the window is dropped from that window's control set. (Comparative Politics and the Synthetic Control Method, donor-pool exclusions; Estimating and Testing Linear Models with Multiple Structural Changes; Amazon search glossary, "continuous experimentation")
- **P-effect-measurement-38.** The change log's expected range for a content change starts small: about one percent or less of revenue or conversion, unless the seller's own pooled history (P-40) says otherwise. A larger logged expectation carries its reason. (What works in e-commerce – a meta-analysis of 6700 online experiments [vendor white paper]; Seven Rules of Thumb, Rule #2; A/B Testing with Fat Tails)
- **P-effect-measurement-39.** What happens on *cannot tell* (keep, revert, extend once, or move to MYE) is written in the change log before the change. The report presents it as a decision under uncertainty, weighed by the cost of being wrong, and not as a finding about the change. (Sufficient trial size to inform clinical practice; Equivalence Tests; The preregistration revolution)
- **P-effect-measurement-40.** Once the seller's change log holds enough verdicts of one change type, cx reports a pooled estimate for that type beside each new verdict. A single new reading is described as pulled toward that pooled figure, not taken alone. (Bayesian Data Analysis, ch. 5; Data Analysis Using Stein's Estimator…; Objective Bayesian Two Sample Hypothesis Testing…; Why We (Usually) Don't Have to Worry About Multiple Comparisons)
- **P-effect-measurement-41.** A manual alternation of two versions by week (A/B/A/B) counts as a randomized test only if the order of the periods was randomized in advance. Each switch is logged as a change with Amazon's latency, and the report names carryover as a threat. Without MYE, cx does not present a fixed alternation as stronger evidence than one before/after. (Design and Analysis of Switchback Experiments; Amazon search glossary, latency; Focusing on the Long-term)
- **P-effect-measurement-42.** If a mix moved materially between the windows, the report shows that mix shift before the total. The mixes are the B2B share of units or sessions, and the browser versus mobile-app share of sessions. Where Amazon gives the split (B2B), the report reads the movement within each part. (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; A Dirty Dozen §5.8, Simpson's paradox; Sales Dropped? Split It Before You Raise Ad Spend, Seller Labs [opinion])

A verdict given sooner says that returns are not yet in. (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Seller Analytics Dataset Use Case Guide)

### The seat's own (not for a skill)

- **Poisson arithmetic for window size** [seat]:
  - With Poisson counts, the difference between two equal windows' order totals N₁ and N₂ has a standard deviation of about √(N₁+N₂).
  - Putting σ² = mean into Kohavi's 16σ²/Δ² gives roughly **16/r² orders per window** to detect a relative change r at about 95% confidence and 80% power. That is about 1,600 orders for a 10% change, 400 for 20% and 180 for 30%.
  - At 5 orders a day, a 20% change needs about 80 days in each window. A low-volume ASIN can almost never earn *held* or *not held* on orders within a month.
  - Real daily sales are overdispersed, so the true need is larger.
  - This is the seat's derivation from two sourced facts. It should be checked against a real ASIN's history (Open question 5) before any figure goes near a skill.
- **Sign of the competitor-control bias** [seat]. Competitors lose what we win, so "our change minus their change" overstates our effect. A competitor-set sum *including* our own ASIN, compared with our share of it, may be the better reading: the change should move share, while total demand moves with the market. This is not tested.
- **The settling gap** [seat]. The seat would leave out of both windows the days between the write and the Catalog showing the change, plus three days for search. Amazon's pages give only latency figures, not a rule for windows.
- **Baseline length** [seat]. The seat would use a baseline at least as long as the observation window, and at least two whole weeks. The roadmap's "≥ 7 days baseline + ≥ 14 days observation" is shorter than the counts above can support for most ASINs on orders. It may be enough for impressions or sessions, where counts are larger.
- **Exposure verdicts are the easiest to earn** [seat]. Impressions and sessions run to hundreds or thousands a week while orders run to tens. A title or search-terms change is more often judgeable on SQP impressions for the words bet on than on orders, so orders serve as a guardrail ("did not fall") more than as the test.
  - *Revised 2026-10-14* [seat]: raw impression counts overstate this. SQP counts repeat searches and page turns (Perpetua's reading of Amazon), so impressions cluster within shoppers. The variance is inflated by 1 + (m − 1)ρ (Deng, Knoblich, Lu). Sponsored placements are included, so ad spend moves exposure too. Exposure is still likely the most judgeable stage, but by less than the raw counts suggest, and only with ad spend read (P-23, P-28).
- **Poisson arithmetic is a floor** [seat, 2026-10-14]. The 16/r² figures above assume Poisson. With a negative binomial, variance μ + μ²/k, the needed count grows by (1 + μ/k), with μ the daily mean and k fitted from the ASIN's own history. Ver Hoef and Boveng are the source for the two models; the parameterisation is the standard one, not taken from the abstract read.
- **Worked example of a detectable change on a share** [seat, arithmetic only]:
  - A click share p = 0.10 read over n = 10,000 impressions per window has a binomial standard error of √(p(1 − p)/n) = 0.003 per window, and about 0.0042 for the difference of two windows.
  - At about 95% confidence and 80% power the detectable change is 2.8 × 0.0042 ≈ 0.012, about 12% of the share in relative terms.
  - With a design effect of 2 (an assumed m and ρ, not an Amazon figure), the standard error grows by √2 and the detectable change is about 17%.
  - None of these inputs is a real seller's figure.
- **A default for *cannot tell*** [seat]:
  - Keep the change unless a guardrail (P-30) breached.
  - Extend the window once, by the logged length, then stop.
  - A revert is a new change (P-36).

  This follows the regret framing (Manski, Tetenov) and the low cost of a neutral content change, but no source states this rule for listings.
- **Re-read cadence** [seat]. Catalog reads at each week boundary (P-26) are the minimum. Reading at each heartbeat is better if the call is cheap, because Keepa refreshes on its own schedule (`lastUpdate`) and keeps no content history.
- **Durability window** [seat]. The seat would re-read 8 to 12 weeks after the change. The basis is eight weekly points after the change (Penfold, Zhang) and MYE's own recommended eight weeks. No source gives a figure for listings (Open question 16).
- **Own siblings as controls** [seat]. Untouched ASINs of the same brand are less affected by our change than competitors are, because shoppers seldom switch between a brand's own products on one content change. But they share the brand's ads account, Featured Offer conditions and brand-level shocks. ASINs in the same variation family share parent content and reviews, so they are excluded. This is reasoning, not tested.
- **CausalImpact-style modelling in the box** [seat]. A small Bayesian structural time-series model could be run in the box's shell with the control series as covariates. Whether a model should do this, or the skill should stay with window totals plus named confounders, is a design choice for the package. No source read requires either.
