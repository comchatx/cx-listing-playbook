# How Amazon search ranks and the funnel: which element serves which stage

Read 2026-10-14 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own page or document; [opinion] a named practitioner; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, never a skill position.

## What this covers

**Stage: all three, and the gate before them.** This file covers exposure (the ASIN is in the match set and shown), click-through (the search result is clicked) and conversion (the detail-page visit ends in a purchase). It also covers the step before exposure, which is whether the ASIN can be found at all. It checks the roadmap's §1.1 hypothesis, "sales = exposure × CTR × conversion, and each listing element serves one stage", against Amazon's own pages, Amazon's own scientists, and published research.

**Skills it feeds:**
- `listing-review`: the funnel position, "which stage fell, and what shows it", and the element-to-stage map.
- `effect-review`: which metric belongs to which stage; why stages interact (ranking learns from clicks and purchases); what a listing change cannot fix and so must be ruled out as a confounder.
- `listing-change-log`: which stage a change targets, recorded before the change.

**Why a senior operator cares.** Three mistakes are common in listing work:
1. Rewriting copy for a drop that came from the offer: price, stock, delivery promise, or the Featured Offer.
2. Treating "A9/A10" folklore as Amazon's own rules.
3. Reading a click-through or conversion change without the page around it. The page includes the competitors and the ads that share the results.

Amazon's own report schemas define the funnel exactly. Its help pages say which fields feed matching and which gates make an ASIN unsearchable. Its Featured Offer page lists factors that no listing text touches. The beta run showed what happens without this method: cx gave a search-term table with no file behind it, and stated conversion as sessions divided by orders, which is the division upside down (day-1 report, finding 7). Day 2's list item 7 says cx did not name "the funnel stage that dropped" unasked.

### A. What Amazon says search matches on (exposure)

**A1. GUIDANCE — keywords are matched against the information the seller provides.** "One way customers find your products is to search by entering keywords, which are matched against information such as title and description that you provide for a product." [official] (Optimize your product discoverability, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G10471, read 2026-10-14). The page sits under the help node "Amazon Search" and covers four levers: titles, bullet points and description, generic keywords, and product classification. A second live page says the same of keyword search: it "will try to match the keywords to all the possible ASINs by looking at the ASIN's attributes (for example, title, bullet points, product description, and so on)". [official] (Classify your products using Browse Tree Guides - BTG, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G1661, v15, read 2026-10-14 from its capture capture `G1661-v15` at reconciliation). `elements/description.md`'s first-pass "no Amazon page states that the description is indexed" was written before these pages were read and that file has withdrawn it; the two files now agree.

**A2. RULE (Amazon's discretion) — not all supplied text is used for retrieval.** "We reserve the right to not use all supplied content from the product description, including bullet points, for retrieving products in customers' shopping search results." The reasons given "may include, but are not limited to, search computational efficiency, potential manipulation of shopping results, irrelevant search terms, and offensive or illegal terms." [official] (Optimize your product discoverability, G10471). The glossary says the same about bullets: "They are not always indexed by Amazon Search, but always appear in full on product detail pages." [official] (Amazon search glossary, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GF6B8ZPNY2AP72VV, read 2026-10-14). An Amazon forum moderator's roundup repeats it: "bullet points are not always indexed by the Amazon shopping algorithm." [official, forum moderator post] (Elevate sales with search optimization, SEAmod, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/fd6212c5-06ec-46e8-a5ea-ee5fdec119d6, shown as "2 years ago", read 2026-10-14)

**A3. GUIDANCE with a RULE inside — generic keywords (back-end search terms).**
- What they are: "Search terms are keywords that are used by the search engine for lexical matching between an ASIN and a query." [official] (Use search terms effectively, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G23501, read 2026-10-14)
- Guidance from the same page: synonyms, spelling variations (not common misspellings), abbreviations, lowercase, spaces between words, no repetition, no articles or short prepositions, singular or plural.
- RULE, with suppression as the enforcement: "Violating the rules below may result in your ASIN being suppressed." Prohibited: brand names (own or other), ASINs, profanity, temporary statements ("new", "on sale now"), subjective claims ("best", "cheapest", "amazing"), and offensive terms. [official] (G23501)
- Discretion: "Amazon determines the relevancy of search terms automatically … the terms deemed relevant may change over time … Amazon may not use all search terms supplied by sellers." [official] (G23501)
- Figures, for human readers only. **Amazon's own texts disagree on the generic-keywords length:**
  - Optimize your product discoverability: "We limit the length of the Generic Keyword attribute to less than 250 bytes." [official] (G10471 v13)
  - Keyword attributes explained: its table gives `generic_keywords` a length limit of "249" bytes, and its text says "should be less than 250 bytes long". [official] (GF2C2L6RCFZGWBXC v4)
  - The live US Use search terms effectively states no figure ("Maintain the text length limit of search terms"). It says "Amazon search does not count spaces or punctuation" and that "the Generic keyword field in Seller Central stops accepting inputs when the byte limit is reached." [official] (G23501 v14). The UK page (G23501 v9 en-GB) says "less than 250 bytes", as `method/help-page-access.md` C3 records.
  - The SP-API Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting page lists "`generic_keyword`: 500 bytes" under issue 90225, and third-party copies of the product-type schema carry `maxUtf8ByteLength` 250 in one store and 2000 in others, with `maxLength` 500 (both as read by `elements/search-terms.md` and `elements/title.md`; not re-opened here). `elements/search-terms.md` separates the help pages' indexing figures from the schema's write limit.
  - **No figure here is binding.** The write limit for a listing is the one `getDefinitionsProductType` returns for its product type and marketplace, read at run time (`elements/search-terms.md` owns the attribute). Note the names: the help pages say `generic_keywords`; the schema attribute is `generic_keyword`.
- Required for some product types: G23501 lists 23 product types for which the field has been required since 2023-12-06. That list also belongs to the schema's `required`, not to memory.
- RULE — over the limit, the whole field is dropped: "Search Terms (generic_keywords) should be less than 250 bytes long … If it exceeds the limit, the attribute is completely ignored by Amazon Search." For the other keyword attributes, "Amazon Search indexes up until the length limit and ignores any excess." The same page's table gives per-attribute byte limits (249 for `generic_keywords`) and says `specific_uses_keywords`, `thesaurus_attribute_keywords` and `target_audience_keywords` "are being phased out", and that keyword attributes other than free-text ones "require valid values" from the Browse Tree Guides. [official] (Keyword attributes explained, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GF2C2L6RCFZGWBXC, v4, read 2026-10-14 by the verifier; capture capture `GF2C2L6RCFZGWBXC-v4`). The "completely ignored" rule is therefore on a live Amazon page, not only in a 2021 archive or a practitioner source. The UK page says the same in its own words: "If Search Terms are over the limit, none of the Search Terms for that ASIN will be indexed" (G23501 v9 en-GB, per `method/help-page-access.md` C3).
- Page id. The live page is G23501 (v14, capture above). The content call serves no topic for G201047930 (`method/help-page-access.md` A3), so G201047930 (cited by SellerSprite) is not the live page. `elements/search-terms.md`'s gap fill reaches the same answer and withdraws its first-pass reading.

**A4. RULE — the glossary's matching statement.** "Amazon Search does not do partial matching. This means that results for fitbit charge bands contain all of those words in their catalog data." [official] (Amazon search glossary). See Published research, R2: Amazon's own scientists describe semantic matching that adds products to a lexical match set. The glossary is the seller-facing simplification. The safe reading for a listing is that **a query word absent from the catalog data has no lexical route to the ASIN**.

**A5. RULE — classification gates where an ASIN can appear.**
- "Amazon may restrict shopping results to return products that belong to specific item type keyword when customers search in All Departments … Insufficient browse classification can hinder a product's discoverability for relevant search queries." The example is a brownie mix classified as "cooking-and-baking-supplies", which "may not appear in shopping results for otherwise relevant queries." [official] (Accurately classifying your products, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G25771, read 2026-10-14)
- The glossary's "Search constraint": "The search query, red dresses is constrained to show shopping results that are assigned to a dress browse node and to a red color refinement picker." And: "Valid values refer to the acceptable inputs of catalog attributes that are used in assignment queries on browse nodes … blue is a valid value … whereas midnight blue is not." [official] (Amazon search glossary)
- So structured attributes such as color, size and item type serve exposure through refinements and constraints, not only through the free text.

**A6. RULE — five reasons an ASIN is unsearchable (the gate before exposure).** "Five common reasons for an ASIN being unsearchable are: Parent ASIN; No Browse Node; No Offer; Future Launch Date; Listing Quality." [official] (My ASIN is unsearchable, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GHGHDMU4X5K9QGCV, read 2026-10-14). Decisive phrases from that page:
- "Parent ASINs are not searchable or buyable by design."
- "ASINs that don't have a browse node assigned are not indexed by Amazon."
- "In order to be searchable, an ASIN needs a buyable offer. If an ASIN's offer expires, this causes it to become unsearchable."
- "ASINs with future launch dates are not searchable."
- "ASINs with search suppressed or detail page removed status are not searchable."
- Latency: "Any changes to an ASIN's offer, browse node, launch date can take up to 72 hours to reflect in shopping results."

The glossary says the same for "Index suppressed": when it is true, "the ASIN is suppressed from search … Once fixed, the products may become searchable within 72 hours." It also gives the general "Latency" entry: "It can take up to 72 hours for changes to new or existing ASINs to go live." [official] (Amazon search glossary)

**A7. RULE — suppression for incomplete data.** Amazon will "hide (or suppress) from search and browse non-media listings that do not meet certain standards. This means that a customer will not be able to find a listing in search if that listing is suppressed." The page's standards include images, item type, titles and price per unit, plus category-specific child-listing attributes such as shoe size and color. [official] (Suppressed listings, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200898440, read 2026-10-14). The same page's opening sentence carries title-length figures (80 characters for apparel, shoes and luggage; 130 for the rest). Those figures disagree with the current title page that the sibling file `elements/title.md` read. This is one more reason a title limit is read from the schema, not from a help page.

**A8. RULE as machine output — the Listings Items model names the exposure states.**
- `summaries[].status` takes `BUYABLE` ("The listings item can be purchased by shoppers") and `DISCOVERABLE` ("The listings item is visible to shoppers").
- An issue's `enforcements.actions[].action` includes `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` ("the catalog item is hidden from search results"), `LISTING_SUPPRESSED` ("takes down the current listing item's buyability"), `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED` and `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED`.

[official] (listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/models/listings-items-api-model/listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, read 2026-10-14)

**A9. RULE — Amazon may re-classify or rewrite.**
- Warning 18367 "occurs when Amazon categorizes your product under a different product type based on the attributes that you had provided, such as, title, product description, and bullet points." [official] (Warning code 18367: Product type modified by Amazon, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GXMA2QU3JZTG8JNL, read 2026-10-14)
- Brand representatives can see "upcoming or recently published Amazon-initiated content changes". "You have 14 days to review an upcoming change before it publishes automatically." The view "does not currently include updates to product images or browse nodes." [official] (Review listing changes, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G7T47V48CGE6FS32, read 2026-10-14)

What the shopper sees, and so what search indexes, is therefore not always what the seller submitted.

**A10. What Amazon does NOT say.** No Amazon page read this session lists ranking factors with weights, or says in so many words that the algorithm is not disclosed. The closest statements are three. Amazon determines search-term relevance "automatically" (G23501). It reserves the right not to use supplied content (G10471). And "The product discovery and shopping landscape is subject to continuous experimentation." [official] (Amazon search glossary)

The current Amazon seller blog says "Listing quality and your account health can contribute to search rankings", and also: "Adjusting your list price to stay competitive can improve your product ranking and increase sales." [official, GUIDANCE] (Amazon SEO: 7 ways to improve your product's search rankings, Daisy Quaker, 2025-09-03, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-seo, read 2026-10-14). The same post also says "Cost-per-click ads like Sponsored Products and other forms of ecommerce marketing can help offers rise through search results" (it does not separate sponsored from organic placement), tells sellers to give image "alt-text" "a simple sentence describing the image that includes 1-2 keywords", and suggests "offering faster delivery through Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA)" alongside the filter sentence (B2). [official, GUIDANCE] (same page, re-read by the verifier). That is the nearest Amazon comes to naming ranking inputs on a public page read today. The alt-text advice is a blog recommendation; no help page read says alt-text is indexed for search.

### B. What Amazon says the search result shows (click-through)

**B1. GUIDANCE — the parts of a result.** "A search result is made up of a title, description, image, price, and other features, such as badges." The glossary also defines "Position" as the position an ASIN occupies "in the featured shopping results. Featured is Amazon's default sort option." [official] (Amazon search glossary)

**B2. GUIDANCE — filters.** "Customers refine a search by using different filters like delivery day, customer reviews, and price." [official] (Amazon SEO: 7 ways…, sell.amazon.com, 2025-09-03). A filter removes a result from the shopper's view entirely. So delivery day, rating and price act at the exposure stage as well as the click stage.

**B3. Figure: Amazon measures delivery messages at every search-funnel step.**
- The Search Catalog Performance report counts, per ASIN, impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases "with the same day delivery message", one-day and two-day. [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)
- The Search Query Performance report does the same per query. [official] (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json, read 2026-10-14)

Amazon treats the delivery promise shown on the results page as a funnel variable worth splitting out. That is Amazon's own evidence for the roadmap's "到货日" (delivery date) in the conversion column, and it puts the delivery promise at click-through too.

**B4. GUIDANCE — titles.** "The product title is often the first piece of information customers see about your offering … your Amazon product title should be similar to what would appear on the physical packaging." [official] (G10471). Title length rules are in `elements/title.md`. **Amazon's own texts disagree on the figure** (figures for human readers only):
- The 2025-09-03 seller blog says "the Amazon product title character limit is 200, and we recommend no more than 60."
- Suppressed listings (G200898440 v13) carries "80 characters" for apparel, shoes and luggage and "130 characters" for the rest.
- The current title requirements page carries 75: "Titles must not exceed 75 characters, including spaces", and "75 or fewer characters is required because mobile screens truncate long titles." Its scope is "all product types, except media product types, listed in all stores, except for Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Türkiye, and the United Arab Emirates." [official] (Product title requirements and guidelines, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC, version 21, read 2026-10-14 by the verifier; capture capture `GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC-v21`). The page as read does not show the date the 75 took effect; `elements/title.md` holds that reading.
- The SP-API Listings Items API Issues Troubleshooting page still lists "`item_name`: 200 characters" under issue 90225 (as `elements/title.md` read it; not re-opened here).

This file picks none of them. The binding limit for a listing is the one `getDefinitionsProductType` returns for its product type and marketplace, read at run time.

**B4a. Item highlights and title enforcement (click-through and exposure).** The same title page says "Item highlights will be visible below titles in search results and on product detail pages", giving "an additional 125 characters"; that a non-compliant title "might be automatically corrected or it might not appear in our search results"; and that a title or item-highlights update "can take 24 to 48 hours to be processed and displayed on Amazon stores, including search results and the product detail page." [official] (Product title requirements and guidelines, GYTR6SYGFA5E3EQC v21; each phrase matched in the capture at reconciliation). Item highlights are therefore a click-through element the roadmap's table does not list; their limit, like the title's, is read from the schema at run time.
- The "24 to 48 hours" is on Amazon's own title page, in its FAQ and again in its troubleshooting table ("Allow 24 to 48 hours for the change to be processed and displayed on search results and the product detail page"). The first passes of `method/listing-writes.md` (SellerEngine only) and of `method/effect-measurement.md`'s verifier (forum posts and blogs only) were wrong on the source; both files' gap fills now cite this page.
- On a non-compliant title the page names two outcomes, "automatically corrected" or "might not appear in our search results", and its troubleshooting table has a "Title suppression" row ("check if your title has been suppressed"). `method/listing-policies.md` reads the 2026-07-27 announcement as "rewrite, not takedown" for over-length titles only and keeps this page's two outcomes for the other title rules, which agrees with this file. The owner of the rule is `elements/title.md`.

**B5. Sponsored results share the page.**
- Sponsored Products ads "may be displayed at the top of, alongside, or within shopping results and on product pages".
- "Products must be in one or more eligible categories and be eligible for the Featured Offer in order to advertise", and ads "Only appear when your advertised items are in stock."

[official] (Sponsored Products - Help increase product sales | Amazon Ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/sponsored-products, read 2026-10-14). In the Search Catalog Performance report, `impressionCount` is "Total number of impressions for the given ASIN, which originates from the search results page across all search queries … Impressions include sponsored search results." [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json, re-read as raw JSON at reconciliation, 2026-10-14). The Search Query Performance report schema says nothing either way about sponsored results (re-read the same day; no "sponsor" in the file); for SQP the statement is on its dashboard help page (E1). An exposure gain in the search reports can therefore be a paid gain.

### C. What Amazon says converts on the detail page (conversion)

**C1. GUIDANCE — bullets and description.** "Crafting well-written, informative bullet points can help boost sales, as customers rely on this content to quickly understand a product's distinguishing characteristics and decide if it meets their needs." The description "should be concise, clear, and engaging, enticing potential buyers while providing all necessary information to make an informed purchasing decision." [official] (G10471). Taken with A2: Amazon names bullets and description as **conversion** content first and **exposure** content only conditionally. That partly contradicts the roadmap's table, which lists bullets under exposure without qualification.

**C2. RULE (offer level, not listing content) — the Featured Offer decides whether this seller's offer carries the Add to Cart button.**
- "The Featured Offer is the offer that appears with Add to Cart / Buy Now buttons in the Offer Display on a product detail page."
- Factors: "Price: Total price (item price plus shipping fee)"; "Delivered promise: Faster offers and offers with a specific delivery promise (instead of a range) are more likely to be featured"; free shipping; free returns; seller performance (order volume, chargeback rate, Voice of the Customer complaints).
- Stock: "Your offer can't become featured if you're out of stock."
- Not considered: "Customer reviews and star ratings", "Prime eligibility of the offer", "Fulfillment method". The reviews line means seller reviews: "We evaluate seller performance along the dimensions listed above, not through customer-submitted seller reviews or star ratings."
- Featured Offer is per shopper context: it "is determined separately for each variation", customers "may see different Featured Offers when shopping from different shipping locations" or with Prime, and "When customers activate a search filter such as delivery date search, they may see different Featured Offers".
- "We rank offers the same way for every product, and we never use manual overrides."
- New: "As of September 2026, Amazon no longer maintains a seller eligibility requirement, and offers from all sellers are now eligible to be evaluated for the Featured Offer." Also: "Amazon will not feature any offer if none of the available offers meet our customer experience requirements, even if the product has only one available offer." The removed page Featured Offer eligibility (G200418100 v23, capture capture `G200418100-v23`) carries the same "As of September 2026" sentence. Amazon's News announcement dates the change differently, as a store-by-store rollout from July 2026 to the end of 2026 (E8). The two texts differ in scope (the US help layer against the global rollout); this file picks neither, and whether a given store has dropped the seller requirement is read at run time from that store's own Becoming the Featured Offer page. `method/triggers.md`'s first-pass "Featured Offer eligibility tests" RULE predated both texts; its gap fill now marks it superseded and agrees with this file.
- "Your Featured Offer percentage is calculated as the total number of page views in which your offer is featured divided by the total number of page views for your products."

[official] (Becoming the Featured Offer, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G201687550, read 2026-10-14; the older page G37911 "Featured Offer" now reads "This page has been removed" and points here)

**C3. Other offers sit one tap away.** "The offer shown at the top of the All-Offers Display is the Featured Offer." Amazon says it made the All-Offers link a button, which "has had a large impact on purchases from the All-Offers Display." [official] (All-Offers Display, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GYZNTFDKYWU75VZ8, read 2026-10-14). A seller who shares an ASIN can lose a sale on a detail page whose content is perfect.

**C4. The conversion definitions Amazon uses.**
- Search channel: Search Catalog Performance defines `clickRate` as "The number of clicks divided by the number of impressions for the ASIN" and `conversionRate` as "The number of purchases divided by the number of clicks for the ASIN"; `impressionCount` counts "every time the ASIN displays in the search results page." [official] (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json). The report needs Brand Registry and the Brand Analytics role; periods are `WEEK`, `MONTH`, `QUARTER`; "Requests cannot span multiple periods." [official] (Analytics Reports, https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/docs/report-type-values-analytics, read 2026-10-14)
- All traffic: `unitSessionPercentage` is "calculated by dividing unitsOrdered by sessions". Sessions count "All activity within a 24-hour period". `buyBoxPercentage` is "The percentage of page views where the buy box … appeared." [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/reports/sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, read 2026-10-14)

So Amazon's own definitions give the funnel identity exactly for the search channel: **search purchases = impressions × clickRate × conversionRate**. For all channels together, units = sessions × unit session percentage. The two "conversions" are different ratios with different denominators. `angles/own-traffic-funnel.md` holds the full trap list.

**E2. Business Reports: Featured Offer percentage and the all-traffic funnel.**
- "Featured Offer percentage: The percentage of page views where the Featured Offer (the 'Add to shopping cart' link) appeared on the page". It is below 100% when "The product was out of stock", when "The Featured Offer would result in the customer purchasing from another seller", or when the product "was not available for purchase using the Featured Offer". "This is not necessarily an indication of the availability of your product for purchase." [official] (Business reports glossary, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G27691, v7 r1, read 2026-10-14)
- **A summing trap.** In "Detail Page Sales and Traffic", "page views are summed only for days when there was sales activity on the ASIN". In "Detail Page Sales and Traffic by Child Item", "page views are summed for the entire date range, regardless of sales activity." "When a child ASIN is visited, it does not also count as a visit to the parent ASIN." [official] (G27691)
- **Timing.** The "by date" reports "are updated every hour with the data from the past few days"; other reports "are updated daily"; "A week runs from Sunday through Saturday." [official] (Business Reports FAQ, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G28761, v7 r0, read 2026-10-14). "Data for these reports is generally available for up to two years." "Business reports will have a lag of 24 hours when compared to the real-time data." [official] (Business reports, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G200633570, v8 r1, read 2026-10-14)
- **Two denominators for one name (the inter-file contradiction, settled at the source).** The Sales and Traffic report's `buyBoxPercentage` is "The percentage of page views where the buy box (the add to shopping cart link) appeared" [official] (sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_salesAndTraffic_2024_04_24.graphql, read 2026-10-14). The Data Kiosk seller-analytics `featureOfferPct` is "calculated by dividing the number of times your offer is the Featured Offer by the number of times customers viewed it (that is, Glance Views)" [official] (analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, https://github.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/blob/main/schemas/data-kiosk/analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql, read 2026-10-14). They are two metrics. The same schema marks `conversationRatePct` "@deprecated … Use conversionRatePct instead", and defines `conversionRatePct` as "the percentage of purchases to clicks for ASINs originated from the search results page", which is the search-channel conversion of C4, not unit session percentage.
- **Amazon's own pages give a third conversion formula.** "Sales conversion rate is calculated as total orders for your products minus returns divided by the total page views", and the page then sends the seller to "the Order item session percentage metric" [official] (Pricing Health, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/GSTH6YN3BR8XNWBW, v22 r0, read 2026-10-14). The glossary defines Order Item Session Percentage as total order items "by the number of distinct session counts" (G27691). The two Amazon pages disagree. A skill names the field it reads, not a "conversion rate".

[official] (Create A+ content, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/G202134820, v17 r1, read 2026-10-14). An Amazon staff reply quotes this sentence to a seller who asked whether A+ hurts search. [official, forum staff reply] (A+ Content and SEO, Glenn_Amazon, shown "a year ago", https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/79c48d14-7001-470d-ae54-d21d9b301312, read 2026-10-14). The staff reply does not answer the question about A+ body text.
- Body text: no Amazon page read says it is or is not indexed. Amazon's discoverability pages (G10471, G23501, Search optimization GNYWHX2TP7C8GXHK, glossary) never name A+ as matched text. ## Published research

- **R1. [research] Daria Sorokina, Erick Cantú-Paz (A9.com), "Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products", SIGIR 2016, pp. 459–460, DOI 10.1145/2911451.2926725** (PDF from amazon.science).
  - Narrow findings, stated by Amazon's own engineers as of 2016:
    - Ranking models are per category and marketplace.
    - Training labels are "based on customers actions, such as purchases, add-to-basket, or clicks".
    - "Behavioral features drive the rankings in Amazon Search to a much larger extent than they do in Web Search. Typically, they account for most of the variance reduction in gradient-boosted trees."
    - Click data is corrected for position bias with a scheme that "adapts the bias correction from day to day".
    - Query understanding detects the product type in a query (e.g. "dress" in "casual dress") and labels products with product-type expressions, so non-matching product types can be excluded.
    - "All Product Search" interleaves category rankings by the estimated category intent of the query.
  - Transfer: high for the mechanism (ranking learns from clicks, carts and purchases, so CTR and conversion feed back into exposure). Low for any current weight: the paper is ten years old.
- **R2. [research] Priyanka Nigam, Yiwei Song, Vijai Mohan, Vihan Lakshman, Weitian Ding, Ankit Shingavi, Choon Hui Teo, Hao Gu, Bing Yin (Amazon; Ding listed at Carnegie Mellon University), "Semantic Product Search", KDD 2019, pp. 2876–2885, DOI 10.1145/3292500.3330759** (arXiv 1907.00937). The online experiments ran "on a large e-commerce website"; the paper does not name the site in that sentence.
  - A lexical match set from an inverted index misses synonyms, hypernyms, morphological variants and misspellings ("sneakers" for "running shoes"). It also wrongly retrieves "latex free examination gloves" for "latex examination gloves".
  - A model trained on behavior was used to augment the match set. In three online experiments (toys and games, kitchen, pets), "the conversion rate, revenue, and other key performance indicators (KPIs) statistically significantly increased."
  - Product attributes ("title, brand, and color") were concatenated, because embedding them separately gave up "to 5% lower recall".
  - Transfer: a word missing from a listing can still be matched semantically. Lexical presence remains the route Amazon describes to sellers (A3, A4), and wrong-attribute matches are a known precision problem.
- **R3. [research] Chandan K. Reddy, Lluís Màrquez, Fran Valero, Nikhil Rao, Hugo Zaragoza, Sambaran Bandyopadhyay, Arnab Biswas, Anlu Xing, Karthik Subbian (Amazon), "Shopping Queries Dataset: A Large-Scale ESCI Benchmark for Improving Product Search", arXiv 2206.06588, 2022** (used in KDD Cup 2022).
  - About 130 thousand queries and 2.6 million human relevance judgements.
  - Relevance is graded Exact / Substitute / Complement / Irrelevant. Irrelevant includes an item that "fails to fulfill a central aspect of the query (e.g. … a wheat flour bread for a 'gluten–free bread' query)".
  - Product fields in the data: title, description, bullet points, brand, color.
  - Transfer: a vocabulary for "is our ASIN an exact match for this query?" Labels are human judgements, not ranking weights.
- **R4. [research] Changlong Yu, Xin Liu, Jefferson Maia, Tianyu Cao, Laurence (Yang) Li, Yifan Gao, Yangqiu Song, Rahul Goutam, Haiyang Zhang, Bing Yin, Zheng Li, "COSMO: A Large-Scale E-commerce Common Sense Knowledge Generation and Serving System at Amazon", Companion of SIGMOD 2024 (SIGMOD-Companion '24), DOI 10.1145/3626246.3653398.**
  - COSMO mines intent knowledge from search-buy and co-buy behavior with LLMs. It was evaluated for search relevance offline, on the public ESCI data and on "private ESCI datasets of four different locales" (which locales could not be read from the extracted figure). The amazon.science blog post reports macro-F1 gains, which are offline.
  - The one online result: integration into search navigation, "online A/B tests carried out over several months in total, targeting approximately 10% of Amazon's U.S. traffic", produced "a notable 0.7% relative increase in product sales" and "an 8% increase in navigation engagement rate".
  - Transfer: Amazon uses intent knowledge ("winter camping" → winter boots) in the results-page experience. It does not show how a single listing's text is scored. Practitioner claims that COSMO "ranks" listings go beyond the paper.
- **R5. [research] Raluca M. Ursu, "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions", Marketing Science 37(4):530–552, 2018, DOI 10.1287/mksc.2017.1072.** Read in the open-access dissertation version (University of Chicago Knowledge, record 1646) and the journal abstract.
  - Setting: a field experiment at Expedia in which some users saw a random hotel ranking.
  - Finding: "rankings affect what consumers search, but conditional on search, do not affect purchases." Under random ranking, "the conversion rate … is approximately constant across positions."
  - Under the platform's own ranking, higher positions show higher conversion too. That is endogeneity, so "the position effect is overestimated in the absence of experimental variation".
  - Transfer: hotels on Expedia, not products on Amazon. The narrow lesson that transfers is that a better position should show up in clicks and orders, not in conversion per click. Observational rank-versus-sales correlations overstate the rank effect.
- **R6. [research] Chiara Farronato, Andrey Fradkin, Alexander MacKay, "Self-Preferencing at Amazon: Evidence from Search Rankings", NBER Working Paper 30894, January 2023** (the NBER version was read; Crossref lists a published version, AEA Papers and Proceedings 113:239–243, 2023, DOI 10.1257/pandp.20231068, which was not opened).
  - Panel: 184 US users who shopped mostly with Chrome on desktop, 3,019 searches, two pilot studies in summer and fall 2022.
  - "46.5% of product pages are reached from a search result page"; "In 72.1% of searches consumers do not click past the first results page"; "only half of the products on a full page of results are actually seen".
  - Being sponsored moves a product up about 7 positions on average.
  - Among the observed features, "low prices, fast delivery, and high ratings tend to increase product prominence". This is a correlation.
  - Transfer: the only Amazon-specific, independent measurement read. It is a small, desktop-only panel. It shows that search is the main channel but not the only one, and that the results page is partly ads.
- **R7. [research] Thorsten Joachims, Laura Granka, Bing Pan, Helene Hembrooke, Geri Gay, "Accurately Interpreting Clickthrough Data as Implicit Feedback", SIGIR 2005, DOI 10.1145/1076034.1076063.**
  - Eye-tracking on Google results shows a "trust bias", which "leads to more clicks on links ranked highly … even if those abstracts are less relevant".
  - It also shows a "quality bias": the click decision depends "also by the overall quality of the other abstracts in the ranking", so "clicks have to be interpreted relative to the order of presentation and relative to the other abstracts."
  - Transfer: web search, 2005, a lab study. The lesson that transfers is that click-through is relative to position and to neighbouring results. A CTR change must be read against the same-period movement of the competitors on the same page.
- **R8. [research] Judith A. Chevalier, Dina Mayzlin, "The Effect of Word of Mouth on Sales: Online Book Reviews", Journal of Marketing Research 43(3), 2006, DOI 10.1509/jmkr.43.3.345.** Read as NBER WP 10148, 2003.
  - Amazon.com vs BarnesandNoble.com books: "an improvement in a book's reviews leads to an increase in relative sales at that site", and "the impact of 1-star reviews is greater than the impact of 5-star reviews."
  - Transfer: Amazon itself, but books, two decades ago. Reviews move sales, and reviews are not listing content a seller can write.
- **R9. [research] (gap fill) Eric T. Anderson, Gavan J. Fitzsimons, Duncan Simester, "Measuring and Mitigating the Costs of Stockouts", Management Science 52(11):1751–1763, November 2006, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.1060.0577.** Read as the abstract in the Crossref record; the INFORMS page answered 403 and no open full text was found, so the field setting and effect sizes in the body were not read.
  - Narrow finding: "a large-scale field test that measures the short- and long-run opportunity cost of a stockout" finds that "the adverse impact of a stockout extends to both other items in the current order as well as future orders."
  - "failing to account for the long-run effects of a stockout will lead to suboptimal inventory decisions."
  - On remedies: "Offering discounts to encourage customers to backorder rather than cancel their orders is widely used in practice, but that was the least profitable of the responses that we evaluated."
  - Transfer: a retailer's own customers and their future orders with that retailer, not a marketplace ranking. It is peer-reviewed field evidence that a stockout's cost outlasts the stockout. It does **not** test Canopy's claim that Amazon's ranking penalises an ASIN after restock. [seat] On Amazon the nearest analogue is the shopper who buys a competitor's ASIN during the stockout; that cost would show in the seller's orders after restock even with an unchanged rank. The paper gives no window length that a skill could quote.
- **R10. [research] (gap fill) Marshall L. Fisher, Santiago Gallino, Joseph Jiaqi Xu, "The Value of Rapid Delivery in Omnichannel Retailing", Journal of Marketing Research 56(5):732–748, 2019, DOI 10.1177/0022243719849940.** Read as the abstract in the Crossref record; full text not opened.
  - Setting: "a quasi-experiment involving the opening of a new distribution center by a U.S. apparel retailer, which resulted in unannounced faster deliveries to western U.S. states through its online channel", analysed by difference-in-differences.
  - Finding: "online store sales increased, on average, by 1.45% per business-day reduction in delivery time, from a baseline of seven business days", with "a positive spillover effect to the retailer's offline stores."
  - Dynamics: "These effects increase gradually in the short-to-medium run as the result of higher order count." Drivers named are "customer learning through service interactions" and existing brand presence. "Customers with less online store experience are more responsive to faster deliveries in the short run, whereas experienced online store customers are more responsive in the long run."
  - Transfer: causal evidence that faster delivery raises sales. The mechanism differs from Amazon's, though. Here the speed-up was unannounced, so customers learned it by experience. On Amazon the promise is shown on the search result and filters on it (B2, B3, R6), so ([seat]) the effect at click-through plausibly arrives sooner. The 1.45% figure is from a seven-day baseline and does not transfer to one- and two-day promises. What transfers is that a delivery-speed change is a confounder whose effect builds over weeks, so it is not confined to the day it happened.

## Stable enough for a skill

- Amazon matches a shopper's keywords against the catalog data the seller provides: title, description, bullets, generic keywords. (Optimize your product discoverability; Use search terms effectively)
- Amazon may not use every word supplied. Bullets and description are not always indexed, and search terms can be dropped as irrelevant or prohibited. (Optimize your product discoverability; Amazon search glossary; Use search terms effectively)
- An ASIN with no browse node, no buyable offer, a future launch date, search-suppressed status, or that is a parent ASIN does not appear in search. (My ASIN is unsearchable)
- Classification at the most specific item type is an exposure lever, because Amazon may restrict results to a specific item type and to valid refinement values. (Accurately classifying your products; Amazon search glossary)
- Generic keywords must not contain brand names, ASINs, temporary or subjective words. Violations can suppress the ASIN. The byte limit lives in the product-type schema, and a generic-keywords value over the limit is ignored by search as a whole, not cut. (Use search terms effectively; Keyword attributes explained; getDefinitionsProductType)
- Item highlights show below the title in search results, so they are click-through content; a non-compliant title may be auto-corrected or left out of search results. (Product title requirements and guidelines)
- A search result shows title, image, price and badges, and delivery messages are measured at each search step. These are the click-through elements. (Amazon search glossary; Search Catalog Performance schema)
- Amazon's search reports count sponsored impressions together with organic ones. (Search Catalog Performance schema; Search Query Performance dashboard; the Search Query Performance report schema itself is silent)
- Amazon defines search click rate as clicks ÷ impressions, search conversion as purchases ÷ clicks, and unit session percentage as units ÷ sessions. (Search Catalog Performance schema; Sales and Traffic schema)
- The Featured Offer is chosen on total price, delivery promise, free shipping and returns, seller performance and stock. Seller reviews and ratings, the Prime badge and fulfillment method are not factors. No listing-text change alters these. (Becoming the Featured Offer)
- An out-of-stock offer cannot be featured, is not searchable without a buyable offer, and cannot carry Sponsored Products ads. (Becoming the Featured Offer; My ASIN is unsearchable; Sponsored Products page)
- Amazon may change a product's type or its content, so the live catalog can differ from the submission. (Warning code 18367; Review listing changes)
- Amazon states a latency between a change and its appearance in shopping results, and its pages give different windows for different kinds of change. The figure is read at run time from the page that covers the change, and liveness is confirmed by reading the listing and the catalog, not assumed. (Amazon search glossary; My ASIN is unsearchable; Product title requirements and guidelines; Product image guide)
- Amazon's shopping assistant, now called Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus), answers product questions from the detail page, reviews and Q&A, and shows AI overviews on search results. (Alexa for shopping; aboutamazon Rufus and Alexa for Shopping pages; Amazon Science Rufus post)
- "A10" is not an Amazon name. No ranking weights are published by Amazon. (Seller Labs, Canopy and Eva on the name; Amazon pages read contain none)
- Amazon's ranking is trained on customer clicks, add-to-carts and purchases, so click-through and conversion changes can later move exposure. (Sorokina & Cantú-Paz 2016)
- (Gap fill.) The Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance dashboards count organic and Sponsored Products results from the search results page, and exclude widgets such as "Top Rated". They name no other ad type. (Search Query Performance dashboard; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard)
- Amazon advises against comparing same-named metrics across dashboards, because each dashboard attributes and defines actions its own way. (Search Query Performance dashboard; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard)
- The Amazon Search Terms report shows, per popular query, the top three clicked products with their click and conversion shares. It is Amazon's own view of who wins a query. (Amazon Search Terms report)
- Business Reports' Featured Offer percentage is a share of page views, and the Data Kiosk seller-analytics `featureOfferPct` is a share of glance views. They are different metrics. (Business reports glossary; Sales and Traffic schema; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql)
- In Business Reports, "Detail Page Sales and Traffic" sums page views only over days with sales, while the "by Child Item" view sums over the whole range, and a child visit is not also a parent visit. (Business reports glossary)
- The Listing Quality Indicator ignores price, fulfillment and seasonality, and Amazon calls it directional, not a predictor of visibility or sales. (Enhance Listings)
- Products classified as adult are restricted from All Departments search by design. (Search optimization)
- Search position differs by the shopper's location, account type and device, so one observed position is not "the" rank. (Search optimization)
- Variation structure is a funnel lever. Reusing existing ASINs keeps sales history and reviews that Amazon says help discovery, and reviews are shared only across minor differences, which changes the rating shown. (Variation relationship FAQ; Guidelines for reviews shared across product variations)
- A main image that breaks the image rules can take the listing out of search until a compliant one is provided. (Product image guide)
- Deal badges show in search results as well as on the detail page, so a deal changes the click-through stage. (Prime Day readiness playbook)
- The NCX rate compares an offer's customer-reported product or listing issues with similar offers, draws on returns, reviews and contacts, and reacts to fixes over weeks. (Voice of the Customer)
- An offer priced well above recent or external reference prices can be made ineligible for the Featured Offer, and a suspected pricing error can deactivate the listing. These are offer gates, not listing-content problems. (Pricing Health; Listing blocked due to potential pricing error)
- Amazon is removing the seller-level Featured Offer eligibility requirement store by store, and selection itself is unchanged. Whether a given store has changed is read from that store's own help page, not from a remembered date. (Updates to Featured Offer eligibility requirements; Featured Offer eligibility; Becoming the Featured Offer)
- The Sales and Traffic report splits sessions and page views into mobile app and browser, so an ASIN's app share can be computed from Amazon's own file. Since 2022-03-29 the totals include app traffic. (Sales and Traffic schema; Business Reports FAQ)
- Amazon says A+ image alt-text helps products in search results. No Amazon page read says whether A+ body text is matched, and A+ hides the plain-text description from the page. (Create A+ content; A+ Content)
- Amazon's AI surfaces (Alexa for Shopping, Help Me Decide, AI Shopping Guides, ad prompts) draw on detail-page and catalog data, reviews and price history. (Alexa for shopping; Help Me Decide; AI Shopping Guides; Sponsored Products prompts and Sponsored Brands prompts)
- A stockout's cost can outlast the stockout through lost future orders, and a faster delivery promise raises sales with an effect that builds over weeks. Both are confounders, not listing effects. (Anderson, Fitzsimons & Simester 2006; Fisher, Gallino & Xu 2019)

## Positions

1. **P-funnel-and-search-1.** A listing proposal first names the stage that fell (findable, exposure, click-through, conversion, or the offer) and the file and field that show it. Sources: Search Catalog Performance schema; Sales and Traffic schema; My ASIN is unsearchable.
2. **P-funnel-and-search-2.** For search traffic, purchases = impressions × click rate × conversion rate by Amazon's own definitions. For all traffic, units = sessions × unit session percentage. The two "conversions" are not compared with each other. Sources: Search Catalog Performance schema; Sales and Traffic schema.
3. **P-funnel-and-search-3.** Before any wording change, cx checks the gates: parent ASIN, browse node, buyable offer, launch date, and search suppression. A gate failure is fixed first, and no copy is rewritten for it. Sources: My ASIN is unsearchable; Listings Items model (`DISCOVERABLE`, `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED`); Suppressed listings.
4. **P-funnel-and-search-4.** Exposure elements are the title, generic keywords, the item type and browse classification, and structured attributes with valid values. Bullets and description serve exposure only when Amazon chooses to index them, and serve conversion always. Sources: Optimize your product discoverability; Amazon search glossary; Use search terms effectively; Accurately classifying your products.
5. **P-funnel-and-search-5.** A shopper's query word that the seller wants to be found for should appear in the catalog data. Lexical matching is the route Amazon describes, and a word that is absent relies on semantic matching Amazon does not promise. Sources: Use search terms effectively; Amazon search glossary; Semantic Product Search.
6. **P-funnel-and-search-6.** Click-through elements are what the search result shows: main image, title, item highlights, price, badges, rating, and the delivery message. A click-through problem is not fixed by bullets or A+. Sources: Amazon search glossary; Product title requirements and guidelines (item highlights); Search Catalog Performance schema; Amazon SEO: 7 ways (filters).
7. **P-funnel-and-search-7.** Conversion elements are the detail-page content (images, bullets, description or the A+ content that replaces it on the page) together with the offer. Amazon says its shopping assistant answers product questions from "the product's detail page" and names no single element, so cx says only that, and treats a wrong attribute on the page as a conversion defect. Sources: Optimize your product discoverability; A+ Content (hides the plain-text description); Alexa for shopping; 'Amazon Rufus' AI experience comes to the Amazon Shopping app. *(Narrowed in the gap fill: the earlier wording listed images, bullets and description as what feeds the assistant, which Amazon does not say.)*
8. **P-funnel-and-search-8.** Price, delivery promise, free shipping or returns, seller performance and stock decide the Featured Offer, and no listing-text change alters them. A drop traced to them is reported as an offer problem, not a listing problem. Sources: Becoming the Featured Offer; All-Offers Display.
9. **P-funnel-and-search-9.** A stockout makes an effect window unreadable: without a buyable offer the ASIN is unsearchable, cannot be featured, and its ads stop. Sources: My ASIN is unsearchable; Becoming the Featured Offer; Sponsored Products page.
10. **P-funnel-and-search-10.** A demand decline is read from Amazon's search query volume for the same weeks. A listing change is not proposed for a drop that the query volume explains. Sources: Search Query Performance schema.
11. **P-funnel-and-search-11.** Search impressions in Amazon's search reports and dashboards include Sponsored Products results, so ad spend is checked before an exposure gain is credited to a listing change. Sources: Search Catalog Performance schema; Search Query Performance dashboard; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard; Sponsored Products page; Self-Preferencing at Amazon (NBER WP 30894). *(Gap fill: the two dashboard pages added as sources.)*
12. **P-funnel-and-search-12.** Click-through is read relative to position and to the other results on the page, so the competitor set's same-period movement is taken out before a CTR change is credited. Sources: Accurately Interpreting Clickthrough Data as Implicit Feedback; Search Query Performance schema (shares).
13. **P-funnel-and-search-13.** A change that targets exposure or rank is judged on impressions, clicks and orders, not on conversion per click. Sources: The Power of Rankings (Ursu 2018), with the transfer caveat stated; Search Catalog Performance schema.
14. **P-funnel-and-search-14.** Because Amazon's ranking learns from clicks, add-to-carts and purchases, a click-through or conversion change may later move exposure. effect-review expects that lag and does not call the stages independent. Sources: Amazon Search: The Joy of Ranking Products.
15. **P-funnel-and-search-15.** The observation window starts only after the change is live in the catalog, which cx confirms by reading it, not by assuming a figure. Amazon's pages state different go-live windows for different kinds of change; cx quotes the window the page covering that change states when read at run time, as an allowance before exposure is read, never as proof that the change is live. Sources: Amazon search glossary; My ASIN is unsearchable; Product title requirements and guidelines; Product image guide; Catalog Items model.
16. **P-funnel-and-search-16.** What shoppers see is the Catalog's reconciled data, which Amazon may re-classify or rewrite. cx reads the Catalog, not only its own submission, before judging what search is matching. Sources: Warning code 18367; Review listing changes; Catalog Items model.
17. **P-funnel-and-search-17.** cx says "Amazon search", not "A9" or "A10". It states no ranking weight as Amazon's, because Amazon publishes none. Sources: Seller Labs, Canopy and Eva on the A10 name; Amazon search glossary ("continuous experimentation").
18. **P-funnel-and-search-18.** Amazon's shopping assistant is called Alexa for Shopping (formerly Rufus). The only Amazon guidance to sellers about it is to keep detail-page facts current. Sources: Alexa for shopping; Amazon Rufus … smarter and more personal.

19. **P-funnel-and-search-19.** (Gap fill.) Before reading a search-funnel change, cx states that Search Query Performance and Search Catalog Performance count organic and Sponsored Products results from the search results page only, exclude widgets, and do not say whether Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Display are counted. Sources: Search Query Performance dashboard; Search Catalog Performance Dashboard.
20. **P-funnel-and-search-20.** (Gap fill.) cx never compares a metric across Amazon dashboards or files by its name alone. It names the file and field it read, and the denominator of every rate: page views, glance views, sessions, impressions or clicks. Sources: Search Query Performance dashboard ("we do not recommend comparing the metric names"); Business reports glossary; Sales and Traffic schema; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Pricing Health.
21. **P-funnel-and-search-21.** (Gap fill.) Featured Offer share is read from one source per series. Business Reports or the Sales and Traffic report give a share of page views, and Data Kiosk `featureOfferPct` gives a share of glance views. A window that spans the seller-eligibility rollout in the seller's store, as that store's help page and Amazon's announcement date it when read at run time, notes the rollout as a confounder. Sources: Business reports glossary; Sales and Traffic schema; analytics_sellerAnalytics_2025_03_31.graphql; Updates to Featured Offer eligibility requirements; Featured Offer eligibility; Becoming the Featured Offer.
22. **P-funnel-and-search-22.** (Gap fill.) Page views from the "Detail Page Sales and Traffic" view are not used as a traffic baseline, because Amazon sums them only over days with sales. cx uses the by-child view or the report's per-ASIN sessions. Sources: Business reports glossary.
23. **P-funnel-and-search-23.** (Gap fill.) The findability check adds three gates: adult classification (restricted from All Departments search), a main image that breaks the image rules (may be removed from search), and a listing deactivated for a potential pricing error. The seller's Fix Your Products, Pricing Health and Amazon's "Determine why an ASIN is not searchable" tool are the human views. Sources: Search optimization; Product image guide; Fix image compliance issues; Listing blocked due to potential pricing error; Pricing Health.
24. **P-funnel-and-search-24.** (Gap fill.) An offer that Pricing Health shows as ineligible for the Featured Offer, because it is priced above the competitive external price or well above recent prices, is reported as an offer problem. No listing rewrite is proposed for it. Sources: Pricing Health; Becoming the Featured Offer.
25. **P-funnel-and-search-25.** (Gap fill.) cx does not quote the Listing Quality Indicator as a forecast of traffic or sales; Amazon calls it directional and says it ignores price, fulfillment and seasonality. Sources: Enhance Listings.
26. **P-funnel-and-search-26.** (Gap fill.) A variation change (adding, removing, splitting or re-theming children) is logged as a funnel change in its own right. It can move the shared rating and review count shown in search and on the page, and Amazon ties existing ASINs' history to search relevancy. It is never bundled in one window with a copy change. Sources: Variation relationship FAQ; Guidelines for reviews shared across product variations; Product variations overview.
27. **P-funnel-and-search-27.** (Gap fill.) A deal, coupon or other badge that appears in search results is a click-through change. A window in which one starts or ends is not used to judge a title or image change. Sources: Prime Day readiness playbook (deal badging "in search"); Amazon search glossary (badges in a result); Enroll products in our Climate Pledge Friendly program.
28. **P-funnel-and-search-28.** (Gap fill.) Return-driven listing fixes (size charts, compatibility, images) are judged on Voice of the Customer NCX rates and the frequently-returned badge over weeks, not on the first week's conversion. Sources: Voice of the Customer; Resolve your CX Health issues; Frequently returned item badge; Enhance Listings.
29. **P-funnel-and-search-29.** (Gap fill.) When a change targets click-through, cx reads the ASIN's mobile-app share of sessions for the baseline and the window, because results render and rank differently by device. Sources: Sales and Traffic schema; Search optimization; Product title requirements and guidelines.
30. **P-funnel-and-search-30.** (Gap fill.) cx says Amazon states that A+ image alt-text helps search, and that no Amazon page says whether A+ body text is searched. An A+ change is judged at conversion. Sources: Create A+ content; A+ Content guide; A+ Content and SEO (Glenn_Amazon); A+ Content (hides plain-text description).
31. **P-funnel-and-search-31.** (Gap fill.) cx tells the seller that Amazon's AI surfaces (Alexa for Shopping, Help Me Decide, AI Shopping Guides, Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands prompts) read detail-page and catalog data, reviews and price history. It offers no "AI ranking" advice, because Amazon publishes none. Sources: Alexa for shopping; Help Me Decide; Amazon AI Shopping Guides; Sponsored Products prompts and Sponsored Brands prompts.
32. **P-funnel-and-search-32.** (Gap fill.) No verdict is given for a window that overlaps a major sale. The year's event dates are read at run time from Amazon's announcements, never from memory (the 2026 dates read today are in E13, for human readers). Sources: When is Amazon Prime Day 2026?; Prime Big Deal Days is back October 6-7; Holiday 2026: Same fees, same eligibility, earlier deadlines.
33. **P-funnel-and-search-33.** (Gap fill.) A stockout or a delivery-promise change is logged as a confounder whose effect may outlast it. The window restarts only after the offer is buyable again and Amazon's stated offer-change latency has passed, and cx compares the following weeks with the competitor set before crediting any listing change. Sources: Search optimization (offer-change latency, figure read from the page at run time); My ASIN is unsearchable; Measuring and Mitigating the Costs of Stockouts (Anderson, Fitzsimons & Simester 2006); The Value of Rapid Delivery in Omnichannel Retailing (Fisher, Gallino & Xu 2019).
