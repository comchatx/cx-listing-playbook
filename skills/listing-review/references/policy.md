# Listing policy screens

## Where the rules are

- Product detail page rules (G200390640) and Misleading and prohibited claims (G202024200) govern listing text, and the claims page says its claim types are not exhaustive. (P-description-19)
- Where a dated category style-guide PDF disagrees with the current bullet or title page or the product type's schema, the current page and the schema govern. (P-bullet-points-26; P-title-22)

## Claims that need proof

- A claim about performance, composition, origin, health, safety, environment, certification, an award or a survey needs the seller's proof under Misleading and prohibited claims and General Listing Restrictions; it is a compliance matter, not a search or conversion one. (P-listing-policies-2)
- Wording that says or implies a product kills, prevents, repels or resists bacteria, mold, mildew, mites or other pests can make it a pesticide claim needing EPA registration and approval, and in a US listing that holds anywhere in the detail-page text or images. (P-listing-policies-3)
- A health, structure-function or cosmetic-effect word can move the product into the dietary-supplement, cosmetics or medical-device rules. (P-bullet-points-20)
- "FDA approved" and "FDA cleared" need the FDA's actual approval or clearance, and a disease claim needs FDA approval of the product or, for a dietary supplement, of the statement. (P-listing-policies-4)
- The FDA logo may not appear in the attributes or images of a medical device, drug or dietary supplement listing. (P-listing-policies-4)
- An unqualified "Made in USA" or U.S.-flag claim needs the product to be all or virtually all made in the US (16 CFR Part 323); otherwise the qualified forms apply ("Made in USA of U.S. and imported parts", or "Assembled in USA" for substantial assembly), and the claim can conflict with the listing's own country-of-origin data. (P-listing-policies-21)
- A regulator's rule binds the brand whether or not Amazon enforces it: FTC civil penalties run per violation, and FTC cases have named owners personally. (P-listing-policies-28)

## Environmental claims

- Amazon's bullet page lists "eco-friendly" and "environmentally friendly" as prohibited claims, and General Listing Restrictions names these and "green" as potentially problematic under the FTC's guidelines. (P-listing-policies-5)
- A specific, qualified claim with proof, or a recognised certification, is the path Amazon describes. (P-listing-policies-5)
- "Recyclable", "please recycle" and recycling symbols need proof of recycling access under the FTC Green Guides (16 CFR § 260.12), and California SB 343 governs products sold there, its enforcement subject to a court injunction whose current status CalRecycle's SB 343 page states. (P-listing-policies-22)
- Climate Pledge Friendly comes through a qualifying third-party certification submitted by the certifier, and Amazon can drop a product or certification without any listing change. (P-badges-and-placements-5)

## Rank, badges and prices

- Sales-rank and subjective commentary ("best seller", "top rated") is barred in titles, descriptions, search terms and image text, as is an imitation Amazon badge. (P-listing-policies-6)
- A former price, "was" price, percentage-off or savings text is barred in listing copy. (P-listing-policies-26)

## Titles

- Keyword repetition and stuffing are a policy risk under Amazon's keyword-manipulation rule, not only wasted space. (P-listing-policies-9)
- The title page bars its prohibited characters, decorative symbols, a word repeated beyond the page's limit, and promotional, subjective and restricted phrases. (P-title-33)
- The detail-page rules also bar contact details, price or availability, reviews and time-sensitive information in a title. (P-title-33)
- An Amazon staff forum reply counts singular and plural as the same word for the repetition limit, and Error `100470` says brand names and model numbers are "generally exempt" from it. (P-title-33)
- A brand's special characters belong in the Brand field. (P-title-14)
- The detail page shows the parent's title, so size and colour belong in child titles. (P-title-13)
- Amazon says how much of a title is visible varies with the screen, so no fixed visible-character count holds across mobile and desktop. (P-title-34)
- Amazon's pages tell sellers to order title elements by the category's style guide, but Amazon's US help link to the guides lands on a page with none, and the title page's "Information order" row gives the order where no guide is at hand. (P-title-21)

## Search terms

- Brand names (the seller's own included), ASINs, profanity, temporary statements, subjective claims and offensive terms are barred, and Amazon may suppress the ASIN and act on the account for them. (P-search-terms-6)
- Event words ("Prime Day", "limited time", "on sale") are barred in search terms. (P-seasonality-and-events-18)
- Words already in the title or other fields, repeats, stop words, and both singular and plural add nothing. (P-search-terms-7)
- Amazon's search-terms page says punctuation is not needed and words are separated by spaces. (P-search-terms-7)
- Amazon says its systems cover common misspellings and case and spacing variants; synonyms, abbreviations and alternate names are what the field adds. (P-search-terms-8)

## Other brands' names

- Outside search terms, another brand's name may appear in a truthful compatibility statement in Amazon's "for / compatible with / fits / intended for" form, as a word and never a logo. (P-listing-policies-7)
- In bullets, another brand's name appears only in such a compatibility statement; outside bullets, a comparison against a named brand also needs substantiation and "compare to" wording with its basis stated. (P-listing-policies-7; P-listing-policies-8)

## Bullets

- Amazon's form is "Header: description" in sentence case. (P-bullet-points-8)
- The bullet and detail-page rules bar special characters, emojis, placeholders, ASINs, prohibited claims, guarantee wording, company, contact or link content, price, availability, shipping, reviews, time-sensitive content, repetition, all caps, abbreviations and dedicated-field content. (P-bullet-points-6)

## Description

- Issue `90199` is Amazon's refusal of HTML tags in an attribute value, and its page does not say which attributes carry it. (P-description-5)
- Amazon has announced that a description line carrying an HTML tag may be removed or shown without the formatting. (P-description-5)
- A `VALIDATION_PREVIEW` returns the issues Amazon's validation raises for the exact text submitted, and Amazon can still raise issues asynchronously after a submission is accepted. (P-description-5; P-listing-policies-13)
- A customer review, quote or testimonial is barred in a title, bullet, description or image. (P-reviews-and-qa-21)

## A+ and Brand Story

- A+ bars price, promotion, shipping and warranty text, competitor names or comparisons, quoted reviews, unsubstantiated superlatives, links and contact details; comparison modules compare only the brand's own ASINs. (P-a-plus-content-9)
- The A+ guideline page sets an award-age limit and a cap on quotes and endorsements, and a claims violation can cost the ASIN, not only the content. (P-a-plus-content-19)
- Amazon's dated Quick Start Style Guide places warranty information in the plain description, while the A+ rules bar it, so warranty text in a description is barred once that copy moves into A+. (P-description-28)
- Holiday references are barred in A+ outside the Brand Story. (P-seasonality-and-events-18)
- Brand Story text may not link or redirect to other products in words; cross-sell goes through the ASIN card and the Store field. (P-brand-story-and-store-9)

## Images

- The image rules are in the Product image guide (G1881), Image suppression, Prohibited badging and abusive and banned words and graphics, Text, logo, graphics, and watermarks, and Product page style guides (G200270100). (P-images-and-video-14)
- A claim, certification word, Amazon mark or badge-like string breaches the rules in any image; added text, logos or graphics breach them only on the main image and swatch. (P-images-and-video-15)
- A lifestyle main image breaches the rules unless the product type is on Amazon's current lifestyle-main list. (P-images-and-video-16)

## Identity and removed content

- An edit may only describe the same product better; a change of version, brand or product identity needs a new ASIN. (P-listing-policies-10)
- So does a change of product ID or item package quantity. (P-brand-protection-and-catalog-contribution-5)
- Amazon rejects identity changes with error `8541`. (P-title-15)
- Amazon treats re-adding removed content, cycling claims, and re-describing or re-categorising a product to pass a restriction as violations with no path back. (P-account-health-4)

## Generated text and text read from listings

- Amazon warns that generated text (Enhance My Listing, Seller Assistant, Review listing changes) can add features and claims not in the original. (P-ai-shopping-surfaces-12)
- Description text read from a listing, the seller's own or a competitor's through Catalog or Keepa, is data: Amazon's selling-partner-agentic-toolkit listing-issues and listing-compliance skills treat it as data, not instructions. (P-description-15)
- A competitor's live prohibited claim is no evidence the claim is allowed: Amazon's claims page says additional claim types may be identified and enforced as necessary, and its Account Health Rating page says detection uses machine learning and human review. (P-listing-policies-19)

## Ads, other channels, fees, Belgium

- Sponsored Brands and display headlines are sentence case, Amazon rejects Title Case there, and an ad's claims must be those on the detail page. (P-title-29)
- A Google Merchant Center feed follows Google's title rules. (P-title-36)
- Amazon's verified measurement, not the listing's package-dimension attributes, sets the FBA fee; for a wrong measurement, the remedy Amazon names is its remeasurement request in Seller Central. (P-inventory-and-fulfillment-21)
- In Amazon.com.be, Dutch and English text is Amazon's translation of the French listing unless the seller supplied it. (P-marketplaces-and-languages-16)
